TITLE: 10. LAY FOLLOWERS MUST ALSO HAVE VIRTUE
TOMTAT: Mr. Ly, a workman, took refuge in the Buddha and volunteered at the pagoda — but carried offerings home to share with his wife and children, and sat in meditation demanding to be waited on. His wife came to argue the matter and received a teaching on the virtue a lay follower must have.
-----
{align:center|**LAY FOLLOWERS MUST ALSO HAVE VIRTUE**}

{align:justify|In 1993, Mr. Ly, a workman, came to my home to hear the Master teach the Dharma and aroused faith in the Buddha. Half a month later he requested refuge in the Three Jewels, and even brought his "refuge certificate" to show me.}

{align:justify|From then on, every Sunday holiday he went to the pagoda for volunteer service, willingly serving as a guard keeping order for Dharma assemblies.}

{align:justify|Half a month passed. I was discussing the Buddha Dharma with friends at home when Mr. Ly arrived with his wife; greetings done, they sat aside listening to our talk.}

{align:justify|When our discussion ended, Mrs. Ly spoke out:}

{align:justify|"Today I came intending to argue reason with you, brother. But just now, hearing what you told everyone — all guiding and leading them toward the good... Last month, when my husband visited your home, he went back saying he 'wanted to be a lay follower.' Half a month later he took refuge as a lay Buddhist..."}

{align:justify|She paused, then spoke with grievance:}

{align:justify|"But I do not understand what a 'lay follower' is! I had hoped my husband learned something good or would do some good deed. Who knew — from that day on, every Sunday he goes to the pagoda, calling it volunteer service, Dharma protection... At night he brings home fruit, incense, oil... saying that nowadays so many believe in the Buddha, offerings pile up beyond counting — oil, incense, fruits, cakes... everything. The monastics cannot eat it all! So lay volunteers must share it out. Hearing that, I thought it no different from going out to earn money — yet he boasts of volunteering to protect the pagoda! I find it most unreasonable. More absurd still: every day home from work, he folds his legs, boasting he sits in meditation... Yet every moment he calls me to bring water, every moment calls for food. I say 'I am busy cooking — serve yourself'... and he says he is sitting, his legs cannot unfold! It infuriates me to death — being a 'lay follower' like that is being... everyone's master, is it? Has he been enthroned, become some high-ranking personage? If so, I too want to be a lay follower sitting in cultivation — and we shall see who waits on whom! Today I come with a bellyful of anger to dispute reason with you, brother!"}

{align:justify|Her outburst shook me utterly. So the rumor I had heard — of "Buddhist lay followers scrambling to divide pagoda offerings" — was true? Today I heard it with my own ears: living, evident proof. I have even heard that uneven shares of offerings led to brawls!}

{align:justify|I once read in a sutra: "A single grain of a donor's rice is heavy as Mount Sumeru; eat without practicing, and you must repay it wearing fur and bearing horns."}

{align:justify|Then how can the goods the faithful offer the Three Jewels — supporting monks and nuns in cultivation, sustaining the Dharma forever — be carried home by laypeople? And brawls of greedy contention besides — a most guilty affair!}

{align:justify|Monastics receive offerings of the ten directions; if they fail to practice rightly, they fall into evil paths — how much more laypeople at home? In future they will not only fall into hell, hard to climb out, but the blessings they presently enjoy may be utterly lost! Take an example: if a monk's discipline is pure, his merit is like rich, fertile, productive soil. The good of the ten directions come to offer — sowing seeds of blessing upon that soil. The soil will surely repay the diligent sowers richly.}

{align:justify|If a monk's discipline is impure, he is like poor, barren, arid soil, yielding no good harvest to anyone. Even his own harvest is overrun by wild weeds — in the end, crop failure is hard to escape.}

{align:justify|How much more we, as lay followers, whose field of merit cannot compare with monastics' — how can we bring our foul habits, brimming with greed, hatred, and delusion, to trouble the pure gate of the Buddha?}

{align:justify|Master Miaofa once said: "If donors offer food the monks cannot finish, inconvenient to store, it may be shared with laypeople to carry home — but only with the abbot's consent. And laypeople receiving gifts, wishing to conserve blessings, may roughly value what they take and drop money into the merit box to create further merit. Or volunteers may carry goods to market, sell them, and bring the money back to the pagoda — this too accords with the Dharma: knowing to accumulate merit, knowing to fear wasting blessings. Protecting the pagoda is a lay follower's duty — how can it be used as pretext, in that name, to rake in goods? And to stir greedy hearts into brawling contention? Such deluded conduct only brings the worst disrepute upon Buddhism."}

{align:justify|On this occasion, I earnestly beg all lay Dharma friends: after work, find time to recite the Kshitigarbha Bodhisattva's Original Vows Sutra and the Emperor Liang Repentance Sutra. With sincere and fervent hearts in recitation, we shall surely arouse great shame and remorse, dissolve hindrances, and reap immeasurable blessing.}

{align:justify|Our purpose in studying Buddhism is to reap wisdom, to turn our backs on dust and unite with awakening — washing away the three poisons of greed, hatred, and delusion steeped so long in this turbid age, from beginningless lives until now. Once a wise person recognizes a bad trait or habit as the cause of falling into the three evil paths (hell, hungry ghosts, beasts), how can he see the error and offend again? Plunging himself into the abyss, through ten thousand lives hard to climb out?}

{align:justify|Laypeople coming to the pagoda must study the Buddha Dharma so as to "plunge deeply into the sutra treasury, wisdom vast as the sea" — ever learning and practicing the Buddha's conduct. Only when true wisdom opens shall we know deep faith in karmic retribution and take care; only then shall we be moved by the toilsome saving hearts of Buddhas, bodhisattvas, and saintly monks.}

{align:justify|The Buddha never shields or indulges anyone for bearing a "refuge card," nor sends down punishment upon whoever lacks one.}

{align:justify|The Buddha ever proclaims the truth with a compassionate and earnest heart, hoping all may awaken from delusion and return, leaving suffering and gaining joy.}

{align:justify|But the path of this human life each of us must walk ourselves. Understanding this, we lay followers must know how to conserve virtue and accumulate blessing — coming to the pagoda with hearts of self-correction, humility, Dharma-protecting and monk-supporting, sincerely studying the teaching, acting rightly as the Buddha taught, perfecting self-benefit and the benefit of others.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story LAY FOLLOWERS MUST ALSO HAVE VIRTUE is a stern wake-up call to lay followers "in name but not in deed." Mr. Ly took refuge and volunteered at the pagoda, yet carried offerings home to share with wife and children, and sat in "meditation" demanding his wife wait on him — conduct greedy and arrogant, wholly contrary to the Buddhist spirit of "benefiting self and others." His wife asked on all our behalf: "What is a lay follower? Is it to be waited upon and to carry offerings home?" The answer: a lay follower must have virtue — conserve blessings, know shame, and practice truly. Receiving offerings requires the abbot's permission, and one should drop money in the merit box to create merit. The story warns: "A single grain of a donor's rice is heavy as Mount Sumeru; eat without practicing, and you must repay it wearing fur and bearing horns." Whoever bears the lay follower's name, examine your own heart — never let "the Buddha's pure gate" become a nursery for foul habits.}
