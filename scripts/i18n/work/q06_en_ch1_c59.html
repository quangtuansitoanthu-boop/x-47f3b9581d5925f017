Matanga's daughter was a prostitute, but her time had come; when Ananda returned to Jetavana Monastery, she followed. Right after hearing the Buddha speak Dharma, she certified to the third Arhat fruition. In the end she certified to the fourth Arhat fruition. And she was the first prostitute to certify to fruition. So to rescue prostitutes, Bodhisattvas may transform into prostitutes, for if they join the same profession and are friendly, whatever they say is trusted by those they wish to rescue. For instance, a college student may say, "I believe the Buddhadharma; it is truly sublime. I shall investigate this sutra, this sutra right now." The other student will say: "I wish to go too. I also wish to study that sutra." So they go together to study the Shurangama Sutra. It is just so.

Thus you will never understand who is a Bodhisattva, who is an Arhat. But if you are one of them, do not tell anyone. Do not go about proclaiming, "I am a Bodhisattva. You should listen to what I say." Why must you not do so? Because the Buddha forbids it. So Bodhisattvas and Arhats do the same things people do, but in doing so, they praise the Buddha Vehicle, causing their body and mind to enter samadhi. They may immerse themselves in the same work as others, but to speak the Buddhadharma at every fitting moment. "The Buddhadharma is most fine! Nothing compares." And in this way, they will captivate listeners, as if they were eating a candy stick.

That reminds me of a story in the Sayings Record. Long ago in China, in the Tang dynasty, there was a monk named Du Shun. He often lectured sutras and spoke Dharma, and he also taught Chan investigation. Sometimes he taught people to recite the Buddha's name. He used every method to teach people. He had a disciple who had left home to study with him for more than ten years. Daily, the disciple paid close attention to his teacher's conduct. He tried to understand who his teacher was — that is, whether the teacher was a Bodhisattva, an Arhat, or perhaps a Buddha? Finally, after ten years, he concluded that his teacher, Dharma Master Du Shun, was an ordinary person, nothing unusual about him at all. The teacher ate as everyone else ate. The teacher dressed as everyone dressed. The teacher slept as everyone slept. The teacher had nothing more than others. So the disciple decided his teacher was not a Buddha, Bodhisattva, or Arhat. Therefore he came before the teacher, bowed farewell, and asked to leave the teacher.

What was his intention?
He intended to go to Wutai Mountain to bow to Manjushri Bodhisattva.
He intended to seek wisdom from Manjushri Bodhisattva hoping for enlightenment. He said: "Master, I have stayed with you more than ten years, and I find I have learned nothing. I understand nothing; I am truly stupid, and I have decided to go bow to Manjushri Bodhisattva hoping I shall gain wisdom." "Very well!" the teacher said, "You wish to pilgrimage to Wutai Mountain, then go. I have two letters; on the way please deliver them for me. One to deliver to Miss Qing Liang and one to Old Mother Pig."
