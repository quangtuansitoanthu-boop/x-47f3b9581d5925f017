TITLE: 15. A FATHER BEGGING FOR DELIVERANCE
TOMTAT: Dr. Hoang suffered a strange illness — punching and kicking by night, normal by morning — because his father in an evil realm possessed his son's body to cry for rescue. Meeting Master Miaofa, the father was delivered, the son took refuge, and the illness ended.
-----
{align:center|**A FATHER BEGGING FOR DELIVERANCE**}

{align:justify|There was a physician named Hoang with a most strange illness: as soon as night fell, his eyes stared fixed, he spoke no word, slept not at all, and punched with his fists and kicked with his feet... yet at dawn he returned to normal.}

{align:justify|They say that since his illness began, six specialists had examined him, all helpless and without remedy. His wife tried the qigong treatments then in fashion, but Dr. Hoang believed in none of it and flatly refused to go.}

{align:justify|Afterward, Dr. Hoang's wife, warmly introduced by Professor Ly, resolved to beg the Master to examine her husband.}

{align:justify|Mrs. Hoang said her husband believed in not even qigong, so she feared he could never be brought for the Master to see — her husband held "it is all superstition!"}

{align:justify|Home again, Mrs. Hoang told the tale to sound out her husband. To her surprise, Dr. Hoang's eyes lit up and he hurriedly asked:}

{align:justify|"What is that monk's name?"}

{align:justify|Hearing the words "Master Miaofa," he grew so eager he could not wait — insisting on contacting Professor Ly at once, even voicing the wish to honor the Master as his teacher.}

{align:justify|Days later I joined Professor Ly and Dr. Hoang and his wife in a car to Mount Wutai to honor the Master before nightfall. Since I had suggested telephoning ahead, the Master was prepared.}

{align:justify|The Master asked Dr. Hoang:}

{align:justify|"Is your father living or dead?"}

{align:justify|Mrs. Hoang answered for her husband: "Dead nearly two years..."}

{align:justify|The Master said:}

{align:justify|"You have two elder brothers, yet your father loved you most?"}

{align:justify|By now night had fallen; Dr. Hoang's eyes began to stare fixed, and he uttered not a word.}

{align:justify|His wife still answered for him:}

{align:justify|"Master, you speak most truly. His two brothers live in their homeland, while my husband lives in the city. Since childhood, his father loved him most."}

{align:justify|The Master explained:}

{align:justify|"His father in life bore rather heavy killing karma, so after death he must suffer in an evil realm. Knowing his son possessed Buddhist affinity and could rescue him, he repeatedly visited him in dreams. But he did not believe. So to escape the bitter realm, he was forced to the desperate measure of 'possessing his son's body' — compelling him to the Buddha's gate to beg rescue. See — are his eyes not already normal again?"}

{align:justify|At this all turned to look at Dr. Hoang: his face had grown rosy and fresh, his eyes lively and normal once more.}

{align:justify|The Master continued:}

{align:justify|"Wait a moment — first take him up to the main hall to honor the Buddha."}

{align:justify|Tomorrow a deliverance tablet shall be enshrined for his father in the Kshitigarbha Hall; through the monks' power of sutra recitation, his father shall leave suffering and gain joy.}

{align:justify|Suddenly Dr. Hoang knelt at the Master's feet, his face resolute, pleading:}

{align:justify|"Master, I wish to honor you as my teacher! Please accept me!"}

{align:justify|Hearing this, Mrs. Hoang's face showed worry and tension, at a loss what to do.}

{align:justify|Only the Master smiled and said:}

{align:justify|"Very well — be a Buddhist disciple at home!"}

{align:justify|When I explained to Mrs. Hoang what a "disciple at home" meant, she breathed relief — all along she had anxiously misunderstood, thinking her husband demanded... to leave home as a monk!}

{align:justify|We too bowed farewell to the Master and carried offerings up to the main hall, all honoring the Buddha together.}

{align:justify|That night we stayed in the guest quarters outside the monastery. After more than half a year of turmoil, Dr. Hoang at last slept in peace.}

{align:justify|Now Dr. Hoang practices most diligently. By nature he has ever been a man of few words, but hearing others praise him, his face brightens and he merrily says:}

{align:justify|"This is my 'disaster' turned to 'blessing'!"}

{align:justify|The experienced physician — once the victim, directly tasting a bizarre illness no medicine could cure — through an accomplished eminent monk's aid saw his father freed from old karma, and himself now deeply joined to Buddhism.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story A FATHER BEGGING FOR DELIVERANCE teaches of "creditors of former lives — possessing a son's body to beg rescue." Dr. Hoang's strange illness (punching and kicking by night, normal by morning) came because his father, in an evil realm and knowing his son's Buddhist affinity, "possessed his son's body" to force him to the pagoda to pray for deliverance. Once the father received a tablet in the Kshitigarbha Hall and sutras were recited for his deliverance, the son's illness ended at once. The story stresses: "With illness, seek the very root" — not every sickness is of the body; it may be past-life creditors or the spirits of kin begging rescue. Reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra and enshrining deliverance tablets for parents and ancestors is great merit — freeing them from suffering while dissolving calamity for descendants. That is why so many, after repenting to karmic creditors or delivering kin, see illness and ill fortune dissolve.}
