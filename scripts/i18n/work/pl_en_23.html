CHAU THI

The orchid house stands quiet and lonely, sparse chrysanthemums by the fence; autumn's scene desolate, yet the lotus woman's faith and vows hold firm! Such was the household of Chau Thi, keeping her hours in earnest Buddha-recitation.

Chau Thi was the wife of the Chuong family. From youth she revered Great Being Avalokiteshvara, deeply believed cause and effect, and was gentle, filial, and fond of good. At sixty-eight she first heard of the Pure Land gate's benefits, and at once kept lifelong vegetarian diet, aloud reciting ten thousand utterances, silently reciting in every time and place besides. Such practice continued more than ten years. In the final years, to avoid disturbance, Chau Thi often sat quietly in a private room, practice ever more intimate. At times she sat still, eyes closed, facing west, for two or three days without food or drink, the pulse at her wrist no longer beating — yet her face bright and fresh, her whole body warm. Afterward, when the family asked of her state then, she answered: "I saw myself seated reciting upon a large fair lotus throne, forgetting time itself, my heart bright, light, and joyful!".

In the twentieth year of the Republic, about the sixth month, Chau Thi suddenly fell ill. Her son led the household in turn-by-turn supporting recitation. Some days before her passing, she told her family: "I shall linger two days more; rest yourselves. Prepare fragrant water for my bath, and robes and shoes all fresh and clean!". Two days later, at the Si hour, she sat upright facing west, eyes closed, saying nothing. Her son, seeing it, called the household to gather and loudly chant together. Some dozens of minutes later, Chau Thi sat peacefully and breathed her last, her face fresh, joyful, and gentle. More than two hours later, her crown was still warm.

Most wondrous: during the encoffining rite, her little granddaughter, weeping excessively, collapsed in a faint. The child's mother — Chau Thi's daughter-in-law — had herself been reborn through recitation some years before. When the family revived the little girl, she said: "I saw from the West a great vast radiance stream forth. Avalokiteshvara, majestic and fair, willow branch in hand, followed by countless bodhisattvas riding midair, came flying. In a moment Mother and Grandmother, taller and fairer than in life, followed the bodhisattvas flying to the West. Countless strange blossoms, banners, and jeweled canopies moreover appeared midair, all adorned in extraordinary splendor. Beholding the scene, my heart grew suddenly joyful, forgetting grief and weeping. Now I sorrow no more, for I clearly know Mother and Grandmother have returned to the Buddha's land!".

A comment: All born into the Land of Bliss, regardless of sex, age, or youth, manifest as male bodies complete with the thirty-two excellent marks. The child's vision of mother and grandmother still in female bodies is skillful manifestation of former forms, that she might easily recognize them. Readers reaching this passage should not doubt!
