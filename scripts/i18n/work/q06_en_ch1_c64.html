Translation: Like a poor person falsely calling himself emperor, bringing execution upon himself. How much more the Dharma King's position — how dare one falsely usurp it! With the causal ground not straight, the fruition will be crooked. Seeking the Buddhas' bodhi like one trying to bite one's own navel — who shall succeed?

Commentary: "Do you know?" Someone says, "I am the king of this country?" Speaking so, like a poor person falsely calling himself emperor, bringing execution upon himself.

The king will imprison that person at once, and that person's entire family will also suffer death. All friends and relatives will likewise be condemned to death. Then where does the "king" go? Proclaiming oneself certified to sagely fruition when uncertified is like that poor person calling himself king. He will be executed for that crime. And if one cannot casually call oneself king in worldly matters,
How much more the Dharma King's position — how dare one falsely usurp it!
How dare one falsely claim Buddhahood?
With the causal ground not straight, the fruition will be crooked.

On the causal ground, while cultivating the Way, if you do not cultivate truly, the results you enjoy in the future will be crooked. There will be a great many snags. You will never perfect the fruition directly. If you cultivate this way, you must pass through countless great kalpas, yet still not perfect it. Seeking the Buddhas' bodhi like one trying to bite one's own navel — who shall succeed?

If you practice in this way — continuing to indulge in lying and boasting while seeking the Buddhas' bodhi — then you are like one trying to bite one's own navel. How can there be success?

You will never bite your own navel, for your mouth can never reach it.

Sutra text: 若諸比丘心如直絃 ,一切真實入三摩提永無魔事。我印是人成就菩薩無上知覺。

Transliteration: Nhược chư tỷ-khưu tâm như trực huyền, nhất thiết chơn thật, nhập tam-ma-đề, vĩnh vô ma sự. Ngã ấn thị nhân thành tựu Bồ-tát vô thượng tri giác.

Translation: If bhikshus' minds are like straight strings, all true and real, entering samadhi, forever without demonic deeds, I certify such people perfect the Bodhisattvas' unsurpassed knowing and enlightenment.

Commentary: If bhikshus — and the lay assemblies — minds are like straight strings, all true and real, entering samadhi, forever without demonic deeds. Your minds should be straight like lute strings, not crooked, like a lute's body. You should be true in all matters and never lie. Lying is a case of:
Beginning with a single hair,
Ending a thousand miles in the future.

If you lie in this life, it will set your perfection back several million great kalpas in later lives. Examine carefully and see who is losing out. If you can be straightforward and true, you can enter samadhi, and no demonic obstructive deeds will arise. I certify such people perfect the Bodhisattvas' unsurpassed knowing and enlightenment. All who have minds true and straight like strings can become Bodhisattvas. They can perfect a Bodhisattva's unsurpassed wisdom and enlightened insight.
