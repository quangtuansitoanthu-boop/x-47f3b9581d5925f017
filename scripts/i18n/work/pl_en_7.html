VUONG CO

Layman Vuong Co, styled Man Trong, lived under the Song at the Eastern Capital. He was the great-great-grandson of the statesman Vuong Van Dang. For seven generations his family had kept the precept against killing, and in his turn the layman vowed to release one million living beings.

While serving in Jiangxi, he studied Zen together with elder masters such as Hoi Duong and Duong Ky. He authored the Direct Pointers Resolving Doubts on the Pure Land, spreading Buddha-recitation. In leisure his rosary never left his hand; walking, standing, sitting, or lying, he cultivated pure contemplation without rest. He wrote a preface to the Precious Pearl Collection on the Pure Land as follows:

"When beings' minds are pure, the Buddha-land is pure; the Dharma-nature is unborn yet ever being born. Now there is a World Honored One dwelling in the Land of Bliss. That Buddha is now expounding the Dharma, named Amitabha. In that land, conditions are excellent and beautiful, the lifespan long. Its transforming lord's compassion and vows are vast and deep, receiving all kinds. The Buddha's light is boundless, gathering in all conscious beings of the ten directions. The scenery is wondrously fair and pure, adorned beyond conception! Jeweled nets stretch bright across the sky; jeweled trees stand in rows upon the ground. Pond waters hold the eight virtues; jade lotuses emit myriad colors. Six times daily one hears heavenly music, clear and lingering; a hundred million lands scatter wondrous flowers in offering. The Buddhas of the six directions join in praise; beings of the ten quarters take refuge in recitation and vows. The conscious within the mind are immeasurable; thought after thought they are reborn. Amitabha within beings' minds ever gathers and transforms. Entrusting one's substance to the lotus, one never leaves this very place. Roaming spiritually in the pure land, one never goes outside one's own mind. Like a mirror holding myriad images, with no coming or going; like the moon hidden in a thousand rivers, neither rising nor setting! The Dharma-gate is perfect and sudden, and all are of the rank of one more birth as successor Buddhas. The door opens to the provisional vehicle, arraying the nine grades of lotus. Reciting Immeasurable Light within one's nature — from the first there is no reciting. Born into the Land of Nurturing of the mind-only — fundamentally there is no birth. Escaping delusion and suffering, ten recitations carry one beyond to the Lotus Pond. Returning to the realm of suchness, the three vehicles together realize the fruit of wondrous awakening. A great stone, carried across, need not sink. A light boat with the current meets no obstruction. In delusion the road, though near, is far. In awakening the path is never distant.

Alas! Those of little learning and many hindrances, deep doubt and shallow wisdom, who denounce Buddha-recitation as a lesser provisional gate, or who are deluded about whether the Pure Land exists or not — do they know that to scorn Buddha-recitation is to scorn the patriarchs Yongming and Zhiyi, and to deny the Pure Land is to deny the great beings Nagarjuna and Ashvaghosa? Such persons neither believe they themselves will receive the prophecy of enlightenment nor consent to return to the homeland of original awakening. And so, caged birds and netted fish, they still sport heedlessly; in a life of wind-blown torch and fleeting shadow, they mistake a dream for permanence. When the retribution body ends, where are life's pleasures? Revolving endlessly, they vainly enter the destinies of suffering! Never thinking: the Awakened Lord exhorts us, his golden mouth never ceasing. So that one may gain holy companions in an instant and perfect marks in a moment. First receiving bliss far from the five defilements, then with increased compassion rescuing beings from the three lower paths. To neglect this is truly pitiable!".

Under Emperor Huizong, he served as Vice Minister of Revenue, was slandered and dismissed, and thereupon recited the Buddha's name and passed away. A monk whose spirit roamed the Pure Land saw Vuong Co seated upon a lotus together with Cat Phien. Cat Phien, a native of Chengjiang, rose to the office of Grand Master for Court Discussion. Whether at his office or at home, he kept a separate pure room for venerating the Buddha. One day
during worship, relics fell from the sky. Later, without illness, he sat upright facing west, recited the Buddha's name, and passed away.
