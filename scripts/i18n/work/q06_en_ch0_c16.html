Sutra text: 若有諸龍樂出龍倫。我現龍身而為說法令其成就。

Transliteration: Nhược hữu chư long nhạo xuất long luân, ngã hiện long thân, nhi vị thuyết pháp linh kỳ thành tựu.

Translation: If there are dragons who delight in transcending their draconic kind, I manifest a dragon's body, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.

Commentary: If there are dragons who delight in transcending their draconic kind. The common view of the present age is that dragons are not real. Some people acknowledge the ancient legends of dragons, very huge and terrifying. There is no way to speak with certainty about the former view, but dragons are real. Where are they? The dragon palace lies beneath the sea. "We have plumbed the depths; why have we never passed that place?" you will ask. If you could find their dwelling, they would no longer be true dragons, for dragons are spiritual beings. They have spiritual powers and can transform themselves to be large or small at will. They can make themselves as vast as empty space. They can shrink as small as a mote of dust if needed. They can suddenly vanish, then reappear in an instant.

Their spiritual powers grant them the ability to transform in countless ways. Why, with such powers, do they bear the bodies of animals? Because in former lives' cultivation, they set their minds on the Great Vehicle, but they refused to keep the precepts. They were of the type "hasty with the vehicle, lax with the precepts." They were most careless. Because they were most zealous toward Great Vehicle cultivation — hasty with the vehicle — they obtained spiritual powers. But because they did not keep the precepts, they fell among animals. If dragons resolve to transcend the dragon's body, Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva will manifest a dragon's body before them, speak Dharma for them, and enable them to accomplish their wish.

Sutra text: 若有藥叉樂度本倫。我於彼前現藥叉身。而為說法令其成就。

Transliteration: Nhược hữu dược-xoa nhạo độ bổn luân, ngã ư bỉ tiền hiện dược-xoa thân, nhi vị thuyết pháp linh kỳ thành tựu.

Translation: If there are yakshas who delight in transcending their own kind, I appear before them in a yaksha's body, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.

Commentary: If there are yakshas. "Yaksha" is Sanskrit, translated into Chinese as "swift," meaning "speedy." It is also translated as "courageous and strong," meaning brave and mighty. Yakshas are a kind of ghost-spirit. There are three main kinds of ghost-spirits:
1. Earth-traveling ghosts;
2. Flying ghosts;
3. Space-traveling ghosts.

In the Shurangama Mantra there is the line: yaksha graha. In the mantra, the names of the kings of all the ghosts are called forth. Each ghost king commands many lower ghosts, and when the king's name is called, all the other ghosts and spirits must reverently obey the king's command. If they wish to transcend their own kind — they no longer wish to be ghost-spirits — I appear before them in a yaksha's body, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.

Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva will manifest as a yaksha ghost to help them accomplish their wish.

Sutra text: 若乾闥婆樂脫其倫。我於彼前現乾闥婆身。而為說法令其成就。

Transliteration: Nhược càn-thát-bà nhạo thoátkỳ luân, ngã ư bỉ tiền hiện càn-thát-bà thân, nhi vị thuyết pháp linh kỳ thành tựu.

Translation: If there are gandharvas who delight in transcending their kind, I appear before them in a gandharva's body, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.
