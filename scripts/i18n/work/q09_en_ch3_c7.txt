<p><strong>The empty mind manifesting, even growing the understanding of eternal severance.</strong></p>
<p>The more it thinks, the more right it feels:</p>
<p>&#8220;Oh! Right &#8211; it is empty. Once you die, everything ends &#8211; all is empty!&#8221; Then it becomes convinced: everyone vanishes forever after death. The sutra text says: &#8220;Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not the realization of sagehood.&#8221; A line quoted from a sutra whenever it is printed from the original &#8211; we should print this line appended.</p>
<p><strong>This is called: &#8220;the settled mind scattered, bereft of illumination.</strong>&#8221;</p>
<p>The settled mind is now gone, hence thoughts clinging to emptiness arise, losing the ability to discern what is right. <strong>Recognized, it is without fault. That is not sage realization.</strong></p>
<p>This is not the state of realizing the sage fruition.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu không ma, nhập kì tâm phủ. Nãi báng trì giới, danh vi tiểu thừa, bồ-tát ngộ không, hữu hà trì phạm. Kì nhân thường ư, tín tâm đàn việt , ẩm tửu đạm nhục, quảng hành dâm uế. Nhân ma lực cố, nhiếp kì tiền nhân, bất sanh nghi báng. Quỷ tâm cửu nhập, hoặc thực thỉ niệu, dự tửu nhục đẳng, nhất chủng câu không. Phá Phật luật nghi, ngộ nhập nhân tội. Thất ư chánh thọ, đương tòng luân trụy. </em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: But if interpreted as sagehood, a demon of emptiness enters the very heart. It then slanders precept-keeping, calling it the Small Vehicle, saying the Bodhisattva awakened to emptiness &#8211; what keeping or violating could there be? Such a person, at the homes of faithful donors, drinks wine, eats meat, and wantonly indulges in lust. Through demonic power it subdues those before it, who raise no doubt or slander. The ghost-mind long entered, it may eat excrement and urine, wine and meat together, taking all as equally empty &#8211; breaking the Buddha's moral code, misleading people into offenses. Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>But if interpreted as sagehood</strong> &#8211; taking this for sage-fruition realization &#8211; <strong>then a demon of emptiness enters the very heart</strong>: the demon enters possessing the cultivator's body. The demon <strong>then slanders precept-keepers, calling them &#8220;Small Vehicle.&#8221;</strong> The demon says:</p>
<p>&#8220;Do not keep precepts &#8211; that is the Small Vehicle Dharma. Great Vehicle Bodhisattvas should not be troubled by such restraints. You should not pay so much heed to those precepts.&#8221;</p>
<p>Or the demon says: &#8220;The great elephant does not walk the rabbit's path. The greatly awakened should not cling to trifles. Once you are greatly awakened, nothing concerns you anymore. All dharmas are empty. Did I not say this before? Wine and meat pass through the belly while the Buddha stays in the mind. All dharmas come from mind; mind is Buddha, Buddha is mind.&#8221;</p>
<p>Such is what the demon says. The demon further slanders precept-keepers:</p>
<p>&#8220;Only those clinging to the Small Vehicle keep precepts. Great Vehicle disciples do not bother with such matters.&#8221;</p>
<p>In fact, Buddhists cultivating the Great Vehicle keep the precepts most strictly &#8211; precept-violation is rare among them. The demon only deceives the ignorant &#8211; people who never study the Buddhadharma nor carefully investigate the principles the Buddha taught. That is why, whatever the demon says, they take as a command others must obey, forcing everyone to believe the demon's words true. Why do they believe the demon's words? Only because they have never heard the Buddhadharma taught &#8211; and even hearing, would they understand what the Buddhadharma is?</p>
<p><strong>Saying: the Great Vehicle Bodhisattva awakened to the empty nature of dharmas &#8211; what keeping or violating could there be?</strong> Why keep holding the precepts or fear violating them? There are no such things. <strong>Such a person</strong> possessed by the demon, <strong>at the homes of faithful donors</strong> &#8211; Buddhist disciples who regularly support the Buddhadharma and trust it &#8211; <strong>drinks wine, eats meat, wantonly indulges in lust.</strong></p>
<p>In the sutra text, wanton lustful indulgence is most significant: for Buddhism teaches people to have no lustful mind, yet its lustful mind grows. The demon even encourages lustful acts &#8211; and people still believe, for it has demonic power. <strong>Through demonic power it still subdues everyone, raising no doubt or slander</strong> &#8211; they believe it fanatically.</p>
<p><strong>After the demon has entered the mind, it makes the person eat excrement, drink urine, eat meat and drink wine, taking all these as equally empty.</strong></p>
<p>Since the cultivator is already demon-possessed, he does not know excrement unclean, and regularly drinks urine. It claims excrement and urine are &#8220;neither defiled nor pure,&#8221; borrowing phrases from the Prajna Sutra to argue falsely &#8211; misunderstanding the sutras' meaning. The demon behaves showing disregard for everything, clean or filthy alike &#8211; proclaiming eating meat and drinking wine empty, eating excrement and drinking urine empty: in short, taking all as empty.</p>
<p><strong>It breaks the Buddha's moral code, misleading people into offenses</strong>. Then <strong>losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong>10 &#8211; MAD GREEDY LOVE (THE DEMON OF GREEDY LOVE)</strong></p>
<p><strong><em><u>Sutra text</u></em></strong><strong><em>: Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch. Vị kì không minh, thâm nhập tâm cốt. Kì tâm hốt hữu vô hạn ái sanh. Ái cực phát cuồng, tiện vi tham dục. Thử danh định cảnh, an thuận nhập tâm, vô huệ tự trì, ngộ nhập chư dục. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Translation</u></strong><strong>: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; delighting in savoring that empty brightness, deeply entering to heart and bone &#8211; suddenly boundless love arises in the mind. Love carried to the extreme turns to madness, becoming greed and lust. This is called the samadhi-state settling smoothly into the mind, lacking the wisdom to restrain oneself, mistakenly entering the desires. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization.</strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Commentary</u></strong>: <strong>Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve.</strong></p>
<p>When the cultivator has attained samadhi within the scope of the feeling skandha &#8211; seeing the form skandha dissolve, the form skandha gone &#8211; <strong>the feeling skandha clearly revealed</strong>, clearly perceiving the feeling skandha. <strong>Delighting in savoring the empty brightness</strong> &#8211; the state the cultivator has realized &#8211; now <strong>deeply entering to heart and bone</strong>: a sudden psychological shift occurs. <strong>Suddenly boundless love arises in the mind</strong> &#8211; like someone saying: &#8220;I love everyone, and I love gossiping about such things.&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>Love carried to the extreme turns to madness, becoming greed and lust.</strong> When love and craving rise to the extreme, the cultivator goes mad with lust. The cultivator cannot restrain his emotions; grown mad, the cultivator is like the woman Liu Jin-tong I spoke of before: on meeting men she embraced them, weeping and making seductive gestures &#8211; a textbook example of this kind of demon. How could an ordinary man keep his heart unmoved before such seduction? Having enchanted men, she would say: &#8220;Take this money and buy me that piece of jewelry.&#8221; He would answer &#8220;Yes, yes!&#8221; &#8211; then she told him to take money and buy her something else, and he obediently complied. Why? Simply because she satisfied the man's lust and he was deluded by demonic power. When love reaches the extreme, a man goes mad with greed and lust &#8211; lust arising from the greedy mind.</p>
