<p><strong><em>Free translation:</em></strong><br />
<em>The ignorant teaching the ignorant,</em><br />
<em>The more learned, the more benighted;</em><br />
<em>The master falls into hell,</em><br />
<em>The disciples follow at his heels.</em></p>
<p>These words accord with the Sutra's principle: meeting no enlightened teacher, the disciple also of dull faculties &#8211; when seeing the master enter hell, he follows his master right in. The master turns and asks: &#8220;What are you doing here?&#8221;</p>
<p>The disciple answers: &#8220;Seeing my master enter here, of course I must follow.&#8221;</p>
<p>The master says: &#8220;Oh! You should not. This is no peaceful place. You should never have come!&#8221;</p>
<p>The disciple replies:</p>
<p>&#8211; &#8220;But my master entered first! How could I not follow? I studied with my master &#8211; where my master goes, I follow.&#8221;</p>
<p>The master thinks: &#8220;Ah! I myself fell into hell, dragging my disciples in with me. I truly wronged you. Alas!&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>You should know these matters beforehand, lest you enter the turning wheel. Deluded and unknowing, one falls into the uninterrupted hell.</strong></p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong><em>7 &#8211; GREED FOR KNOWLEDGE OF PAST LIVES</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Hựu thiện nam tử, thọ ấm  hư diệu, bất tao tà lự, viên định phát minh. Tam-ma-địa  trung, tâm ái tri kiến, cần khổ nghiên tầm, tham cầu túc mạng.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Again that good man &#8211; the feeling skandha empty and wondrous, untroubled by deviant concerns, the perfect samadhi disclosed. Within samadhi the mind fond of much knowledge &#8211; diligently, bitterly investigating, greedily seeking past lives.</strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Commentary</u></strong>: <strong>Again that good man &#8211; the feeling skandha empty and wondrous, untroubled by deviant concerns, the perfect samadhi disclosed.</strong> In the empty, penetrating, wondrous state after the feeling skandha has dissolved, the cultivator no longer has deviant concerns, dwelling in the bright, perfect samadhi. <strong>Within samadhi the mind fond of gaining more knowledge</strong>.</p>
<p>In samadhi the cultivator craves to understand more matters, longing for understanding of lives in past eons &#8211; putting forth all effort, fearing no hardship.</p>
<p><strong>Hence diligently, bitterly investigating, longing to know lives in past eons.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhĩ thời thiên ma, hậu đắc cầu tiện, phi tinh phụ nhân, khẩu thuyết kinh pháp. Kì nhân thù bất giác tri ma trước, diệc ngôn tự đắc vô thượng niết-bàn.  Lai bỉ cầu tri thiện nam tử xứ, phu tòa thuyết pháp. </strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Then the heavenly demon gets its sought chance, sending its essence to possess that person's body, using that person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma. That person, wholly unaware of demonic possession, likewise claims to have realized unsurpassed nirvana. Coming to the place of the good man seeking knowledge, it spreads its seat and preaches the Dharma.</strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Commentary</u></strong>: <strong>Then the heavenly demon gets its convenient chance, sending its essence to possess another's body, using that person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma</strong>. <strong>That person, unaware of being demon-possessed</strong> &#8211; wholly unaware his body has been seized and commanded by demons.</p>
<p><strong>Likewise claiming to have realized unsurpassed nirvana. Coming to the place of the good man seeking knowledge, spreading its seat, preaching the Dharma</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Thị nhân vô đoan, ư thuyết pháp xứ, đắc đại bảo châu. Kì ma hoặc thời, hóa vi súc sanh,  khẩu hàm kì châu, cập tạp trân bảo, giản sách  phù độc, chư kì dị vật. Tiên thụ bỉ nhân, trước kỳ thể. Hoặc dụ thính nhân, tàng ư địa hạ. hữu minh nguyệt châu, chiếu diệu kỳ xứ. Thị chư thính giả, đắc vị tằng hữu. Đa thực  dược  thảo,  bất xan gia thiện. Hoặc thời nhật xan, nhất ma nhất mạch, kỳ hình phì sung, quỷ lực trì cố. Phỉ báng tỳ-khưu, mạ lị đồ chúng, bất tị kị hiềm.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: That person, for no reason, at the Dharma-preaching place obtains a great precious pearl. At times the demon transforms into an animal, its mouth holding that pearl, along with assorted jewels, bamboo tallies, talismans, and sundry strange things &#8211; first presenting them to that person, then possessing his body. Or it deludes its listeners by burying something underground, saying: there is a moon-bright pearl illuminating that place &#8211; so its listeners feel they gain what never was. It mostly eats medicinal herbs, not fine prepared food; or at times eats but a sesame seed, a wheat grain a day, its form plump and full, upheld by ghost power. Slandering bhikshus, scolding its followers, heedless of ridicule and suspicion.</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Commentary</em></strong>: <strong>At the Dharma assembly, that person suddenly as if obtains a great precious pearl</strong> &#8211; perhaps a diamond or a wish-fulfilling pearl.</p>
<p><strong>At times the demon transforms into an animal, its mouth holding jewels, bamboo tallies, talismans, and other strange things</strong>, claiming Bodhisattvas can transform their bodies into anything &#8211; the animal-mouth the demon has transformed holding jewels, bamboo tallies, talismans.</p>
<p><em>Bamboo tallies (jian-ce)</em> were used for records in ancient times: characters written on one face, then the bamboo slip split in two, the writing showing on each face's surface. When these tallies were needed as credentials, the two halves were joined: matching meant the tally counted as a credential; non-matching halves meant untrustworthy.</p>
<p><em>Talismans</em> are used to subdue and seize demons, ghosts, and goblins &#8211; and the animal the demon transforms into may carry other strange things, all bizarre, rare, and very valuable.</p>
<p><strong>First presenting them to that person, then possessing his body.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Or the demon deludes the Dharma-listeners by burying something underground, saying: there is a &#8220;bright pearl&#8221; shining there.</strong></p>
<p>It buries the pearl underground, fooling the Dharma-listeners, saying: &#8220;Beneath here is a bright pearl, its light like the moon shedding light here.&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>So its listeners all feel they gain what never was.</strong></p>
<p>They marvel: &#8220;Oh! This person is genuine &#8211; he cannot possibly be a demon!&#8221;</p>
<p>He is squarely a demon, yet they insist he is not. Pity those who come to hear it lecture.</p>
<p><strong>The demon usually eats only medicinal herbs, not prepared food.</strong></p>
<p>It usually eats only medicinal herbs, not ordinary food &#8211; no fine food: it takes ginseng and some tonics.</p>
<p>When I was in Hong Kong, I met a person claiming never to eat ordinary food &#8211; he served as a spirit-medium for those praying for long life, sons, wealth, and other things. Whenever at someone else's home, he announced he ate no ordinary food. What then did he eat? Walnuts &#8211; walnut kernels containing much oil, very nourishing for the brain &#8211; a little suffices for the body to function. If one usually eats a bowl of rice, half a bowl of walnuts suffices. Or it also eats pine kernels &#8211; in short, only the most nourishing things.</p>
