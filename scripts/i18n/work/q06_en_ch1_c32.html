The monk told them: "She is Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva. The people of this village do not believe in the Buddha, so the Bodhisattva manifested as a beautiful girl to teach you, who are so lustful, to understand the Buddhadharma. Then she departed."
When the groom named Ma heard this, he renounced everything in the world and left home. Later, he certified to sagely fruition. This story is true; it happened in China.

Sutra text: 佛告阿難,汝常聞我毘奈耶中,宣說修行三決定義。所謂攝心為戒,因戒生定,因定發慧。是則名為三無漏學。

Transliteration: Phật cáo A-nan, nhữ thường văn ngã tỳ-nại-da trung, tuyên thuyết tu hành tam quyết định nghĩa. Sở vị nhiếp tâm vi giới, nhơn giới sanh định, nhơn định phát huệ. Thị tắc danh vi tam vô lậu học.

Translation: The Buddha said to Ananda: "You often hear me in the Vinaya proclaim the three decisive meanings of cultivation: gathering the mind is called precepts; from precepts samadhi arises; from samadhi wisdom unfolds. These are called the three unconditioned studies."

Commentary: When everyone in the Dharma assembly was of one mind to protect the teaching, the Buddha said to Ananda: "You often hear me in the Tathagata's Vinaya proclaim the three decisive meanings of cultivation."

"Vinaya" comprises the precepts for both the Great and Small Vehicles. Within it are the precept rules. These three certain aspects cannot be changed. That is: gathering the mind is called precepts; from precepts samadhi power arises; from samadhi there is wisdom. These are called the three unconditioned studies.

That is precepts, samadhi, wisdom. You gather the mind, like a magnet drawing all iron filings. This refers especially to our seeking, clinging minds — always seeking advantage. Driven by the seeking mind, one is always noticing others. One is always scheming to draw near the rich or the powerful. Such thoughts continue day after day. That is called the seeking mind, meaning one has not yet gathered the mind. Gather the mind back; do not let it seek to satisfy advantages. Do not let it run wild. But the mind is what we chase after, though we do not wish it so.

We may forbid it to have false thoughts, but a moment later another false thought arises. This false thought stops, another arises. The first thought has just vanished, the next at once recurs. And before the first thought has entirely vanished, the next has already arisen. The mind clinging to other things never stops. What you must do is gather your own mind. Bring the mind back to one place. The greatest reason we have not yet become Buddhas, not become enlightened, not certified to the Way, is that we cannot control our minds. If you gather all your mind-thoughts, there is nothing you cannot do. You will accomplish everything. So precepts serve to gather the mind. The precepts put an end to all evils and prevent transgressions.

From precepts samadhi power arises. Upholding precepts is like letting a cup of muddy water settle, until the mud sinks and the water becomes clear. Samadhi means "stopping — unmoving." And from samadhi there is wisdom.

As Manjushri Bodhisattva's verse says:
Samadhi utmost, perfectly bright.
When you become enlightened, within samadhi true wisdom arises. These are called the three unconditioned studies.
Through keeping precepts, from precepts there is samadhi. Through samadhi wisdom unfolds.
