TITLE: 08. BORING A HOLE TO REMOVE THE TUMOR
TOMTAT: Mr. Cao, a former navy man, developed a brain tumor requiring repeated skull drilling — from killing fish by nailing their heads to boards and gutting them alive. Meeting the Master, he repented, became vegetarian, and recited the Buddha's name, but his karma was heavy and his life was shortened.
-----
{align:center|**BORING A HOLE TO REMOVE THE TUMOR**}

{align:justify|Mr. Cao was past 40, sturdy in build, working as a guard at a large company. Three years earlier, frequent headaches sent him to hospital, where doctors discovered a brain tumor pressing on a nerve — the cause of his headaches. So the surgeons bored a hole in his skull to remove the growth. Two years later another large lump was found inside his head, and a second operation was unavoidable. This year examination showed that where the first hole had been bored, an even larger lump had grown. The doctors declared that his skull could not be bored again — it would endanger his life.}

{align:justify|Before this desperate situation, he came to beg Master Miaofa's teaching.}

{align:justify|The Master said:}

{align:justify|"You formerly served at sea, did you not? You killed a great many turtles, and caught a great many huge live fish — pinning them with nails to a board before scaling and gutting them."}

{align:justify|Mr. Cao started:}

{align:justify|"Yes! I served three years in the navy, living long years on the ocean. Every day we caught fish to eat. The deep sea yielded especially huge fish, some over a meter long; when a big fish thrashed too wildly, I would drive a great nail through its head into the board, and only then begin scaling and gutting it. Master, how can you see through my whole past so perfectly?"}

{align:justify|The Master slowly answered:}

{align:justify|"Because long ago you drove nails into fishes' heads, now you must have your own skull bored again and again for treatment. A living fish nailed to a board, thrashing in agony — still alive while you scaled it, slit its belly, scooped its entrails... a scene of utter cruelty. And so retribution visits you, time after time. I fear your skull will be bored not merely two or three times — and a swift death would be nothing to dread. What is dreadful is years of unending torment on a sickbed, a life worse than death, with no peaceful end."}

{align:justify|The Master's words struck like thunder, waking a man from a stupor.}

{align:justify|Mr. Cao pleaded urgently:}

{align:justify|"Master, your powers are vast — save your disciple, I beg you."}

{align:justify|The Master said kindly:}

{align:justify|"'Bodhisattvas fear the cause; ordinary beings fear the result.' The day has come to repay the sins of former days — you tied the knot, you must untie it. If you sincerely repent, vow to end killing for good, resolve to be fully vegetarian, and recite the Buddha's name often, you may redeem the old offense."}

{align:justify|That very day Mr. Cao began worshiping the Buddha, reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra, and enshrining deliverance tablets in the monastery for the creatures he had killed. For more than two years afterward, until he passed away, he always recited sutras and the Buddha's name. He suffered no more tormenting anguish. Only it is a pity he died before reaching 50.}

{align:justify|Reciting sutras and worshiping the Buddha could indeed spare him continued torment — but his killing karma was too heavy, and his lifespan was shortened. Vast though the Buddha's power is, it cannot outweigh beings' karma. Therefore only sincere repentance, vowing to end killing, eating no foul meaty foods, and cutting off the very seed of guilt is the true root of blessing and long life.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story BORING A HOLE TO REMOVE THE TUMOR is horrifying proof of "as you sow, so shall you reap" in the trade of killing. Mr. Cao once drove nails through living fishes' heads and gutted them while they thrashed — an act of cruelty — so his retribution was repeated skull drilling to remove brain tumors. Though he later repented, became vegetarian, recited the Kshitigarbha Sutra and the Buddha's name, his killing karma was too heavy and his life was shortened (he died before 50). The story warns: "Bodhisattvas fear the cause; ordinary beings fear the result." Once karma is sown, even practice can only lessen suffering, never wholly erase it. Whoever fishes or slaughters for a living should awaken soon and end killing for good, lest retribution fall so wretchedly upon their own bodies.}
