REPENTANCE FOR KARMIC CREDITORS: WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW
1. WHY MUST WE REPENT TO OUR KARMIC CREDITORS?
After death, a great many spirits linger in the world of the living, refusing to move on because they cannot release their attachments and hatred for this world. Among them, the most fearsome are the karmic-creditor spirits.

They are the spirits of humans or animals with whom, in this life and through countless past lives, we have forged deep hatred. In one life we seized and slaughtered them as animals for food; in another we beat, tortured, robbed, cheated, or killed them as fellow humans; in another they were fetuses aborted by their own parents — weaving countless knots of loathing and revenge.

When these karmic-creditor spirits find the one who harmed them, even if that person has since been reborn, they can still recognize them. What inevitably follows is that they attach themselves to that person, seeking revenge.

In what ways do they take revenge?
These spirits possess a measure of psychic power, greater or lesser, and they are highly intelligent, devising a rich variety of ways to strike back. They can make us fall ill — cancer in severe cases, aches and pains in mild ones, every kind of sickness. They can turn a family upside down with quarrels and torment. They can "let ghosts lead the way and demons clear the path," so that our business fails; they can even shove us or blind our eyes as we ride down the road, causing crashes, injury, or death, and so on.

Not every calamity or suffering in life is caused by karmic creditors, for often disaster is simply our own karmic retribution ripening into pain, with no spirit involved at all.

Yet vengeful karmic-creditor spirits causing illness and calamity account for a very large share — and this, too, is a form of karmic fruit, never outside cause and effect. So if you find life hemmed in by hardship, pain, sickness, and bad luck, try repenting to your karmic creditors: if after a few sessions things take a turn for the better, you are on the right path. And if not, you will have lost little — no one charges you a fee or taxes you for it.

Truthfully, nearly everyone walking this earth is trailed by vengeful karmic-creditor spirits. Most of us have killed this creature or that — or if never killing directly, have eaten the flesh and drunk the blood of a great many lives, and those beings after death mostly cling to the one who killed and ate them, waiting for a chance at revenge and becoming karmic creditors. Many have lived through the slaughter of war; many have had abortions; many have forged blood debts of love and hatred in the course of life, binding themselves as creditors to these spirits. So everyone needs to repent to their karmic creditors.

And even if someone insists they have been utterly innocent since childhood, a great many creditors from past lives are still seeking them out.

Consider National Master Ngộ Đạt, compiler of the famous Compassionate Water Repentance under Emperor Yizong of Tang. In a past life in the Han dynasty, when he was named Viên Án, he framed a man named Triệu Thố, who died wrongfully, severed at the waist. Triệu Thố's spirit trailed Master Ngộ Đạt for ten consecutive lifetimes without being able to take revenge, because through all ten lives the Master practiced diligently, and the Dharma protectors guarded him so closely that the spirit could not approach.

But in the tenth life, once he had risen to National Master at the peak of glory, pride stirred in him; the Dharma protectors withdrew, and Triệu Thố's vengeful spirit seized the moment, entering him and inflicting a monstrous illness: a human-faced abscess on his knee — what we would today call a malignant cancer — so agonizing that no imperial physician could cure it.

If even a greatly virtuous master like Ngộ Đạt, with ten lifetimes of exemplary practice, could still be struck by a karmic creditor, then none of us ordinary mortals should imagine we have no creditors. It is only that their hour to strike has not yet come.

How, then, can we dissolve the hatred of these karmic creditors? Only by repenting to them and redressing our wrongs by dedicating merit to them, until they agree to let go — and only then are we free. That is the purpose of the "Rite of Repentance for Karmic Creditors."

A great many people have swiftly recovered from illness after repenting to their karmic creditors, for the simple reason that their sickness was caused by those very spirits — and once the spirits relent, the illness vanishes. Some were dogged by terrible luck, yet merely repenting to their karmic creditors swept the bad luck away.

It is like riding a bicycle when the wheel snags a plastic bag or a wad of fiber and jams tight: no matter how hard you pedal, the bicycle stays put. But step down, pull the wad from the wheel, and it rolls normally again.

Not every case, however, shows fine results so quickly. Each person's karmic creditors differ in number: the gentle and those who seldom kill have few creditors, and repentance soon shows results.

Others, who have killed a great deal over many lives, face a host of creditors, and one or two sessions of repentance are not enough. The minds of these spirits differ too: some forgive readily, releasing you after a single repentance; others nurse grudges, still unappeased after several sessions, and need many more.

Persistence is therefore vital in delivering our creditors. If one or two sessions bring little visible change, persevere through ten or twenty more (they need not be on consecutive days — one session every few days will do), and most people will notice clear, marked improvements within that span.

In the minority of cases where creditors are legion (marked by many illnesses, much bad luck, many kinds of suffering at once), it may take a hundred sessions to see things turn for the better. And best of all, if possible, everyone should keep repenting to their karmic creditors a few times a month for years on end.

2. POINTS TO NOTE BEFORE BEGINNING
VENUE
The "Rite of Repentance and Deliverance for Karmic Creditors" may be performed right in the home of the person repenting, and that is the best place, since the creditor spirits are already there and can readily receive the repentance and deliverance.

If that is inconvenient, find another suitable place as circumstances allow — an acquaintance's house, or some quiet spot where no one will disturb you.

Temples, shrines, and even martyrs' cemeteries usually have guardian Dharma-protecting gods; sometimes the spirits are admitted, sometimes not. If you choose a temple, best obtain the abbot's consent and invite the Dharma protectors to admit the spirits.

If held at home and the house has a Buddhist altar, proceed before it (arrange the offerings for the creditors and for unborn babies on a separate table placed to the left or right of the Buddha altar).

Otherwise, arrange any clean spot (even a bedroom) where no one and nothing will disrupt the ceremony.
TIME
The best time is in the evening, after sunset, when spirits can attend more easily. Some spirits can move about by day, but not all can.

In the seventh lunar month, spirits from the underworld roam the living world freely, so deliverance rites performed then draw the largest assembly of spirits.

Still, the rites may be performed on any ordinary day of the year, with no need to fuss over choosing a date. A day spent doing good is a good day; a day spent doing evil is a bad one. Arrange whatever day suits your circumstances.
NUMBER OF PARTICIPANTS
Anyone may organize the repentance rite for karmic creditors — monastic or lay, Buddhist or not. If you can invite others to support the chanting, wonderful; if not, you can conduct it entirely alone.

In repenting to creditors, you yourself — "the one who must repent" — matter most. For every debt has its debtor and every creditor its name: your sincere words of repentance are what those spirits most wish to hear — the key that moves karmic creditors and unborn babies to forgive, lay down their hatred, and accept deliverance.

Repentance for creditors may be performed by many people together.
PREPARATIONS
However the offering tray is arranged, for any rite it must be vegetarian — absolutely no meat, fish, savory foods, or alcohol. No need for a lavish feast, nor for votive paper goods, for the sound of chanting sutras and the Buddha's name is countless times more precious than any worldly offering.

This was witnessed in unison at Trường Sơn Martyrs' Cemetery by the psychic Nguyễn Văn Nhã together with a host of renowned Vietnamese psychics. Standing amid the cemetery, Nguyễn Văn Nhã joined his palms in reverence and recited "Nam Mô A Di Đà Phật" 108 times, whereupon the spirits of the fallen martyrs — whatever religion they had held in life — all joined their palms and recited the Buddha's name fervently along with him.

And those martyrs' spirits told Nguyễn Văn Nhã and the psychics present that the chanting of sutras and the Buddha's name is the most precious gift the living can send them.

So arrange many offerings or few, as the family's heart moves you, without rigid rules: if means are modest, a little fruit, a few cakes, some porridge, a cup of water, a stick of incense will do.

In any case, during the rite we will recite the Buddhas' wondrous mantras — the Food-Multiplying Mantra, the Sweet Dew Mantra, the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel Wheel Mantra — whose power multiplies the offerings manifold in the unseen world, so that all are fed to the full.

Bells and chimes during the rite are welcome but not required. Kneeling throughout is excellent, but standing or sitting as needed is also fine.

In these rites, any words in parentheses (...) are instructions — do not recite them, only follow them. Where circumstances prevent following them, adapt flexibly rather than mechanically.

Outer forms are only supports; the core deciding a deliverance ceremony's success is sincerity. Many spend fortunes fetching ritual masters and monks for grand, costly rites, burning mountains of votive paper — yet sense little response and see no change in life. That is because the spiritual realm values only sincerity, not show.

A hired ritual master who comes only for money, however professional the performance, harbors greed, selfishness, and profit-seeking — where is the sincerity for Buddhas and Bodhisattvas to bless? What power to deliver spirits? Better the family itself set out a simple vegetarian tray and pray deliverance with a sincere heart — a thousand, ten thousand times more effective.

In the "Rite of Repentance and Deliverance for Karmic Creditors," sincerity means genuine remorse for the wrongs and offenses we committed against our karmic creditors and aborted babies.

Reading each line of repentance with an aching, ashamed heart, longing to voice apology from the heart's depths — until some burst into tears — longing to make amends, to help them be delivered and reborn in the Pure Land: that is sincerity.

While reading the teachings and sutra passages, focus the mind on their meaning, giving rise to intentions in line with their principles. While reciting mantras, there is no need to understand their meaning (for the meaning of dharanis like the Great Compassion Mantra, the Wish-Fulfilling Jewel Wheel Mantra, the Rebirth Mantra... lies beyond ordinary comprehension) — only firm faith in the wondrous power of their sound transforming spirits in the unseen is enough.

While reciting the Buddha's name, let the mouth chant while the heart rises in reverence for the Buddha; though the eyes do not see, keep imagining Amitabha Buddha and the Bodhisattvas arriving in the unseen to escort the departed souls to rebirth, drawn by your fervent chanting — that is sincerity.

A sincere heart unfailingly meets with response: though the offerings be simple, with no bells or chanting frames, fervent recitation still works powerfully to relieve suffering and grant joy — far beyond outwardly grand rites with hollow hearts.
