TITLE: 02. THE TRAGIC END OF DIRECTOR ZHAO

-----
{align:justify|We usually think of death as bad and frightening, but there are cases where "being unable to die" is far more miserable and agonizing—a punishment heavier than death itself!}
{align:justify|This thorny problem arose at a mineral extraction company owned by a Mr. Zhao, founder of a "Coal Mining Company" in a small town.}
{align:justify|The company carried no workplace-safety insurance at all; everything was sustained through private dealings with powerful figures. Naturally, Mr. Zhao enjoyed very strong protection, so after a mining accident occurred, the locality could hardly investigate smoothly, and the case in that remote region was handed to us.}
{align:justify|A gas explosion collapsed the coal mine, killing one man and injuring two. Ordinarily such accidents were all settled smoothly according to an "unwritten rule": the owner need only deftly pay compensation to the workers' families to make it go away.}
{align:justify|But as ill luck would have it, just then a nationwide inspection order came down and had to be strictly enforced—so the company owner was arrested.}
{align:justify|When another investigator and I went to gather evidence from Mr. Zhao, the guard told us:}
{align:justify|"Mr. Zhao has diabetes and has been taken to the hospital."}
{align:justify|I rushed to the "Ministry of Justice Hospital" to meet Mr. Zhao. He looked past fifty, tall and striking, with a hearty voice and an extremely tough, forceful character—clearly not one to cooperate easily.}
{align:justify|He sat on the bed, indifferent to the questions I raised, cold and unconcerned, until, just as I was about to leave, he declared bluntly:}
{align:justify|"Within a month someone will bail me out of here!"}
{align:justify|He was not wrong: with the investigation blocked by many interfering causes, the work proceeded poorly, and in the end the case was settled by releasing him on bail, with only residential surveillance. But on the very night he rejoiced, eager to instantly regain his freedom, his diabetes and gallstones flared up violently. Though neither the Ministry of Justice nor the hospital had the least wish to keep him, his illness detained him and forced him to stay.}
{align:justify|From then on, Mr. Zhao never left the hospital again. More than four months passed before the case was finally decided: Mr. Zhao was sentenced to six years in prison. But he could never enter a jail, for his health steadily declined—so his sentence could only be served at the "Public Security Hospital."}
{align:justify|My work constantly took me to the "Public Security Hospital," so I often met Mr. Zhao. Thanks to his money, he still occupied a clean single room, but the window was barred with iron; every time we met, his eyes opened wide as if brimming with unspoken thoughts. Once he asked me:}
{align:justify|"Do you believe in cause and effect?"}
{align:justify|I answered:}
{align:justify|"Of course! I am a Buddhist, after all."}
{align:justify|He suddenly sighed and said nothing more.}
{align:justify|After a while his illness worsened and he had to be transferred; the prison guards handled the paperwork to move him out. Strangely enough, not only did neither the guards nor the hospital wish to keep him, but even the King of Hell refused to take him!}
{align:justify|When I met him again he had lain in a hospital bed for nearly eight months. One meter eighty tall, his weight had plummeted. We often met and talked, and he grew to trust me more and more… at times even entrusting me with certain errands.}
{align:justify|Though I came from the medical profession, I had never seen anyone so emaciated. His eyes had sunk deep into their sockets, his cheekbones jutted out, his lips were pale, his ribs protruded—each one plainly visible.}
{align:justify|As he breathed, the bones rose and fell, giving the feeling that the slightest touch would snap them. His arms and legs were even more dreadful to behold—skinny sticks with not a scrap of flesh. Gallstones required surgery, but because of his diabetes the wound lingered unhealed, then grew infected, ulcerating and spreading to the surrounding flesh. He looked like a skull mounted on a skeleton—enough to haunt one's dreams all night!}
{align:justify|I studied the course of his illness and found that, by all ordinary reckoning, a patient like him—his organs failing, his heart barely able to keep beating—could hardly stay alive, yet he lived on and on in torment, suffering extreme pain without dying. Although he often told me his sole wish now was… to die! If he could only die, that would be happiness itself. But even this "reward" heaven stingily withheld from him! He lived on, with every second steeped in utter agony.}
{align:justify|Once, craving death, he stopped eating and drinking, fasting for more than ten days until body and mind wasted to the utmost; the doctors were certain he must die. Yet he lived on—he simply could not die!}
{align:justify|A long while passed; buried in work, I never visited him. Then one day he had an aide call and invite me over.}
{align:justify|That evening, mustering his last strength, he told me his secret in a feeble voice…}
{align:justify|"More than ten years ago, when he had just begun mining, being short of funds he sent men to the railway station to trick simple-minded drifters ignorant of the law, hiring them to work down his pits.}
{align:justify|In this way he paid no wages at all, needing only a few guards to watch over these laborers. Once they had amassed his first pot of gold for him, he sealed up that mine shaft to bury the truth, abandoning the trapped workers to exhaustion, hunger, and thirst in the darkness—until at last they all suffocated!"…}
{align:justify|(What he told was later confirmed: when the police excavated the site, they brought up more than twenty skeletons.)}
{align:justify|After that, Mr. Zhao lay in the hospital for nearly half a year. Throughout those six months, almost every minute he endured extreme peril. Yet no matter how his body was racked and tormented with pain, he did not die. His entire fortune drained away into hospital fees, and in the end even his family stopped visiting him. Though his suffering peaked day and night, he wailed and moaned without pause, his cries tearing the heart. With his immune system gravely deficient, no wound anywhere on his body would heal; all grew infected, ulcerating and rotting.}
{align:justify|In the end, not a single sound patch could be found on him—his whole body was one festering, putrid mass.}
{align:justify|After that I never saw him again. From the nurses who cared for him I heard that after he died, when they wrapped his corpse in a sheet to move it, his bones crumbled and his flesh—rotten and suppurating—dissolved into a slimy heap; before his body reached the morgue it had nearly turned into a pile of rotting bones and flesh.}
{align:justify|What follows is an extraordinarily strange case that occurred last winter.}
