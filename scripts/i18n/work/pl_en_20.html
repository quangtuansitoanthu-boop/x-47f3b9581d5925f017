DIEP CUU THANH

Layman Diep Cuu Thanh, Dharma name Hue Co, was a native of Yuyao county, Zhejiang. From childhood he followed his father about learning trade. After his father's passing, Cuu Thanh settled his affairs and returned home to rest. In dealings he was praised as keen and bright.

On the Buddhist path, Cuu Thanh first received from Layman Ha Mai Son the Guide for Beginners to Still the Mind. Reading it through, faith was born, and he joined the Buddhist society of Yuyao. In time, reminded by Layman Ha and fellow practitioners, his faith and vows grew ever firmer. At that time local women still held the superstition that reciting one Buddha-name earned a coin's merit. Cuu Thanh and his nephew Layman Chieu Khong expounded fully to dispel the error. Thus the women of Yuyao learned that recitation's benefit is birth in the West, and Pure Land practitioners grew daily more numerous. Then, when the laymen invited Dharma Master Tinh Quyen to the society to lecture on the Mahayana scriptures, Cuu Thanh's understanding deepened; certain that the West truly exists and the turbid land easily engulfs, his delight and renunciation deepened. Each morning he rose early to recite, his daily practice fixed in course. Refraining from killing and releasing life he observed most strictly.

In the summer of the Dinh Mao year of the Republic, Cuu Thanh formally took the Three Refuges with Dharma Master Bao Tinh. In the Canh Ngo year, on Ksitigarbha's feast, he received the five precepts as well. In the ninth month he contracted dysentery, medicine proving ineffective, and resolved to cast off myriad involvements, dwelling alone in a quiet room in diligent Buddha-recitation. On the night of the nineteenth, in pure recitation, Cuu Thanh suddenly beheld the fair majestic
adornments of the Land of Bliss clearly manifest. After a while the excellent vision faded, but its radiance still shone before his eyes. Some nights later he beheld it again. The illness then worsened, and he knew he would not recover. Fellow practitioners and Layman Chieu Khong came to comfort and exhort. Cuu Thanh said: "Each day I beg Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva to bless me, that karmic hindrances swiftly dissolve and the pure conditions soon mature!".

Some days later he called his two sons to instruct them on funeral matters, asking fellow practitioners to come daily for supporting recitation. He told his wife: "From this hour, I think only of the Buddha's escort. As my passing nears, do not let daughters-in-law and children come near, lest rebirth be hindered!". The night before his passing, Laymen Ha and Ly led the fellowship in supporting recitation. Cuu Thanh silently recited along with the assembly. At seven next morning, Layman Ha, seeing his expression slightly changed, warned: "This hour is most critical — strive on!". The assembly stood round chanting loudly; Cuu Thanh's lips faintly moved along. Some fifteen minutes later, lips and mouth moved no more; a fellow practitioner gently tested — he had breathed his last. This was the twenty-first day of the eleventh month in the nineteenth year of the Republic. He lived fifty-five years.

A comment: The sutra says: "Amitabha ever emits light throughout the ten directions, gathering in Buddha-reciting beings!". Thus whoever sincerely and purely recites Amitabha's sacred name, through responsive communion, shall behold that light, and by that radiance behold the Buddha or the Pure Land. Diep Cuu Thanh's beholding of the light and the Western scene proves the Land of Bliss truly exists — no mere parable, nor a provisional device to guide practitioners.
