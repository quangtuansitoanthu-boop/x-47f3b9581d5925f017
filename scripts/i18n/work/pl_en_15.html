LADY CHAU

Lady Chau lived under the Qing, the wife of Vuong So Than of Shanyin county, Zhejiang, and mother of Vuong Tam Tam, a noted layman of the time. At twenty-six she married into the Vuong family. Her husband's house was wealthy and deeply devoted to Buddhism; the Lady likewise revered the Three Jewels, once donating to build a temple to Great Being Avalokiteshvara and a shrine to the Heavenly Physician spirit. She gave alms to the poor and sick, released living beings, cherished written paper, and in short promoted and practiced every good deed with full effort. Though Master Vuong held the leadership in these charities, in truth the Lady's quiet encouragement and aid contributed greatly.

Upon marrying, the Lady at once kept a lifelong vegetarian diet and wore coarse cloth; by day she sewed and wove, by night she venerated the Buddha and recited sutras. Keeping this balanced routine, for whole years she never stepped beyond the gate. Quiet and taciturn by nature, when relatives visited, after brief greetings she would explain the retribution of wrongdoing and blessing. When Master Vuong passed away, the two children still small, the Lady served her mother-in-law and taught her young, enduring every hardship with perfect filial devotion and kindness. She had engaged a Miss Lu for Tam Tam, but before the wedding the girl fell ill and went blind in both eyes. Relatives all urged breaking the engagement, but the Lady refused, brought her home as bride, and told Tam Tam: "When you went to propose, your wife's eyes were still bright and fair. But before the wedding she went blind — that too is your lot. If Mother and son cast her off, we shall ruin and shame a young woman's life. You must care for and love your wife, keeping a husband's duty fully until her passing!". This one matter alone shows the Lady full of compassion and kindness.

Formerly, each dawn the Lady recited the Heart Sutra in fixed number. After widowhood she devoted herself to Buddha-recitation, resolved upon birth in the West. In the third month of the first Xuantong year, her mother-in-law died; the Lady managed funeral and burial with thorough care, and grief and toil made her ill. She lay abed more than forty days before passing. Three days before the end, she instructed her two sons on funeral matters, saying: "Three days hence, at noon, Mother will return to the Buddha. Both of you should advance in virtue, strive to be complete persons, and follow Mother's example in reciting the Buddha's name, seeking the West!". At the appointed hour, she raised herself to sit, closed her eyes, and silently recited, most peacefully. Tam Tam asked whether she saw anything; the Lady answered: "Hang the lantern before the gate and do not question and disturb my right mindfulness!". Having spoken, she sat quietly awhile, then silently
passed away. Hours later, Tam Tam felt both her hands still soft and supple, her crown very warm.
