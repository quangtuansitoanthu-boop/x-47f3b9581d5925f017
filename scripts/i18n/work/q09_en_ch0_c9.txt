<p><strong>Nhữ húc tu hành, dục đắc bồ đề, yếu trừ tam hoặc. Bất tận tam hoặc, túng đắc thần thông. Giai thị thế gian, hữu vi công dụng. Tập khí bất diệt, lạc ư ma đạo. Tuy dục trừ vọng, bội gia hư ngụy. Như Lai thuyết vi, khả ai lân giả. Nhữ vọng tự tạo, phi Bồ-đề cữu. Tác thị thuyết giả, danh vi chánh thuyết. Nhược tha thuyết giả, tức Ma Vương thuyết. </strong></p>
<p><strong>You urge cultivators who wish to attain Bodhi to end the three delusions. If the three delusions are not ended, then even with spiritual powers, all is but worldly conditioned functioning; with habits unextinguished, one falls into the demonic paths. Though wishing to remove falseness, one only increases deceit. The Tathagata calls such a one pitiable. Falseness is self-created; it is not the fault of Bodhi. To speak thus is right speech; to speak otherwise is the speech of Mara.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: The Buddha told Ananda: &#8220;You should urge cultivators who wish to attain Bodhi to end the three delusions.&#8221; The three evil acts are killing, stealing, and lust; they are called delusions because karma is created from false thought &#8211; if beings were not deluded and deceived, karmic obstructions would not be created. &#8220;If the three delusions are not ended&#8221; &#8211; that is, if even the tiniest false thought of the three evil acts remains, as tiny as a hair and not given up &#8211; &#8220;then even with spiritual powers, all is but worldly conditioned functioning.&#8221;</p>
<p>They may gain a little spiritual power or a little wisdom, but these cannot be counted as unconditioned spiritual powers; they abide in form and shape according to the functioning employed &#8211; mere spiritual powers clinging to form. &#8220;With habits unextinguished, one falls into the demonic paths.&#8221; If habits such as killing, stealing, and lust are not stopped, you will fall into the demonic paths in the future.<br />
Now you see why I say that such-and-such a cultivator is a demon king: they do not observe the precepts against killing, stealing, and lust. If a person acts so, how could they gain genuine wisdom? They cannot.</p>
<p>&#8220;Though wishing to remove falseness, one only increases deceit.&#8221; They add error to error, confusion to confusion: they begin with lying and speaking wrongly, then claim they did not lie &#8211; adding more deceit. If you lie, there is no reason to argue it: confess, and it is but one offense. But in denying the lie, they bear two offenses, doubling the offense this way. &#8220;The Tathagata calls such a one pitiable.&#8221; Those who never do what is right are pitiable. &#8220;Falseness is self-created; it is not the fault of Bodhi.&#8221; All deluded false thought and karma is self-created; it is not Bodhi's fault. So they cannot say: &#8220;If we are Buddhas, why are there false thoughts?&#8221; They are mistaken to speak so.</p>
<p>&#8220;To speak thus is right speech.&#8221; If you speak thus, you speak according to the right Dharma. &#8220;To speak otherwise is the speech of Mara.&#8221; Explanations not following the true path are the speech of evil demons. You should clearly distinguish right from wrong: Dharma spoken by demon kings is based on fabricated false thought &#8211; for example, a demon king knows something but says he does not know. That is lying. Cultivators should know that straightforward wisdom is Bodhi. Always be straightforward in every situation; do not deceive.</p>
