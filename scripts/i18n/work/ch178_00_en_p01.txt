{align:justify|JOURNEY TO THE WEST AND THE INVESTITURE OF THE GODS}
{align:justify|Master, hearing your analysis of the White Snake tale, I find it most reasonable. I have further questions to ask: Journey to the West is a famed classic beloved by the masses, filmed and cheered by so many…}
{align:justify|But watching the films as a child, I found Tang Sanzang so cowardly and weak… why did Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin) Bodhisattva make Sun Wukong take him as master? What is more, She gave him the Tightening-Circlet spell to tame a "punish-evil, praise-good" champion like the Great Sage? Tang Sanzang is no more gifted—how could he teach his disciples? How should this story be explained from the Buddhist view?}
{align:justify|Master Diệu Pháp answered:}
{align:justify|"I think, understanding Buddhism, Journey to the West should be explained thus:}
{align:justify|As the books state, Tang Sanzang himself followed the Lesser Vehicle—so Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin) Bodhisattva manifested as an old monk, pointing the way, advising Tang Sanzang to journey West to audience with the Buddha and request the Great Vehicle sutras.}
{align:justify|The road West for sutras is in truth the path Venerable Xuanzang walked—self-liberating and liberating others. But the practicing road is never smooth—always full of perils and hardships.}
{align:justify|The three disciples following Tang Sanzang represent the three evil habits—greed, anger, delusion—in every being. Zhu Bajie stands for those sunk in craving: craving wealth, beauty, fame, gain, food, sleep… hence he must keep eight precepts—meaning only precepts quench craving; only precepts win wisdom. Hence he is named Wuneng (Awakened Power).}
{align:justify|Sun Wukong stands for the dutiful and gifted, yet greatly wrathful! Naming him Wukong (Awakened Emptiness) tells such people to grasp the truth: "All is impermanent; all dharmas are empty."}
{align:justify|Sha Wujing denotes the honest, good-hearted yet deluded, who, not understanding Buddhism, created much evil karma (meat-eating, killing)—he once lived in the Flowing Sands River devouring humans. Naming him Wujing (Awakened Purity) bids him practice pure conduct: to escape the three realms, one must be compassionate and never kill.}
{align:justify|Tang Sanzang as master sees the four elements all empty, untempted by wealth, beauty, fame, gain; though less magically gifted than his disciples, yet at moments on life-and-death's edge he still takes it lightly, declaring:}
{align:justify|"Better step West and die than step back and live"—when Wukong broke precepts killing, disobeying his master, he had no choice but the Tightening-Circlet spell… meaning: use precepts to rein him in, turning him from deviance to right.}
{align:justify|Tang Sanzang cannot ride clouds or transform—only single-mindedly recite the Buddha's name—meaning: "Practice aims not at miracles."}
{align:justify|As for Wukong, vastly magical, meeting demons too mighty—was he not helpless too? Yet in the end evil never beats right!…}
{align:justify|Life imperiled, Tang Sanzang single-mindedly recites the Buddha's name, and Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin) Bodhisattva surely aids him—turning peril to safety, perils to peace. Besides, most demons Wukong cannot handle are attendants of saints or beasts of their households running wild…}
{align:justify|Explain it thus: Bodhisattvas and immortals, to help Tang Sanzang soon attain sainthood, deliberately contrived obstacles…}
{align:justify|All are trials and tests—watching how he responds? Tang Sanzang, even at death's door, never once blamed Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin) for sending him fetching sutras!}
{align:justify|The Monkey King can ride clouds a hundred and eight thousand miles, yet never leaves the Tathagata Buddha's palm. If Buddhism wished to hand the Great Vehicle scriptures to Tang Sanzang, how easy—need the Buddha but lift a hand? Or simply send Sun Wukong fetching sutras, sparing the trouble?…}
{align:justify|So this Journey to the West warns us: practice is most arduous; in a Buddhist practicing community there are always all kinds of seekers. The Buddhist assembly is a great forge "gathering essences and storing all"…}
{align:justify|The Buddha's teachings are supremely hard—nothing can break them; the Buddha's teachings are supremely soft—nothing can divide them…. If all ever keep right mindfulness in practice, together they can build the great work. So through a thousand years' perils and dangers, at last master and three disciples meet the Buddha. But entering the "Sutra Repository" for scriptures, they meet obstacles—hinting: "Till Buddhahood, ignorance remains"—also explaining: "Let go, and you shall receive."}
{align:justify|On the return road, all scriptures fell into water—at last only six words remained: "Nam mô A Di Đà Phật!" In the "Diamond Sutra" the Buddha also said: "To say the Tathagata preached any Dharma is to slander the Buddha"—likewise hinting: in the latter Dharma age, all written scriptures shall gradually vanish—first the "Shurangama Sutra"… till at last only six words "Nam mô A Di Đà Phật!" remain.}
{align:justify|Truly, my explanation merely strives to "straighten the bent"—forced to do so.}
{align:justify|In fact, "Journey to the West" and "Investiture of the Gods" both arose from Buddhist-Daoist rivalry—born of mutual attacks.}
{align:justify|Recently, Venerable Hư Vân—the Zen North Star—explained these two works' origins thus:}
{align:justify|"In those years in Beijing, Venerable Bach Van preached the 'Dao De Jing' at Bach Van Temple; many Daoists came listening, then joined Buddhism becoming monks—enraging the Daoists at Truong Xuan Temple, who sued the officials…}
{align:justify|The court, to dissolve both sides' conflict, ordered Truong Xuan Temple (Daoist) renamed Truong Xuan Pagoda, while Bach Van Pagoda (Buddhist) must become Bach Van Temple.}
{align:justify|The Daoists, unconvinced, wrote the novel Journey to the West to libel Buddhism—so Journey to the West readers need wisdom and bright eyes; understanding that history, they see the truth at once.}
{align:justify|Most cunning: the novel pictures Tang Sanzang fetching sutras, returning to the Heaven-Reaching River—where all scriptures fell in the river, their words all lost—at last only six words "Nam mô A Di Đà Phật!" remaining. This is them saying: Venerable Xuanzang's whole sutra-translation enterprise was all false.}
{align:justify|Pity that later generations mistook and fanatically believed Journey to the West—burying, distorting, effacing Venerable Xuanzang's true westward journey…}
{align:justify|Answering Journey to the West, the monks "returned the salute"—writing the "Investiture of the Gods" to libel Daoists. Seen thus, everywhere in the tale… Daoists are mocked!}
{align:justify|For instance: Daoist immortals surely wield power—yet one by one fall under the immortal-slaying blade.}
{align:justify|Reading these two novels, not understanding that Buddhist-Daoist rivalry's history, readers mistake false for true!}
{align:justify|So reading books demands bright wisdom—clearly seeing right from wrong, well distinguishing deviant from true…}
{align:justify|Note: please read Venerable Hư Vân's teachings and instructions. Herein are only Master Diệu Pháp's explanations. He further urges us to study the "Shurangama Sutra" closely, to gain wisdom and bright eyes distinguishing true from deviant, right from wrong… to read many works by virtuous high monks themselves, opening our own wisdom.}
