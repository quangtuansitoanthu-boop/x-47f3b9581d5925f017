DUONG KIET

Layman Duong Kiet, styled Thu Cong, lived under the Song, in Wuwei county, and so called himself theinactive One. In the Yuanfeng years he served as Minister of Rites. At first he favored Zen, regularly attending the Dharma assemblies of Zen Master Thien Y Hoai and investigating the sayings of Layman Pang. When by imperial command he went to offer sacrifice on the summit of Mount Tai and saw the red sun rise like a blazing round tray, he suddenly attained great awakening.

At the end of the Xining years, while mourning his mother at home, he read through the canon in his leisure and turned toward the Pure Land. He had an image of Amitabha painted, one zhang and six tall, which he carried with him for contemplation. His writings in life mostly pointed toward the Land of Bliss. He once wrote a preface to Master Tiantai's Ten Doubts on the Pure Land as follows:

"If the love of this world does not weigh heavily, one is not born in the Saha world. If Buddha-recitation is not single-minded, one is certainly not reborn in the Land of Bliss. The Saha is a defiled evil land; the Land of Bliss is a fair and pure realm. Life in the Saha is limited; the lifespan in the Land of Bliss is endless! In the Saha there are abundant sufferings and little joy. In the Land of Bliss affliction and suffering are gone and peace is immeasurable. — In the Saha one revolves with karma, never knowing when liberation will come. In the Land of Bliss, once reborn, one never retrogresses and advances step by step to the fruit of great awakening. If one wishes to deliver beings in the ten directions, one goes freely at will, no longer bound by karma. Comparing the two lands — defiled and evil versus fair and pure, affliction and suffering versus peace and joy, brief life versus long lifespan, endless wandering versus swift realization — they are opposites in every way. And yet sentient beings in their delusion do not know it. Is that not deeply pitiable?"

Amitabha is the one who gathers and escorts beings in the Land of Bliss. Shakyamuni Buddha is the guide to the Pure Land in the Saha world. Hence in his Mahayana teachings he again and again earnestly exhorts beings to rebirth there. The bodhisattvas Avalokiteshvara and Mahasthamaprapta assist Amitabha, riding together the boat of the great vow into the sea of rebirth, abiding neither on this shore nor the other nor midstream, working at deliverance. Thus the Amitabha Sutra says: 'If a good man or good woman, hearing of Amitabha Buddha, holds fast his name, whether for one day up to seven days, with single undistracted mind — when life ends, Amitabha Buddha with the host of holy ones will appear before that person. At life's end, the mind free from distortion, that person will at once be reborn in Amitabha Buddha's Land of Bliss!' Thus in former days at the Hall of Impermanence in Jetavana, the Buddha taught the sick to lie facing west and contemplate rebirth in the Pure Land. Why? Because Amitabha emits light illumining the whole Dharma-realm, gathering in Buddha-reciting beings without exception. Since sage and ordinary share one mind-essence, the responsive communion accords: where there is recitation, there is response. Thus sentient beings within the Buddhas' minds, if they turn back, find every thought a Pure Land. The Buddhas within sentient beings' minds shine their sacred light everywhere; every place is compassion.

Judging by this: the wise are easily reborn, for they cut off doubt. The meditative are easily reborn, for their minds are unscattered. Precept-keepers are easily reborn, for they are far from defilement. The generous are easily reborn, for they cast off worldly goods. The patient are easily reborn, for they harbor no resentment. The diligent are easily reborn, for pure mindfulness swiftly matures. Those who have done neither good nor evil are also easily reborn, for their whole heart turns in single refuge. Those who have done evil, whose karmic retribution has already appeared, are also easily reborn, if they know shame and fear and recite the Buddha's name. On the contrary, those who have performed many wholesome meritorious deeds, if they lack faith, vows, and dedication, will surely not attain rebirth!

Oh! Amitabha's name is so easy to recite, the Pure Land so easy to be born into! Beings who will not recite and will not seek rebirth leave the Buddha, for all his pity, no means to help them. Moreover: creating evil karma one falls into painful destinies; reciting Amitabha one is born into a joyful land — both are the Buddha's words. Beings fear only falling into hell, yet doubt rebirth — is that not delusion too?

In the Yuanyou years he served as Judicial Commissioner of the Two Zhe regions, and died in office. At the end, he spoke this verse:

In life, nothing to cling to;

In death, nothing to cast away.

Between the vast void,

Let be whatever may!

Bring delusion to deliverance,

O Western Land of Bliss!

Earlier, the Right Counselor Vuong Trong Hoi, his neighbor, had studied Buddha-recitation under Thu Cong and asked: "How may it become uninterrupted?". He answered: "Once faith is certain and doubt is gone, it is uninterrupted!". Trong Hoi understood, departed joyfully, and returned home. The next year, when Thu Cong served at Don Duong, one night he dreamed that Trong Hoi came and said: "Thanks to your instruction on the Pure Land, I have now attained rebirth, and have come to thank you!". Having spoken, he bowed and withdrew. A few days later the layman received the funeral notice from Vuong Trong Hoi's son, recounting how his father had foreknown his rebirth and gone about bidding farewell to relatives and friends. After Thu Cong himself had passed away, Lady Kinh Duong dreamed she wandered to the Western Land and saw one of dignified bearing seated upon a lotus, wearing a jeweled cap and jeweled necklaces, his robe fluttering lightly in the breeze. Asked who it was, she was told it was the Inactive One, Duong Kiet.
