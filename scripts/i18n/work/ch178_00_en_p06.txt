{align:justify|We common mortals, still deluded and unclear, should never judge recklessly—lest we create evil speech karma, offending boundlessly. Great Master Tinh Am passed at forty-nine—we must not with mortal eyes comment: 'his practice was poor, hence short-lived!' For all states before our eyes are trials testing practitioners—'if truly a true practitioner, one sees no worldly faults!'"}
{align:justify|"'Seeing no worldly faults' means not that, seeing people do evil, we never criticize, advise, or restrain… but precisely that we must meet things with the Buddhist view."}
{align:justify|CAREFUL SPEECH}
{align:justify|One autumn morning in 2000, Master Diệu Pháp was teaching dozens at a layman's house—when suddenly through the gate stepped a middle-aged man—kneeling before Master Diệu Pháp, urgently begging the master to come saving his wife.}
{align:justify|The master told him not to hurry—slowly tell all.}
{align:justify|It seems last month his wife began painfully difficult defecation—before long urination too grew hard—half an hour, urinating unfinished—then two hours, toileting undone. So hospital examination revealed internal hemorrhoids—grown big as steamed buns (no exaggeration). Now walking is hard—mind most miserable. Doctors scolded her—why wait till now for examination? She said painless and itchless—never knowing so grave an illness.}
{align:justify|He sighed reporting:}
{align:justify|"Doctors say immediate surgery is needed—yet affirm great danger—for before, he operated internal hemorrhoids many times—among them some died of great hemorrhage—but their piles were never big as hers. We are perplexed, not knowing what to do—hearing a master is passing this region—we venture begging your compassion saving my wife's life."}
{align:justify|The master settled a while, then said:}
{align:justify|"Your wife practices most well—most diligent! Have not husband and wife already slept in separate rooms?"}
{align:justify|"Yes, Master. This region has several couples practicing the 'Fourfold Pure Clear Instructions' in the 'Shurangama Sutra.' Since my wife firmly wishes so, I comply. Though less devout than she—unwilling—still I know she acts rightly—so I bear with her. Perfecting her perfects me. Why, practicing so diligently, does she draw such results?"}
{align:justify|The master smiled, saying:}
{align:justify|"Are such results not good?… Bodhisattvas fear causes, beings fear fruits! Lawful practitioners surely meet retribution-obstacles—it may be called karma manifesting before us—precisely through her diligent practice earning early karma-repayment—wholly no evil! Else, so great a pile—why painless? But presently she is most unstable—lead me to your house at once!"}
{align:justify|His house was not far—those present surging along.}
{align:justify|The patient, Mrs. Tran, long practiced outsider paths—knowing Buddhism, she guided the whole Tran clan—siblings down to grandchildren… dozens all taking Buddhist refuge. From sixty-year-old elders down to ten-year-old children—all willingly quit meat for vegetarianism—in common words: 'one knife cutting all clean!'… Moreover most memorized the Shurangama Mantra—even Tran's second and third elder sisters, illiterate, studied character by character—yet at last memorized the whole Shurangama Mantra—besides diligently temple-going, sutra-reciting, morning-evening practice…}
{align:justify|Witnessing this, I was utterly moved. Other morning-evening liturgies like the Great Compassion Mantra, the Ten Minor Mantras—her grandchildren mostly knew by heart. Laywoman Tran, the great family's 'command headquarters'—even her brother, a plant director, followed her practicing.}
{align:justify|Now she lay peacefully on bed—looking as if awaiting death's arrival…}
{align:justify|Spying Master Diệu Pháp entering, she seemed most moved—gratefully saying:}
{align:justify|"Oh! The master comes saving me!…"}
{align:justify|Speaking, palms joining, about to sit—the master stopped her.}
{align:justify|The master sat on the chair by her bed, gently asking:}
{align:justify|"Did you once open a cloth shop?"}
{align:justify|Mrs. Tran answered:}
{align:justify|"Yes—but closed now."}
{align:justify|Quarreling with customers then, did you curse them? the master asked again.}
{align:justify|"Business often brings such matters—especially odd customers—but I never cursed anyone!"}
{align:justify|The master pressed:}
{align:justify|"Like… a woman past thirty insisting returning you a cloth length—both sides quarreling—you once uttered wounding words—think hard and check whether this happened?"}
{align:justify|The master's words hinting, Laywoman Tran looked moved, saying:}
{align:justify|"Oh—I remember—truly it happened! Daily I always examine myself—whatever faults remembered, repented… How could I forget this clean away?"}
{align:justify|Then she told:}
{align:justify|"Roughly six years ago, a sister pointed at cloths displayed outside my shop, wishing to buy. So I took that very kind inside the shop—cut her several meters. But next day, she came wandering back, demanding returns. How could I accept then? I said:"}
{align:justify|"'You chose carefully—then I cut and sold—now returning—whom shall I sell to?…'" }
{align:justify|But she stubbornly said:}
{align:justify|"'The flowered cloth you cut inside the shop differs from that displayed outside…'" }
{align:justify|And holding her bought cloth, she compared it with that displayed outside. Since outside goods, displayed many days—sun- and dew-bleached—naturally colors faded a little paler. But the cloth I sold her was true original color—never weathered… Yet she insisted it differed! Shouting loudly, demanding returns!}
{align:justify|Then truly I flared—retorting:}
{align:justify|"'How can indoor and outdoor cloth match? Like your face and your… bottom!'"}
{align:justify|Then truly I meant an analogy explaining for her understanding—could that count as… cursing?}
{align:justify|Seeing Mrs. Tran most agitated, the master smiled saying:}
{align:justify|"Then were there not crowds ringing around?"}
{align:justify|Mrs. Tran said:}
{align:justify|"Yes—for a bustling market—so many people. And her shouting loudly, arguing unreasonably so—naturally drew crowds ringing—moreover none backed her returns—all deeming her most unreasonable!"}
{align:justify|The master said again:}
{align:justify|"Your analogy given, the ringing crowds roared laughing—even teasing her to… drop her trousers comparing face and bottom for ease?…"}
{align:justify|"Yes… it seems so!…"}
{align:justify|"Then think hard—your analogy—reasoned, may hold. Toward men, the matter bears no great fault—but toward women, it must rank a kind of humiliation! Now you have received lay Bodhisattva precepts—such speech karma created—how unrepented? It seems at last you still allowed her returns?…"}
{align:justify|"Yes. Then someone advised me—thus harms business—better generous a little—bearing loss letting her return. So many ringing now—may even advertise for me… I heard reason—so acted. And she then had nothing more arguing… and I agreed to her returns. But that my analogy humiliated her—then I never thought. Now your reminder: those bygone words truly stirred humiliating effects. Why did I never think then?"}
{align:justify|Master Diệu Pháp said:}
{align:justify|"One resolving great practice—not only ending all evil, practicing all good… but this life's created karma—must surely recall repenting—only so may karma dissolve. Precisely through your diligent practice—yet forgetting that bygone speech karma—so itknotted into this evil fruit. Evil fruits ripening—one must fall. Now seal yourself examining—then tomorrow meet me!"}
{align:justify|Next morning Mrs. Tran cycled to audience with the master.}
{align:justify|She said:}
{align:justify|"Master—truly miraculously unexpected—the steamed-bun pile has flown wingless away!"}
{align:justify|This taught surrounding Buddhist learners greatly. Above all Mrs. Tran's younger sister. Once she opened a shoe shop several years—then famed as a chatterbox yielding to none—whoever provoked her—she scolded rattling near two hours. After learning Buddhism, though knowing repentance—still Mrs. Tran's story this time moved her to mightily reform—striving examining—sifting all her own faults.}
{align:justify|Frankly, my own mind fared no better—startled too: carefully checking all my own faults.}
