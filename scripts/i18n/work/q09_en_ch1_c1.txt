<p>What is the most precious property in one's own house?</p>
<p>I shall speak plainly and simply so that you must remember this well &#8211; you must believe what I say. Whatever you do, do not forget my words and cast them aside. Why? Because this concerns your own future and life.</p>
<p>What is your own precious treasury? It is the treasury of the inherent true nature, the Tathagata, within you. Can you afford to lose that Tathagata-store nature? Do you not remember that I have already spoken before of the essence, energy, and spirit of the Tathagata store? If you wish to recover your own Tathagata store, you must first preserve your own <strong>essence, energy, and spirit</strong> (jing, qi, shen). If you do not take good care of these three, then you let your property be robbed. Be extremely careful.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Hựu phục ư trung đắc tiểu vi túc. Như đệ tứ thiền Vô Văn tỉ-khưu vọng ngôn chứng thánh. Thiên báo dĩ tất, suy tướng hiện tiền, báng A-la-hán , thân tao hậu hữu. Đọa a-tì ngục.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, in the midst of cultivation you feel satisfied and content with a little attainment &#8211; like the Unlearned Bhikshu who reached the Fourth Dhyana and rashly claimed to have realized sagehood. When his heavenly blessings ended, the signs of decay appeared, and he slandered Arhatship as still subject to birth and death, falling into the Avichi hell.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary:</strong> <strong>Further, in the midst of cultivation</strong>, <strong>you feel satisfied and content with a little attainment</strong>.</p>
<p>During cultivation, even if you are not possessed by ghosts and spirits, you must have genuine wisdom and the <em>Dharma-selecting eye</em>: <em>selecting</em> means choosing, <em>Dharma</em> means the Buddhadharma, and <em>eye</em> means your own eye. If you can recognize the Buddhadharma, you will know for yourself what level your cultivation has reached. Do not feel satisfied upon gaining a little understanding of the principles.</p>
<p><strong>Like the Unlearned Bhikshu who reached the Fourth Dhyana and rashly claimed to have realized sagehood</strong>.</p>
<p>He is called the Unlearned Bhikshu because he did not understand much &#8211; he knew only a little Buddhadharma. Why is he called Unlearned? Fundamentally, one who realizes the fourth fruition of Arhatship is born no more; the Buddha taught that whoever realizes the fourth Arhat fruition no longer undergoes birth-and-death in the turning wheel. One who realizes the second fruition, called Once-Returner, must still undergo one birth in the heavens and one return among humans &#8211; one more birth and death to pass through. A sage of the first fruition (among the four Arhat fruitions) must still pass through seven births and deaths. All these stages transcend the heavens of the Fourth Dhyana.</p>
<p>The Unlearned Bhikshu had only reached the Fourth Dhyana stage in his cultivation, yet he thought he had realized the fourth Arhat fruition. In fact, at the Fourth Dhyana level one has realized no fruition at all and is still an ordinary person (having samadhi power but no wisdom yet).</p>
<p>Yet the Unlearned Bhikshu proclaimed he had realized the fourth Arhat fruition. Even today there are people who consider the fourth Arhat fruition low compared to themselves, brazenly proclaiming that they themselves are Buddhas. But a Buddha is complete with the three bodies, four kinds of wisdom, five eyes, and six spiritual powers. You may ask those who proclaim themselves Buddhas how many spiritual powers they have &#8211; surely the <em>extinction-of-outflows power</em> is lacking. I am certain that those who claim to be Buddhas do not have even a single spiritual power, let alone five or six. Only those without spiritual powers wish to boast that they are Buddhas; those who truly had even one spiritual power would never dare utter such a great false claim.</p>
<p>When his heavenly blessings ended &#8211; when his life in the heavens came to an end &#8211; the signs of decay appeared. Do you remember the five signs of decay? Do you remember the five signs of decay I lectured on in an earlier section? When a being's life in the heavens ends and death approaches, five signs appear, called the five signs of decay: first, the flower garland on the head (the crown) withers; second, the clothes become soiled; third, sweat comes from the armpits; fourth, the body becomes foul and filthy; fifth, discontent with one's seat.</p>
<p>He <strong>slandered Arhatship as still subject to birth and death, and fell into the Avichi hell.</strong></p>
<p>When the heavenly blessings are nearly exhausted, the five signs of decay appear, signaling that life in the heavens is about to end. The Unlearned Bhikshu grew angry &#8211; angry at what? He thought: &#8220;I have been deceived by the Buddha; the Buddha is a fraud. The Buddha said that whoever realizes the fourth Arhat fruition no longer passes through birth-and-death in the turning wheel. Why is my life about to end? Why must I be reborn? Why must I transmigrate? Why must I be born again? The Buddha is a liar.&#8221; We know at once what happened when he slandered the Buddha thus: he fell straight into the Avichi hell.</p>
<p>The Avichi hell is also called the uninterrupted hell. Since he had not realized Arhatship yet claimed he had, when his heavenly blessings were enjoyed to the end and his life there ended, he fell into a lower realm. He did not recognize his own error but claimed the Buddha's teaching was wrong. In truth the Buddha had said: &#8220;You have not realized the fourth Arhat fruition; had you realized it, you would have no more birth and death.&#8221;</p>
<p>Why did the five signs of decay appear? Because of slandering the Buddha. Therefore the Unlearned Bhikshu fell straight into the uninterrupted hell.</p>
<p>You can imagine where those who proclaim themselves Buddhas will go. I do not know where their lives will end.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhữ ưng đế thính, ngô kim vị nhữ, tử tế phân biệt.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: You should listen attentively. I shall now explain in detail for you.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary:</strong> <strong>You should listen attentively</strong>: Ananda, you should pay special heed &#8211; listen well. <strong>I shall now explain in detail for you</strong> &#8211; explaining every detail thoroughly for Ananda and the assembly. So do not be discouraged; do not give rise to thoughts of disappointment.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: A-nan khởi lập, tịnh kì hội trung, đồng hữu học giả, hoan hỉ đảnh lễ, phục thính từ hối. </strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Ananda rose, and together with those in the assembly still in need of study, joyfully bowed and quietly listened to the compassionate instruction.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary:</strong> <strong>Ananda rose, together with those in the assembly still in need of study</strong> &#8211; the Bodhisattvas, the great Arhats, the great Bhikshus, those in need of study, those who had realized the first, second, and third Arhat fruitions.</p>
<p><strong>Joyfully bowed</strong>: because the Buddha was about to explain the matter in great detail, everyone was delighted, together joyfully bowing to the Buddha. They restrained their own minds, <strong>quietly listening to the compassionate instruction of the Buddha</strong> &#8211; not allowing a single scattered thought to arise, so as to listen with complete attention to the Dharma the Buddha was about to teach.</p>
