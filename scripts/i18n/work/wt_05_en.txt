TITLE: 06. THE "HEAVEN-SENT" ILLNESS
TOMTAT: Mr. Gia broke out in sores all over his body from sexual misconduct — violating many women, betraying his wife, and scorning his parents. Meeting Master Miaofa, his offenses were laid bare one by one; he repented and recovered.
-----
{align:center|**THE "HEAVEN-SENT" ILLNESS**}

{align:justify|Early in 1994, Mr. Gia, husband of Mrs. Ly, contracted scabies. His whole body broke out in sores, oozing pus and aching wretchedly; he sought cures everywhere, Eastern and Western medicine alike, all without result.}

{align:justify|Mrs. Ly telephoned me, wishing to bring her husband to Master Miaofa, but she shrank from her husband's body covered in sores, foul with blood and pus — most inconvenient — so she hesitated, not knowing what to do.}

{align:justify|Unexpectedly, three days later she called again, joyfully reporting that within a single night her husband's festering sores had dried, oozing no more pus. A strange and hard-to-believe turn since his illness began — she was most excited, and resolved to bring her husband to honor the Master.}

{align:justify|That evening Mrs. Ly came with her husband. He stood 1.8 meters tall, well-built, handsome and elegant in appearance — seemingly a man of talent. The moment the couple stepped through the door they bowed to Master Miaofa, urgently begging his compassionate rescue.}

{align:justify|Seated, Mrs. Ly at once asked the Master:}

{align:justify|"In a single night my husband's sores stopped oozing; now his body is no longer ulcerated, the illness much improved. What does this mean? We beg the Venerable's teaching."}

{align:justify|The Master asked:}

{align:justify|"Have you ever gone to a pagoda to pray to Guanyin Bodhisattva?"}

{align:justify|She answered:}

{align:justify|"Yes. Half a year ago, seeing that his illness would not yield to treatment, I often went to the pagoda to bow before Guanyin Bodhisattva, begging her to help my husband soon recover."}

{align:justify|The Master said slowly:}

{align:justify|"That you two could come here today is entirely thanks to your persevering worship of Guanyin Bodhisattva — you have now received a sympathetic response. I must state clearly: it is thanks to your deep wholesome roots and your affinity with the Buddha — but your husband's illness is his own personal retribution, born of his own faults and wrongs. Only if he himself recognizes his errors, repents, and vows never to repeat them, will the illness fully heal. The Buddhas and bodhisattvas ever hope that offenders will reform and return to the right."}

{align:justify|Then the Master turned to Mrs. Ly's husband and asked:}

{align:justify|"Do you find what I say correct?"}

{align:justify|Mr. Gia nodded repeatedly:}

{align:justify|"Yes! Yes! The Master speaks no error."}

{align:justify|The Master continued:}

{align:justify|"Each of us, not knowing the Buddha Dharma, constantly errs, even does evil. If toward the evils you once did you sincerely confess and repent, your illness may heal."}

{align:justify|But Mr. Gia declared boldly:}

{align:justify|"I am a man of honor — I have never harmed anyone, never even quarreled or exchanged angry words."}

{align:justify|Seeing this, the Master spoke softly and invited Mrs. Ly to step outside; only then did he ask again:}

{align:justify|"Please, Mr. Gia, examine yourself — have you done anything shameful in your life?"}

{align:justify|Yet Mr. Gia still insisted he had "never done anything shameful," his face calm and dry, even telling the Master that if he knew, to speak it out.}

{align:justify|The Master sighed:}

{align:justify|"Gravely ill as you are, still deluded and unawakened! See how your wife, for your sake, devoutly worships and prays for the Buddha's blessing! Now I ask you one thing —" The Master's voice grew stern. "When you were 20, did you befriend a girl a little shorter than you, oval-faced, fair-skinned, hair in two braids?"}

{align:justify|Seeing Mr. Gia start, the Master added:}

{align:justify|"She often wore the white-flowered blouse you gave her — is that so?"}

{align:justify|As calm and proud as he had been, now Mr. Gia's face went pale, his eyes staring at the Master in terror; he stammered:}

{align:justify|"She... was a schoolmate who followed me from the city to the countryside, living in the same household. How do you know her?"}

{align:justify|"Then tell me — what was between you two?"}

{align:justify|"Only a fleeting youthful feeling..."}

{align:justify|"Is that so? It seems to me not so simple — for you used deceit to force yourself upon her, and in the end abandoned her. Is that right?" — the Master demanded sternly.}

{align:justify|Mr. Gia shook with shock. He hastily knelt at the Master's feet and whispered tensely:}

{align:justify|"Master, ten thousand times I beg you — do not let my wife know. I truly loved that girl, and afterward..."}

{align:justify|"Afterward there were several more girls, likewise lured and possessed by you... And even after marriage, before this illness seized you, you still carried on with another man's wife — is that right?" the Master said sternly, his manner severe.}

{align:justify|At this, every wicked act of lust Mr. Gia had committed over the years was exposed without omission. Moments before he had raised his voice, proudly proclaiming himself a gentleman; now he looked like a convict, trembling all over, prostrate at the Master's feet, knocking his head and ceaselessly begging forgiveness.}

{align:justify|"Master... I am guilty! I will never dare again..."}

{align:justify|Witnessing this, my own heart shook with alarm. Truly "heaven's net is vast and wide, yet not a single hair slips through." Though his lustful offenses had escaped the punishment of state law, he now trembled in heart and flesh as though sentenced to death, like a man whose soul has fled.}

{align:justify|The Master, august as a judge, issued his verdict:}

{align:justify|"First rise and be seated. You not only wantonly possessed other men's wives and daughters, but deceived your parents, betrayed the wife at home — disloyal and unfilial, a true thief... Of all lustful, deceitful evils there is none you have not done — yet you never reformed. Before long your whole body will ulcerate miserably and you will die. Dead, you will surely fall into hell!"}

{align:justify|Mr. Gia knelt once more, knocking his head fervently toward the Master in ritual homage, promising never again to do evil from this day. He wept loudly, begging the Master to accept him as a disciple, vowing to change, to mend his faults, to cleanse his heart — to become a true Buddhist. The Master nodded faintly in acceptance. Then he had Mrs. Ly invited back inside. To the couple the Master taught the Five Precepts and the Ten Good Deeds, instructing them how to repent and dissolve karma. They listened reverently until the sun sank westward, then took their leave, carrying the Dharma joy to the full.}

{align:justify|After meeting Master Miaofa, husband and wife devoutly recited sutras every day; before long Mr. Gia was cured. Worshiping the Buddha and reciting sutras became their daily practice; husband and wife of one heart and one virtue became a pure Buddhist family.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story THE "HEAVEN-SENT" ILLNESS is a profound lesson in "lustful deceit — retribution in sores over the whole body." Outwardly respectable, calling himself a man of "honor," Mr. Gia had forced himself on many women, betrayed his wife, and been unfilial to his parents. His present retribution was a body ulcerated and reeking of blood and pus. When Master Miaofa laid bare each detail of his offenses (even the white-flowered blouse given to his lover), he trembled and repented sincerely. The result: his illness healed, and the couple became pure Buddhists. The story warns: "Heaven's net is vast and wide; not a hair slips through." Though earthly law may not punish lustful deceit, the law of cause and effect never misses. Whoever has a family, a wife and children, should guard chastity, loyalty, and filial conduct — lest they suffer a wretched retribution like Mr. Gia's.}
