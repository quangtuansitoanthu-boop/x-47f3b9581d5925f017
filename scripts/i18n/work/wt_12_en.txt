TITLE: 13. EATING MEAT OBSTRUCTS A PEACEFUL DEATH
TOMTAT: A woman past 50 who had recited the Buddha's name for years suffered diabetes from eating live sea crabs. Meeting Master Miaofa, she was taught that "illness enters through the mouth," repented, and became vegetarian.
-----
{align:center|**EATING MEAT OBSTRUCTS A PEACEFUL DEATH**}

{align:justify|There was a woman past 50 who had recited the Buddha's name for years, now suffering diabetes. Master Miaofa said it was summoned by her excessively heavy killing.}

{align:justify|She told how as a child she was timid — not daring to kill even a worm. When the Master asked whether she often ate live sea crabs, she froze in shock, and at once confessed everything.}

{align:justify|Her daughter had been posted to Japan for years, and every year brought her to Japan for a month's visit. Since she ate no meat, her daughter drove her every week to the fresh-seafood restaurants on the coast, explaining that steamed crab meat involved no killing of her own.}

{align:justify|Master Miaofa told her:}

{align:justify|"Those sea crabs, because diners demand to eat them, are steamed to death by the cooks."}

{align:justify|He continued:}

{align:justify|"These dishes — whether you saw them dead when bought, or bought them from the freezer, or had others cook them ready for you to eat — strictly speaking, all count as creating killing karma. For if eaters grew fewer, killing would lessen. Ultimately speaking, eating animals' flesh is creating killing karma!"}

{align:justify|Seeing her still dazed and uncomprehending, the Master taught further:}

{align:justify|"Some kill a great deal, even working at 'assisting killing,' yet appear most healthy today. While some, having killed a single creature or just eaten some meat, fall ill at once."}

{align:justify|The reason is that each person's accumulated karma and blessings differ. Just as one man may drink a full liter of white liquor daily for a long while with no effect, while another cannot take even a sip of wine or beer without drunkenness — this is because each person's capacity for liquor differs.}

{align:justify|But for those with evil habits — fond of killing, fond of eating animals' flesh — retribution comes sooner or later! Craving tasty food and a pleased palate, people slaughter animals at will; craving the delicacy of bloody meat, they draw upon themselves the seeds of hatred and calamity. Hence: 'Illness enters through the mouth.'"}

{align:justify|The Master asked her further:}

{align:justify|"Do you keep an imitation-antique bronze censer at home?"}

{align:justify|"Yes," she said, plainly astonished, staring wide-eyed at the Master.}

{align:justify|"Where do you keep it?"}

{align:justify|"On the terrace, I think!"}

{align:justify|When you return home, take it down, wash it clean, and let it dry. Whatever is for offering, if unused, must be kept and handled properly — or given to others. Never set things down carelessly anywhere; that is irreverent.}

{align:justify|The Master said earnestly:}

{align:justify|"I have reminded you of two things. If you know your fault and repent, your illness will gradually turn for the better. If you would be truly healthy, you should end fish and meat for good and keep a lifelong vegetarian diet. Reciting the Buddha's name while eating beings' flesh means greed uncut, offenses uncut — the Buddha's name and the heart can hardly unite. Recite a hundred years and still you can hardly reach paradise."}

{align:justify|In Hong Kong and the Kowloon suburbs, people often abandon statues of Guanyin Bodhisattva, Patriarch Bodhidharma, Ji Gong... casually by the roadside. That is irreverent. Please carefully avoid it. I hope that knowing your error you will at once correct it. A bodhisattva is one self-awakened to his own nature, skillfully saving all — like Kshitigarbha Bodhisattva and Guanyin Bodhisattva, both great bodhisattvas delivering beings from suffering.}

{align:justify|Human life holds much suffering; fortunately, in the sea of suffering bodhisattvas still aid us. That in this life you may draw near and hear a bodhisattva's name is thanks to many lives of wholesome roots, deep merit, and affinity — a precious and hard-won thing. A bodhisattva is the foremost treasure in the human world. If you abandon a bodhisattva's statue by the road, you abandon treasure, sever wholesome affinity — and the greatest loss is your own. Truly a pity.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story EATING MEAT OBSTRUCTS A PEACEFUL DEATH stresses that "illness enters through the mouth." Though the woman had recited the Buddha's name for years, she suffered diabetes from eating live sea crabs (even "steamed"). Master Miaofa states plainly: eating animals' flesh is creating killing karma, and "illness enters through the mouth" is its certain fruit. Even abandoning bodhisattva statues by the road is irreverent and wastes blessing. After repentance and vegetarianism, illness will turn for the better. The story warns those who "recite the Buddha's name yet still eat meat": "Reciting the Buddha's name while eating beings' flesh means greed uncut, offenses uncut — the Buddha's name and the heart can hardly unite." For recitation to bear fruit, first end killing and eat vegetarian.}
