MA VINH TO

Layman Ma Vinh To, styled Tru Luong, was a native of Xiushui, Zhejiang. As a boy he showed literary talent, and at fifteen was admitted among the licentiates. His parents died early, and Vinh To served his stepmother with devoted filial care. At thirty he contracted spitting of blood. After five years the illness worsened until whatever he ate he vomited up.

A monk named Tuong Phong, an old acquaintance of Vinh To before leaving the household life, came to visit. Fresh from traveling study, his dormant wisdom had opened and his understanding was penetrating. Hearing the monk discourse, Vinh To marveled and asked for a cure. Tuong Phong answered: "Fellow practitioner's illness springs from past karma and cannot be cured by medicine. If you can cast off myriad involvements and devote yourself to Buddha-recitation, in time, as the work deepens, the ignorance and karmic hindrances of immeasurable kalpas will gradually be severed. Then even the root-illness of birth-and-death rebirth can be liberated — how much less this bodily illness to worry over!". Hearing this, Vinh To started awake, thereafter kept a vegetarian diet and cultivated the pure karma. From then on the illness gradually eased.

Before that time, Ma Vinh To had dreamed that amid the sky arose countless characters for "longevity," emitting brilliant light. Reading Buddhist books, he learned that Amitabha means Immeasurable Life, also called Immeasurable Light. Recalling the dream, he saw its fulfillment and secretly rejoiced, counting himself karmically bound to the Pure Land gate. He then read the Platform Sutra and awakened to the source of mind, redoubling his joy and grateful sorrow. Afterward Vinh To took the five precepts, daily recited the Diamond and Amitabha sutras, recited the Buddha's name thirty thousand times, and practiced pure contemplation. In leisure he invited fellow practitioners to gather at his home, organizing day-and-night Buddha-recitation.

In the fifty-sixth year of Qianlong, in the last ten days of the first month, Vinh To returned from Suzhou; just a month later he fell ill. By early third month he lay abed unable to rise, telling a close friend: "Since thirty-five I have aroused the bodhi-mind, seeking only the adornment of the Buddha-land and the benefit of beings, regarding the glories of high office as worn-out sandals. Now, though bearing a grave illness, my one joyful thought has never changed. At present I only keep right mindfulness, awaiting life's end to be born straight into the West!". A friend asked: "When you are gone, with several mouths at home,
who will support them?". The layman answered: "That belongs to each one's karmic blessings; it is beyond my bearing!". On the evening of the eighteenth he said: "Tomorrow must be a great repentance!". Next morning he struggled up, donned his robe, venerated the Buddha in repentance, sat facing west, recited the Buddha's name some hundred times, then knelt and read Master Lianchi's vow text before returning to bed. Three days later he said: "The Three Holy Ones of the West manifest before me and preach the Dharma for me. I ever dwell within piercing brilliant light!". The next day he said again: "The Bodhisattva sprinkles pure water upon my body, washing away past debts, leaving me cool — a joy beyond words!". That evening a fellow practitioner, Tram Hong Dieu, visited, asked whether he recited, and cautioned him against attachment to marks. The layman answered: "Still reciting — mind itself is Buddha; what attachment to marks!". He then gave up food, drinking only a few cups of tea daily, and two days later only pure cool water. On the twenty-ninth day of the third month, at noon, he told his family: "The Buddha has come to escort me!". Having spoken, he recited the sacred name continuously, then lay on his right side and attained rebirth, at forty-eight. Childless, he left instructions for an heir to be established.

At that time Abbot Tuong Phong was in closed retreat at Wenxing Pavilion in Suzhou, cultivating Buddha-Recitation samadhi. That winter, one night he dreamed three venerables entered his hut and sat on the southern side facing him. In the middle sat a monk, flanked by two laymen, their bearing solemn and dignified, each head encircled by a halo. Tuong Phong bowed and asked: "Where have the three venerables come from?". The one in mourning dress said: "We come from the Western Land!". The Abbot asked: "Are you from Amitabha Buddha's Land of Bliss?". The monk answered: "Yes!". Tuong Phong asked their names, but they did not answer, asking instead: "What is your Dharma name?". The Abbot answered: "This disciple's Dharma name is Dat Van.". The monk said: "Why speak falsely?". Tuong Phong said: "It is truly so, not false!". The monk asked again as before; the Abbot answered as before. The monk again said: "Still false speech!". Tuong Phong said: "This disciple speaks truly, not falsely!". The monk said: "All beings take the false for true and cling to the unreal as real, hence their rebirth. The wise know all dharmas are false and nominally designated, and revolve no more. Do not delude yourself and lose the true mind. Understand: the true mind is no-mind, true knowing is not-knowing; to have knowing is Mara. The Buddha is spiritually knowing yet unknowing; believe and accept without doubt!". At these words Tuong Phong suddenly awakened and said: "Your reverences' teaching is truly the Buddha's inconceivable Dharma! Since you come from the Land of Bliss, may I ask: have you beheld Amitabha Buddha?". The one on the left answered: "If I told you, you might not believe. You must see for yourself!". Then all three rose; each tapped Tuong Phong upon the head and spoke a verse in turn:

Strive on with diligence,

Cultivating the pure karma unremittingly.

Deep the cause, true the fruit —

Be heedful and never doubt!

All dharmas arise from mind

And return to ceasing from mind.

The true nature was ever empty;

Taking or casting off cannot be done!

You are already partly clear:

Buddha and beings share one mind,

Like the Shurangama samadhi,

Like scenes within a dream.

The verses finished, Ma Vinh To appeared in dignified demeanor and bowed to the three. All three rose into the sky and flew westward. Then music resounded through the air, and the sound of Buddha-recitation, high and low, clear and free. Vinh To bowed thrice more to Tuong Phong, saying: "Thanks to your reverence urging me to cultivate the pure karma and take refuge in the Three Jewels, I have been blessed with birth in the West, receiving great peace and benefit. Hence I begged the Bodhisattva to come and speak the wondrous Dharma, repaying your profound kindness!". Having spoken, he bowed farewell thrice more, joined his palms, recited the Buddha's name, and flew to the West. Just then the midnight bell rang, and Tuong Phong woke with a start. The Abbot at once took brush and paper and recorded the omen, calling it the Record of a Dream.

Later, Abbot Tuong Phong secluded himself at Phuoc Thanh Cloister in Lujiang. At the end he foreknew the day and hour, and passed away seated, reciting the Buddha's name.

A comment: Before, Can Duong borrowed a spirit-medium sitting to exhort his companions. Now Tru Luong repays profound kindness through a responsive dream. Who then says that birth in the Land of Nurturing leaves no tidings! Yet with those whose conditions are unripe, all cases cannot be judged alike!
