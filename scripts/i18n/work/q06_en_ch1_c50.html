Translation: I teach bhikshus to beg for food in sequence, causing them to abandon greed and perfect the bodhi way. The bhikshus do not cook for themselves, lodging their borrowed remaining lives as travelers in the Triple Realm. Manifesting a single passing, gone never to return.

Commentary: I teach bhikshus to beg for food in sequence, causing them to abandon greed.

When begging time comes, each bhikshu heads in a fixed direction and circles a fixed area. They carry their bowls begging food. Why did the Buddha teach them to beg food? First, when lay Buddhist donors offer food to monastics, they gain blessed retribution and end suffering. Second, when bhikshus beg food, they take whatever is given. If tasty, they eat tasty; if bland, they eat bland. Thus they abandon greed. If one cooks for oneself, one will think, "What I cooked today is not good; yet tomorrow I shall cook something better, and the day after something more wonderful." There would be no end. When begging food, there is no chance to choose. One cannot discriminate which food or drink is good or bad. One cannot say: "What I begged today is most tasty," then eat with relish. And the next day, if the food is not good, one cannot eat. That way is not permitted. One eats both the tasty and the untasty. The general spirit is to eat to fullness and forget everything. That is to counter greed. In this way, the cultivator can perfect the bodhi way.

That is because of the saying: "The wise care for the Way, not for food." Those who come to investigate the Buddhadharma should not concern themselves with food.

The bhikshus do not cook for themselves, lodging their borrowed remaining lives as travelers in the Triple Realm. Manifesting a single passing, gone never to return. They need only eat enough to sustain life. Our lives, whether on land or on water, are like staying in a hotel — brief and fleeting for a moment. Do not be attached to it. The bhikshus have ended greedy minds, so when life in these three realms ends, they return no more. "This place is a defiled land; I shall not return." That is the bhikshus' thought. Even in America, with most beautiful bathrooms and most magnificent houses — enough to stay there once. Do not return! Do not crave those bathrooms. First, they are most foul-smelling; why do you like them? In fact this whole world is foul. Do not suppose it a clean place. This world is itself a bathroom.

Sutra text: 云何賊人假我衣服。禆販如來造種種業,皆言佛法。却非出家,具戒比丘為小乘道。由是疑誤無量眾生墮無間獄。

Transliteration: Vân hà tặc nhơn giả ngã y phục, tì bản Như Lai tạo chủng chủng nghiệp, giai ngôn Phật pháp. Khước phi xuất gia cụ giới tỷ-khưu vi tiểu thừa đạo. Do thị nghi ngộ vô lượng chúng sanh đoạ vô gián ngục.

Translation: How can thievish people borrow my robes, trade in the Tathagata, create all kinds of karma, and call it all Buddhadharma; yet slander fully-precepted monastic bhikshus as followers of the Small Vehicle way — thereby deluding countless beings into falling into the Uninterrupted Hells?

Commentary: How can thievish people borrow my robes? They wear monastic robes and tell people, "I am a sutra-lecturing Dharma Master. You should believe entirely in me."
