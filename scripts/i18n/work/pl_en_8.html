NGU THUAN HY

Layman Ngu Thuan Hy, styled Truong Nhu, was a native of Qiantang. At birth he lay on his back, alert, without closing his eyes. At three years old his mouth recited the Buddha's name unceasingly, and he often saw lotus blossoms and jeweled trees appear in the house. When Thuan Hy described these visions, his grandmother said: "Those are auspicious signs of the West!", and thereupon taught him to practice meditation. From then on he would often sit upright in lotus posture with lowered eyelids. His younger brother Thuan Trinh, styled Tang Nhu, also had wholesome Buddhist roots, and in youth the two were deeply intimate. While mourning their mother, both cultivated the Tiantai practice of calming and insight.

Grown up, Thuan Hy worked as a village tutor, often instructing his pupils in the gate of contemplation. This displeased his landlord, but he paid it little mind. He then passed the provincial examination and moved to Bi Mountain to teach. He once joined a society of friends in reciting the Liang Emperor Repentance, whereupon auspicious clouds gathered in the house, strange light shone into the chamber, sweet dew fell wetting the walls, and rain brought forth yellow rice, black glutinous grain, aloeswood, and incense. Though it was midwinter, a hundred flowers bloomed all around. Moved by these miraculous signs, Thuan Hy's meditative power grew firmer, so that he could foretell events. When Great Master Van The heard of this, he cautioned: "These are only passing wholesome visions. If you take them for realization, you will surely fall into Mara's net!".

In the eleventh year of Wanli under the Ming, he passed the metropolitan examination, but owing to his father's death returned home to keep vigil in a hut beside the grave for three years. In his leisure he received the refuge precepts from Master Van The. Dwelling in the grave-hut near the mountains, each day he offered rice to the deer and hares, shouting to drive away any tigers or leopards that came. When mourning was fulfilled, he took office as a secretary in the Ministry of Justice. Before long he resigned again and climbed Mount Tianmu, sitting before the coffin-stupa of the ancient Zen Master Gaofeng Miao, practicing Zen day and night. On the twenty-first day, weary in body and about to rest awhile, he dreamed as it were that Zen Master Gaofeng Miao severed his left arm — and suddenly, vastly awakened. He brought this state to Master Van The for confirmation. The Master said: "When one newly wakes, if instead of rising to wash and bind one's kerchief one still lies easy among quilts and pillows, one will surely fall back into dreams. So it is with one newly awakened to the Way: without adorning oneself with effort, but lingering amid defilement, one will surely grow deluded again. Young bamboo breaks easily; lotuses near fire soon wither. You must take care of yourself, and not let one small glimmer hinder your further progress!". Thereupon the Master urged the layman to recite the Buddha's name and dedicate the merit toward the Pure Land, continuing the wholesome causes of former lives. Thuan Hy bowed in thanks and vowed lifelong observance.

A visitor came who said he did not believe in Buddha-recitation. The layman said: "To awaken oneself and awaken others, with the conduct of awakening complete — that is called Buddha. Reciting the Buddha is reciting awakening. In every thought to be unawakened rather than ever awakened — can that be right? Birds roost on mounds and tall trees; people dwell in hamlets, lands, and countries. Not to settle in wholesome ground but in unwholesome ground — can that be right? Reciting the Buddha and seeking the Land of Bliss is precisely going toward awakening and being born in wholesome ground, to safeguard that awakening!". The man seemed to wake as from sleep, bowed gratefully, and asked how to recite the Buddha's name. The layman answered: "In each utterance of the sacred name, arouse right mindfulness,
and simply continue thus. For a hundred thousand skillful means lie in the single word 'awakening.' If one keeps every thought upon Amitabha — Immeasurable Life, Immeasurable Light — how could one fail to enter the Buddha's knowledge and vision? Students of the Way seek precisely to escape the round of birth and death. Keeping every thought thus, how could one fail to escape?

Before long he was recalled to serve as vice director in the Ministry of Rites, then promoted to director of honors. But after a while he again retired, and with his brother Thuan Trinh lived in seclusion, nourishing their nature and roaming among mountains and waters. At that time Master Van The was lecturing on the Complete Enlightenment Sutra at Nanping. The Master founded a Life-Release Society, raising funds to dig the Pond of Ten Thousand Works. Monastics and laity numbering in the tens of thousands gathered in support, and the sound of sutra chanting and Buddha-recitation shook mountains and forests. The brothers Thuan Hy rendered great service in leading many Confucian scholars and gentlemen to join that assembly. When the work was complete, three release ponds with dikes had been dug and many pavilions built for freeing birds and fish.

When all was finished, Thuan Hy withdrew into the mountains of Nanping, while Thuan Trinh secluded himself on Vulture Mountain. The two brothers resolved to spend their remaining years devoted to Buddha-recitation, to accomplish rebirth and appear no more.
