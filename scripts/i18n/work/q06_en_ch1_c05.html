Commentary: The first line of this verse is:
The sea of enlightenment, its nature clear and perfect;
Perfect, clear, the enlightened source sublime.
The perfect nature of the pure bodhi source is infinitely sublime. Now the verse speaks of: Empty space arises within great enlightenment. We all know of the existence of empty space, but do we know where empty space comes from? I believe no one knows. For empty space is too vast. One cannot know the limits of empty space or its source. Who is the mother of empty space? No one knows. But now the Shurangama Sutra points out its source. Empty space is born from the nature of great enlightenment. And so within that interval, it is extremely subtle. Is it large? It is like a single bubble rising in the sea — like foam-bubbles upon the great ocean.
Outflow lands as numerous as dust motes
All arise from space.

There are three kinds of outflows:
1. The outflow of desire.
2. The outflow of existence.
3. The outflow of ignorance.

Countless outflow lands all arise from empty space. Empty space arises from great enlightenment, and lands arise from empty space.
When the bubble bursts, space fundamentally is no more.
Like a bubble dissolving into the sea, empty space also vanishes into great enlightenment. How much less the Triple Existence!

Sutra text: 歸元性無二 方便有多門 　聖性無不通 順逆皆方便 　初心入三昧 遲速不同倫。

Transliteration: Quy nguyên tánh vô nhị Phương tiện hữu đa môn, Thánh tánh vô bất thông, Thuận nghịch giai phương tiện, Sơ tâm nhập tam muội, Trì tốc bất đồng luân.

Translation: Returning to the source, the nature is non-dual; Expedients have many doors; The sagely nature is all-penetrating; Accord and opposition are both expedients; Beginners entering samadhi Differ in quickness and slowness.

Commentary: In the verse, Manjushri Bodhisattva says that empty space is like a floating bubble in the sea of enlightenment, and the lands born within that empty space are also extremely subtle. If empty space vanishes, then all lands, mountains, rivers, lands, all beings and sentient and material worlds will also vanish. Returning to the source, the nature is non-dual.

"Returning to the source" means accomplishing Buddhahood. There is only one place to return to: your own home base. The world we are living in is not our own nature's home. You at once object, "I have a home!" But that home is not your true home. Becoming a Buddha is recognizing your true home. Before becoming a Buddha, you are a wanderer drifting from one hotel to another. You are ever moving.

Expedients have many doors. As for "expedients," they still involve entanglement. For example, once a child was crawling toward the mouth of a well on the ground. If the child kept crawling, it would fall into the well. The Buddha saw this, but he knew that if he called the child to come back, it would not obey but would crawl forward all the more. So he clenched his fist, raised it high, and called, "Child! Come back! I shall give you candy. Do you like candy?" When the child heard of candy, it at once crawled back. There was in fact no candy in the Buddha's hand. But did the Buddha lie? No. That is an example of expedient dharma. The Buddha used "the empty fist to rescue the child" because at the time there was no other way. Expedient dharma-doors are many. In general, any method you use to rescue people is called an expedient dharma.

The sagely nature is all-penetrating.
Accord and opposition are both expedients.
