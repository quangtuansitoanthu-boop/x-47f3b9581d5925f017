Commentary: The eye cannot see beyond obstructions.
"Eye" here is not the Buddha eye, wisdom eye, Dharma eye, heavenly eye, or flesh eye — it is not the flesh eye. What is meant here is the ordinary eye. These eyes can see only within limits; they cannot see penetratingly or above. They cannot see through obstructing things. For instance, if we close the windows and let down the curtains, we cannot see the scenery outside at all. Even in perceiving things close at hand for use, a single layer of skin blocks us from seeing the way. As with our inability to see anything inside the body with ordinary eyes. The eyes of sages are another matter, but here we are discussing the capacity of the ordinary eye. It cannot see through even a thin sheet of paper! Likewise, looking right it cannot see left, and looking left it cannot see right.
Mouth and tongue are likewise.
Between breaths there is also a pause when no air enters or leaves the nose. And the tongue must have flavor before the tasting-nature is known. With nothing to taste, the tongue is useless. So these two roots are both more or less like the eye root.
The body knows only in union.
The body must contact the object of touch before touch-awareness exists; thus it too is not complete.
Mind-thoughts are tangled without order.
Thought-moments are scattered and confused, with no order to them at all; so they too are not entirely good for cultivating perfect penetration.

Sutra text: 隔垣聽音響 遐邇俱可聞 五根所不齊 是則通真實。

Transliteration: Cách viên thính âm hưởng Hà nhĩ câu khả văn Ngũ căn sở bất tề Thị tắc thông chơn thật

Translation: Through walls, sounds are heard; Far and near, all can be heard. The five roots cannot match this — This is true penetration.

Commentary: With the ear root, through walls, sounds are heard. You still hear what is on the other side of a wall. Far and near, all can be heard.
The five roots cannot match this. Eyes, nose, tongue, body, and mind do not function like the ear root — it is more perfect than these roots. Its function is entirely interfused and unobstructed. The other roots cannot compare with the ear root. This is true penetration. Those other five senses lack this true penetration.

Sutra text: 音聲性動靜 聞中為有無 　無聲號無聞 非實聞無性。

Transliteration: Âm thinh tánh động tĩnh Văn trung vi hữu vô Vô thinh hiệu vô văn Phi thật văn vô tánh.

Translation: The nature of sound has movement and stillness; Within hearing these become "existing" and "non-existing." When soundless, it is called "not hearing" — But truly the hearing-nature is not absent.

Commentary: The nature of sound has movement and stillness.
Sometimes there is sound, sometimes no sound. When there is no sound, it is called stillness. Within hearing these become "existing" and "non-existing." The hearing-nature recognizes the movement of sound and the stillness when there is no sound. When soundless, it is called "not hearing." We call it so. But truly the hearing-nature is not absent. The hearing-nature definitely does not vanish. When we say we do not hear, the hearing-nature in fact never vanishes.
