{align:justify|THE UNFORTUNATE BRIDE}
{align:justify|In 1965 I was eighteen—joining the Youth Corps—stationed at Hoang Duong, Mongolia. In the village, near our team, lived the Pham family. The Phams had an eldest son and a youngest daughter. The Pham son wed a Shaanxi wife (her name I no longer clearly recall)—orphaned young—formerly dwelling with her brother and sister-in-law—gentle-natured, most capable and upright.}
{align:justify|Husband and wife loved each other most closely. But married long without children, the bride suffered her unmarried sister-in-law joining her mother-in-law treating her most ill—pointing at cats scolding dogs—hinting, breathing bitter sarcastic words, implying: 'Our house, ill-fated, wedded the thing that cannot bear!'"}
{align:justify|They often abused and tormented the bride—feeding only leftovers. New Year came, yet no new clothes sewn—but this gentle, honest, simple bride never once retorted—enduring all cruel torments most well.}

{align:justify|"'You ill-omened thing! Forbidden from attending my daughter's wedding!'"}

{align:justify|When her brother and sister-in-law visited—asking why their sister died? {align:justify|MY SECOND UNCLE}
{align:justify|I pondered very long—mustering courage to tell a cause-and-effect story touching my elders. Rightly, as a junior telling elders' tales is truly disrespectful—but I hope this story's contents shall mightily warn all and deepen faith in cause and effect.}
{align:justify|Now I tell of my Second Uncle: 'In my home village Second Uncle is society-recognized as an exemplary good man—honest, loyal-natured—yet his end was unwholesome: in 2005 he fell gravely ill and passed.}
{align:justify|Second Uncle was a Party cadre—diligent all his life. Living, he served as Provincial Party Secretary of the State Security Bureau.}
{align:justify|Born in 1949, Uncle served on the provincial executive committee—just and clean-natured. Then city-level leading cadres were just granted private cars—each with own car and driver. Second Uncle too was granted one—yet rarely used it—often cycling to work—while his driver must drive trailing behind him. Making our clan all laugh at this: private cars granted, yet refusing rides—'blessed yet unknowing enjoyment!'…}
{align:justify|Outside officials too deemed him old-fashioned—unknowing keeping with times—excessively upright—so working went poorly—often criticized.}
{align:justify|Later he caught a rare strange illness: bloody urine and stools—then intestinal hemorrhage—undergoing many surgeries—even once, barely operated, bleeding torrentially—long hemorrhaging—malnourished—moreover much cut open—gravely ill till all understood his organs had rotted, ruined…}
{align:justify|Grave illness racked him two, three years—at last every blood vessel too burst and bled—he passed in utmost painful torment at fifty.}
{align:justify|He left amid unceasing buzzing public talk. All deemed him a good cadre rare in a century—yet an honest loyal man—lifelong never erring—why did his end bear so piteous illness?! (While corrupt officials opposing him thrived in power—freely enjoying pleasures, in peace)… Then where is fair karmic retribution?…}
{align:justify|Later I gradually understood all causes…}
{align:justify|Since Uncle served the State Security Bureau, guns and ammunition abounded… plus his nature loved hunting—often mountain hunting and dam fishing…}
{align:justify|Hence, over a dozen years, he wantonly killed—slaying countless lives—so at last drew piteous painful retribution… for with ready guns and ammunition, he massacred many creatures: leaving them bleeding unceasing—burst bellies, pierced intestines—organs protruding all… precisely why his end bore strange painful illness—scenes exactly like that!'…}
{align:justify|Telling thus far, I drew one conclusion: however cleanly, justly you live—lifelong straight and upright—yet planting killing seeds—when retribution arrives, still you must bear it—never through other good deeds shielding, escaping sins…}
{align:justify|All loving killing or rejoicing joining killings—surely take warning…}
{align:justify|Cause and effect's law is absolutely fair—each person's straits project their created karma. Some evil yet see no retribution—because: past or former times they did much good—so this present life—that fortunate blessings remain most great—sufficient suppressing evil fruits from ripening (naturally their evil deeds never vanish—or perhaps meanwhile they secretly do good unknown to us—unseen, or undeemed good—for instance, filial piety counts as great good).}
{align:justify|So living, sow good seeds clean and whole—never live drunken, die dreaming… Living disbelieving cause and effect—carelessly creating evil—consequences most piteous… Please be careful!}
{align:justify|THE STORY THAT MADE ME WEEP}
{align:justify|That autumn I was twelve—my family's most piteous time: Father gravely ill, operated—working ability lost. My Second Brother failed university exams—while Mother misused pesticides—ten acres of heading rice lost. Also then I fell gravely anemic—gasping—needing urgent transfusion.}
{align:justify|Our house then drowned in debt—Mother, brow-knitting, pondered every way—yet knew not where to find my transfusion money.}
{align:justify|While the family fretted over my critical illness—neighbor Granny Ly stepped over—gravely telling my mother:}
{align:justify|"'Fourth Aunt—hurry rearward and see—your sow has farrowed!'"}

{align:justify|When Mother helped the sow farrow done—seeing the sow tired to foaming mouth—panting unceasing… Mother knew her age—having enriched our house near nine years… this litter numbered sixteen—seeing Mother-sow tired—hastily sending my brother fetching soy milk for her drinking—the sow gratefully flicking both ears—Mother viewing the rosy chubby piglet litter—telling my brother:}
