It is the six roots and nothing else. If you know how to use them, they will help you. If you do not know how to use them, they will destroy you. It is like money: when you have money, if you know you should do meritorious deeds and good works to benefit people, then the money is not wasted. But if you use money for drugs, gambling, and other unwholesome things, you have used your money to create karmic obstructions. This principle is the same with the six roots.

When sound is gone and root and sense-object are perfectly interfused, there is no more opposing subject and object. It is non-dual; it has become one substance. Thus there are no more six roots or six sense-objects; and so the six roots are just the six roots and the six sense-objects are just the six sense-objects. There is no more conflict between the six roots and the six sense-objects when the cultivator reaches this state. Thus I can enable beings to leave greed, anger, delusion, and hatred far behind.

I can make beings' hot tempers, fierce minds, and resentful hearts vanish. "Anger and hatred" refer to wrathful minds and afflictions. The Dharma Flower Sutra says: "If a person is full of anger, constantly reveres and recites Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's name, then anger can be eradicated."

The essential point here is constancy of recitation. It is not reciting today and forgetting tomorrow; reciting in the morning and not reciting at night; nor reciting this month and not the next. You must recite daily in your effort to deserve to be called constant in recitation. "Reverence" does not mean you recite the Buddha's name yet do not believe in the Buddha. You harbor doubt: "Is it really so? Is there such power?" Once you start raising questions, you will never succeed. Thus with faith, constantly and reverently reciting Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's name, you will no longer have an angry mind, and you will no longer have a violently afflicted mind. You have left those behind.

Sutra text: 十一者,銷塵旋明法界身心。猶如瑠璃,朗徹無礙。能令一切昏鈍性障,諸阿顛迦永離癡暗。

Transliteration: Thập nhất giả, tiêu trần triền minh pháp giới thân tâm. Du như lưu li, lãng triệt vô ngại. Năng linh nhất thiết hôn độn tánh chướng, chư a-điên-ca vĩnh ly si ám.

Translation: Eleventh, sense-objects dissolved, I turn the hearing back to the originally luminous nature; body, mind, and Dharma Realm bright as vaidurya, clear through and unobstructed. I enable all dull and stupid beings with obstructed natures, all atyantikas, forever to leave delusion and darkness.

Commentary: The eleventh fearlessness power is: sense-objects dissolved, I turn the hearing back to the originally luminous nature. When the realm of the six roots and six sense-objects has dissolved, I emit light. Body, mind, and Dharma Realm bright as vaidurya. Body and mind are the Dharma Realm; the Dharma Realm is body and mind. They become one. Body and mind pervade the Dharma Realm — is that not the Buddhas' realm? That is also Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's realm. Body and mind become like vaidurya jewels, clear through and unobstructed. From within one can see straight through to the outside; from the outside one can see straight through to the inside. There is no more inside or outside, small or large. Like the Chan Master Da Xiu of Ling Yen Mountain. He deserved such a name. The Master made himself a stone tomb just large enough to sit in. Then he made a stone door outside, carving upon it this couplet:

Vô đại vô tiểu, vô nội ngoại
Tự tu tự liễu, tự an bài.

In English:
Neither great nor small, neither inside nor outside.
Cultivate oneself, understand oneself, arrange for oneself.
