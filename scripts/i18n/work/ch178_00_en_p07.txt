{align:justify|When others asked why friend X would harm him, Kiet said jealousy of his talent. For his computer skills were better…}
{align:justify|So Kiet ever worried—eating, sleeping uneasy—papering walls with foil—saying it blocks electronic interference—also building a big hollow iron chest, ventilated—to sleep inside—covered above, lined below…}
{align:justify|But lying days, still feeling electronics' attacks unstoppable… So Kiet took a metal basin—upturned on his head—then hid under beds or in corners—anytime, anywhere—as crises peaked… His spirit nearly collapsed. Doctors called Kiet paranoid schizophrenic, prescribing drugs. But Kiet firmly refused—insisting he was not ill!}
{align:justify|Ms. Dien worried greatly—fearing her brother's illness worsening—consequences past imagining. For in her clan two already (a cousin-uncle and Third Uncle's son) suffered exactly this paranoid illness years before—results: the cousin-uncle suicided—Third Uncle's son, though living, lies long hospitalized—living yet as if dead.}
{align:justify|Now that illness descends on her brother—surely undruggable… She wished to ask the master—what fault causes this? Why do both parents' lines—all three—suffer identical strange ills? Shall hereafter this illness bequeath to descendants?}
{align:justify|The master told Ms. Dien:}
{align:justify|"Among your forebears, one was a writer—fond of horror-ghost fiction… for amusement—gravely harming countless readers. These works frightened viewers' hearts trembling—breeding fearful superstitions; nights panicked, sleep uneasy. Many youths, reading these novels, minds terrorized—deeply imprinted. Some fear darkness—dare not walk nights… Some, in special straits—for instance, startled by some sound—panicked into illness and death…"}
{align:justify|"'Such causes, such fruits.' For these horror novels, spreading among people, harming generations of readers—and at last retribution returns—harming the scribbler's own descendants."}
{align:justify|Whichever books people love reading—spread the longer. But wholesome books—granting people knowledge, peace—helping people advance—guiding filial piety, humanity; deference above, kindness below; just, clean; law-abiding… draw great blessed retribution—improving society's moral climate.}
{align:justify|Conversely, writing base things—novels, plays, television, films trumpeting terror, lust, theft, robbery, lewd filth… harms not only present society but bequeaths calamity many generations after.}
{align:justify|So pen-holders must surely understand cause and effect—never for momentary fun and profit scribble nonsense—leaving people's poison—bequeathing endless calamity to descendants' generations.}
{align:justify|Know: though now they fame and taste myriad joys—impermanence still comes. Eyes closing, nothing carried away—only evil karma carried—leaving disasters, miseries… to later lives and descendants. 'Harming others harms oneself!' Tell me, Ms. Dien—did any forebear write horror fiction?"}
{align:justify|"My family straits are rather complex—as a child I knew little… for political reasons the family scattered each direction… So of grandsires and forebears… I know still less. Grown, once I heard mother sketch a little. Thereby knowing my father sprang from famed scholar-gentry stock—grandfather and great-grandfather both Qing-dynasty scholars. I heard an aunt once translated some great author's horror fiction into Chinese. But who wrote horror fiction I know not… yet in a family mostly writers—most fond of composing thrillers… surely it exists…"}
{align:justify|I deeply believe the master's teaching—else why would my clan suffer identical strange ills in succession? Three have caught it already. Now my heart worries greatly—not knowing when those horror-ghost novels shall vanish from the world? Then must this dreadful illness bequeath generation after generation tormenting descendants? I respectfully ask master—is any way to save my brother? Any way banning this bequest?"}
{align:justify|The master smiled answering:}
{align:justify|"Fret not—naturally this must break. Without solutions—would I not add your heart-ill? Just now I spoke of 'karmic links'—for indeed sufferers of this illness exist. The patient's former life surely sowed like evil seeds. Suppose: they once frightened people—scaring some to death… so this life they are born into like families—merging into one clan bearing 'karmic retribution' descending…"}
{align:justify|Because you know and believe Buddhism—moreover striving Dharma-protecting—so blessings stored and Buddha affinities formed for family—so your brother hopes to recover. But surely you are not wholly vegetarian—am I right?"}
{align:justify|"Right, Master."}
{align:justify|"Then… returning home—can you lifelong vegetarianism?"}
{align:justify|"No problem, Master."}
{align:justify|"Good. Then hold a repentance assembly at a temple—delivering those harmed reading those horror novels. Repent on forebears' behalf (for elder authors) composing such poisonous novels."}
{align:justify|Afterward you yourself may dwell home—reciting ten straight 'Shurangama Sutra' rounds dedicating to Dharma-realm beings—using this merit dissolving forebears' sinful karma. Done—henceforth your clan shall know none catching this illness! Besides, daily remember offering a water cup before Buddhas—daily sincerely reciting one 'Universal Gate Chapter of Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin) Bodhisattva' round and three Great Compassion Mantras—then giving your brother to drink—his spirit shall gradually mend."}
{align:justify|Ms. Dien reverently noted Master Diệu Pháp's instructions.}
{align:justify|Now Ms. Dien has returned to America. Since this story warns well of cause and effect, I write it out sharing for your counsel—also hoping through this short piece reminding all to vigilance—careful sowing seeds.}
{align:justify|I dedicate this merit to Mr. Dien Kiet—praying his swift recovery.}

{align:justify|"Laypeople learning Buddhism a decade—also resolving everywhere giving—yet sutra and mantra texts unwilling reciting fluently! Giving is precious and hard—but studying sutra treasuries to deepen—growing wisdom, breaking ignorance… more needful and weightier!"}
{align:justify|Hearing the master's teaching, I felt ashamed—thinking hereafter surely striving diligent practice. My family life is most happy—my daughter teens—clever and bright—yet still I thirst bearing one more boy—silently wishing him Buddha-teachings' influence from womb-teaching—then lifelong acting on the Buddha's words—truly continuing His wisdom-life. This dream ever burned in me—but now my daughter is twelve—long years passed, still unconceived—so my heart regrets greatly.}
{align:justify|After learning Buddhism, I diligently practiced, reciting sutras… then miracles appeared. One day marketing—just seeing fresh fine apples—I stood transfixed—staring… my heart suddenly craving intensely—like pregnant craving. Then hospital examination—truly pregnant!}
