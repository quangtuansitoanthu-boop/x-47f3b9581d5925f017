TITLE: 12. THE EXPECTANT MOTHER WHO RECITED THE KSHITIGARBHA SUTRA
TOMTAT: Hai Ba, seven months pregnant, was tormented by a kicking fetus. Advised to recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra and eat vegetarian, she delivered smoothly; the baby grew healthy and never cried at milk tainted with meat.
-----
{align:center|**THE EXPECTANT MOTHER WHO RECITED THE KSHITIGARBHA SUTRA**}

{align:justify|Four years ago I met a young woman named Hai Ba, a student — calm, beautiful, most intelligent. Her family came from the Northeast, born into a devoutly Buddhist home.}

{align:justify|Early this year, Hai Ba was seven months pregnant. She told me the child in her womb kept thrashing and kicking, causing her pain, and asked if I knew any remedy.}

{align:justify|I told her to recite one copy of the Kshitigarbha Sutra each day, continuously for 7 days — it would both dissolve the karmic hindrances of former lives and pacify the debt-laden affinities of past lives between mother and child. Besides, at delivery she would suffer little pain, and the child born would be peaceful and easy to raise. Even if illness struck at times, it would soon heal. If she could now begin eating vegetarian, that would be better still!}

{align:justify|Hai Ba asked whether hen's eggs could be eaten. I said that today's market hens are raised to lay; if the eggs are unfertilized, she might eat them.}

{align:justify|Two months later Hai Ba telephoned her thanks, crowing that she had just come home from the maternity hospital. All had gone smoothly. She happily reported that when I suggested 7 copies of the Kshitigarbha Sutra, she had silently vowed 49 copies. Most fortunately — finishing the 49th, she entered hospital, and the next day delivered. At birth the doctor told her to push; she wished to recite Guanyin Bodhisattva's name, but no sooner had the thought arisen than she heard the doctor say: "I see the baby's head."}

{align:justify|When the doctor announced a chubby healthy boy, she wept for joy. She stayed four days in hospital and saw the other mothers all delivered by surgery — she alone delivered easily.}

{align:justify|The reason is that hospitals increase revenue: mothers, wanting smooth delivery, let doctors take the child by surgery. Yet all understand that surgery both wounds the body and wastes money. Truly it is karma summoning it, that is all.}

{align:justify|Ten days later Hai Ba called to say that on coming home from hospital the baby had never cried — not once. But three days later it began crying, all week now, crying even at the breast. Nothing satisfied it. It even ran a fever — no one knew why.}

{align:justify|I asked:}

{align:justify|"Have you eaten anything different?"}

{align:justify|She explained that her mother-in-law had come from the countryside and, seeing her eat vegetarian without meat, was most angry. She insisted Hai Ba eat herring to nourish her body. So she reluctantly ate to please her. Then she lamented:}

{align:justify|"My mother-in-law demands a stewed hen to feed me. What shall I do?"}

{align:justify|I said:}

{align:justify|"The baby cries and screams because ever since you recited those 49 copies of the Kshitigarbha Sutra for him, his karma dissolved and his wisdom-root grew. So he will not drink milk tainted with meat. If you eat no fish or meat, surely the baby will not cry."}

{align:justify|Hai Ba said: "Tomorrow my mother-in-law will kill a hen — how shall I meet it?"}

{align:justify|I said:}

{align:justify|"To avoid wounding your mother-in-law's feelings, you may yield and eat a little chicken or fish — but never kill live creatures; buy what is ready-sold in the market. Even so, each day you must recite the rebirth mantra for that chicken and fish, dedicating the merit to them, lessening their hatred at being slaughtered and eaten."}

{align:justify|Hai Ba negotiated with her mother-in-law. When the baby nursed it still cried, but afterward it slept in silence. Hai Ba thought: "The little one seems to understand his mother's dilemma!"}

{align:justify|Hai Ba's own mother had long been a pure vegetarian reciter of the Buddha's name; days later she came from the Northeast to visit her grandchild. Hai Ba said the baby smiled the moment it saw its grandmother — but cried whenever its paternal grandmother reached to hold it, and did not cry in its maternal grandmother's arms. Its paternal grandmother could not understand why.}

{align:justify|Hai Ba's mother knew why the baby cried at the breast, and would not have Hai Ba continue under pressure to eat meat — so on the pretext that the rooms were too cramped, she asked the mother-in-law's leave to take Hai Ba and the baby back Northeast to nurse.}

{align:justify|Today, seeing the little boy bright and lovely, growing sturdy day by day, the whole family rejoices.}

{align:justify|Explanation:}

{align:justify|In the sixth chapter of the Kshitigarbha Sutra, the Buddha teaches: "Hereafter, in this Saha world, if a newborn son or daughter is born, within seven days, early on the newborn's behalf recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra and recite Kshitigarbha Bodhisattva's name a full one thousand times — then the newborn child shall escape the sins of former lives it once created, and shall be peaceful, happy, easy to raise, and long-lived."}

{align:justify|And if the child already possesses abundant blessing, its fortune and lifespan shall increase further still.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story THE EXPECTANT MOTHER WHO RECITED THE KSHITIGARBHA SUTRA proves the merit of the Kshitigarbha Sutra for fetus and mother. Reciting 49 copies dedicated to her child, Hai Ba's fetus kicked less, delivery went smoothly (no surgery), the baby grew healthy and cried little. When the mother ate meat, the baby cried and refused the breast; when she ate vegetarian, it was content. This proves the fetus possesses spiritual awareness — receiving merit from the sutra and reacting to meat. The story urges expectant mothers: "For a healthy, easy child, early become vegetarian and recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra dedicated to your child and past-life creditors." It is the priceless gift parents can give their child while still in the womb.}
