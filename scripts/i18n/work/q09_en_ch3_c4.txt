<p><strong>Constantly asking others to destroy their own lives, that liberation come soon.</strong></p>
<p>He constantly tells others: Please kill me! If you have any way to help me end my life, that would be wonderful &#8211; I shall be liberated.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Thử danh tu hành, thất ư phương tiện. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng. Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu nhất phần, thường ưu sầu ma, nhập kì tâm phủ. Thủ chấp đao kiếm tự cát kì nhục, hân kì xả thọ, hoặc thường ưu sầu, tẩu nhập sơn lâm, bất nại kiến nhân. Thất ư chánh thọ, đương tòng luân trụy.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: This is called cultivating while losing skillful means. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization. But if interpreted as sagehood, a kind of ever-sorrowful demon enters the very heart: grasping knives and swords, slashing one's own flesh, delighting in casting life away; or ever sorrowful, fleeing into forests and mountains, unable to bear seeing people. Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>This is called cultivating while losing skillful means.</strong></p>
<p>Because the cultivator does not clearly understand expedient Dharma-doors, this mistaken attachment arises. Once the attachment has arisen, if the cultivator <strong>recognizes it, it is without fault</strong> &#8211; nothing is amiss &#8211; <strong>not sage realization</strong>: this does not mean he has realized sagehood. Now that you have heard the principles from this Sutra, you must be extremely cautious &#8211; do not let the mind drift freely in false thoughts. Do not give rise to thoughts of wanting to die, or to the thought that you cannot attain what you wish. If you keep such thoughts, you will attract this kind of demon at once &#8211; in this world they are very many. So you should not let your mind drift in false thoughts or speak carelessly and casually. If you do, this kind of demon will possess you at once; when that happens, you will be unable to bear it &#8211; losing freedom, even if you do not wish to die, there will be no way to avoid death.</p>
<p>But <strong>if you think you have realized sagehood, a kind of ever-sorrowful demon</strong> will come. You think you are sad and dejected &#8211; and that dejected sorrow also involves a kind of demon. Why are you disappointed or angry? If you opened your own Buddha eye, you would see: if people do not get angry, there are no demons at all &#8211; but the moment people get angry, demons taunt: &#8220;Flare up! Rage! Make havoc!&#8221;</p>
<p>Behind the scenes it urges you to flare up. Whether or not you believe me, what I say is still the truth &#8211; and if you do not believe my words, there is only the waste of time. Try it and see!</p>
<p><strong>Entering the very heart</strong> &#8211; possessing your body &#8211; <strong>grasping knives and swords, slashing one's own flesh.</strong></p>
<p>Have I not told you about Filial Son Wang? He is Master Chang Ren, who practiced filial conduct beside his parents' graves. One day, in samadhi, he saw bandits seizing his nephew and about to shoot him some five miles from where Master Chang Ren was &#8211; but just as a bandit was about to pull the trigger, from within samadhi Master Chang Ren brushed the gun aside with his hand so the bullet missed, and the nephew escaped. Behind this event, a ghost wanted to take the nephew's life &#8211; it had made the bandits come seize and try to kill the nephew.</p>
<p>Such are karmic connections: the ghost sought to kill the nephew, but Master Chang Ren blocked it, so the ghost could only stand behind him, unable to do anything to him &#8211; then it went to his younger brother, Second Master Wang, possessing him in place of the elder. Second Master Wang, knife in hand, went to the graves seeking Master Chang Ren, intending to kill himself before him.</p>
<p>The ghost wanted to kill the younger brother, yet strangely, knife raised ready to strike his own head, the ghost could not bring its murderous hand down. Meanwhile Master Chang Ren was reciting the Diamond Sutra; and while he recited, my own teacher (Master Chang Zhi), then not yet left home, felt uneasy within, as if something terrible were happening &#8211; worried and restless, wondering whether something had happened to Filial Son Wang out at the graves. He hurried to the cemetery to see &#8211; and indeed saw Second Master Wang holding a knife about to stab himself.</p>
<p>Filial Son Wang (Master Chang Ren) had written a few words beforehand for my teacher (Master Chang Zhi): &#8220;Go buy some paper spirit-money (by Chinese custom, people burn paper money as offerings for the dead). I shall recite the Diamond Sutra praying for this ghost's deliverance. There is a ghost involved in this matter &#8211; such is my vow.&#8221; After Filial Son Wang recited the sutra for the ghost's deliverance, nothing more happened.</p>
<p>However, this is only an example of the kind of demon under discussion &#8211; but in this case the ghost came wanting to take a life, not because of cultivation effort: for Filial Son Wang, while cultivating, still cared about the affairs of his kin. He blocked this ghost's attempt to kill his younger brother; the ghost could not kill Filial Son Wang because he had samadhi power and gave rise to no false thoughts, so the ghost could do nothing to him and went to harm the younger brother instead.</p>
<p><strong>Delighting in casting life away</strong> &#8211; delighting in dying.</p>
<p><strong>Or ever sorrowful</strong>: now that you have heard this sutra passage lectured, be careful not to grieve all day long anymore; do not constantly complain and feel afflicted. If you keep feeling sad and disappointed, this kind of demon will come possess your body at once. This happens partly through the demon's power and partly through your own mind: the demon borrows your spirit to act. If your mind abides in right views, nothing will go wrong; but if your thinking is not upright, the demon will command you &#8211; if it tells you to grieve, you grieve, until you flee into deep hiding, shunning everyone.</p>
<p><strong>Fleeing into forests and mountains, unable to bear seeing people. Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p>Such a person is sure to fall into the hells in the future.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong>6̉ &#8211; BOUNDLESS JOY (THE DEMON OF JOY)</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text:  Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch. Xử thanh tịnh trung, tâm an ổn hậu. Hốt nhiên tự hữu, vô hạn hỉ sanh. Tâm trung hoan duyệt, bất năng tự chỉ .</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; dwelling within purity, the mind secure and stable &#8211; suddenly, of itself, boundless joy arises; the heart rejoicing, unable to restrain itself.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed.</strong> Again, when the cultivator in samadhi sees the form skandha dissolved and clearly recognizes the feeling skandha &#8211; <strong>dwelling within the purity</strong> of right samadhi, <strong>the mind secure and stable</strong>: the mind utterly calm and steady, without a trace of distraction or false thought. Right at that point, the feeling skandha gives rise to another function.</p>
