In fact, everyone has a Buddha in mind, but cultivation effort is needed to recognize the Buddha-nature. Even with determined cultivation, one cannot become a Buddha in a single thought – a very long time is required. Though that time may be shortened through understanding and practicing the right Dharma, becoming a Buddha in a single thought is still hardly possible. One who cultivates without a good spiritual friend's guidance, however bitterly he practices, will only grow wrong views throughout his cultivation: seeing that after such long cultivation it amounts to becoming a Buddha anyway, he decides he is a Buddha – elevating himself to equality with the Buddhas. This situation occurs as the feeling skandha dissolves: he says he is equal to the Buddhas. Indeed, with that single mistaken thought he joins Mara's retinue.

One becomes incomparably arrogant toward everyone; the mind to the point of above seeing no Buddhas, below seeing no people. Why? Because it thinks it is itself a Buddha – and sees no one below itself anymore.

What then does it see? It says everyone is a Buddha; it itself is also a Buddha. There is no Buddha above it anymore – nor will there ever be a Buddha surpassing it. That is the state of seeing no one below itself anymore.
