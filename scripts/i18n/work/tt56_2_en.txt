THE LOWER FASCICLE
{align:center|**THE MAHĀYĀNA SŪTRA ON THE MERIT OF MAKING BUDDHA IMAGES**}

{align:center|**THE LOWER FASCICLE**}

{align:center|**Under the Great Tang Dynasty: The Land of Khotan**}

{align:center|**Tripiṭaka Śramaṇa Devaprajñā, Translating by Imperial Order**}

{align:center|**Rendered into Vietnamese by Bhikkhu Thích Thiện Thông of Vietnam**}

{align:justify|At that time, the Buddha sat upon the lion throne[35] at the sanctuary in the land of Sāṃkāśya. In the hearts of the four assemblies, each one thought: “We long to hear the Tathāgata expound the merit of making statues. If a being fashions one, what blessings does such a one reap?”}

{align:justify|Then the Great Bodhisattva Maitreya, discerning their thoughts, rose from his seat, bared his right shoulder, knelt upright with palms joined, and addressed the Buddha:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! King Udayana made a Buddha image. Whether the Buddha is in the world or after his nirvāṇa, if a believer fashions a Buddha image according to their share, what merit does such a one reap? We humbly beg the World-Honored One to expound these marks at length.}

{align:justify|The Buddha told the Bodhisattva Maitreya:}

{align:justify|- Maitreya, listen and bear it well in mind; I shall speak for you.}

{align:justify|- If there is a good man or good woman of pure faith, wholly intent in mindfulness upon the Buddha's virtues, ever contemplating the Tathāgata's sovereign august virtue, complete with the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses[36], the eighteen exclusive attributes, great kindness, great compassion, all-aspect knowledge, the thirty-two marks of a great man, and the eighty minor characteristics — every pore emitting infinite lights of varied hues, adorned and perfected by hundreds of thousands of millions of surpassing blessings, radiant and unobstructed with measureless wisdom, measureless concentrations, measureless supernormal powers... all such virtues measureless, free of faults, beyond compare.}

{align:justify|Whoever contemplates thus and gives rise to profound faith and delight, and fashions a Buddha statue according to those fine marks — that person's merit is vast and boundless, beyond reckoning.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! If someone embroiders a Buddha image with silk threads and yarns[37], or casts one in gold, silver, copper, iron, lead, or zinc, or carves one from fragrant sandalwood, or fashions one from pearls, shells, brocade and woven silks, red earth, cement, plaster, clay, or various woods... using such materials according to their means to make a Buddha statue[38], even one as small as a thumb, so that others who see it know it is a venerable statue — that person's blessed reward I shall now describe:}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! Such persons, though still drifting through birth and death, will never be born into desperately poor families, nor into lowly or ignoble lineages, nor lonely and remote on a country's fringes. Nor will they be born into wrong-view families, traders' or merchants' homes, or butchers' households, down even to lowly lines, impure brothels, or the households of heterodox ascetics holding wrong views — unless through the power of compassionate vows; apart from that, they will never be born into such homes.}

{align:justify|Such a person is often born into the clan of a righteous wheel-turning king of great power, or into a Brahmin line of pure conduct, or into a wealthy house free of grave offenses. Wherever born, they always meet Buddhas and reverently serve and make offerings to them; or they become kings who safeguard the true Dharma, teaching it to others without going against the path; or mighty wheel-turning sage-kings, perfected in the seven treasures, endowed with a thousand sons, flying through the sky to teach the four continents, free and blissful all their lives. Or they become Śakra, kings of the Yāma Heaven, kings of the Tuṣita Heaven, kings of the Nirmāṇarati Heaven, kings of the Paranirmitavaśavartin Heaven — heavenly and human delights, none unenjoyed. Such blessed rewards continue unbroken. Wherever born, they are always men, never taking a woman's body, nor the base hermaphrodite or dual-sex body. The body they receive at birth is never ugly: eyes never blind or defective, ears never deaf or misshapen, noses never crooked or broken, mouths never twisted, lips never drooping nor pursed, teeth never gapped, missing, black, or yellow, tongues never short or drawn back, necks never goitered, backs never hunched, skin never blotchy, hands never stubby, feet never lame — never too gaunt, never too stout, neither too tall nor too short... none of the unlovely features at all.}

{align:justify|That person's body is upright and dignified, face full and round. Hair deep blue, soft, bright, and clean. Lips red as lacquer, eyes like blue lotuses, tongue broad and long. Teeth white, even, and close; speech skillful and fine, delighting all who hear. Elbows well-proportioned, palms level, back and thighs full, shoulders and chest broad, hands and feet soft as cotton floss, all marks complete with nothing lacking, sinews most strong — like the god Nārāyaṇa[39] himself.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! Suppose a man falls into a cesspit, then climbs out, shaves away the filth, washes himself clean with water, anoints his body with perfumes, and dons fresh clean robes... Between such a one and one still stuck in the cesspit, not yet out — how far apart are they in cleanliness and fragrance? World-Honored One, the distance is incalculably great!}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! If someone still amid birth and death gives rise to faith and fashions a Buddha image, the distance between that person's state after fashioning and before is just the same. Know that such a one, in life after life, is cleansed of karmic hindrances; all manner of arts they master without being taught. Though born human, their faculties are as those of the heavens. If born in heaven, they far surpass the many gods. Wherever born, they are free of deformity — never leprous, never cancerous, never possessed by demons and sprites, never seized by madness, consumption, jaundice, malaria, stones, foul sores, deep scars, unremitting dysentery, indigestion, aching joints, or hemiplegia... such four hundred and four maladies, none at all[40].}

{align:justify|Nor are they harmed by poisons, weapons, staves, tigers, wolves, lions, flood, fire, thieves, or robbers — no such sudden calamities can injure them. Ever fearless, they commit no offenses.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! If beings formerly created evil karma and must undergo all manner of torment — clamped, manacled, cangued, chained, beaten, scolded, burned, branded, flayed, scalped, hung upside down, even disemboweled and burned alive... should they give rise to faith and fashion a Buddha image, none of those painful fruits shall they undergo. They shall never be born where brigands rage, cities collapse, baleful stars work transformations, famine and pestilence strike... to such places they shall never be born; to say they are born there would be false speech.}

{align:justify|Then the Great Bodhisattva Maitreya addressed the Buddha:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! The Tathāgata always says: “Good and evil karma are never lost.[41]” If beings create grave karma and must be born into base, lowly lines — poor, deformed, short-lived — then later give rise to faith and fashion Buddha statues, must they still undergo those sinful fruits, or undergo them not?}

{align:justify|The Buddha told the Bodhisattva Maitreya:}

{align:justify|- Now listen carefully; I shall speak for you. If beings who created offenses then resolve to fashion statues, seek repentance, firmly cut off the evil, and vow never to transgress again, then the offenses' creation and blessings alike wipe them away. Now I shall illustrate this matter at length for you.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! Suppose a man, formerly miserly, through those causes suffers poverty — without wealth, lacking necessities — when suddenly he meets a bhikkhu fresh from deep concentration; the man at once offers food and drink in alms, and having given, leaves poverty behind; whatever he seeks accords with his wish. Maitreya! Does that poor man's former evil karma and due retribution still remain?}

{align:justify|Maitreya replied:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! Through offering food, his old evil karma is wholly destroyed; he leaves poverty behind and gains wealth in abundance.}

{align:justify|The Buddha taught:}

{align:justify|- Bodhisattva Maitreya! Just as you say. Know that the other is just the same: through fashioning a statue, old evil karma is ended utterly, nothing remaining; what was due to undergo, they undergo no more.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! There are three kinds of karma[42]: first, retribution undergone in this very life; second, retribution undergone in the next life; third, retribution whose fruit ripens only after many lives... Of these three kinds, each may be fixed or unfixed. For one who with faith fashions a Buddha statue, only fixed karma is partially undergone in this present life; all other kinds are never undergone.}

{align:justify|Then the Bodhisattva Maitreya addressed him:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! The Tathāgata always says there are five deeds of the gravest weight, certain to plunge one into the uninterrupted hell[43]: killing one's father, harming one's mother, killing an arhat, maliciously shedding a Buddha's blood, and breaking the harmony of the sangha. If beings who formerly created such offenses later give rise to faith in the Buddha and fashion statues, do such persons still fall into hell, or fall not?}

{align:justify|The Buddha told the Bodhisattva Maitreya:}

{align:justify|- Now I shall tell you another parable: Suppose a man with a mighty bow amid a forest aims upward and looses at the leaves; the arrow flies straight through, unhindered. If beings who committed those transgressions later fashion Buddha statues and sincerely repent, gaining faith not yet fully firm, and imagine their offenses grown thin and light — then even should they fall into hell, they emerge at once, like the arrow shot straight through without pause; so it is with such persons... Or like a bhikkhu accomplished in supernormal power, passing from this shore across the sea around all four continents with nothing to bar him. So it is with them: through former offenses they briefly fall into hell, yet that former karma cannot obstruct them.}

{align:justify|The Bodhisattva Maitreya questioned further:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! The Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, possess a Dharma-nature body, not a body of form and marks. If form and marks were taken as the Buddha's body, then the bhikkhu Nanda and wheel-turning kings would rightly be Buddhas, for they too possess all the fine marks. If a being violates the Buddha's Dharma-body[44] — calling Dharma non-Dharma and non-Dharma Dharma — then later gives rise to faith and fashions a Buddha statue, is that grave offense destroyed together, or destroyed not?}

{align:justify|The Buddha told the Bodhisattva Maitreya:}

{align:justify|- If a being calls Dharma non-Dharma and non-Dharma Dharma, using only the mouth without corrupting right view, then later gives rise to faith and delight and fashions a Buddha statue, the former evil karma meets only light retribution in the present body; they fall not into evil paths, though from birth and death they are not at once liberated.}

{align:justify|The Bodhisattva Maitreya questioned further:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! If someone steals property from a Buddha stūpa, steals goods of the permanent sangha, steals goods of the sangha of the four quarters or of the present sangha — consuming them personally, giving them to others to consume, treating them as their own. The World-Honored One always says: “Consuming goods stolen from a Buddha stūpa or from the sangha is a most grave offense.[45]” If beings who created such offenses then deeply reproach themselves, give rise to pure faith, and fashion Buddha statues, are the offenses of such persons destroyed?}

{align:justify|The Buddha told the Bodhisattva Maitreya:}

{align:justify|- If beings who consumed such goods then reflect, embrace shame and remorse, restore the usual amount, and vow never to transgress again, now I shall tell you a parable: As a poor man formerly deep in debt who suddenly finds a buried treasure of countless gold and jewels — his debts repaid in full with great surplus remaining. So it is with such offenders: having restored those goods and further fashioned a Buddha statue, they escape calamitous suffering and ever abide in peace.}

{align:justify|Then the Great Bodhisattva Maitreya again addressed the Buddha:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! As the Buddha once taught: “One who commits an expulsion offense within the Buddha's teaching[46] is not called alive.” If someone who committed such an offense then recollects the Buddhas' virtues and fashions a statue, within the Buddha's teaching does such a one “live” again? And in this life, or the second, third, or fourth life, may they realize the Dharma?}

{align:justify|The Buddha told the Bodhisattva Maitreya:}

{align:justify|- It is as with a man bound and fettered, hands, feet, and neck — should he be freed, like a bird escaped from the net flying off at will; so it is with the other. Should they give rise to faith, contemplate the Buddhas' virtues, and fashion a Buddha statue, all karmic hindrances are wiped away; amid birth and death they swiftly emerge, unobstructed. Maitreya, know that there are three vehicle Dharmas[47]: the Śrāvaka vehicle, the Pratyekabuddha vehicle, and the Great Buddha Vehicle. Makers of Buddha statues, according to whichever vehicle they aspire and incline toward, in that very vehicle attain liberation. And if they seek only to accomplish the Buddha path, craving no other reward, then though hindrances be heavy they are swiftly destroyed; though amid birth and death, no calamitous suffering befalls them, until they realize supreme awakening, gain a pure land, possess all fine marks, and enjoy a lifespan long and unending.}

{align:justify|Then in the assembly, those who had not yet aroused the Great Vehicle mind all conceived doubts, thinking: “Did our Tathāgata in past times fashion Buddha statues or not? If he did, why was his lifespan beset with impurities, not pure and clean?”}

{align:justify|King Prasenajit, through the Buddha's spiritual power[48], at once rose from his seat, joined his palms, knelt upright, and addressed the Buddha:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! I see the Tathāgata's faculties, fine marks, and lineage are all foremost; in my heart I am resolved, with no room for doubt. Yet the Tathāgata once had a khadira thorn pierce his foot. Once Devadatta rolled a boulder whose shard drew the Buddha's blood. Once before he declared himself ill and summoned Jīvaka to prepare fine medicine. Once he suffered pain in the back and told the venerable Mahākāśyapa to recite the seven factors of awakening, whereupon the pain was dispelled. Once he suffered aching pain and sent Ānanda to a Brahmin house to beg cow's milk. Once before, at the village of Ta La, for the three months of the rains retreat he ate only horse-feed barley. And once he begged for alms and got nothing, returning with an empty bowl.[49]”}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! As the World-Honored One says: “If anyone fashions a Buddha statue, all karmic hindrances are wiped away, leaving all suffering and sickness behind, with no afflictions.” Did the World-Honored One in former kalpas fashion statues or not? If in the past he fashioned statues, what causes explain those events?}

{align:justify|The Buddha told King Prasenajit:}

{align:justify|- Listen! Listen and bear it well in mind; I shall analyze and explain for the great king. O great king! In former kalpas I, seeking awakening, used precious jewels, fragrant sandalwood, paint, and drawings to fashion Buddha statues — more than all the gods and humans in this present assembly. Through that blessing, though still coursing through birth and death undeluded, I received a body durable as diamond, indestructible.}

{align:justify|Further, O great king! I recall that countless kalpas ago, still amid birth and death, when I fashioned Buddha statues I was still beset with greed, anger, and delusion[50]... measureless afflictions joined with my mind — yet never for a single thought-moment did I, through offenses, suffer the four elements in disarray, or possession by demons and spirits, or the least affliction of sickness, while of necessities not a single thing was ever lacking — how much less now that I have realized the path of awakening? How could there still be matters “not as wished”?}

{align:justify|- Great king! Had I in former times fashioned Buddha statues, and now some residual karma of retribution remained to undergo, how could I utter the fearless proclamation, declaring: “Fashioning statues unfailingly wipes away all evil karma”?}

{align:justify|- Great king! In past times I gave alms of measureless food, drink, wealth, and jewels[51]; how could I in this life beg and get nothing, reduced to eating horse-feed? If such a thing were really so today, how could I in countless sūtras praise the perfection of giving in many ways, saying the blessings of generosity never prove false?}

{align:justify|- Great king! I am one who speaks truth, speaks reality, never speaks to deceive. If I ever spoke falsely, whom could I liberate?}

{align:justify|- Great king! Long ago I ended all evil karma, abandoned what is hard to abandon, did what is hard to do, gave up life and limb hundreds of thousands of millions of times, fashioned measureless images of Buddhas, and repented measureless evil offenses — how could I suffer wounds, sickness, horse-feed fare, and hunger? Had I once gained supreme fruits only to lose them now, how could I urge the cultivation of those wholesome blessings?}

{align:justify|- O great king! The Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, possess an eternal body, a Dharma-nature body[52]; to liberate beings they manifest the above events — they are not real. Pierced feet, aching backs, begging milk, taking medicine... down to entering great nirvāṇa, dividing their relics among many places, building stūpas, and so on... all are the Buddhas' fine skillful means, letting beings behold such marks.}

{align:justify|Great king! That I display such sicknesses in the world aims to let beings know clearly that karmic retribution is never lost, making them fearful so they cut off offenses and cultivate wholesome deeds — and thereafter come to know the eternal body, know the Dharma-nature body, its lifespan boundless, its land pure.}

{align:justify|- Great king! The Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, never speak falsely; all is great compassion and skillful wisdom, and therefore they can in every way manifest thus.}

{align:justify|Hearing these words, King Prasenajit leapt with joy, and together with countless hundreds of thousands of beings, all aroused the mind of the supreme path[53].}

{align:justify|Then the Great Bodhisattva Maitreya continued questioning the Buddha:}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! There are women of narrow will, prone to jealousy, anger, frivolity, flattery, and deceit, nursing grudges unrelinquished, ungrateful for kindness received. Though they seek awakening, few keep a lasting resolve; ever wishing to delude everyone, they are in turn deluded by others[54].}

{align:justify|- World-Honored One! If such a woman fashions a Buddha statue, are those deeds wiped away? Can she become one who knows and repays kindness? Can she be perfected in wisdom, great kindness, and great compassion? Toward birth and death, can she grow weary of it? Apart from vow-power, shall she ever take a woman's body again? Can she become like Gautamī and the Buddha's mother, Lady Māyā?}

{align:justify|The Buddha answered the Bodhisattva Maitreya:}

{align:justify|- If a woman fashions a Buddha statue, she shall surely never take a female body again. Should she take one, it shall be as the noblest, foremost of women. Yet women possess five virtues, and women who fashion statues gain them beyond all womankind. What are the five virtues?}

{align:justify|First: bearing sons and daughters.}

{align:justify|Second: noble lineage.}

{align:justify|Third: a chaste and virtuous nature.}

{align:justify|Fourth: beauty of both substance and features.}

{align:justify|Fifth: a graceful and perfect figure.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! All women, through eight causes, ever take female bodies for many lives. What are the eight?}

{align:justify|First: cherishing the female body.}

{align:justify|Second: craving the sensual pleasures of womanhood.}

{align:justify|Third: praising with the mouth the fair form of women.}

{align:justify|Fourth: a crooked heart, hiding one's deeds.}

{align:justify|Fifth: despising one's husband.}

{align:justify|Sixth: a heavy heart, longing for another.}

{align:justify|Seventh: knowing kindness received yet turning against it.}

{align:justify|Eighth: adorning oneself with guile, longing to entrance men.}

{align:justify|Should they utterly cut off these eight and fashion Buddha statues, until becoming Buddhas they shall ever be men; taking a woman's body again is out of the question.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! There are four causes by which men take women's bodies. What are the four?[55]}

{align:justify|First: mocking Buddhas, Bodhisattvas, and holy ones in a woman's voice.}

{align:justify|Second: slandering precept-keepers, calling them precept-breakers.}

{align:justify|Third: delighting in flattery to deceive others.}

{align:justify|Fourth: envying whoever surpasses them.}

{align:justify|Should any man do these four, after death he shall surely take a woman's body, besides enduring measureless suffering in the evil destinies. Should he give rise to deep faith, repent former deeds, and fashion a Buddha statue, such offenses are all destroyed, and never again shall he take a woman's body.}

{align:justify|- Maitreya! There are four causes by which men take hermaphrodite bodies[56]. What are the four?}

{align:justify|First: maiming the forms of humans and beasts.}

{align:justify|Second: raging in anger, mocking and slandering śramaṇas who keep pure precepts.}

{align:justify|Third: abounding in lust, deliberately breaking precepts.}

{align:justify|Fourth: keeping company with precept-breakers and urging others to break them.}

{align:justify|Should any man formerly do these, then give rise to faith and fashion a Buddha statue, until becoming a Buddha he shall never undergo such retribution, ever a man with faculties complete.}

{align:justify|Maitreya! There are four deeds by which men take dual-sex bodies[57], lowest among all people. What are the four?}

{align:justify|First: lewd defilement in venerable precincts.}

{align:justify|Second: though in a man's body, stained indulgence with men.}

{align:justify|Third: performing sexual acts upon oneself.}

{align:justify|Fourth: trading in female beauty for others.}

{align:justify|Should any being do these things, deeply reproach themselves, repent former transgressions, give rise to pure faith, and fashion a Buddha statue, until becoming a Buddha they shall never take such a body.}

{align:justify|Maitreya! There are further four conditions by which men ever conceive a woman's lust in their hearts[58], delighting when others treat them as men treat women. What are the four?}

{align:justify|First: doubting or jesting to slander others.}

{align:justify|Second: delighting in women's dress and adornment.}

{align:justify|Third: lewd indulgence with kinswomen.}

{align:justify|Fourth: truly without virtue, yet accepting others' homage.}

{align:justify|Through these causes men give rise to such aberrant afflictions. Should they repent offenses committed, commit no new ones, and give rise to faith and delight to fashion a Buddha statue, those offenses are destroyed and those minds likewise ended.}

{align:justify|Maitreya! There are five kinds of stinginess[59] that ruin beings. What are the five?}

{align:justify|First: begrudging one's village and neighborhood haunts — hence born in desolate, lonely wilds.}

{align:justify|Second: begrudging shelter under one's eaves — hence becoming a worm of the privy, ever dwelling amid foul excrement.}

{align:justify|Third: begrudging one's fair beauty — hence a form unsightly, against one's wish.}

{align:justify|Fourth: begrudging one's goods and wealth — hence poor and needy, lacking clothes and food.}

{align:justify|Fifth: begrudging the Dharma one knows — hence the fruits of a dull, brutish birth.}

{align:justify|Should they repent their former deeds and fashion a Buddha statue, they shall leave that former stingy mind behind and never undergo such fruits as described!}

{align:justify|Maitreya! There are further five conditions by which beings are born among barbarians, in border marches[60], or in ages without the Buddhadharma. What are the five?}

{align:justify|First: giving rise to no pure faith toward the blessed field of the Three Jewels.}

{align:justify|Second: against truth and awry of right principle, yet presuming to instruct.}

{align:justify|Third: teaching and transmitting to others what accords not with truth.}

{align:justify|Fourth: breaking the sangha's harmony, splitting it in two.}

{align:justify|Fifth: down to the least — setting two bhikkhus at odds, breaking their harmony.}

{align:justify|Cutting off the above deeds and fashioning Buddha statues, they shall ever meet Buddhas and ever hear the essential Dharma.}

{align:justify|Maitreya! Beings have further five causes for being ever loathed and driven off by the many, until even kin dislike meeting them[61]. What are the five?}

{align:justify|First: double-tongued speech.}

{align:justify|Second: harsh and cruel speech.}

{align:justify|Third: much quarreling.}

{align:justify|Fourth: much resentment.}

{align:justify|Fifth: skill in innuendo aimed at disparaging others.}

{align:justify|Should they later resolve to fashion Buddha statues, repent old deeds, and vow to create them no more, that person's offenses are all wiped away, and all people cherish and draw near them. Why? Because the Buddhas possess measureless, boundless surpassing blessings, measureless, boundless vast wisdom, measureless, boundless concentrations and liberations[62] — replete with rare Dharma-nature virtues.}

{align:justify|- O good men! Suppose one ground a trichiliocosm of worlds to fine dust, then ground that dust again, dividing every mote into as many motes as the dust of one great chiliocosm — how many multiplied motes of those world-grounds could there be! Suppose one took a ground mote and, by supernormal power, traveled eastward, in one instant crossing as many worlds as the ground dust of one great chiliocosm (a billionth part); in the second instant, the third instant, just the same, journeying on through as many kalpas as that ground dust; and in every instant of every kalpa, crossing as many worlds as the ground dust of a whole great chiliocosm; having crossed those worlds, dropping one mote. Then returning for another mote, journeying eastward past even more worlds to drop it once more; with the third mote surpassing the last, and so on in turn, doubling each time, until all the ground dust is exhausted.}

{align:justify|As with the east, so with the south, west, and north. The lands of the four quarters that traveler crossed — all those worlds ground wholly to dust. All beings together reckoning these dust-motes might still know their number; yet the virtues of each single hair of the Tathāgata's body can never be known. Why?}

{align:justify|Because the virtues the Buddhas possess are all measureless and inconceivable.}

{align:justify|O good men! Suppose as many people as those dust-motes just described, each endowed with Śāriputra's wisdom — all their wisdom together would not equal one thought-moment of the Tathāgata's wisdom. Why?}

{align:justify|Because in every thought-moment the Tathāgata ever manifests right concentrations, liberations, retentive dhāraṇīs, and the like — more numerous than the dust of the parable — each replete with measureless surpassing virtues.}

{align:justify|The surpassing virtues of the Buddhas — all Śrāvakas and all Pratyekabuddhas cannot even comprehend their names to call them! Therefore whoever with pure faith fashions a Buddha statue sees every karmic hindrance destroyed, gains measureless, boundless virtues, until realizing supreme awakening and wholly uprooting suffering for all beings.}

{align:justify|Having spoken this sūtra, the Buddha was met with joy: the Bodhisattva Maitreya and Śakra, Lord of Gods, King Udayana, and the whole world of gods, humans, asuras, and Gandharva spirits, hearing the Buddha's teaching, all rejoiced, believed, accepted, and practiced accordingly.}
