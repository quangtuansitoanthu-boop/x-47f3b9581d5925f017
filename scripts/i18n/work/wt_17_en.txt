TITLE: 18. THE FETUS WHO HEARD THE SUTRA AND RELEASED ITS GRUDGE
TOMTAT: Chung Hong's pregnancy was tormented by a kicking fetus — a past-life creditor (her husband's grandfather was a pig butcher). Through reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra, the fetus dissolved its karma, ascended to the heavens, and bore no more hatred.
-----
{align:center|**THE FETUS WHO HEARD THE SUTRA AND RELEASED ITS GRUDGE**}

{align:justify|The young man Manh Vi and the young woman Chung Hong were sweethearts sharing the Buddhist faith. After marriage, both of one heart and one purpose, they recited sutras together, kept the precepts, and practiced — a model Buddhist family, living most happily, the envy and longing of fellow practitioners.}

{align:justify|Chung Hong's friend Lan, about her age, had married earlier. During pregnancy, thanks to Lan's sincere recitation of the Kshitigarbha Sutra and the Universal Gate Sutra, and her urging the family not to slaughter creatures to nourish her, Lan went on to bear one lovely darling after another. (Babies born through parents' sutra recitation usually share a trait: most intelligent, pretty, lively, smiling often, crying little, never troubling their parents.)}

{align:justify|When Chung Hong became pregnant, out of love for the child the couple began reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra, hoping to bear a baby of deep wholesome roots, beautiful and pleasing.}

{align:justify|During the pregnancy, the fetus in Chung Hong's belly reacted violently, tormenting its mother fiercely — nausea, irritability, swelling, sciatic pain... no appetite, no peaceful night. In short, she tasted more suffering than other mothers-to-be.}

{align:justify|Their only consolation was that on the evening before delivery, after the final prenatal check, the fetal heartbeat — all — was normal. The doctor predicted birth on New Year's Day itself — a day of still greater joy for the whole family.}

{align:justify|On New Year's Eve, every house rejoiced in reunion, firecrackers crackling crisp, and the baby in Chung Hong's belly waved its arms and danced, punching and kicking wildly. Chung Hong's mother-in-law said:}

{align:justify|"This little one seems most eager to come out and play with firecrackers!"}

{align:justify|Her jest made everyone laugh.}

{align:justify|But all things are impermanent: at birth they learned the baby had died, the cord wound around its neck. Dead on New Year's Eve night, dead after its last great movement. While all rejoiced and thronged, longing and waiting for its birth.}

{align:justify|This evil news was truly thunder in a clear sky. It not only stunned the couple Chung Hong and Manh Vi with uttermost grief, but left me too shocked and questioning, unable to comprehend.}

{align:justify|That morning, when Chung Hong entered hospital awaiting delivery and received the dreadful news that the fetus had died the night before, I was so "shocked" I clean forgot it was New Year's Day.}

{align:justify|Watching a young father stand waiting before the delivery room — knowing he would never hear his infant's first cry. A young mother awaiting labor — knowing she could never touch her child's living body again. At that moment, truly I could think of no words on earth to comfort them.}

{align:justify|Before this scene, think as I might, nothing was clear: why would heaven insist on imposing the great calamity of a lost child upon two sincere Buddhists? This not only robbed both parents' families of faith in the Buddha; their kin fiercely blamed Manh Vi and Chung Hong for superstitious devotion to Buddhism — the couple might well lose their resolve in the Way.}

{align:justify|Therefore, though standing before the delivery room, I telephoned Master Miaofa and told him everything.}

{align:justify|Most beyond imagining: hearing it, the Master uttered not half a sentence of pity or condolence, but said "Excellent, excellent!"}

{align:justify|He even sent me to find Manh Vi and ask at once whether in former days his grandfather had worked as a butcher and slaughtered a great many pigs.}

{align:justify|The Master further explained to me:}

{align:justify|"This fetus was truly a creditor-enemy — a vengeful foe resolved to come and collect its debt. Thus from the moment its spirit entered the womb, every day it tormented the mother, granting the whole house not a moment's peace. Yet Manh Vi and Chung Hong sincerely repented, recited the Buddha's name, recited sutras... so the fetus gradually received the Buddha Dharma. What is more, the parents recited the Kshitigarbha Sutra for the fetus — and this sutra 'ESPECIALLY DISSOLVES THE KARMIC HINDRANCES OF PAST LIVES.' And so through nearly ten months of hearing the sutra, the fetus truly benefited. As its karmic hindrances dissolved, its heart dissolved too the hatred and vengeance toward Manh Vi's grandfather, wholly abandoning the thought of revenging itself upon his present descendants. Now the fetus's spirit has ascended to the heavens to practice; it shall never return to this world seeking enemies for revenge."}

{align:justify|I asked:}

{align:justify|"Then why did the fetus die only at the point of birth?"}

{align:justify|"For two reasons. One: mother and child's affinity was not yet exhausted. Two: the fetus loved hearing sutras and the Dharma. So it persevered to the very last minute before departing. Conversely, had the couple not believed in the Buddha, never devoutly recited the Buddha's name and sutras, this child surely could not have died — it would have been smoothly born. But then the whole family, as the child grew year by year, would have known ever-growing affliction. In the end the Manh line would have faced 'house ruined, lives lost,' utterly destroyed by this child's hand. Therefore when the fetus died, onlookers all deemed it misfortune, the whole family embracing bitter anguish — yet truly you are enjoying blessing, calamity dissolved. This is cause for congratulation!"}

{align:justify|Hearing the Master's exposition, my spirits lifted, the dark clouds of doubt scattering. I went at once to Manh Vi, drowned in grief, and verified everything the Master had bidden me ask — it turned out exactly as he had said.}

{align:justify|Manh Vi acknowledged that his grandfather in life earned his living as a pig butcher. Before dying he developed a most bizarre illness: he ordered people to hang him up and beat him unceasingly with mallets, saying only so did he feel relief. He died in great agony — not only vomiting fresh blood, his gaze most fierce. Never before had Manh Vi mentioned his grandfather's wretched dying to outsiders.}

{align:justify|Then Manh Vi told of Mr. Hung, a friend of his grandfather's, a cattle butcher slaughtering several head a day. Mr. Hung grew rich by the trade. One day, home from an outing, resting in the house, he suddenly complained of a tight chest, hard breathing. Moments later his wife came to see him, found him lying motionless, unresponsive to shaking or calling, and sent for the doctor.}

{align:justify|When the doctor arrived, Mr. Hung bellowed in pain with a sound like an ox being poleaxed, then convulsed horribly and vomited blood and died. Dead half a day, fresh blood still oozed from his mouth. All who saw it were terrified. On his father's advice, Mr. Hung's children closed the slaughterhouse and followed the killing trade no more.}

{align:justify|Manh Vi said he had witnessed many dying scenes of livestock butchers and found that most — dying young or old — died most wretchedly, and before death always vomited fresh blood.}

{align:justify|This too is why he set his heart on studying Buddhism. All Manh Vi's uncles and aunts suffer unending illness; his father aches in spine and cartilage; his uncle lies half-paralyzed, bedridden these ten years. In Manh Vi's own generation, his brother before forty developed mental illness, forever suspecting others speak ill behind his back. He cannot work; and Manh Vi and his sister both suffer constant spinal pain — his sister even underwent surgery that never healed.}

{align:justify|Master Miaofa also said that had Manh Vi not kept a strict vegetarian diet, studied Buddhism, and practiced, he could hardly have married. For with a family killing karma so deep, fate had decreed the descendants sickly and ruined.}

{align:justify|Master Miaofa's teaching, just bestowed, was like a timely rain quenching the blazing evil fire burning hearts. It dissolved every doubt in their hearts — above all for Manh Vi and Chung Hong, who turned sorrow to joy; now the couple study Buddhism and practice with still more courageous vigor. I believe, so sincere are they, the Buddhas and bodhisattvas will bless them to soon bear a noble son.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story THE FETUS WHO HEARD THE SUTRA AND RELEASED ITS GRUDGE is among the most moving and profound tales of "past-life creditors — the fetus come to collect its debt." Chung Hong's fetus kicked painfully as the creditor-enemy of Manh Vi's grandfather (the pig butcher). Yet through the parents' recitation of the Kshitigarbha Sutra throughout pregnancy, the fetus dissolved its karma, released its grudge, and ascended to the heavens to practice — never to return for revenge. The fetus's death on New Year's Eve (a day that should have been joyful) was truly "calamity turned to blessing" — saving the whole Manh line from "house ruined, lives lost." The story affirms: "The Kshitigarbha Sutra especially dissolves the karmic hindrances of past lives." Parents who wish healthy children, early become vegetarian and recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra dedicated to your children and past-life creditors — the greatest gift to child and lineage alike.}
