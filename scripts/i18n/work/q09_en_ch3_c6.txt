<p><strong>These images are only gold, bronze, clay, wood&#8230; Sutras are only leaves, paper, silk.</strong></p>
<p>Buddha images are made of gold, bronze, stuffed with clay, or carved of wood; sutras are written on leaves or on cloth and silk &#8211; what is worthy of worship in them? Why do you bow to such things? They are merely insentient objects.</p>
<p><strong>This flesh body is truly real</strong> &#8211; this flesh body of mine is truly, eternally real &#8211; <strong>yet not revered</strong>: you do not revere this body of mine, <strong>but instead worship clay and wood</strong>.</p>
<p>What benefit is there in preferring to bow to wood and clay? What benefit in bowing to such idols? Better that you bow to me than to them.</p>
<p><strong>Truly</strong> completely <strong>upside down</strong>: you bow to those idols, but they are only slabs of wood, insentient &#8211; how crazy to bow to them! <strong>Its deep believers</strong> in the demon &#8211; those deeply believing in the demon &#8211; <strong>follow its words, smashing Buddha images, burning sutras, burying them all in the ground</strong>. Through such conduct, <strong>for misleading beings with doubts and errors</strong> &#8211; making them disbelieve the Buddhadharma, doubtful and confused instead &#8211; sowing such doubts among beings, they are sure to <strong>fall into the uninterrupted hell. Losing right samadhi, they are sure to fall.</strong></p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong>8 &#8211; BUOYANT EASE (THE DEMON OF EASE)</strong></p>
<p><strong><em><u>Sutra text</u></em></strong><strong><em>:  Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch. Ư tinh minh trung, viên ngộ tinh lí, đắc đại tùy thuận. Kì tâm hốt sanh vô lượng khinh an. Dĩ ngôn thành thánh, đắc đại tự tại. Thử danh nhân huệ, hoạch chư khinh thanh. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Translation</u></strong><strong>: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; within the essential brightness, perfectly awakening to the subtle principle, gaining great compliance &#8211; the mind suddenly gives rise to boundless buoyant ease, claiming to have become a sage, gaining great self-mastery. This is called gaining lightness and clarity through wisdom. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization.</strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Commentary</u></strong>: <strong>Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; within the essential brightness</strong>.</p>
<p>Awareness becomes bright and essential as never before. <strong>Perfectly awakening to</strong> <strong>the subtle, wondrous truth</strong>: the cultivator now gains penetrating understanding of the subtle, wondrous truths. <strong>Gaining great compliance</strong>: <strong>the mind suddenly attains boundless buoyant ease, claiming to have become a sage</strong> &#8211; a Buddha &#8211; <strong>gaining great self-mastery</strong>, attaining supreme bliss beyond all.</p>
<p>This is called: <strong>gaining lightness and clarity through wisdom</strong> &#8211; just opening a little wisdom, at once reaching a light, clear state, and that is all; surely nothing compared with the ultimate state.</p>
<p><strong>Recognized, it is without fault</strong> &#8211; nothing is amiss. <strong>Not sage realization</strong>: you should not take this state for the sage fruition, for it is not.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu nhất phần, hiếu thanh khinh ma, nhập kì tâm phủ. Tự vị mãn túc, cánh bất cầu tiến. Thửđẳng đa tác, Vô Văn  tỷ-khưu, nghi ngộ hậu sanh, đọa a-tì ngục. Thất ư chánh thọ, đương tòng luân trụy.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: But if interpreted as sagehood, a kind of ease-loving demon enters the very heart, deeming itself complete, seeking no further advance. Such as these mostly act like the Unlearned Bhikshu, misleading later beings with doubts and errors, falling into the Avichi hell. Losing right samadhi, they are sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: But <strong>if interpreted as sagehood</strong> &#8211; gaining this light, easy state and thinking oneself to have attained the sage fruition &#8211; <strong>then a kind of ease-loving demon enters the very heart</strong>: this kind of demon has also passed through the light, easy state and will possess such a person. <strong>Deeming itself complete</strong> &#8211; having accomplished everything &#8211; <strong>seeking no further advance</strong>: above, no Buddhahood to attain, for it is already a Buddha; nor need beings be saved, for the saving is already complete. It has attained Buddhahood as wished and saved beings in the sense it wished to save &#8211; hence seeking no advance at all.</p>
<p>Most such cultivators <strong>act</strong> <strong>like the Unlearned Bhikshu</strong> mentioned before: lacking wisdom, he mistook the Fourth Dhyana state for the fourth Arhat fruition.</p>
<p><strong>Misleading later beings with doubts and errors</strong>: hence they do not recognize the true path nor understand the Buddhadharma &#8211; and for deluding and obstructing beings, they <strong>fall into the Avichi hell</strong> &#8211; in the future such people fall into the uninterrupted hell. Why? Losing right samadhi, they are sure to fall: once right samadhi is lost, the fall into the uninterrupted hell is certain.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong>9 &#8211; CLINGING TO EXTINCTION, DENYING CAUSE AND EFFECT</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch. Ư minh ngộ trung, đắc hư minh tánh. Kì trung hốt nhiên, quy hướng vĩnh diệt, bát vô nhân quả, nhất hướng nhập không. Không tâm hiện tiền, nãi chí tâm sanh trưởng đoạn diệt giải. Thử danh định tâm trầm một, thất ư chiếu ứng. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; within bright awakening, attaining an empty, bright nature &#8211; suddenly therein turning toward perpetual extinction, denying cause and effect, inclining wholly to emptiness. The empty mind manifesting, even giving rise to the understanding of eternal severance. This is called the settled mind sunk and lost, bereft of illumination. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed.</strong> Then <strong>within bright awakening</strong> &#8211; when one seems clear yet has not truly awakened &#8211; <strong>attaining an empty, bright nature</strong>: within that awakening attaining a kind of empty, bright light unable to ascertain reality. <strong>Suddenly therein turning toward perpetual extinction, denying cause and effect, taking everything as empty.</strong> Therein &#8211; within that empty brightness &#8211; a change suddenly occurs. What? The cultivator inclines toward extinction, denying cause and effect, taking all dharmas as empty.</p>
<p>It says: &#8220;When one dies, one is gone forever. So talk of cause and effect is mistaken: there is no cause and effect &#8211; at death nothing remains, so how could there be cause and effect? All dharmas are empty: creating offenses is empty, doing meritorious good is empty &#8211; all is empty.&#8221;</p>
