<p><strong>Using the unmoving nature of consciousness to extinguish investigation, so that within the boundless the nature of ending is proclaimed; as if remaining yet not remaining, ending yet not ending; such a class is called the Station of Neither Thought nor Non-Thought.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: This is the Station of Neither Thought nor Non-Thought, the highest station of all. The gods' lifespan is eighty thousand great eons. Yet after enjoying heavenly blessings for a time, they still fall back into birth and death. In the Station of Consciousness, consciousness still stirs from time to time, but now it does not stir at all.</p>
<p>&#8220;Using the unmoving nature of consciousness to extinguish investigation.&#8221; In the end even investigation and the skillful means of cultivating wisdom come to an end. &#8220;So that within the boundless the nature of ending is proclaimed.&#8221; &#8220;The boundless&#8221; refers to the absence of everything, as described; within the boundless they proclaim the nature of ending, and in proclaiming the nature of ending, &#8220;as if remaining yet not remaining, ending yet not ending.&#8221; Everything seems to exist yet does not exist. &#8220;Such a class is called the Station of Neither Thought nor Non-Thought.&#8221;</p>
<p>In the previous stations a little thinking remained, but at this stage thought no longer functions. They abide in samadhi for eighty thousand great eons &#8211; the gods with the longest lifespan.<br />
Cultivators who reach this stage no longer give rise to ideas. But when ideas do arise, they still fall into the turning wheel of rebirth in the future &#8211; they must go through that.</p>
<p>For example, a cultivator in the past cultivated the samadhi power of Neither Thought nor Non-Thought and was able to enter the Station of Neither Thought nor Non-Thought. One day, while cultivating by the seashore and on the verge of entering samadhi, a fish in the water disturbed him. In the Chan hall, people who specialize in talking are like a string of machine-gun fire: they constantly think of ways to bother others with questions and chatter, disturbing the Chan hall. Creating disturbances is like mosquitoes, ants, and cockroaches causing affliction.</p>
<p>In this case it was a fish creating the disturbance. It leaped across the water with a &#8220;plunk,&#8221; and its acrobatics distracted the cultivator about to enter samadhi. Unable to enter samadhi, this cultivator grew very angry and thought: &#8220;What a troublesome fish! I want to enter samadhi, and it comes to bother me. I have no spiritual powers yet, but when I get them I shall turn into a fisherman and catch and eat you &#8211; that will teach you for sabotaging my cultivation.&#8221;</p>
<p>Of course, once he grew angry, the fish leaped no more; frightened, it swam away. The cultivator gained quiet, attained the stage, and was reborn in the Station of Neither Thought nor Non-Thought. Yet after enjoying eighty thousand great eons in the heavens, guess what happened: the karma ripened from that one angry false thought he had had that day by the sea, and he fell into the turning wheel, reborn as a big mackerel. For many lives in succession he ate small fish, life after life, until the eon when Shakyamuni Buddha came into the world, visited the seashore, and spoke Buddhadharma for him to hear. Only then did he escape the life of a fish and get reborn as a human. He left home to cultivate, and this time he attained the fruition of Arhatship.</p>
<p>Whatever you do in cultivation, do not get angry. Whether people treat you well or not, you should maintain thoughts of kindness and compassion toward them. Bear hatred toward no one; do not be upset because of them. It would not matter if you had attained a stage of cultivation and transcended the six paths of rebirth &#8211; but if you are still within the six paths, you must undergo the retribution of cause and effect for your hatred. There is an old saying:<br />
You may stir the waters of a thousand rivers,<br />
But do not disturb the samadhi of a cultivator.</p>
<p>Disturbing a cultivator and making him angry is a grave matter, for the karma will ripen in the future.</p>
<p><strong>Thử đẳng cùng không, bất tận không lý. Tùng Bất hoàn Thiên, Thánh đạo cùng giả. Như thị nhất loại, danh bất hồi tâm, độn A-la-hán.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Such beings have investigated emptiness to its end but have not exhausted the principle of emptiness; considered from the holy path of the Non-Returning Heavens, such a class is called the Dull Arhats Who Have Not Turned the Mind.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: &#8220;Such beings have investigated emptiness to its end but have not exhausted the principle of emptiness; considered from the holy path of the Non-Returning Heavens.&#8221; Do you remember how, in the heavens of the Realm of Form, the path of cultivation split into two directions &#8211; one toward the fruition of Arhatship for those who can turn the light around to illumine within? Now these cultivators have reached the end of the road with nowhere else to go &#8211; cultivators who do not turn the light around to illumine within. They do not thoroughly understand the Dharma-nature of emptiness: though they cultivate, they do not clearly understand; they lack right wisdom. &#8220;Such a class is called the Dull Arhats Who Have Not Turned the Mind.&#8221; They lack genuine wisdom and cannot turn the Small Vehicle wheel into the Great Vehicle. &#8220;Dull&#8221; means stupid and dull-witted.</p>
<p><strong>Nhược tùng vô tưởng, chư ngoại đạo Thiên, cùng không bất quy. Mê lậu vô văn. Tiện nhập luân chuyển.</strong></p>
<p><strong>If, from the No Thought Heaven of the outer paths, one clings to emptiness as real and does not turn back, deluded and outflowing, unhearing of the Dharma, one then enters the turning wheel.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: &#8220;If, from the No Thought Heaven of the outer paths, one clings to emptiness as real and does not turn back, deluded and outflowing.&#8221; Those in this realm and in the Four Stations of the Formless Realm will go to the end of this road, cling to emptiness, and not know how to return to the true mind and inherent nature. They go on to become deluded and dull-witted. &#8220;Unhearing of the right Dharma, one then enters the turning wheel.&#8221; In cultivation you must keep the mind straight and not stray onto a wrong path.</p>
<p><strong>A-nan! Thị chư Thiên thượng, các các Thiên Nhân. Tức thị phàm phu, nghiệp quả thù đáp, đáp tận nhập luân. Bỉ chi Thiên Vương, tức thị Bồ Tát. Du tam ma đề, tiệm thứ tăng tiến. Hồi hướng Thánh luân, sở tu hành lộ.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Ananda! The gods above, each and every god, are still ordinary people repaying their karmic fruition; when the repayment is exhausted, they enter the turning wheel. The Heavenly Kings of those realms are all Bodhisattvas who, roaming in samadhi, gradually advance, turning toward the cultivation path of the holy wheel.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: &#8220;Ananda! The gods above, each and every god, are still ordinary people.&#8221; Do not think they have succeeded in cultivation; they are still ordinary people who have not yet attained any fruition. They are still &#8220;repaying their karmic fruition.&#8221; Though they live very long, they still must pay their debts when the karmic fruition comes upon them. &#8220;When the repayment is exhausted, they enter the turning wheel.&#8221;</p>
<p>&#8220;The Heavenly Kings of those realms are all Bodhisattvas&#8221; &#8211; transformation-bodies of Bodhisattvas &#8211; &#8220;who, roaming in samadhi, gradually advance, turning toward the cultivation path of the holy wheel.&#8221; They advance to attain fruition and awakening, receive predictions, and turn toward the cultivation path of the holy sages &#8211; the same cultivation path as all other cultivators.</p>
<p><strong>A-nan! Thị tứ không thiên, thân tâm diệt tận. Định tánh hiện tiền, vô nghiệp quả sắc. Tòng thử đãi chung, danh vô sắc giới.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Ananda! In these Four Stations of Emptiness, body and mind are utterly extinguished; the nature of samadhi manifests, with no karmically-requited form. All the way to the very end, this is called the Formless Realm.</strong></p>
