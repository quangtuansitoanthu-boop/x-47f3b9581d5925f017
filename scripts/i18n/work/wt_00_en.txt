TITLE: 01. Translator's Preface
TOMTAT: The translator Hanh Doan introduces the origins of the book and her heartfelt wish to share true stories of cause and effect so that readers will deeply believe in karma and live better, happier lives.
-----
Translator's Preface

This book is divided into three parts: the first by layman Guoqing; the second a collection of stories by many authors from many countries (who witnessed events with their own eyes or heard them told) and sent to the Chinese Ethical Society; the third written by a reporter from direct interviews or from hearsay.

I spared no effort hunting through the vast forest of memoirs in the original Chinese to find stories that are good and useful; then I distilled and selected them, and translated them to share with readers. You have every right to believe or not. But this volume is not mere reading "to buy a moment's idle amusement"; it carries the love for humanity of both its writers and its translator.

In these present days, open a newspaper or a website and we see crimes abounding — cruel and horrifying to an alarming degree. It all comes from people not believing in cause and effect. Because they do not believe, they know no fear, and so take no care to guard themselves. So much so that an education researcher had to lament: this is the age when "evil reigns supreme!"

There is a saying: "Sages fear the cause; ordinary beings fear the result." Why do sages fear the cause? Because they have witnessed much, have suffered, and have felt it to the very marrow, so they are always careful, guarding every act and thought. As for us, we witness little, understand little, believe little, remember little... so we lose all control and sow causes recklessly and at random. Take, for example, a cashier guarding a "vault of gold" for a company or a private employer: if he does not believe in the retribution of wrongdoing and blessing, he will very easily embezzle and greedily create offenses. But if he believes in cause and effect, understanding that "ill-gotten gains can not only never be enjoyed but will bring calamity upon oneself and one's descendants"... then even with no supervisor watching, he will keep himself pure and honest.

My only wish is that after reading this book you will see cause and effect more clearly, and live a better and happier life. If this work brings its readers even a little spiritual benefit, or joy in the meaning of life, that is the translator's greatest reward.

I would like to offer my gratitude to Hong Minh Chau, to Mrs. Nguyen Tam, and to the silent contributions of so many people who helped me in spirit and in substance so that I could complete this translation and offer it to readers, even though the gifted copies are limited and modest in number.

I dedicate this field of merit to all my benefactors, to my elder sister Nguyen Huy Phuong, to the spirit of my grandchild Carol Chi Phan,... and to all people living in this world. May you always be peaceful and happy, and may your wisdom and compassion ever shine so that together we build a beautiful human realm.

Finally, I beg your forgiveness for the translator's errors.

Hanh Doan, 29/03/2013

COMMENTARY:
The translator's preface skillfully lays the foundation for the whole book: cause and effect is not some distant matter after death, but is present in every thought, word, and deed each day. The translator stresses that "sages fear the cause, ordinary beings fear the result" — the wise take care over every cause they sow, while the deluded are terrified only when retribution arrives. This book does not aim to threaten with fear, but to awaken a deep faith in the law of cause and effect so that each person willingly lives virtuously, carefully, and accumulates blessings. That is the most precious gift the translator wishes to entrust to us.
