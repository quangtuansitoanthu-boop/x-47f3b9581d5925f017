{align:justify|Answer: Please read the "Ksitigarbha Sutra" closely—never snip lines expounding personal meanings. Know: whenever Buddhas and Bodhisattvas teach, mentioning those gaining benefit, the passage always opens: "Good men, good women." Clarifying: all "good men, good women" bowing to Buddhas, reciting sutras, urging others to believe and learn Buddhism, gain a hundred thousand ghosts and spirits guarding day and night. Inversely: "If not good men, good women, yet bowing to Buddhas, urging others practicing… they merely form Buddha affinities, planting wholesome seeds—never at once drawing heaven, earth, ghosts, spirits guarding—for they merely believe, never truly learn Buddhism, never practice! Unsown good seeds—whence good fruits? So never truly gaining benefits."}
{align:justify|Then what measures "good men, good women?"}
{align:justify|The Buddha calls home-dwelling and home-leaving men and women "good men, good women." Good means: loving teachings, believing Buddhas. Though home-dwellers keep spouses, all their words and deeds guard like monastics'. But drinkers, gamblers, drug users, brawlers, adulterers, philanderers, robbers, thieves, liars, ten-evil doers… none are called "good men, good women." Planting evil seeds, they must reap evil fruits—how hope heaven, earth, ghosts, spirits guard them?}
{align:justify|Ms. Lam, working in China, asked Master Diệu Pháp thus:}
{align:justify|"A layman in Canada prospered greatly. Temple donations, sutra and wholesome-book printings and gifts—no less than hundreds of thousands of dollars—but recently a car crash gravely injured him—single-mindedly reciting the Buddha's name… till his last breath never ceasing—passing before fifty. So some doubt Buddhism—please explain."}
{align:justify|The master asked:}
{align:justify|"Do you know this layman's private life?"}
{align:justify|"Not well!"}
{align:justify|The master explained:}
{align:justify|"Before taking the Three Jewels' refuge he defiled many young girls—after refuge, though restrained, still unpure—moreover never truly repented former lustful conduct. Thanks to last-moment unceasing Buddha-recitation, boundless sins dissolved too—temples held deliverance rites besides—plus lifetime merits donating temples, printing sutras, books… so now he is already born into the Trayastrimsha Heaven. Without Three-Jewel donation merits and last-moment recitation, he would suffer 'spirit dissolved and scattered.' Meaning: the more promiscuous or deviant offenses, the more the spirit dissolves—hells must be borne—then if humanly reborn, reborn intersex (half male, half female), low wisdom—not only self-miserable but scorned, despised…"}
{align:justify|So refuge-taking disciples must surely end evil, practice good—never presuming 'donating Buddhas, Dharma, Sangha, printing many sutras… surely Pure Land rebirth—so sleep pillowed high'… Know 'good repaid with good, evil with evil'—an unchanging law for all. The Buddha, toward precept-breaking fallen disciples, pities—but cannot help. Then how hope a few incense sticks or sutra rounds grant every wish?"}
{align:justify|Understand clearly: precepts the Buddha laid down never control disciples—but warn them: 'Unguarded precepts mean falls, punished by cause and effect's law.' So, wishing neither hell after death nor calamities in life—each must consciously keep precepts! Else, repentance comes too late…}

{align:justify|Question: Why do virtuous high monks too fall gravely ill and die?}
{align:justify|Answer: Buddhas and successive high monks all manifest common mortal forms born into the world—their physical bodies too are earth, water, wind, fire combined. Birth, age, sickness, death are common law—and practitioners are intimately tied to beings besides. When their worldly missions complete, most manifest sickness to leave the dust. Some bear sickness' signs without torment—for instance, liver, lung, stomach cancers unfelt—needing no painkillers. (Only modeling practices like sitting meditation, Buddha-recitation, sutra recitation, mantra holding… giving rise to Buddha-power blessing miracles—urging disciples and fellow practitioners to diligence, good practice meeting impermanence.)}
{align:justify|Naturally some manifest sitting, lying, or illness-free passing. Called fine, wholesome manifestations—teaching people diligent practice. Buddhist biographies record a great many!}
{align:justify|Master Diệu Pháp tells: when Master Hsuan Hua fell ill, sometimes before disciples he too manifested pain—but hearing disciples' sorrowing tears, he at once switched to peaceful looks, cheerfully saying:}
{align:justify|"You make mountains of trifles!"}
{align:justify|Then speaking and laughing normally. Later disciples learned the master was testing, trying all.}
{align:justify|BODHISATTVAS IN SECRET PRACTICE}
{align:justify|Master Diệu Pháp tells: once visiting the City of Ten Thousand Buddhas to audience Master Hsuan Hua, he chanced upon a Dharma-protecting layman at the City killed in a car crash.}
{align:justify|This layman had not seen wife and children for years—now reunited. Then driving them from the airport home—nearing the City of Ten Thousand Buddhas—disaster struck—wife and children lightly hurt—he alone died.}
{align:justify|Someone asked Master Diệu Pháp:}
{align:justify|"This layman left family in Taiwan—alone coming to the City of Ten Thousand Buddhas Dharma-protecting, also teaching the City's students. His heart is most good—yet why so violent a death?"}
{align:justify|Master Diệu Pháp answered:}
{align:justify|"This man is an Equal-Enlightenment Bodhisattva reborn—come aiding Master Hsuan Hua! Dharma-protecting is his original vow! His sudden death repays former lives' grievous debts—simultaneously warning the world life is impermanent—practice diligently! For in a past life he once harmed a pair of great birds—so returning from the airport, repayment's hour come—the two birds' (creditors') consciousnesses spread wings before the windshield, blocking his view—so, road unseen, he struck a pole, dying!"}
{align:justify|The master asked his wife and children:}
{align:justify|"Did your husband urge you both to America quickly?"}
{align:justify|His wife said:}
{align:justify|"Yes—days earlier 'he' kept urging by telephone—saying though no longer beside us, we must strive practicing well. Then I grew annoyed hearing it repeated—now reflecting, I understand 'he' had prepared beforehand…"}
{align:justify|The master explained:}
{align:justify|"He wished you both in America meeting him one last time—seeing him off."}
{align:justify|The master's words greatly consoled the wife and children—also urging their faith and practice. They bowed to him.}
{align:justify|Those present, believing the master, reverently bowed too.}
{align:justify|But many doubters raised objecting voices reaching the master:}
{align:justify|"That layman—what goodness? No great gifts to the City of Ten Thousand Buddhas—how call him 'Equal-Enlightenment Bodhisattva reborn'? If he is an Equal-Enlightenment Bodhisattva, then I am… a Buddha reborn!"}
{align:justify|In the 'Fourfold Pure Clear Instructions' of the 'Shurangama Sutra' the Buddha teaches most clearly: even 'prostitutes, widows, cheats, thieves, butchers'… may be 'Bodhisattvas, Arhats reborn manifesting'—how much more laypeople and monks?… These reborn ones, even awakened, never beat breasts boasting 'I truly am a Bodhisattva, an Arhat'… except at life's end secretly leaving last words—or after passing, wise ones revealing their identities… They themselves silently come, silently go. All to teach people—manifesting 'life is impermanent'—all acting as the Buddha arranged… awaiting your awakening, all shall clear naturally.}
