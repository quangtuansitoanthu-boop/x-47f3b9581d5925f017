{align:justify|In my home village the old folks often told us young people a story as a warning:}
{align:justify|Thirty years ago in Taiwan, some poor people earned a living as itinerant peddlers. Once a cloth seller brought his goods into a village, and the women gathered around to look. They passed the cloth from hand to hand, examining and commenting in turn, yet no one bought anything. Seeing this, the seller packed up his cloth—but on checking his goods he found a bolt of fabric missing. Everyone denied taking it. Among the women, one fiercely insisted on her innocence and swore a terrible oath:}
{align:justify|"If I took the cloth as you say, may a car run me over and kill me!"}
{align:justify|Later she moved to Kinmen. One day, as she sat inside her house, a truck with failed brakes smashed straight into the house and crushed her to death.}
{align:justify|When the news spread, everyone buzzed with discussion, calling it a terrifying instance of karmic retribution.}
{align:justify|TALES TOLD BY A FORENSIC POLICE OFFICER}
{align:justify|I am a medical-legal police officer—what people usually call a forensic pathologist. I provide medical expert findings to two courts and four branches of the judicial police. Simply put, wherever a homicide occurs, the body is brought to us so we can examine the corpse, determine the cause of death, write up a complete report, and hand it to the court or the police bureau.}
{align:justify|All humans are born and must die, yet the causes of death come in unimaginable varieties…}
{align:justify|When we draw up our reports, we work with great seriousness and thoroughness. We must analyze the cause of death: the direct cause leading to death, the contributing causes, the overall conclusion on mortality…}
{align:justify|I dwell on these technical terms because I want to say that our society always has a proper, lawful explanation for death.}
{align:justify|For example, an old man over seventy was struck by an electric scooter, fell, hit his head on a cement pole by the road, and died. Examination showed head trauma, a fractured skull leading to death. Here the head injury is the underlying cause, the skull damage the direct cause, and immune-system failure a contributing cause.}
{align:justify|But does such an explanation truly supply a sufficient answer to the great question of "why people die"? In twenty-six years at the forensic institute, I have always felt bewildered by this phenomenon. For with the very same injuries, some die and some live?! That is, some with very slight wounds die, while others, gravely injured and given up for dead by all, miraculously survive…}
{align:justify|Later, when I encountered the Buddha's teachings and gained some understanding of the path, I could make sense of all these questions.}
{align:justify|Death is a part of this world, the endpoint of a life. Seen from this angle, we can understand the true face of death.}
{align:justify|In recent years I have taken care to study every case I handled thoroughly and record it in detail. The deeper I looked, the clearer it became: death never occurs at random—it offers powerful, irrefutable proof of the law of cause and effect.}
{align:justify|I often meet the relatives of the dead who look at me and recount their grief through tears:}
{align:justify|"Why did one so kind pass away so young? Why does that evildoer enjoy a long life? Why does a person careful all his life keep meeting sudden calamities? Why does a careless jaywalker walk away unscathed after being hit by a car?"}
{align:justify|I believe all such resentment stems from not understanding cause and effect.}
