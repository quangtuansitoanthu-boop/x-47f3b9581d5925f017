<p><strong>But if you deem yourself to have realized sagehood</strong> &#8211; saying: Oh! I have become a Buddha &#8211; <strong>a demon of madness at once enters the very heart</strong>.</p>
<p>So you see: if you still do not clearly understand the <em>Fifty Skandha-Demon Phenomena</em>, how could you become a Buddha? This demon is one of the mad, arrogant, egotistic kinds: it slips into your mind, possessing you as its property, governing the possessed person's mind and nature &#8211; once possessed, the demon king controls and acts as that person.</p>
<p><strong>Meeting anyone at all, one boasts of oneself</strong> &#8211; deeming oneself right and everyone else wrong, disparaging and smearing others while praising oneself. Everyone is entirely wrong; only it alone is worthy. It thinks it has become a Buddha.</p>
<p><strong>One becomes incomparably arrogant toward everyone; the mind to the point of above seeing no Buddhas, below seeing no people.</strong> Why? Because it thinks it is itself a Buddha &#8211; and sees no one below itself anymore.</p>
<p>What then does it see? It says everyone is a Buddha; it itself is also a Buddha. There is no Buddha above it anymore &#8211; nor will there ever be a Buddha surpassing it. That is the state of seeing no one below itself anymore.</p>
<p><strong>Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p>Without maintaining right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong> 3 &#8211; EXCESSIVE DEJECTION (THE DEMON OF HEAVY RECOLLECTION)</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch. Tiền vô tân chứng, quy thất cố cư. Trí lực  suy vi, nhập trung đọa địa, quýnh vô sở kiến. Tâm trung hốt nhiên, sanh đại khô kiệt. Ư nhất thiết thời, trầm ức bất tán. Tương thử dĩ vi cần tinh tiến tướng.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; ahead no new realization to attain, behind the old dwelling lost &#8211; wisdom-power weakening, entering a doomed place with nothing in view &#8211; suddenly the mind gives rise to great aridity. At all times sunk in recollection, unrelieved &#8211; mistaking this for the mark of diligence and vigor.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Further, when good men in this samadhi-state see the form skandha dissolved and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; ahead no new state to realize, behind the formerly realized state lost &#8211; wisdom-power weakening.</strong> No new state to realize ahead; retreating, the formerly self-realized state is lost &#8211; hence wisdom-power wanes.</p>
<p>Advancing, there is nothing to attain; behind the cultivator, the state long dwelt in has vanished. Wisdom now grows weak; the cultivator is no longer valorous and keen as at first. This is the case of suddenly progressing, then abruptly halting. <strong>Fallen into a doomed place</strong> &#8211; where the cultivator nearly falls &#8211; seeing <strong>nothing in view</strong>: the cultivator feels or senses nothing anymore.</p>
<p><strong>Suddenly the mind gives rise to great aridity.</strong></p>
<p>The cultivator's feeling now is of utter monotony, dryness, craving: everything seems meaningless and tiresome; craving, because he needs to be moistened by the waters of the Buddhadharma. He feels parched and withered, seeing nothing more. This state resembles a beginner unwilling to cultivate, constantly feeling lonely and dejected &#8211; and that loneliness and dejection surely lead to affliction, the inherent affliction.</p>
<p>I had not planned to raise this point, but I fear some may not understand. Moreover: it is like a woman longing for a man, yearning until she feels restless and uneasy; likewise when a man yearns for a woman, he feels the same unease. Both are lonely and dreary, as if having lost something never to be found again.</p>
<p><strong>At all times</strong> the cultivator <strong>is sunk in recollection, unrelieved</strong>.</p>
<p>Like a man and woman lovesick for each other, yearning for each other, never forgetting.</p>
<p><strong>Mistaking this for the mark of diligence and vigor</strong>.</p>
<p>This state grips thought so tightly it will not disperse, making him mistake it for a kind of vigor in effort.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Thử danh tu tâm, vô huệ tự thất, ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng. Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu ức ma, nhập kì tâm phủ. Đán tịch toát tâm, huyền tại nhất xứ. Thất ư chánh thọ đương tòng  luân trụy.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: This is called cultivating the mind without wisdom, bringing loss upon oneself. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization. But if interpreted as sagehood, a demon of recollection enters the very heart: day and night holding the mind suspended in one place. Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>This is called cultivating the mind without wisdom, bringing loss upon oneself.</strong> Within the scope of the feeling skandha, this is called cultivating without wisdom, bringing error upon oneself.</p>
<p>You have not yet developed wisdom &#8211; but do not fret for not having developed wisdom. <strong>Recognized, it is without fault</strong>: if you understand it is only a state, nothing is amiss. But if you do not understand, you will always get caught in &#8220;<em>clinging to emptiness</em>&#8221; and stagnate in stillness. If you always scatter the mind in fancies, you will sink and never accomplish the Way.</p>
<p>This state is <strong>not sage realization</strong> &#8211; it does not mean you have just realized something or may call yourself a sage. Do not mistake a thief for your son.</p>
<p><strong>But if you deem yourself awakened, a demon of recollection will enter the heart</strong>.</p>
<p>Do you hear? This is truly dangerous. If you regard it as a wholesome state, a kind of recollection-demon will come to help you remember everything well. And if you cannot remember something? It will help you remember. Knowing how much you wish to remember, it enters your mind to help you remember &#8211; to that extent.</p>
<p><strong>Day and night holding the mind suspended in one place</strong>.</p>
<p>Your mind will be drawn and held to a single point.</p>
<p><strong>Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall</strong>.</p>
<p>Lacking right samadhi (not easily attaining right samadhi), one is sure to fall &#8211; in the future falling into the hells.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong> 4 &#8211; SELF-SATISFACTION (TAKING A LITTLE FOR ENOUGH)</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch. Huệ lực quá định, thất ư mãnh lợi. Dĩ chư thắng tánh, hoài ư tâm trung. Tự tâm dĩ nghi, thị Lô-giá-na, đắc thiểu vi túc.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; wisdom-power exceeding samadhi, erring in keen sharpness &#8211; embracing a surpassing nature within the heart, deeming one's own mind to be Vairocana, taking a little attainment for enough.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Further, when good men in this samadhi-state see the form skandha dissolved and the feeling skandha clearly revealed.</strong></p>
