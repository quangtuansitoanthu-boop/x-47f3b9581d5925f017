<p><strong>Sutra text: Phật cáo A-nan cập chư đại chúng: Nhữ đẳng đương tri, hữu lậu thế giới thập nhị loại sanh, bản giác diệu minh, giác viên tâm thể, dữ thập phương Phật, vô nhị vô biệt.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: The Buddha told Ananda and the great assembly: You should know that the twelve kinds of beings in the outflowing world all inherently possess the wondrous bright inherent enlightenment &#8211; the fully awakened mind-substance &#8211; no different, no distinct, from the Buddhas of the ten directions.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>The Buddha told Ananda</strong> <strong>and everyone in the assembly</strong>: <strong>you should all know that the twelve kinds of beings in the outflowing world all inherently possess the wondrous bright inherent enlightenment</strong> &#8211; the enlightened nature present since time without beginning, the inherently bright and wondrous true mind &#8211; <strong>the fully awakened mind-substance</strong>, <strong>no different from the mind-substance of the Buddhas of the ten directions</strong>: completely identical, not different in the least. The Buddhas of the ten directions also possess this wondrous bright inherent mind, and the twelve kinds of beings likewise inherit the fully awakened, bright, wondrous mind-substance (<em>inherent enlightenment, wondrous brightness &#8211; perfect enlightenment, mind-substance</em>), also called the Tathagata-store nature.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Do nhữ vọng tưởng, mê lí vi cữu, si ái phát sanh, sanh phát biến mê, cố hữu không tính. Hóa mê bất tức, hữu thế giới sanh. Tắc thử thập phương vi trần quốc độ phi vô lậu giả, giai thị mê ngoan vọng tưởng an lập.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Because of your false thoughts, deluded about the truth, infatuation and love arise, giving rise to all-pervading delusion, and so there is empty space. Delusion transforming without cease, the world comes into being. Thus the lands as numerous as dust-motes in the ten directions that are not beyond outflow are all established by deluded, obstinate, false thought.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Because of your false thoughts, deluded about the truth</strong>: Ananda, your self-nature and the self-nature of the twelve kinds of beings are no different from the Buddhas &#8211; one, identical. Yet relying on the true, you give rise to mistaken false thoughts and become deluded about the truth, and wrong delusion arises from that.</p>
<p><strong>Hence infatuation and love arise</strong>.</p>
<p>What is the greatest fault? Infatuation and love. They may be spoken of as two things, or gathered into one: infatuated love. Ignorance covers all understanding &#8211; knowing only lustful love, thinking only of lustful love from morning to night, unable to let go of lustful love even for a moment. If you applied yourself to studying the Buddhadharma the way you indulge in lustful love, you would become a Buddha very quickly. But alas, you cannot convert the love of pleasure into love of the Buddhadharma: the more you study the Buddhadharma, the more bored you feel, thinking: &#8220;I have too many faults; the Buddhadharma exposes them all &#8211; I do not want to study anymore. The Buddhadharma calls on me to change my habits &#8211; how could I change them?&#8221; That is an example of infatuated love. Above, the sutra text said: because of false thought, one is deluded about the truth; now I can say that all faults arise from infatuated love.</p>
<p><strong>Giving rise to all-pervading delusion.</strong></p>
<p>Infatuated love causes all-pervading delusion: you become deluded and confused about everything. Once infatuated and in love, you understand nothing about anything; nothing is worth your concern, and you stubbornly insist: &#8220;If I fall into the hells, so be it &#8211; why bother?&#8221; You no longer care about anything at all.</p>
<p><strong>And so there is empty space.</strong></p>
<p>From infatuated love you create faults: a man keeps longing for a woman from morning to night, and a woman keeps longing for a man all day long &#8211; and so a kind of falseness arises.</p>
<p><strong>Delusion transforming without cease, the world comes into being.</strong></p>
<p>Delusion multiplies many times over &#8211; one delusion becomes two, two become three &#8211; delusion turning with no stopping place. Wise people should be cautious at this point; engrave its deep meaning into your very bones. It has pointed out to you the whole of delusion.</p>
<p><strong>Thus the lands as numerous as dust-motes in the ten directions are all established by deluded, obstinate, false thought.</strong></p>
<p>The worlds throughout the ten directions cannot be destroyed &#8211; yet they are not worlds beyond outflow, for they have no separate self-nature of their own: they are only the establishment of mistaken false thought &#8211; delusion together with lack of understanding, delusion together with obstinacy, not knowing how to transform &#8211; and so the world is established from these deluded false thoughts.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Đương tri hư không, sanh nhữ tâm nội, do như phiến vân điểm thái thanh lí. Huống chư thế giới tại hư không da?</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: You should know that empty space arising within your mind is like a wisp of cloud dotting the clear sky. How much smaller, then, are the worlds within empty space?</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary:</strong> Ananda, do not sleep in delusion: you must know &#8211; when I say do not sleep, I mean do not be deluded. Do not be infatuated and in love again; do not mistake the truth again.</p>
<p>The Buddha told Ananda not to sleep in delusion; now I tell you all likewise never to sleep in delusion.</p>
<p>The essential point right now is that <strong>you should know: empty space arising within your mind is like a wisp of cloud dotting the clear sky.</strong> All empty space arises from your mind: take the wisp of cloud in the sky as compared with your mind &#8211; the wisp of cloud in the sky is small, but the sky representing your mind is vast and boundless. In an earlier section of the sutra (volume 6) it was said:</p>
<p><em>Emptiness arises within great enlightenment,</em></p>
<p><em>Like a single bubble in the sea.</em></p>
<p>That is: empty space arises within the great sea of enlightenment, like a bubble rising in the ocean. Now it is said: the empty space within your mind is like a cloud dotting the clear sky. <strong>How much smaller, then, are the worlds within empty space?</strong> All that existing empty space is small &#8211; then how vast is your mind? All worlds are smaller still compared with empty space; if empty space is within your mind, the worlds must be smaller yet. This point describes the all-pervading enlightened nature.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhữ đẳng nhất nhân, phát chân quy nguyên, thử thập phương không, giai tất tiêu vẫn. Vân hà không trung, sở hữu quốc độ, nhi bất chấn liệt?</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: If but one among you discovers the truth and returns to the origin, then the empty space of the ten directions will entirely vanish. How could the lands within space not shatter and fall?</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary:</strong> <strong>If but one among you discovers the truth and returns to the origin</strong> &#8211; discovering the truth and returning to the origin means becoming a Buddha. Realizing the fourth Arhat fruition may also be called recognizing the true nature, returning to the inherent mind. Ananda, if one among you becomes a Buddha, <strong>then the empty space of the ten directions will entirely vanish. How could the lands within space not shatter and fall</strong>?</p>
<p>With empty space gone, how could the worlds still exist? The worlds vanish as well. (Empty space came from infatuated love; when one discovers the inherent nature and becomes a Buddha, infatuated love is also gone, and so empty space vanishes.)</p>
<p>Someone says: &#8220;If many people have already attained Buddhahood, empty space should have vanished completely &#8211; so why has empty space not yet disappeared?&#8221;</p>
<p>From the viewpoint of ordinary people, empty space still exists, but the Buddhas see that there is no more empty space &#8211; that is the difference in viewpoint. So you should not argue in generalities. For example, one who has attained the five eyes can see what is happening in this country and in another country a thousand miles away; can you, who have not yet the Buddha eye, see these things? Therefore one cannot say: I see it, so it must exist. You may see it as existing, but through the Buddha's vision nothing exists at all &#8211; not even empty space exists.</p>
