<p><strong>Suddenly boundless joy arises</strong> in the mind &#8211; feeling joy welling up, knowing that joy boundless, its measure immeasurable. <strong>The heart rejoicing, unable to restrain itself</strong> &#8211; unable to stop itself: even wishing to end that merriment, one cannot.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Thử danh khinh an, vô huệ tự  cấm. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: This is called the mind at ease, but lacking the wisdom to restrain itself. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>This is called the mind at ease, but lacking the wisdom to restrain</strong> that joyful state &#8211; the cultivator lacks the wisdom to control this joyful feeling.</p>
<p><strong>Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization</strong>.</p>
<p>This does not mean you have become a sage.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu nhất phần, hảo hỉ lạc ma nhập kì tâm phủ, kiến nhân tắc tiếu, ư cù lộ bàng, tự ca tự vũ. Tự vị dĩ đắc, vô ngại giải thoát. Thất ư chánh thọ, đương tòng luân trụy. </em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: But if interpreted as sagehood, a kind of joy-loving demon enters the very heart, and on meeting people one laughs, singing and dancing by the roadside. Claiming to have attained unobstructed liberation &#8211; losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>But if you deem yourself to have realized sagehood</strong>.</p>
<p>If you say: &#8220;Oh! Now I have entered the Ground of Joy &#8211; I am a Bodhisattva of the Ground of Joy!&#8221; &#8211; then a joy-loving demon enters your mind. <strong>On meeting people at once laughing, singing and dancing by the roadside</strong> &#8211; like a hippie who has entirely lost self-control: waving both arms, stamping both feet, singing and dancing all kinds of music.</p>
<p><strong>Claiming to have attained unobstructed liberation. Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p>Losing all right samadhi, right views, and all power of reason &#8211; in the end falling into the hells.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong> 7 &#8211; GREAT ARROGANCE (THE DEMON OF ARROGANCE)</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm minh bạch, tự vị dĩ túc. Hốt hữu vô đoan, đại ngã mạn khởi, như thị nãi chí, mạn dữ quá mạn, cập mạn quá mạn, hoặc tăng thượng mạn, hoặc ti liệt mạn, nhất thời câu phát. Tâm trung thượng khinh, thập phương Như Lai, hà huống hạ vị, Thanh văn Duyên giác.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed, deeming themselves already complete &#8211; suddenly, for no reason, great arrogance arises: thus pride, exceeding pride, pride exceeding the excellent, superiority pride, and inferiority pride all arise at once. In the mind even slighting the Tathagatas of the ten directions &#8211; how much more the lower ranks of Hearers and Pratyekabuddhas.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed, deeming themselves already complete.</strong></p>
<p>Seeing oneself as having everything in full &#8211; having realized fruitions, awakened, become a Buddha.</p>
<p><strong>Suddenly, for no reason, great arrogance arises</strong>: arrogance is a form of pride &#8211; revering no one, slighting all, thinking no one equals him.</p>
<p><strong>Thus pride arises</strong> &#8211; lofty self-pride, a very high kind of arrogance. <strong>Pride exceeding pride</strong> &#8211; extreme arrogance. <strong>Superiority pride</strong> &#8211; there is no higher form of pride than this &#8211; pride piled upon superiority pride, and <strong>inferiority pride</strong>: feeling everyone baser than himself and slighting them all. These are differing marks of pride, <strong>all arising at once</strong>.</p>
<p>In the <strong>mind even slighting the Tathagatas of the ten directions</strong>.</p>
<p>To what degree is he arrogant? Arrogant not only toward people but toward the Buddhas &#8211; slighting the Tathagatas, seeing them as not his equal. How grave when you utter such words.</p>
<p><strong>How much more</strong> the <strong>lower</strong> ranks <strong>such as Hearers and Pratyekabuddhas</strong>: he slights these all the more, his attitude being: &#8220;You are nothing &#8211; mere Small-Vehicle Arhats &#8211; what is so good about you?&#8221;</p>
<p>He thought himself higher than the Buddhas &#8211; though unfortunately he never earned such a name.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Thử danh kiến thắng, vô huệ tự cứu. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: This is called holding a surpassing view, but lacking the wisdom to save oneself. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>This is called holding a surpassing view</strong> &#8211; a state occurring incidentally within the feeling skandha &#8211; <strong>but lacking the wisdom to save oneself</strong>: such a person lacks the wisdom to rescue himself. <strong>Recognized, it is without fault</strong>: recognizing it as one's own delusion, demons get no chance to work &#8211; as people say: if you understand, you are not deluded; if you are deluded, you lack understanding. If you understand, it is like grasping the sword of wisdom severing all delusion.</p>
<p><strong>That is not sage realization.</strong> This state is not sage realization &#8211; it is not that you have realized the sage fruition.</p>
<p><strong><em><u>Sutra text</u></em></strong><strong><em>: Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu nhất phần, đại ngã mạn ma, nhập kì tâm phủ, bất lễ tháp miếu, tồi hủy kinh tượng. Vị đàn việt ngôn, thử thị toàn đồng, hoặc thị thổ mộc; kinh thị thụ diệp, hoặc thị chiên hoa; nhục thân chân thường, bất tự cung kính, khước sùng thổ mộc, thật vi điên đảo. Kì thâm tín giả, tòng kì hủy toái, mai khí địa trung. Nghi ngộ chúng sanh, nhập vô gián ngục. Thất ư chánh thọ, đương tòng luân trụy.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Translation</u></strong><strong>: But if the cultivator claims sage realization, a kind of greatly arrogant demon enters the very heart: not bowing to stupas and temples, destroying sutras and images. Telling its donors that images are but bronze or clay and wood; sutras but tree leaves or silk; the flesh body truly constant, yet instead of revering itself, worshipping clay and wood &#8211; truly upside down.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Its deep believers follow its smashing, burying and abandoning them in the ground. For misleading beings with doubts and errors, they enter the uninterrupted hell. Losing right samadhi, they are sure to fall. </strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Commentary</u></strong>: <strong>But if the cultivator claims sage realization</strong> &#8211; thinking: &#8220;This is a good state&#8221; &#8211; <strong>then a greatly arrogant demon enters the very heart</strong>, possessing the body. <strong>Not bowing to stupas and temples</strong>: it does not bow to Buddhas or stupas, nor bow to Buddhas on entering temples. <strong>Destroying sutras and images</strong>: it burns sutras and smashes Buddha images &#8211; burning sutras and destroying images are karmic obstructions leading to the hells &#8211; yet it says: &#8220;these are all fakes.&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>Telling its donors</strong>: it has close disciples and tells them so. <em>Dan</em> (Skt. <em>dana</em>) means giving; <em>yue</em> (越, Skt. <em>pati</em>) means crossing over.
