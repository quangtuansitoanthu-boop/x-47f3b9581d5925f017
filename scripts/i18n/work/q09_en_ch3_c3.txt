<p><strong>Wisdom-power exceeding samadhi.</strong> The power of wisdom surpasses the power of samadhi.</p>
<p><strong>Erring in keen sharpness</strong> &#8211; mistaken through sharp, keen wisdom, passing through a reckless, hasty feeling &#8211; fond of <strong>embracing a surpassing nature</strong> of one's own <strong>within the heart</strong>. <strong>Deeming one's own mind to be Vairocana</strong> &#8211; imagining oneself to be Vairocana Buddha.</p>
<p>In this state the cultivator regards his own nature as surpassing and constantly dwells on it &#8211; cherishing fancies of a surpassing nature, mistaking himself for Vairocana Buddha.</p>
<p>Vairocana &#8211; the Sanskrit root meaning &#8220;<em>light shining everywhere.</em>&#8221; The cultivator must not imagine he is already Vairocana Buddha.</p>
<p><strong>Taking a little attainment for enough.</strong> Content with one's paltry attainment.</p>
<p>The cultivator gains a little progress and grows content &#8211; yet that samadhi power is vastly different from the Buddha's great samadhi power &#8211; while he proclaims himself to have become Vairocana Buddha.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Thử danh dụng tâm, vong thất hằng thẩm, nịch ư tri kiến. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng. Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu hạ liệt, di tri túc ma, nhập kì tâm phủ. Kiến nhân tự ngôn, ngã đắc vô thượng đệ nhất nghĩa đế. Thất ư chánh thọ, đương tòng luân trụy. </em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: This is called applying the mind while forgetting constant self-examination, drowning in views and knowledge. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization. But if interpreted as sagehood, a base demon of easily-contented transformation enters the very heart, and on meeting people claims: I have attained the unsurpassed foremost truth. Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p>Commentary: <strong>This state is called &#8220;<em>applying the mind while forgetting constant self-examination, drowning in views and knowledge.</em>&#8221;</strong> You do not constantly apply effort in examining yourself, becoming stagnant in views and knowledge &#8211; unable to distinguish what you know and think, you stagnate, stuck in your own private ideas and notions.</p>
<p><strong>Recognized, it is without fault</strong> &#8211; nothing will go wrong.</p>
<p>That state is <strong>not sage realization</strong>. But <strong>if interpreted as sagehood</strong> &#8211; thinking you have realized some surpassing state &#8211; then <strong>a base demon</strong> &#8211; the basest, most depraved and disgusting kind of demon &#8211; <strong>of easily-contented transformation enters the very heart</strong>: content in any circumstance, <strong>entering your very heart</strong> &#8211; the demon clings to your mind, possessing your mind as its property, driving your spirit out of your body. All that remains is a deluded shell of a body, yet you do not realize it.</p>
<p><strong>On meeting people at once claiming</strong>: <strong><em>I have attained the unsurpassed foremost truth</em>.</strong> There is now a man who often stands on Grand Avenue in San Francisco's Chinatown shopping district, dressed in gaudy clothes of many colors, telling passersby:</p>
<p>&#8220;I cultivated in the mountains and forests for five years and am now awakened; now I have such-and-such spiritual powers&#8230;&#8221;</p>
<p>He formerly bowed to me as teacher and became my disciple, but he did not follow what I taught. Before I came to America, can you guess what he did? With a gourd bearing a painted stripe slung over his shoulder, he proclaimed himself my disciple, claiming to have learned great skill from me; further claiming he could answer any question anyone asked. He used my name to cheat people. Now that I have come to America, he still cheats people &#8211; though he no longer uses my name to deceive. This is precisely the kind of demon this sutra passage addresses: he claims to have attained the foremost truth.</p>
<p><strong>Losing all right samadhi, one is sure to fall</strong>.</p>
<p>He has lost all right samadhi, no longer following what I taught. Since I came here, he never comes to hear sutra lectures or Dharma talks &#8211; only proclaiming he has attained the Way, is awakened, has spiritual powers. What powers does he actually have? You would never guess! They are money, money, money. So he is sure to fall.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong> 5 &#8211; ANXIETY AND GLOOM (THE DEMON OF PERIL)</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch. Tân chứng vị hoạch, cố tâm dĩ vong. Lịch lãm nhị tế, tự sanh gian hiểm. Ư tâm hốt nhiên, sanh vô tận  ưu, như tọa thiết sàng, như ẩm độc dược. Tâm bất dục hoạt, thường cầu ư nhân, linh hại kì mạng, tảo thú giải thoát.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; the new realization not yet gained, the former mind already lost &#8211; examining both ends, they find themselves in peril. Suddenly the mind gives rise to boundless sorrow, as if seated on an iron bed, as if drinking poison. Not wishing to live, they constantly ask others to harm their lives, that liberation come soon.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Further, when good men are in this samadhi.</strong></p>
<p>Now the cultivator has a somewhat deeper samadhi power. <strong>Seeing the form skandha dissolved and the feeling skandha clearly revealed &#8211; the new realization not yet gained, the former mind already lost.</strong> The cultivator sees nothing more to realize; the formerly realized mind-state has vanished. <strong>Examining both ends, they find themselves in peril.</strong> Closely examining the two shores of past and future, seeing both gone, one feels as if meeting great peril.</p>
<p>He thinks: &#8220;Oh no! This is truly perilous! Terrible!&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>Suddenly the mind gives rise to boundless sorrow</strong>: his heart fills with grief and affliction over everything &#8211; and grief itself brings affliction. Affliction how? <strong>As if seated on an iron bed</strong>: from morning to night he thinks:</p>
<p>&#8220;Oh, I am as if seated on an iron bed in the hells. What shall I do now? Having this body is truly suffering.&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>As if drinking poison</strong>, feeling about to die. <strong>Not wishing to live.</strong></p>
<p>All day he thinks: better for me to die.</p>
<p>In China there is a saying: &#8220;Better to die &#8211; it saves clothes.&#8221;</p>
<p>This person has perhaps thought so, saying: &#8220;Better to die! Better to die!&#8221;</p>
<p>In most cases of suicide, demons are involved: the demon bows to the victim and recites a mantra. But the victim cannot hear what mantra the demon recites. The meaning of the mantra demons usually recite is: &#8220;Better for you to die, better for you to die! Hurry &#8211; the sooner life ends, the better!&#8221;</p>
<p>Because the victim is so focused on suicide, he cannot hear it with his ears, yet through the subconscious the demon's mantra seizes the whole soul. Since demons have the power of knowing others' minds, they can let demonic power seep into the victim's mind, inciting suicide. The victim's mind obeys the demon's temptation and believes what the demon says: so they drink poison, hang themselves, drown themselves in the sea, or leap from the Golden Gate Bridge into the river. That is how victims kill themselves. There are a great many demons around the Golden Gate Bridge. They dare not trouble you if you are full of yang energy. But if someone passes there with weak yang energy &#8211; essence, energy, and spirit all weak &#8211; the demons say: &#8220;Better for you to die, better for you to die!&#8230;&#8221; and the person leaps into the river. Most suicides are incited by demons.</p>
