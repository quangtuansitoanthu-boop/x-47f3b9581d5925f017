After leaving home, he pilgrimaged to Putuo Mountain, Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's Way-place. He entered seclusion on that mountain. He hid himself in a small room and read the Great Tripitaka. Reading daily, it takes three years to finish this Canon. He read this Canon over and over for eighteen years. During those years, he did not descend the mountain. Finally, a group of Shanghai Buddhists invited him to lecture the Amitabha Sutra. He agreed, but not many came to hear the sutra, perhaps because they could not understand his Shanghai dialect. But among them was a Shanghai middle-school girl who had a dream telling her to come hear the sutra lecture. The dream announced, "You should go to that lay followers' Way-place... to hear the Amitabha Sutra lectured by Mahasthamaprapta Bodhisattva." The next day, the student read in the newspaper that Dharma Master Yin Guang was lecturing the Amitabha Sutra at that Way-place. She wondered: "Why did the dream announce that Dharma Master Yin Guang is Mahasthamaprapta Bodhisattva?"

That night, she attended the assembly; after everyone had left, she told Dharma Master Yin Guang her dream. When she concluded that he was precisely Mahasthamaprapta Bodhisattva, Dharma Master Yin Guang was displeased, admonishing her, "You must not tell people this nonsense!" So she told no one of the dream. But she took refuge with Dharma Master Yin Guang. Three years later, Dharma Master Yin Guang passed away; only then did she tell of her dream. Everyone was annoyed she had not spoken sooner, so they could have requested more Dharma from the Dharma Master. But she told them the Dharma Master himself had forbidden her to reveal it. From this event, clearly Dharma Master Yin Guang was a transformation of Mahasthamaprapta Bodhisattva. At the cremation, there were a great many sharira relics. So, approaching life's end, some traces are revealed. Yet even so, it must not be spoken of boastfully. As in the above case, perhaps in a dream, a little indication is given. But you should not blatantly say, "I am Mahasthamaprapta Bodhisattva." That is not the way.

People nowadays often go about proclaiming themselves Buddhas. This runs entirely counter to the teaching in the Shurangama Sutra. Of course everyone is a Buddha, but you must cultivate to become a Buddha. If you do not cultivate, you are no more than a horse, cow, pig, sheep, chicken. You will nearly become a hungry ghost, or fall in the hells; nothing is certain.

Sutra text: 汝教世人修三摩地,後復斷除諸大妄語。是名如來先佛世尊。第四決定清淨明誨。

Transliteration: Nhữ giáo thế nhân tu tam-ma-địa, hậu phục đoạn trừ chư đại vọng ngữ. Thị danh Như Lai Tiên Phật Thế Tôn đệ tứ quyết định thanh tịnh minh hối.

Translation: Teach people of the world to cultivate samadhi by finally cutting off all great false speech. This is called the Tathagatas, the former Buddhas, the World Honored Ones' fourth decisive, pure, clear instruction.

Commentary: "Ananda, have you heard this? Teach people of the world to cultivate samadhi by finally cutting off all great false speech." This means all exaggerated, boastful speech. Even with good intent, do not say, "I am enlightened." Or "I have certified to fruition," or "I am a Bodhisattva." Or "I am an Arhat." That is too cheap.

This is called the Tathagatas, the former Buddhas, the World Honored Ones' fourth decisive, pure, clear instruction. Do not teach people to lie and boast. This teaching is the Buddhas' of the present age and the Buddhas' of the past.
