You cannot say: "I am a Buddha! I am a Bodhisattva! I am an Arhat!" Whoever speaks so is a demon, like those described in this sutra. When may you let people know? After you die. Then people will know. But you must not let them know before you die. What meaning is there in proclaiming yourself a Buddha? What meaning? You say you are a Bodhisattva? Why? What meaning is there in such speech? There is no reason other than making others believe in you. And why do you want people to believe in you? So they will give you money? You do it for offerings and a seeking mind. If that is not your intention, then why do you tell people you are a living Buddha? If you are a Bodhisattva — fine! You are a Bodhisattva; why go about telling people so? This reminds me of some events that took place in China.

One day an official came to Guoqing Temple on Tiantai Mountain to visit the abbot Feng Gan. The two talked together. You will ask the official's name? Do not ask me; I have forgotten. It may be you, it may be I — uncertain. The official said to the abbot: "In the past, Bodhisattvas often manifested in the world, but in this age there are none. I wish to meet a true Bodhisattva but see none."

Abbot Feng Gan said, "Oh! You wish to meet a Bodhisattva? There are two here. I shall introduce you."

The official was utterly amazed. "Two Bodhisattvas here? Do you mean one of clay and one carved of wood?"

The abbot answered: "Not so. These two Bodhisattvas are real people. They are living Bodhisattvas."

The official asked, "You are not joking?"
The abbot answered: "I am the abbot here. How could I joke about such a thing?"
"Where are they?"

"One is cooking rice and one is boiling water. One is Han Shan and one is Shi De. One is a transformation of Manjushri Bodhisattva, one a transformation of Samantabhadra Bodhisattva. They practice ascetic cultivation in this temple, doing service work. They do what no one wants to do. If you wish to meet them, it is most simple — enter the kitchen and you will see them there. You see two smudged, ragged monks, hair and beard unkempt, faces full of dirt, with most ugly appearances. But the abbot says they are two Bodhisattvas, so you dare not slight them. Instead, you bow to them. The two ask: "What are you doing? Why do you bow to me?"

"Abbot Feng Gan says you are transformations of Manjushri Bodhisattva and Samantabhadra Bodhisattva, so I bow to you."
The two said: "Old Feng Gan talks too much!"

Meaning the abbot is one who meddles in others' affairs.

One said: "Now he talks too much!"

So while the official was bowing, they withdrew and vanished; no one knew how far they had gone — perhaps a hundred paces from the kitchen to the rocky shore at the mountain's foot. Their voices echoed back, "Old Feng Gan talks too much. Instead of bowing to Amitabha Buddha, why bow to us?"

The official asked: "Who is Amitabha Buddha?"
"Abbot Feng Gan. He is Amitabha Buddha reborn. Leave us alone. Go bow to him." While the official still stood there amazed, the two grimy monks had gone far and vanished behind the rocky range.
