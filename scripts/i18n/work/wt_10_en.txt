TITLE: 11. THE ANTHILL AND THE HOUSE
TOMTAT: A family that destroyed anthills for years could find no settled home for three years. Meeting Master Miaofa, they were shown the cause and effect, repented, and recited sutras dedicated to the ants.
-----
{align:center|**THE ANTHILL AND THE HOUSE**}

{align:justify|A couple brought their teenage son to request an audience with Master Miaofa for guidance.}

{align:justify|Three years earlier they had emigrated from Taiwan abroad. At first they rented a room to shelter in, planning later to choose a suitable villa to buy.}

{align:justify|But buying a house never succeeded, because all four family members always disagreed: husband liked what wife rejected, or the reverse. Or the children liked what the parents opposed. Rarely did they find a house all four approved — and when the whole family agreed, perversely the owner would not sell. So it dragged on for nearly three years. To this day they have found no suitable house, and they pondered it without understanding why.}

{align:justify|The couple deeply honored the Buddha, and fell to doubting — thinking their own blessings must be insufficient. So on a holiday they crossed to the mainland, climbed Mount Wutai to honor the Buddha, and begged Master Miaofa to resolve their doubt.}

{align:justify|Hearing their tale, the Master only smiled and gently asked the two boys:}

{align:justify|"You mischievous ones — are you fond of poking anthills?"}

{align:justify|At the Master's question, all four burst out laughing as one.}

{align:justify|The mother said:}

{align:justify|"Yes — as little boys they loved wrecking anthills, saying they wanted to study the anthill's structure, praising how cleverly it is built, with a palace for the queen, august barracks for the workers, and great storehouses of food... Yes, they studied and played entranced, forgetting even to eat..."}

{align:justify|The Master said:}

{align:justify|"These two love poking anthills, destroying the many homes the ants built through 'a thousand pains and ten thousand toils,' ruining and scattering the food the ants spent long years hauling from everywhere to store — leaving them no place to rest and dwell."}

{align:justify|For that reason, wherever you live today you are uneasy, seeking a house and finding none! This is only the first retribution. If you do not repent and mend your faults, then in future, even with a house of your own, your house will face sudden destruction. However rich you become, in future you can hardly escape times of struggling to find even food."}

{align:justify|Turning to the two adults, the Master rebuked:}

{align:justify|"And you two — as parents, long taken refuge at the Buddha's gate, how could you stand by watching, letting your children do such merit-damaging deeds without forbidding or guiding them? 'To raise without teaching is the parents' fault.' You must know karmic retribution follows like shadow after form. If you soon repent, create merit, and diligently recite sutras dedicating the merit to the thousands upon thousands of ants you harmed in body and property, perhaps you may yet alter the calamities of the future. Do you understand?"}

{align:justify|Taught by the Master, husband and wife were moved to remorseful repentance:}

{align:justify|"Master, your disciples know their fault. Though long taken refuge, we thought eating vegetarian and reciting the Buddha's name was good enough! Little did we know it was this grave. Home again, the whole family shall repent before the Buddha and recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra and the Great Compassion Mantra, dedicating the merit to the ants — may we?"}

{align:justify|"Excellent, excellent! Very well — ever remember firmly: 'All beings can become Buddhas,' so never despise small creatures, never casually harm beings!" the Master urged earnestly.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story THE ANTHILL AND THE HOUSE is a vivid lesson in "destroying beings' homes — retribution in having no settled home." Two boys wrecked anthills in entranced "study"; their parents never stopped them — the cause sown. The fruit: three years unable to buy a house, family discord, no one ever satisfied. Master Miaofa states plainly: "To raise without teaching is the parents' fault." After the whole family repented and recited sutras dedicated to the ants, they hoped to transform the karma. The story warns: all beings can become Buddhas — never despise tiny creatures. Wrecking anthills, burning beehives, or harming animals' homes also creates karma, and retribution will come as "having no place to rest" for oneself and one's children.}
