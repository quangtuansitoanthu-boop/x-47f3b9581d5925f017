Commentary: After my extinction, if there are bhikshus who resolve to cultivate samadhi with determination. Who before the Tathagata's image can burn a lamp upon their bodies, burn off a finger joint, or burn a stick of incense upon their bodies. I say such people, beginningless old debts repaid at once. These bhikshus, under true guidance, at the right time, and with dignified deportment, use knives to cut flesh from their bodies, then pour a little oil into the hollowed flesh, then light a lamp therefrom as an offering to the Buddhas. Or sometimes they burn off a finger according to ritual, or they burn several sticks of incense upon their bodies, for instance upon the arm. Shakyamuni Buddha teaches that for such people, the karmic debts they created in countless past kalpas can all be extinguished through this simple deed.

Long bid farewell to the world, forever free of all outflows. Though they have not yet brightened the unsurpassed enlightened road, they already have determination toward the Dharma. They have resolute resolve and will never retreat.

Sutra text: 若不為此捨身微因。縱成無為必還生人,酬其宿債。如我馬麥正等無異。

Transliteration: Nhược bất vi thử xả thân vi nhân, túng thành vô vi tất hoàn sanh nhân, thù kỳ túc trái. Như ngã mã mạch chánh đẳng vô dị.

Translation: If one does not plant this small cause ofrenouncing the body, though one perfects the unconditioned, one must still be reborn as a human to repay old debts — just as my retribution of horse-feed, exactly the same.

Commentary: If one does not plant this small cause ofrenouncing the body, though one perfects the unconditioned, one must still be reborn as a human to repay old debts. If one does not perform such body-sacrificing deeds — burning a lamp upon the body, burning off a finger, or burning incense upon the arm, planting a few good dharmas — then though one perfects the Way's work, though one becomes enlightened, even becomes a Buddha, one must still repay old debts. One must still take a human body again and repay past borrowed karma, just as my retribution of horse-feed.

"The Tathagata had to eat horse-feed for ninety days in this life," the Buddha says.
Why did Shakyamuni Buddha undergo such retribution? It happened in the past, when he was a brahman teaching five hundred youths how to cultivate the Way. At the time, a Buddha was manifest in the world. One day, that Buddha and his bhikshus went begging; the Buddha taught the bhikshus who received offerings to set aside a portion of the best from their bowls for a sick bhikshu unable to beg. When they returned from begging, they passed through the mountains where the brahman — Shakyamuni Buddha's former self on the causal ground — was cultivating. When the brahman received only a little food from their very full bowls, he grew jealous: "Why do those old monks get to eat so much? They deserve only horse-feed." The brahman's five hundred disciples echoed him: "Right! They deserve only horse-feed."
