TITLE: 25. UPHOLDING THE DHARMA THROUGH VIRTUE
TOMTAT: A matron who supported the pagoda and gave alms yet contracted lung cancer — from pride, arrogance, and love of fame and gain. Meeting Master Miaofa, she was urged to recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra with a humble heart; after 19 copies the tumor vanished.
-----
{align:center|**UPHOLDING THE DHARMA THROUGH VIRTUE**}

{align:justify|I asked the master:}

{align:justify|"I see that matron most sincerely supporting the monastery, often giving alms and joining Dharma assemblies — yet now she suffers lung cancer no hospital can treat. May I ask the Master the cause of her illness?"}

{align:justify|The Master answered:}

{align:justify|"As a person, never let thoughts of fame and gain burden you; never arouse pride and arrogance. Know that when we came into this world we were naked, and leaving it we must go empty-handed, carrying nothing away. Fame and gain are like passing smoke and cloud — never for fame and gain arouse pride and arrogance. Only the pure heart receives the Buddha's light blessing. If with a corrupt heart, a proud and arrogant heart, you give wealth to a pagoda, the monastery is like a palace storing gold and piling silver — one who offers with a poisoned heart only defiles practitioners of pure conduct. But if you not only offer wealth, but also uphold the monastery with virtue, the monastery becomes a bodhimanda adorned with ten thousand virtues, saving the ten directions far and wide."}

{align:justify|Tell her that besides cultivating her mind, correcting her nature, and keeping a humble heart, she must also recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra to dissolve old karma. Reciting, do not race greedily through it, skimming carelessly. While reciting, sound, mouth, and eyes work in concert and there shall be no drowsiness or distraction. Reciting each word, each line, equals reciting a thousand words, a thousand lines; reciting one copy equals reciting a thousand copies. Only so shall beings throughout the dharma realm benefit. Not only guard speech and conduct against greed, hatred, and delusion — ever keep the heart that tames them. Like weeding: root them out wholly, leaving no trouble behind.}

{align:justify|When I carried the Master's teaching to the laywoman, she said: "The Master criticizes most rightly!" She recognized her own failing and sincerely showed repentance. Then she let go completely, sparing no more thought for her illness, and stayed home reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra.}

{align:justify|Reciting to the 7th copy, she felt the illness in her body half healed. Examined in hospital, the malignant tumor had turned benign! Her joy beyond hoping deepened her faith in the Master.}

{align:justify|When the doctors proposed surgery to cut it out, she refused; home again, she continued reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra. At the 19th copy she felt health brimming over. Daily she bowed to the Buddha more than a hundred times without fatigue. Then she returned for re-examination: the lung tumor had wholly vanished, not a trace remaining. A true miracle!}

{align:justify|To this day, nearly 5 years, in the spring of 2000 she rode from Tianjin to visit me — looking healthier than in her youth by far. Most strange!}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story UPHOLDING THE DHARMA THROUGH VIRTUE is an important lesson in "giving alms with pride and arrogance — retribution in lung cancer." The matron supported the pagoda and joined Dharma assemblies, but with thoughts of fame and gain, pride and arrogance — the cause sown. Its fruit: lung cancer. Taught by Master Miaofa, she let go, repented, and recited the Kshitigarbha Sutra with a humble heart (no racing, sound-mouth-eyes as one); after 7 copies the tumor turned benign, after 19 copies it vanished utterly. The story stresses: "Only the pure heart receives the Buddha's light blessing." Upholding the Buddha Dharma is not only giving wealth, but more importantly upholding with virtue (humility, freedom from greed, hatred, delusion). It reminds those who "volunteer and support pagodas" yet arouse thoughts of fame, gain, and contention over offerings: "The monastery is like a palace storing gold and piling silver — one who offers with a poisoned heart only defiles practitioners of pure conduct."}
