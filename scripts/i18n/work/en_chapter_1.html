Translation: But you still do not know the subtle demonic occurrences that arise during the cultivation of shamatha and vipashyana. When demonic states appear before you, you will not recognize them, for your cleansing of the mind is not in accord with the true Dharma, and you will fall into wrong views.

Commentary: But you still do not know the subtle demonic occurrences that will arise during the cultivation of shamatha and vipashyana.

Earlier Ananda had asked the Buddha to teach the method of cultivation, requesting the Dharma on behalf of beings of future ages.

Although Ananda now understands the principles of cultivation, he still lacks experience in practice: he has grasped the theory but, lacking experience, does not know what will occur during practice – hence the Buddha's instruction here. Cultivating shamatha means cultivating the Great Shurangama Samadhi; cultivating vipashyana is a very subtle contemplative Dharma. Subtle demonic occurrences will appear at this point: in the course of cultivation, many demonic states will manifest. Not every phenomenon appears openly – they are very subtle.

When demonic states appear before you and you do not recognize them. When you apply effort in the method of returning the hearing to hear the self-nature (turning the hearing inward to recognize your inherent self-nature), demonic states will appear. If you do not recognize the demons and do not know what demons are, it is because your cleansing of the mind is not in accord with the true Dharma.

For you have cleansed your own mind, but with a slight deviation, not in accord with right views – hence falling into wrong views. If your right views are not truly correct (not in accord with the right Dharma), you will get caught in demonic states.

Translation: You will be disturbed by a demon of your own five skandhas, or by a heavenly demon, or be possessed by ghosts and spirits, or meet goblins and sprites. With the mind unclear, you will mistake a thief for your own son.

Commentary: You will be disturbed by a demon of your own five skandhas.

That is a kind of demon arising from one's own mind – for instance, one of the ten kinds of demons arising from the form skandha, which also come from your own mind.

Or by a heavenly demon. Why would a demon from the heavens come to harass you? Because in cultivating you aim at attaining samadhi power.

Attaining a little samadhi power in cultivation is no great matter in itself, but it can shake the demon king's palace as in an earthquake. Since the demon king has spiritual powers, the moment his palace trembles he investigates: “Why is my palace suddenly shaking?” He discovers that someone in the world is about to accomplish the Way; that person's samadhi power will shatter his palace. The heavenly demon then thinks: “You want to destroy my palace – I shall destroy your samadhi power first.” And so it comes to sabotage the cultivator's samadhi power.

Or be possessed by ghosts and spirits: when ghosts and spirits see you about to attain results in cultivation, they grow jealous, thinking: “Oh! So you are about to realize the Way.” So first they come to destroy your cultivation effort – entering your mind or seizing your body so your samadhi power cannot come to perfection.

It makes you “go astray and fall under demonic possession,” becoming dependent on it. I have already lectured before on being possessed by ghosts and spirits, have I not? That is very important. Why do you become the demon's property? Because your cleansing of the mind is not true, not in accord with the right Dharma, and because the motive is not upright – with even a single thought of a tiny deviant intention, you will become possessed by ghosts and spirits. That is what is called “going astray and falling under demonic possession.”

Or meet goblins and sprites – or wang-liang goblins. These demons are all kinds of goblins and monsters. With the mind unclear, you will mistake a thief for your own son. If you meet this kind of demon without recognizing it or understanding anything about it, you will come to “mistake a thief for your own son.” Think this over carefully, lest it steal your property: if you let the thief into your house, all the precious property in the house will be carried off by him.

What is the most precious property in one's own house?

I shall speak plainly and simply so that you must remember this well – you must believe what I say. Whatever you do, do not forget my words and cast them aside. Why? Because this concerns your own future and life.

What is your own precious treasury? It is the treasury of the inherent true nature, the Tathagata, within you. Can you afford to lose that Tathagata-store nature? Do you not remember that I have already spoken before of the essence, energy, and spirit of the Tathagata store? If you wish to recover your own Tathagata store, you must first preserve your own essence, energy, and spirit (jing, qi, shen). If you do not take good care of these three, then you let your property be robbed. Be extremely careful.

Translation: Further, in the midst of cultivation you feel satisfied and content with a little attainment – like the Unlearned Bhikshu who reached the Fourth Dhyana and rashly claimed to have realized sagehood. When his heavenly blessings ended, the signs of decay appeared, and he slandered Arhatship as still subject to birth and death, falling into the Avichi hell.

Commentary: Further, in the midst of cultivation, you feel satisfied and content with a little attainment.

During cultivation, even if you are not possessed by ghosts and spirits, you must have genuine wisdom and the Dharma-selecting eye: selecting means choosing, Dharma means the Buddhadharma, and eye means your own eye. If you can recognize the Buddhadharma, you will know for yourself what level your cultivation has reached. Do not feel satisfied upon gaining a little understanding of the principles.

Like the Unlearned Bhikshu who reached the Fourth Dhyana and rashly claimed to have realized sagehood.

He is called the Unlearned Bhikshu because he did not understand much – he knew only a little Buddhadharma. Why is he called Unlearned? Fundamentally, one who realizes the fourth fruition of Arhatship is born no more; the Buddha taught that whoever realizes the fourth Arhat fruition no longer undergoes birth-and-death in the turning wheel. One who realizes the second fruition, called Once-Returner, must still undergo one birth in the heavens and one return among humans – one more birth and death to pass through. A sage of the first fruition (among the four Arhat fruitions) must still pass through seven births and deaths. All these stages transcend the heavens of the Fourth Dhyana.

The Unlearned Bhikshu had only reached the Fourth Dhyana stage in his cultivation, yet he thought he had realized the fourth Arhat fruition. In fact, at the Fourth Dhyana level one has realized no fruition at all and is still an ordinary person (having samadhi power but no wisdom yet).

Yet the Unlearned Bhikshu proclaimed he had realized the fourth Arhat fruition. Even today there are people who consider the fourth Arhat fruition low compared to themselves, brazenly proclaiming that they themselves are Buddhas. But a Buddha is complete with the three bodies, four kinds of wisdom, five eyes, and six spiritual powers. You may ask those who proclaim themselves Buddhas how many spiritual powers they have – surely the extinction-of-outflows power is lacking. I am certain that those who claim to be Buddhas do not have even a single spiritual power, let alone five or six. Only those without spiritual powers wish to boast that they are Buddhas; those who truly had even one spiritual power would never dare utter such a great false claim.

When his heavenly blessings ended – when his life in the heavens came to an end – the signs of decay appeared. Do you remember the five signs of decay? Do you remember the five signs of decay I lectured on in an earlier section? When a being's life in the heavens ends and death approaches, five signs appear, called the five signs of decay: first, the flower garland on the head (the crown) withers; second, the clothes become soiled; third, sweat comes from the armpits; fourth, the body becomes foul and filthy; fifth, discontent with one's seat.

He slandered Arhatship as still subject to birth and death, and fell into the Avichi hell.

When the heavenly blessings are nearly exhausted, the five signs of decay appear, signaling that life in the heavens is about to end. The Unlearned Bhikshu grew angry – angry at what? He thought: “I have been deceived by the Buddha; the Buddha is a fraud. The Buddha said that whoever realizes the fourth Arhat fruition no longer passes through birth-and-death in the turning wheel. Why is my life about to end? Why must I be reborn? Why must I transmigrate? Why must I be born again? The Buddha is a liar.” We know at once what happened when he slandered the Buddha thus: he fell straight into the Avichi hell.

The Avichi hell is also called the uninterrupted hell. Since he had not realized Arhatship yet claimed he had, when his heavenly blessings were enjoyed to the end and his life there ended, he fell into a lower realm. He did not recognize his own error but claimed the Buddha's teaching was wrong. In truth the Buddha had said: “You have not realized the fourth Arhat fruition; had you realized it, you would have no more birth and death.”

Why did the five signs of decay appear? Because of slandering the Buddha. Therefore the Unlearned Bhikshu fell straight into the uninterrupted hell.

You can imagine where those who proclaim themselves Buddhas will go. I do not know where their lives will end.

Translation: Because of your false thoughts, deluded about the truth, infatuation and love arise, giving rise to all-pervading delusion, and so there is empty space. Commentary: Because of your false thoughts, deluded about the truth: Ananda, your self-nature and the self-nature of the twelve kinds of beings are no different from the Buddhas – one, identical. Yet relying on the true, you give rise to mistaken false thoughts and become deluded about the truth, and wrong delusion arises from that.

Hence infatuation and love arise.

What is the greatest fault? Infatuation and love. They may be spoken of as two things, or gathered into one: infatuated love. Ignorance covers all understanding – knowing only lustful love, thinking only of lustful love from morning to night, unable to let go of lustful love even for a moment. If you applied yourself to studying the Buddhadharma the way you indulge in lustful love, you would become a Buddha very quickly. But alas, you cannot convert the love of pleasure into love of the Buddhadharma: the more you study the Buddhadharma, the more bored you feel, thinking: “I have too many faults; the Buddhadharma exposes them all – I do not want to study anymore. The Buddhadharma calls on me to change my habits – how could I change them?” That is an example of infatuated love. Above, the sutra text said: because of false thought, one is deluded about the truth; now I can say that all faults arise from infatuated love.

Giving rise to all-pervading delusion.

Infatuated love causes all-pervading delusion: you become deluded and confused about everything. Once infatuated and in love, you understand nothing about anything; nothing is worth your concern, and you stubbornly insist: “If I fall into the hells, so be it – why bother?” You no longer care about anything at all.

And so there is empty space.

From infatuated love you create faults: a man keeps longing for a woman from morning to night, and a woman keeps longing for a man all day long – and so a kind of falseness arises.

Delusion transforming without cease, the world comes into being.

Delusion multiplies many times over – one delusion becomes two, two become three – delusion turning with no stopping place. Wise people should be cautious at this point; engrave its deep meaning into your very bones. It has pointed out to you the whole of delusion.

Thus the lands as numerous as dust-motes in the ten directions are all established by deluded, obstinate, false thought.

The worlds throughout the ten directions cannot be destroyed – yet they are not worlds beyond outflow, for they have no separate self-nature of their own: they are only the establishment of mistaken false thought – delusion together with lack of understanding, delusion together with obstinacy, not knowing how to transform – and so the world is established from these deluded false thoughts.

Commentary: When you concentrate on cultivating Chan and attain samadhi, your true mind becomes spiritually interfused, uniting with the true minds of the Bodhisattvas and the great unoutflowing Arhats throughout the ten directions who abide in stillness.

You need not seek still purity anywhere else – it is right where you are. The still purity right in your own body and mind is precisely the Tathagata-store nature, pervading the entire Dharma realm.

Why do the minds of the Bodhisattvas and Arhats interfuse with those who cultivate Chan? Because they cultivate the same great samadhi – all using the method of “returning the hearing to hear the self-nature” – turning the hearing inward to recognize one's own nature until the Buddha Way is accomplished.

This connection occurs not only at the level of Buddhas, Bodhisattvas, and Arhats, but often among ordinary people as well. For example, when you think of someone, your thought contacts that person like sending a telegram. Does the other person know? Ask that person's inherent nature – it clearly knows, though their conscious mind may know nothing. On the level of substance, one can indeed know another's mind.

“Then if I think of someone, does that person likewise think of me?”

You may think until you die, to no avail. It is like the infatuated love I lectured on before: if a person is infatuated, he dwells on scenes of lustful love all day long, never able to stop those false thoughts – alone he thinks until he dies. Commentary: When a person attains the Great Shurangama Samadhi, the true great samadhi, the demon hosts tremble in terror. All demon kings, ghosts, spirits, and ordinary gods of the six Desire heavens and the heavens of the Four Dhyanas see their palaces for no reason crumble and fall – a truly incomprehensible situation, with no clear cause for their palaces suddenly falling into ruin.

I once told you about a young disciple of mine in Manchuria: only about fourteen years old, yet his spiritual powers were no small matter – he could ascend to the heavens and descend to earth at will. Although his five eyes were opened, he had not yet realized the six powers; he had five spiritual powers, lacking only the extinction of outflows. When a person attains the extinction-of-outflows power, he is called an Arhat.

One day he went up to the heavens to play. Up there the heavenly demons were delighted and lured him into their palace – a magnificent castle made of transparent crystal. The young novice fell into the trap.

Since he had the five eyes, he could see that his Dharma-body had been detained by the King of the Paranirmita-Vashavartin Heaven. Yet he still found a way to inform me: “Master, I went up to the heavens to play and now cannot return.”

“So you are stuck in the heavens! Hmph! Who told you to go up there in the first place?”

“I thought it would be great fun, so I went up to have a look. Now the people here do not want to let me back.”

I said: “If you want amusement, do not go up there looking for fun. The heavenly demons of the Desire Realm heavens all seek opportunities to destroy a cultivator's samadhi power.” I explained: “Do not fear – I shall bring you back.”

I tried to bring him back, but the demon king would not let him go. By then he was truly frightened and said: “It won't let me return. What now?” I said: “Do not worry – I have a way to bring you right back.”

Then I used the Shurangama Mantra – the fivefold heart mantras for dispelling demonic obstructions that I had already taught you. The demon king's palace instantly disintegrated, and the young novice returned at that very moment. This is a true story.

At that time the demon king's palace crumbled and collapsed. The earth quaked and split in many places. Creatures of water and land flying and leaping in terror.

All creatures in the water, on the ground, flying and leaping, were frightened into uncontrollable panic.

Dull ordinary people are unaware of these upheavals.

Ordinary people lack keen perception; they do not notice the earth undergoing such transformations, lacking sufficient sensitivity to perceive the six kinds of quaking occurring within the earth.

Therefore ghosts, spirits, heavenly demons, goblins, sprites, and sprites all wish to come torment you while you cultivate samadhi.

Commentary: Now do you understand why demons come to harass? Only because these beings all possess five kinds of spiritual powers – the heavenly eye, heavenly ear, knowledge of others' minds, knowledge of past lives, and spiritual travel – lacking only the extinction of outflows. Had they the extinction-of-outflows power, they would no longer trouble you. But lacking it, they remain evil and still wish to come sabotage cultivators of the Way… What does that mean? I shall explain this very thoroughly.

What is called outflow is scattered, wandering thoughts: men thinking of women, women thinking of men. If you do not end these wandering thoughts, you can never escape outflows by yourself.

Now I shall explain this more deeply, and speak very truthfully: if I do not speak truthfully, you will never know how outflows truly concern you. To escape all outflows means keeping your essence intact; to lose your essence means there are outflows.

Now I shall reveal the secret of heaven and earth: if you preserve your essence, there will be no outflows. Moreover, not only preventing the loss of your own nature, you must also be able to cut off lustful thoughts even in the subtlest mental arising – then you have truly escaped all outflows. Now do you understand? Why are heavenly demons not free of all outflows? Because they still have thoughts of lust; ghosts and spirits also still have such lustful thoughts.

For they are still clinging to this worldly toil.

Worldly toil is simply a form of lust – that is what they crave.

How could they let you destroy their dwelling?

Since they still crave lust, they do not want to see you cut yourself off from it; they want you to indulge in lust too. They will say:

“We two are close friends – I shall never cut myself off from lust. So you should not reject it either.”

That is why demons and ghosts come: none of them wants you to transcend this worldly toil.

Hence ghosts, spirits, heavenly demons, goblins, and sprites will come to sabotage you when you cultivate samadhi.

When I mentioned the word sprites before, its meaning was not clear to you; now I shall explain. If in cultivation you attain samadhi power, demons and ghosts all want to devour your flesh.
