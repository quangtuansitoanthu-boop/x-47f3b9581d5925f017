PHUONG HAI SANH

The boy Phuong Hai Sanh was the son of Layman Phuong Duong Thu of Hong Kong. Duong Thu delighted in meritorious charities, and after middle age devoted himself to the Pure Land.

Hai Sanh was only seven, dwelling with his parents in Hong Kong, often following his father in worshipping the Buddha and reciting. He could chant the Cundi Mantra and the Hell-Breaking verses of the Avatamsaka Sutra. Sometimes, remembering his father's teaching, he described the Pure Land to acquaintances, saying: "Be diligent in Buddha-recitation. Reciting Amitabha's sacred name, one shall be reborn in the Land of Bliss.".

In the second month of the ninth year of the Republic, on Avalokiteshvara's feast day, Hai Sanh suddenly fell ill. Duong Thu sat beside him voicing the sacred name in support, and the boy chanted along with his father. Even with eyes closed in stupor, he recited the Cundi Mantra fluently without missing a word. Duong Thu carried his son before the Buddha altar, urging him to contemplate the Three Holy Ones of the West; beholding the images, Hai Sanh loudly recited Nam mô A Di Đà Phật dozens of times, louder than when well.

Past mid-afternoon of the twenty-first, the boy suddenly descended from bed and walked step by step. Duong Thu hastened to support him and asked where he wished to go; he answered: "I am going to venerate the Buddha!". Though his speech had grown labored, he could still burn incense and worship. At ten that night, the nurse, while Hai Sanh's little sister recited for his swift recovery, suddenly saw walls and partitions vanish into emptiness, with golden light of five hues blazing like a moonlit night. Within the golden light, a venerable one carried Hai Sanh up above the roof and flew to the West. Still doubting, as if waking from a dream, she saw walls and partitions restored as before. The nurse hastened down to inquire — Hai Sanh had already breathed his last.

As the end approached, his limbs first turned cold, his eyes fixed upon his parents. Duong Thu urged supporting recitation of the sacred name, described the joys of the Lotus Land, and exhorted his son to cling to nothing else but turn wholly toward the Land of Bliss. Hai Sanh obeyed, devoting himself to Buddha-recitation until his last breath. After his passing, his limbs grew warm again, his countenance fresh as in life. A day before Hai Sanh fell ill, his mother in a half-dream had seen a great majestic hall bathed in golden light, and in its depths a large beautiful lotus bearing a golden-bodied Buddha seated above. From afar she saw only the Thus Come One's feet in crossed-legged sitting. Hai Sanh, having planted deep wholesome roots in a former life, knew Buddha-recitation at seven. Blessed with a wise father to support him, at the end blessed signs appeared and right mindfulness was clear.

At the same time there was Dam Lac Kieu of Nanhai district, earning his living in Hong Kong. Honest by nature, firmly believing cause and effect, he knew Duong Thu, who gave him the Direct Guide to the West and urged rebirth through recitation. Lac Kieu believed and practiced, day and night unwearying. Before long he fell ill, knew he would not recover, settled his affairs, and returned home. Along the road he still strove in single-minded recitation; reaching home, his suffering seemed to dissolve, and he told his wife: "I am about to be reborn in the Pure Land; no need to call physicians — help me chant the Buddha's name!". His wife, long devoted to the Three Jewels, did as urged. As the end neared, Lac Kieu could still descend from bed and walk, asking his wife for fresh clean robes to change into. All done, he sat reciting and passed away, his features most peaceful and fresh.
