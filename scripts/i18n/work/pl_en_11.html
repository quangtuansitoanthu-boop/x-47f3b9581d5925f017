VUONG NHUT HUU

Layman Vuong Nhut Huu, styled Hu Trung, was a native of Luzhou. Under Emperor Gaozong of the Song, he passed the national doctoral examination, but declined office and retired to his homeland to teach. He then gave up teaching as well, kept a lifelong vegetarian diet, wore coarse cloth, and cultivated the pure karma with single purpose. Each day he bowed one thousand prostrations to the Buddha and then recited the sacred name. He authored the Longshu Pure Land Writings, widely circulated in his time. In it the language is simple, with many parables, urging everyone from princes, officials, and scholars down to commoners, butchers, servants, and singers to take refuge and recite the Buddha's name. His arguments are lucid, his intent utterly sincere, moving many to be transformed and practice.

In the Gui Si year of the Qiandao era, Mr. Ly Ngan Bac of Luling fell gravely ill and dreamed of one who called himself Layman Longshu, saying: "When you wake, take plain rice gruel and you will recover. Do you remember a good friend, Khuyet Trong Nha, who urged you to the direct path?". Ngan Bac replied: "I have followed his counsel and recite the Buddha's name daily!". Upon waking, he had plain gruel cooked and eaten, and indeed the illness eased. Ngan Bac then sent his sons to find Vuong Nhut Huu and receive instruction. Before long the sons returned and reported: "Layman Longshu has already been reborn in the Buddha-land. Three days before his passing, he went about bidding farewell to relatives and friends, urging them to practice diligently, saying he was about to journey far and they would meet no more. On the final day he gathered his old disciples, instructed them, and performed his recitation as usual. At the third watch, he suddenly chanted the Buddha's name aloud a few times and said: 'Amitabha has come to escort me!' Then, standing upright, he passed away...". Seeing the portrait of Vuong Nhut Huu which his sons had borrowed and brought home — exactly the person he had met in his dream — Ly Ngan Bac was deeply moved, had an image of Layman Longshu carved, and spread the story far and wide. From then on, devotees throughout Luling resolved to recite the Buddha's name.

In the Xianchun years, Mr. Lu Nguyen Ich re-carved the Longshu Pure Land Writings. When the carving reached the chapter of dedication, three relics suddenly appeared upon the block. His uncle Ly Su Thuyet recorded the event at the head of that chapter.

VIEN LIET TINH

Vien Liet Tinh, a native of Kunshan, often lodged in the Xikou region of Hangzhou, and so called himself Layman Tay Khe. As a boy he studied for the examinations; one day, hearing his grandmother recite the Diamond Sutra as far as "no marks of self," he found an entrance.

Later the layman met Zen Master Duyet Son and diligently investigated the upward path day by day. Just then Old Man Bach Tung came from Xuefeng to Kunshan; after two or three encounters, Liet Tinh penetrated his original nature and received the mind-seal. From then on his style grew daily more flourishing; teaching according to capacity with unhindered eloquence, he promoted the One Vehicle while also spreading the Pure Land. Seeing his companions fond of dry Zen or delighting in arts of immortality, mostly disbelieving the Pure Land, he wrote these words of explanation:

"The Pure Land gate is perfectly interfused and vast. Until now I have secretly practiced and silently realized it, but dared not hastily speak of it to others. That is because the season had not come, and speech would have been fruitless — not because I deliberately hid my practice.

For Buddha-recitation is the Buddhas' most profound Dharma-treasury: even among voice-hearers and solitary Buddhas of the Two Vehicles there are those who do not believe. For those venerables, abiding in the pure Nirvana state with realized spiritual powers, see and know only within a single great chiliocosm, and do not perceive the ocean of majestic Pure Land adornments established throughout the ten directions. Hence the Buddhas have rebuked clinging to the Two Vehicles' state as deviant, as falling into the pit of inaction, as drunken on the wine of samadhi! How lamentable are Zen students of today, who have not yet reached even the pure Nirvana state of the Two Vehicles, yet with empty bellies and lofty hearts claim attainment without attaining, realization without realizing. Sitting quietly until the six senses momentarily stir no more, a glimpse of scenery appears, and at once they suppose themselves awakened. Strutting ever after, they disparage the Pure Land — truly pitiable!

Even supposing they had truly and greatly awakened and touched the tip of the nose of the mother of original nature, could they stand outside the four Pure Lands, from the Land of Common Residence to the Land of Everlasting Quiescent Light? If they argue: 'Though knowing those four lands, I cast them aside!' Do they not recall the venerable Zen Master Mat Van's words: 'You wish to cast it aside — truly a case of telling dreams before the deluded!' The ancients said: 'Zen students of today take penetrating the Dharma-body as essential, yet know not what to do after penetrating. If after penetrating there remains the shadow of anything, that too is sickness!' In short, Zen students of today rarely escape the subtle demon of intellectual understanding, and in turn call Pure Land practitioners ignorant — is that not error!

Moreover, throughout empty space there are ten Dharma-realms, and the Buddhas' Pure Lands are the foremost realm, to which the other nine all return. Practicing the Pure Land in the human realm is very easy, while in the hells and hungry ghost realms, pressed by suffering and ignorant of the Buddha's name, practice is hard. The Buddhas in pity ever emit pure light with spiritual power to save them, but those beings, screened by karma, cannot see. Only when karma ends and the mind opens, hearing and reciting a single Buddha-name, do the hellish and ghostly scenes at once turn cool and clear. As for animals, darkened by folly, and asuras, blazing with anger, they too can hardly behold the compassionate light. As for celestials, enjoying supreme pleasures, they likewise find Buddha-recitation hard to practice.

But examine closely: none are more resistant to Pure Land practice than the celestial immortals. It is not that they do not know the Pure Land is beautiful — but having gained spiritual powers, transformations, flight, and free appearance and disappearance in being or nonbeing, able to emit lights and conjure images of Buddhas, bodhisattvas, and adorned scenes, they suppose the joys of the Pure Land are no more than this, and will not turn toward it. They do not know they have fallen into phantasmal consciousness, and that the conjured scenes all arise from blended essence, energy, and spirit! The Buddha said: celestials who do not follow right awakening have not reached non-action; when their reward ends they fall with karma into the destinies! The true-celestial scene manifests of itself and is not forged by luyện; what is forged by luyện, being produced, cannot escape extinction. Original essence and energy lie nothing apart from original spirit, and original spirit is but another name for karmic consciousness. The great man must return to original nature, cut off spirit-consciousness, sever the root of life — why forge an original spirit within the round of karmic consciousness? Some who forge it find their powers fail midway, decaying before the kalpa-fire arrives. Even if the original spirit can be made to endure, at the great kalpa-fire not only the material body is destroyed but the immaterial body is burned as well. Do not console yourselves that your way is like space — it too is karmic consciousness in phantasmal display, which only one realized in the supreme wisdom-eye sees through. Though celestials may conjure excellent scenes, in the end they attain neither full clarity nor full rectitude. How could they compare with the Buddhas, bodhisattvas, and all the principal and dependent rewards of the Pure Land dwelling together in radiance more piercing than a hundred thousand suns? Realized followers of Buddhism see time and space as illusory, and can manifest a jeweled royal land upon a hairtip and turn the Dharma-wheel within a dustmote — a state celestials cannot match in one part of myriad. Therefore whoever deeply believes the Pure Land gate and truly practices ...

The Pure Land gate is the refuge of all sages. Once reborn in the Land of Bliss, perfecting blessings and wisdom, one returns to the Saha: meeting immortals, delivering immortals; meeting humans, delivering humans; meeting demons and ghosts, delivering demons and ghosts; meeting even creditors of enmity, delivering them too. That is truly the work of the great man! If you firmly believe the Pure Land gate and resolve upon true practice, the lotus in the jeweled pond of the Land of Bliss will bloom fresh with your name. Even now, when Buddha-recitation becomes pure, all fair scenes, Dharma-sounds, wondrous fragrances, subtle lights, and true principles flood eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, and mind. Then your very selves have already become ancient Buddhas!".

In the second month of the fiftieth year of Kangxi under the Qing, the layman recited the Buddha's name and peacefully attained rebirth at Baoshou, at sixty-two. His disciples gathered his remaining words into the Collected Works of Layman Tay Khe.

LADY VIET QUOC

Lady Viet Quoc, nee Vuong, was the wife of Prince Kinh. Prince Kinh was the uncle of Emperor Zhezong of the Song. The Lady recited the Buddha's name day and night unceasingly, and guided her maids and concubines to practice the Pure Land and seek the Land of Bliss. Among them one maid was often lazy. The Lady summoned her and said: "For one person alone you shall not ruin my rule!", and expelled her from the group.

Frightened and repentant, the maid resolved upon diligent Buddha-recitation, sparing no toil. One day she told a fellow servant: "I am about to journey far!". That night strange fragrance filled the room; without any illness she passed away. A few days later the fellow servant reported to the Lady: "Last night I dreamed the departed maid asked me to convey boundless gratitude for your grace. Thanks to your rebuke, she has been reborn in the Land of Bliss!". The Lady said: "If she can appear in my own dream, then I shall believe!". That night the Lady dreamed the departed maid came to thank her as foretold, and asked: "Can the Western Land be visited?". The maid said yes, and led the Lady flying away. In a moment they reached a vast jeweled pond, its lights blending in piercing brilliance. Within the pond, large and small lotuses of many colors and radiances intermingled, some fresh, some withered. Asked why, the maid said: "When a being in the Saha first resolves to recite the Buddha's name and seek the Land of Bliss, a lotus is conjured here. If that person practices diligently day by day, the flower grows ever larger and fresher. If midway one grows gradually lazy, the flower will wither. If practice ceases, the flower fades and vanishes. But if one earnestly recites again, another lotus springs forth. If for long one's effort never retrogresses, the pure fruit will ripen. When that person casts off the retribution body in the Saha, the spirit lodges in that lotus womb and waits until the flower opens and beholds the Buddha.". Gazing afar at a flower dais bearing one crowned with a jeweled cap, necklaced with jewels, robed and featured majestically, the Lady asked: "Who is that?". The maid said: "That is the Inactive Layman Duong Kiet, newly reborn here.". The Lady saw another in court dress seated upon a somewhat lesser lotus and asked again. The maid answered: "That is Layman Ma Vu; when his merit is complete he too shall soon be reborn here.". The Lady asked: "Where shall I be born? ...

Upon waking, the Lady remembered the dream clearly, her joy and grateful sorrow intermingled, and practiced with redoubled diligence. Past eighty, on her birthday morning, she rose early, held a small incense burner in both hands, its fragrant smoke curling, and stood solemnly facing the Avalokiteshvara Pavilion. Her maids in neat dress formed ranks and approached to offer birthday homage. But when they looked again, the Lady had already passed away.

A comment: Dharma Master An Quang said: "The workings of spirit and of good and evil karma are inconceivable! Some who create evil, while still alive in the world above, already suffer in hell with part of body and spirit. And Pure Land practitioners, though presently in the defiled land, are already partly born in the Lotus Land with part of their spirit.". The case of Ma Vu above is just so.

Women born into the Land of Bliss all turn into male bodies complete with the thirty-two excellent marks. Yet the maid appeared in female form to her companion and to Prince Kinh's Lady only as skillful manifestation, so that acquaintances might easily recognize her. Readers should understand this and not doubt.
