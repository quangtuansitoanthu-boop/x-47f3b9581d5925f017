<p><strong>Translation: Again with this mind, completely merging into the nature of empty vastness, the four limbs suddenly become like plants and trees &#8211; burned by fire or cut by knives, yet feeling nothing. The fire's light cannot burn or heat; though the flesh be flayed, it is like planing wood. This is called the sense-objects purified, the nature of the four elements repelled, inclining wholly toward purity. Temporarily gained thus, it is not sage realization. If you do not take it as sagehood, it is a good state; if you interpret it as sagehood, you fall among the deviant.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Again with this mind, completely merging into the nature of empty vastness.</strong></p>
<p>Again, when this mind completely merges into the nature of empty vastness &#8211;</p>
<p>perfectly pervasive yet empty and vast. You may say that mind exists, then say it does not &#8211; you may say that mind does not exist, yet I know it exists.</p>
<p><strong>The four limbs suddenly become like plants and trees &#8211; burned by fire or cut by knives, yet feeling nothing</strong>: if you cut your limbs with a knife or burn them with fire, there is not the least sensation of heat or discomfort. The fire's light cannot burn the body: <strong>burning the body, the body feels no heat. Though the flesh be flayed, it is like planing wood</strong> &#8211; flaying flesh from the limbs is only like stripping bark from a tree, with no pain or disturbance at all.</p>
<p>This state is called: <strong><em>the sense-objects and the nature of the four elements all purified, inclining wholly toward purity</em></strong><em>.</em></p>
<p>The sense-objects gather together, and the natures of earth, water, wind, and fire merge into one substance. <strong>Temporarily gained thus, it is not sage realization.</strong> Even so, it is only a temporary state, an incidental experience &#8211; not a permanent state, not sage realization. Do not think you have realized the sage fruition.</p>
<p><strong>If you do not take it as sagehood, it is called a good state; but if you think you have realized sagehood</strong> &#8211; becoming self-satisfied, greatly arrogant, giving rise to pride &#8211; <strong>you fall into deviant views</strong>, attacked and surrounded by a flock of evil views.</p>


<ol start="8">
<li><strong> TRANSFORMED INTO THE REALM OF THE BUDDHAS</strong></li>

<ol>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Hựu dĩ thử tâm, thành tựu thanh tịnh. Tịnh tâm công cực, hốt kiến đại địa, thập phương sơn hà, giai thành Phật quốc, cụ túc thất bảo, quang minh biến mãn. Hựu kiến hằng diệu chư Phật Như Lai, biến mãn không giới lâu điện hoa lệ. Hạ kiến địa ngục, thượng quán thiên cung, đắc vô chướng ngại. Thử danh hân yểm, ngưng tưởng nhật thâm, tưởng cửu hóa thành, phi vi thánh chứng. Bất tác thánh tâm, danh thiện cảnh giới. Nhược tác thánh giải, tức thọ quần tà.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Again with this mind, accomplishing purity &#8211; the pure mind worked to the utmost &#8211; suddenly one sees the great earth, the mountains and rivers of the ten directions, all become Buddha-lands, replete with the seven jewels, light pervading everywhere. One further sees Buddhas, Tathagatas as many as the Ganges' sands, filling empty space with splendid pavilions and palaces. Below one sees the hells; above one observes the heavenly palaces, gaining no obstruction. This is called likes and dislikes congealing thought ever deeper; long-contemplated imaginings taking form &#8211; not sage realization. If you do not take it as sagehood, it is a good state; if you interpret it as sagehood, you fall among the deviant.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Again with this mind, accomplishing purity</strong> &#8211; <strong>the pure mind worked to the utmost</strong>: focusing the mind on cultivation effort, practicing the samadhi Dharma (right concentration), reaching an extremely pure state.</p>
<p><strong>Then suddenly one sees the great earth, the mountains and rivers throughout the ten directions, all become Buddha-lands</strong>, <strong>replete with the seven jewels, light pervading everywhere.</strong></p>
<p>Every palace is adorned with the seven jewels &#8211; gold, silver, lapis lazuli, mother-of-pearl, red pearls, carnelian, coral &#8211; and these jewels emit light illuminating the ten directions.</p>
<p><strong>One further sees Buddhas, Tathagatas as many as the Ganges' sands, filling empty space with splendid pavilions and palaces. Looking down, one sees the hells.</strong></p>
<p>Looking down one sees the entire hell realms; looking <strong>above</strong> one can <strong>observe</strong> what goes on in the <strong>heavenly palaces without obstruction</strong> &#8211; able to see whatever palaces and towers one wishes to see.</p>
<p>This state is called: <strong><em>likes and dislikes congealing thought ever deeper</em></strong><em>, <strong>long-contemplated imaginings taking form.</strong></em></p>
<p>Why can you see these scenes? Because you habitually harbor liking and disliking: you wish to ascend to the heavens and Buddha-lands and hate descending into the hells.</p>
<p>You concentrate thought on these thoughts of liking and disliking, focusing on them like a hen hatching eggs, like a cat stalking a mouse, like a dragon nurturing its pearl &#8211; thinking of nothing else, only of the beautiful realms of the Buddha-lands and the suffering of falling into the hells &#8211; the mind delighting in holy, blissful places and hating places of pain and misfortune&#8230; In the end those very thoughts create this kind of state.</p>
<p><strong>That is not sage realization.</strong> Do not think you have realized the sage fruition and gained great powers in your effort.</p>
<p><strong>If you do not take it as sagehood, it is called a good state</strong> &#8211; then it is not bad. <strong>But if you think you have realized sagehood</strong> and say: &#8220;This state is truly superb &#8211; I am dwelling with the Buddhas. Indeed, I myself am a Buddha.&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>You fall into deviant views</strong>: thinking so, heavenly demons and outer paths come to draw you into their company. You say you dwell among the Buddhas? Very well &#8211; let us become friends, and we shall keep close to you.</p>


<ol start="9">
<li><strong> SEEING FAR AT NIGHT</strong></li>

<ol>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Hựu dĩ thử tâm, nghiên cứu thâm viễn, hốt ư trung dạ diêu kiến viễn phương. Thị, tỉnh, nhai hạng, thân tộc, quyến thuộc, hoặc văn kì ngữ. Thử danh bách tâm, bức cực phi xuất, cố đa cách kiến, phi vi thánh chứng. Bất tác thánh tâm, danh thiện cảnh giới. Nhược tác thánh giải tức thọ quần tà. </strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Again with this mind, investigating deep and far, suddenly at midnight one sees distant places afar: markets, wells, streets and lanes, relatives and kin, or hears their speech. This is called the mind forced to its utmost flying out, hence seeing much through obstructions &#8211; not sage realization. If you do not take it as sagehood, it is a good state; if you interpret it as sagehood, you fall among the deviant.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong> Again with this mind, investigating deep and far.</strong> When applying the mind in profound investigation, <strong>suddenly at midnight one sees distant places afar.</strong> One can suddenly see various phenomena and scenes in faraway places at midnight. <strong>Markets, wells, streets and lanes, relatives and kin, or one hears their speech.</strong> One can see marketplaces where goods are sold all along the streets, public wells where people draw water, great streets, small lanes; one can even see relatives and kin and hear the words they speak to one another &#8211; hear them conversing together.</p>
