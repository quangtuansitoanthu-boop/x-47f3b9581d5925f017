TITLE: 19. UNEXPECTED RETRIBUTION
TOMTAT: Mr. Ha's son, stubborn and violent toward his father, was in a past life a dog Mr. Ha flayed and gutted. Meeting Master Miaofa, Mr. Ha was urged to repent and teach his son — but he disbelieved, and five years later his son beat him and the family fell apart.
-----
{align:center|**UNEXPECTED RETRIBUTION**}

{align:justify|Five years ago, Mr. Ha was past 40, and his son Cun was 12. Since babyhood Cun had been utterly stubborn. Schooled, he not only played truant from study and loved fighting, but constantly skipped school. Father, mother, and teachers were all helpless before the boy. Cun's rampaging left the whole house no peace.}

{align:justify|Mr. Ha's younger sister, a devout Buddhist, knew this was a creditor-enemy come to collect its debt; she wished through the Buddha Dharma to teach the boy and lift her brother's worry. So she brought her brother to Master Miaofa for rescue — hoping too that her brother, seeing the Master's wisdom, would arouse faith in the Buddha.}

{align:justify|The Master gazed at Mr. Ha and asked:}

{align:justify|"Did you ever kill a dog?"}

{align:justify|"No — never in my life have I killed a dog!" Mr. Ha stressed.}

{align:justify|"Is that so? The dog was yellow-haired with black spots on its back; you were about 20 then," the Master said, naming the time and describing the dog in detail.}

{align:justify|Mr. Ha recalled the old days and said:}

{align:justify|"Ah — back farming in Heilongjiang, some fellow workers, long without a bite of meat, secretly stole the neighboring village's yellow dog. They caught and killed it; I only assisted — flaying, gutting, butchering it for them."}

{align:justify|"And you ate a bowl of its meat besides?"}

{align:justify|"Of course I did — I helped; how could I not eat to ease the craving!" Mr. Ha laughed.}

{align:justify|"You flayed it, then slit its belly and carved its flesh — that is the same as killing it. Now that very dog has been reborn as your son, seeking you out for revenge."}

{align:justify|Mr. Ha's face filled with indignation; he said hotly:}

{align:justify|"That is most unreasonable and wrong! First the dog should seek those who caught and killed it for revenge — that would be fair! Besides, they ate more meat than I did! Why single me out? I am not the one who killed it!"}

{align:justify|The Master explained:}

{align:justify|"It is now more than 20 years past; likely it has already revenged itself upon those others. But revenge need not mean rebirth as their child. All who joined in catching, killing, and eating the dog must sooner or later repay — all of them. Who repays first, who repays after — that is not fixed! For each person's karma and merit differ, so the time of repayment comes early or late, never alike. One of great blessing may pass this life and the next unrepaid, waiting many lives and lives more, blessings exhausted, before repaying. This is by nature unfixed!"}

{align:justify|The Master sighed and strove to warn Mr. Ha:}

{align:justify|"Alas! I know you disbelieve what I say. Today you come here also through affinity with the Buddha. I hope to lift your affliction, and so I teach you cause and effect. Your fate, your family's fate — all lie in your own hands. All can still be transformed."}

{align:justify|Pausing, the Master urged earnestly:}

{align:justify|"If you can repent the sin of killing that dog, daily recite the Universal Gate Chapter on Guanyin Bodhisattva, dedicate the merit to that dog, and teach your son with a patient heart, your son will change for the better. The more sincere you are, the faster your son will change. Conversely, if you cannot do as I say, when your son is 17 your house shall face a grave affair. And afterward your son may cause calamity and fall into prison. Ten thousand times, do not take this lightly!"}

{align:justify|Hearing this, Mr. Ha said nothing; eyes lowered, his face showed disbelief. He wholly rejected the Master's golden counsel.}

{align:justify|Time flew like an arrow; in a blink Mr. Ha's son was seventeen. Through the past years at school, the boy's wicked conduct had him expelled. Mr. Ha spent a fortune preserving his school record. But teaching him was impossible; the boy grew ever more depraved and defiant, set wholly against his father. Over him, Mr. Ha and his wife quarreled constantly, even coming to blows in an uproar.}

{align:justify|Five years later, his family was like a battlefield, not a day of peace. This spring, on New Year's Eve night, husband and wife quarreled over their son and came to full blows. The mother called the boy to help. Cun, now 1.8 meters tall and surging with vigor, heard his mother's cry, rushed over, seized his father's legs, threw him sprawling, and pinned his father's legs fast while his mother beat him to her heart's content — until he passed out, when they called an ambulance to the hospital.}

{align:justify|In the end Mr. Ha divorced; his son went with the mother.}

{align:justify|What happened after, I do not know. I truly pity Mr. Ha: five years earlier, had he heeded the Master's counsel, he might have spared himself and little Cun these evils.}

{align:justify|On this occasion I asked in wonder:}

{align:justify|"Venerable Master, please explain why a dog's body?"}

{align:justify|The Master sighed and answered:}

{align:justify|"Man is honored as the most spiritual of all creatures — knowing to sew clothes to wear, crafting every material civilization to serve his life. If as a man his character and conduct unfit him for the human station — ever acting lawlessly and against the Way, ever plotting to harm others, losing his human dignity — in the end he shall change head and face and live a beast's life."}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story UNEXPECTED RETRIBUTION is a costly lesson in "disbelieving cause and effect — retribution arriving after 5 years." Five years earlier Master Miaofa had stated plainly that Mr. Ha's son was the past life of the dog he flayed and gutted — but Mr. Ha disbelieved, never repented, never taught his son rightly. Five years later, exactly as foretold, his 17-year-old son beat his father senseless; the family shattered in divorce. The story affirms: "Karmic retribution never errs — only early or late." Parents with stubborn, depraved children, examine yourselves for what creatures you once harmed, and early repent, recite sutras in dedication, and teach your children with loving patience — the best way to transform karma for the whole house.}
