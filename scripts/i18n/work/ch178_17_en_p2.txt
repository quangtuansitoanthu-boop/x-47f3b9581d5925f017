{align:justify|THE EARLY-ARRIVING GUEST}
{align:justify|Tomorrow is Tomb-Sweeping Day; since early spring I have bustled without rest, strength utterly spent. This afternoon, teaching the Dharma to Buddhist friends, I grew so weak my voice failed—truly I crave rest, but the dream seems out of reach. Tonight, sleepless, I decided to rise and recite the early-morning liturgy.}
{align:justify|As always, I drew the curtain, opened the window… one look outside and my limbs went weak with fright: though still deep night, the northeastern sky glows brighter than cities'. Unprepared, I suddenly beheld towering before me a female ghost, long hair loose, feet off the ground (her face unseen, wholly veiled by hair). Ordinarily I would not fear—but today, so sudden, I gasped, mouth agape in shock.}
{align:justify|Composing myself, I asked the precept-protecting spirits what this was; they explained:}
{align:justify|"This girl often comes here, quite long now—never discovered till today; perhaps affinities link you."}
{align:justify|Since daily we recite sutras dedicating to all beings, perhaps the ghost came to hear the early liturgy?}
{align:justify|The spirits told me: to commune with this ghost, please settle your mind and speak heart to heart with her.}
{align:justify|So I settled, closed my eyes, communing heart to heart… I asked the ghost in thought:}
{align:justify|"Why appear and startle me?"}
{align:justify|Scarcely asked, the ghost burst into tears; understanding nothing, not knowing how to stop her weeping, I reached to touch and soothe her. Never having touched a ghost-realm being, the touch met hollow emptiness—I marveled, dazed, not knowing how to comfort her.}
{align:justify|Seeing my discomfiture, the ghost had to laugh, saying: I know your name, and know you ever recite sutras praying for all beings. I have listened a month now; today I meant to arrive a little early—never dreaming you would rise earlier still and discover me! Exhausted as you are, daily laboring to recite sutras praying for us—deepest thanks!…}
{align:justify|Pausing, the ghost told me:}
{align:justify|"Please recite 'Nam Mô Diệu Sắc Thân Như Lai' and at once my form shall turn fair, frightening you no more. For fearing my ghostly shape would sow terror, I meant my long hair to veil my face… please also keep your distance from me, for our kind bears heavy yin energy that would harm you—what I wholly wish not…"}
{align:justify|I did as she asked, palms joined, sincerely reciting "Nam Mô Diệu sắc Thân Như Lai" ten times. A miracle appeared: the ghost turned beautiful, hair neatly pinned—I could see her face clearly: most lovely.}
{align:justify|The ghost begged me hereafter to recite many Buddhas' names—it greatly benefits beings. Then she told me she was born locally, dying at nineteen leaping from a building. She said she is now a lonely wronged soul—suicide barring rebirth—abiding in the yin realm suffering, a ghost drifting vagrant through the human world.}
{align:justify|I asked: Why did you not fall into hell?}
{align:justify|She answered:}
{align:justify|"My evil karma deserved not hell. Yet though I went not to hell, the torments I taste differ little from hell. For daily, exactly at the hour I killed myself, I must replay it identically—meaning I must keep standing atop the building, leaping to suicide, tasting death's utmost agony. Ten years now I have performed this leap—every day replaying suicide so, misery past telling—besides a wretched ghostly existence contrary to all wishes, vagrant hunger and thirst, a hundred million times worse than being human. Had I known, I would never have killed myself!"}
{align:justify|The ghost told how ten years before, after her parents divorced, the court placed her with her mother. But her mother remarried—so she gained a stepfather. Then the depraved stepfather raped her; she told her birth mother, who not only disbelieved but drove her from the house. So in fury she leapt from her own building to suicide… She even gave her home's exact address, saying if I disbelieved I might investigate and verify!}
{align:justify|She said:}
{align:justify|"Is not your disciple's house near mine? Only eleven doors from my mother's."}
{align:justify|(That afternoon I sent a disciple to investigate—indeed her mother's house was found; neighbors confirmed it all true).}
{align:justify|The ghost further told how she avenged herself on mother and stepfather—giving her stepfather a malignant cancer, forcing her mother's divorce. Weeping as she told it, she lamented: why, appearing in her mother's dreams begging sutra recitation, did her mother disbelieve?}
{align:justify|She told me she set wild ghosts to torment her mother—body uneasy, day and night wretched… she wants her mother to believe in cause and effect, repent her own sins, and hereafter escape misery…}
{align:justify|I was moved by her trust, thanking her for the truth. My heart ached to see her so pitiable. Barely nineteen—why so miserable…}
{align:justify|I urged her to let go of resentment and promised to send a disciple to find her mother. Explaining my poor health, I wished to rest at home. I would have a disciple contact her mother—for strangers to her, telling plainly her plight and wishes, surely her mother would understand, believe, and see cause and effect clearly.}
{align:justify|Hearing it, she rejoiced, adding that she has listened to my Ksitigarbha Sutra recitation a month now—in six months more she shall be born into heaven, to a most exquisite place…}
{align:justify|She added: people of this world are as foolish now as I once was! Granted human bodies, most never cherish them. So you must write it out, picturing my misery plainly for all to know—urging them never to suicide, to understand suicide's retribution, dreadful and agonizing past imagining! A human body is most rare, yet unknown—if deludedly abandoning it and sinking into ghosthood, misery multiplies ten thousandfold! This torment is past telling. Luckily, through your and others' Ksitigarbha Sutra recitations dedicated to lonely wronged souls, I have the blessed fortune to listen—and future heaven…}
{align:justify|Finished, she told me:}
{align:justify|"The cock crows—I must go at once, for I cannot face the sun!"}
{align:justify|The ghost gone, my heart grew heavy, thinking: "People today believe only what eyes see—wholly unaware that bodily ills come from karmic creditors seeking revenge… Daily they enter hospitals, never knowing at home to recite sutras, release captive animals, repent, sincerely regret… purifying body, speech, mind, turning good, redeeming committed sins… And even practicing, after a few rounds with no visible effect, they hastily conclude: the Buddha deceives—then abandon all practice! Why will they not consider their own countless lifetimes' sins? People habitually grasp in, never give out!"…}
{align:justify|Now I am most weary of communing. For plain-spoken, I easily offend many. But do you know? Few today speak true words!}
{align:justify|I write with no other intent—only saying: constructive words perfecting your character deserve patient hearing and heed. Tomorrow is Tomb-Sweeping Day; people will burn paper money along roads in plenty—but I feel no joy! Do you know why? I think: instead of burning paper, recite many Ksitigarbha Sutras dedicated to the departed—then ancestors and kin gain great benefit! If you can reform yourselves—vegetarianism, doing good, releasing captive animals, giving, reciting sutras… dedicating the merit to kin of many lives, dissolving karma, delivering all departed kin—this is true great filial piety!}
{align:justify|Writing thus, suddenly I heard the old man downstairs had died—his children showing "filial piety" by hiring a whole band: drums and instruments blaring, old songs and new…}
{align:justify|My eyes suddenly moistened: 'Heavens, when shall people understand and practice true filial piety?" It seems tonight's blaring band shall harass me and the whole block all night!}
{align:justify|Chan Ngu}
{align:justify|Translator's note: Once, hearing Master Hsuan Hua teach that suicides must daily replay their suicide, I doubted, disbelieving—but now this story sets me pondering…. Moreover, the sutras say: "The Buddhas' names bear wondrous supreme power, drawn by Their perfect practice-merit and great vows." We in the human realm, wishing to verify, each knows personally—unwidespread, little understood.}
{align:justify|Translating to where the ghost asked to recite "Nam Mô Diệu Sắc Thân Như Lai"—one of seven Buddhas named in the food-bestowal liturgy for the departed—her face at once turned fair—proving the ancient worthies compiling this liturgy understood the holy names' mystery: in the human realm we see no transformation so plain and swift as in the ghost realm—meaning when repentance assemblies are held, invisible beings come hearing sutras, giving rise to joy, wholesomeness, thereby transforming fair in form; hungry ghosts are fed, even reborn at once into good destinies, turning evil minds to good. So ancient sages often raised repentance platforms praying for the living above, the dead below, on shores and waters…}
{align:justify|But others praying for you never equals practicing yourself! The sutras explain: if others pray or create blessings for you, the practitioner enjoys six shares of blessing, the dedicatee but one—and scriptures ever remind: each should practice themselves to reap practice's full fruit.}
{align:justify|The "Ksitigarbha Sutra" is confirmed as a sutra swiftly dissolving sins and karma, swiftly growing merit—through Ksitigarbha's great vows' might. Yet some gravely ill, releasing captive animals, doing good, reciting up to 108 rounds, still saw no result. Asked, Quả Khanh explained: "The reciter still eats meat. So meat-eaters' recitation shall not work! Abstain from the five pungents, keep vegetarianism and precepts, guard body, speech, mind pure, and recite with settled single-minded mind—results come swiftly." Readers may test and verify this themselves.}
