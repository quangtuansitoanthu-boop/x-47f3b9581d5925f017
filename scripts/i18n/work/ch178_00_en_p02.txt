{align:justify|Returning to Đan Lương's house, Chân Minh dared not stay—hearing the procuratorate was sending men sniffing after him. So he hid in a remote land, dwelling in a wild hut, guarding the district. Đan Lương visited daily with food. No passersby here—especially clear and quiet. Eight straight months Chân Minh dwelled here; besides sleep and sitting meditation, he memorized and solely recited the Shurangama Mantra—living exactly like a monastic in sealed retreat: vegetarianism, Buddha-recitation, Shurangama Mantra recitation (guided and watched by Master Diệu Pháp). Nights, no electric light—not even an oil lamp lit.}
{align:justify|Practicing cross-legged sitting, past forty, thick-legged, big-bellied, folding his legs proved most hard; failing, he fretted greatly.}
{align:justify|One night, struggling to sit cross-legged, suddenly he saw a fat monk sitting opposite, teaching him how to sit… saying:}
{align:justify|"You are too fat—sit like me!"}
{align:justify|So he imitated the fat monk—right thigh atop, left leg folded below. Both hands face down, relaxed naturally on both thighs, eyes lightly closed…}
{align:justify|"Oh! Night has turned blue sky—all around vast, silent, soundless… where am I? Why can I no longer see my body? Yet thoughts remain—could… I be dead?"}
{align:justify|A little tense, Chân Minh hastily opened his eyes—dawn already graying. He plainly saw himself still sitting—but the fat monk nowhere… "Was I dreaming?"… Chân Minh looked down, checking—his legs still crossed exactly as the fat monk taught.}
{align:justify|"I was not dreaming—yet dawn has come?… Truly night had barely fallen when I began sitting—then the fat monk came teaching me to sit—only a moment?… But now dawn? Could… I… have sat all night? Yet my spirit is most alert, most well?"…}
{align:justify|That morning Chân Minh pondered endlessly, wondering on—till Đan Lương brought rice; he hastily told the night's tale. Đan Lương said:}
{align:justify|"This region has no monk like you describe!"}
{align:justify|Next noon Đan Lương brought rice, plus Buddha images for Chân Minh to view. One glance, and Chân Minh recognized the foremost seated Buddha, crying:}
{align:justify|"Right—exactly this monk's looks—last night 'he' taught me sitting just so."}
{align:justify|Đan Lương told Chân Minh:}
{align:justify|"This is Maitreya Bodhisattva—the future Buddha, also called Maitreya Buddha. Your practice is no small thing, moving Maitreya Buddha to teach you so… In future you shall surely attain."}
{align:justify|Chân Minh recalled: last night Maitreya Buddha called him Hang Van, saying it was his Dharma name… He asked his friend:}
{align:justify|"What is a Dharma name?"}
{align:justify|Đan Lương explained:}
{align:justify|"A Dharma name is given to refuge-taking disciples—perhaps in a former life you were a monastic, for you sit most well. Sooner or later you shall understand all!"}
{align:justify|Months after, whenever Chân Minh sat, all states appeared—here unwritable in full (but he never clung nor grew proud—ever mindful the "Shurangama Sutra" teaches: "Claiming sainthood, one surely strays deviant"…}
{align:justify|Eight months done, Đan Lương reported: a transport company director needs one able to dwell long-term in Shanxi managing coal distribution. Monthly wage one thousand—so Chân Minh hastened to take the job.}
{align:justify|This thousand, to him, was truly "sleepy meeting the mat"—luck for the afflicted—for he could aid his family with it.}
{align:justify|Chân Minh was a most filial son—eight months never filial to his parents a single coin. Had Master Diệu Pháp not urged him "let go of all conditions," devoting himself to memorizing the Shurangama Mantra, his heart would still brim with cares.}
{align:justify|In a blink three years passed—three years Chân Minh worked steadily—every three or four days dispatching trucks, checking machines, hauling goods, collecting freight—good coal quality sufficing.}
{align:justify|Besides three daily meals, all free time he sat "Maitreya-style," ever fluently reciting the Shurangama Mantra. Master Diệu Pháp required him daily to recite forty, fifty rounds. Nights he sat—hours at a sitting…}
{align:justify|One day the owner told Chân Minh that between him and the plant a conflict arose—they would call his haulage no more; Chân Minh should go settle accounts at the plant—then their contract ends.}
{align:justify|Unexpectedly, meeting Chân Minh, the plant owner wished to work with him—inviting him to keep supplying the plant. Chân Minh hastily said he had no capital… but the owner said: guarantee coal quality, and monthly they would pay his wage. Chân Minh sincerely wished to cooperate—the plant needed a trusted partner, and trusted him. Though capital-less, Chân Minh could work most smoothly with people (not all in this trade meet such luck)—for Chân Minh is good-natured, honest—diligent, entrusting all to diligent practice and the Buddhas' protection.}
{align:justify|The Shurangama Sutra teaches: "The Tathagatas of the ten directions, according to this mantra's mind, skillfully abide in the ten directions, rescuing all miseries—so hells', hungry ghosts', animals' miseries, blind, deaf, mute miseries, hatred-meeting misery, love-parting misery, wish-unfulfilled misery, five-heaps' blazing misery, misfortunes great and small, hunger, thirst, poverty—through this mantra's recitation dissolve… The Tathagatas of the ten directions ever guard practitioners, granting them in the four deportments all provisions as wished"…}
{align:justify|Time passing, when Chân Minh held a two-hundred-thousand check, he went to the bank wishing to repay—bank staff moved, inviting him to tea, saying feelingly:}
{align:justify|"Your great loan—we assumed total loss—never dreaming you would come repaying—deepest thanks!"}
{align:justify|Chân Minh said: At present I can repay principal—interest later. I cannot let the nation lose—please grant me more time…}
{align:justify|Bank staff hastily said:}
{align:justify|"Oh! Granted! Granted!"}
{align:justify|Since taking refuge under Master Diệu Pháp, Chân Minh's wife, children, and parents all became vegetarian Buddha-reciters. Mother- and daughter-in-law relations, long poor, thereby greatly improved.}
{align:justify|Chân Minh's wife confided to me:}
{align:justify|"Since understanding Buddhism, when my mother-in-law angers me, I quarrel no more—but quietly cycle to the fields, gazing at wind combing rice waves… vexations dissolve with them… Each time Chân Minh calls from Shanxi, he ever reminds me to recite diligently, telling how earnestly he practices…"}
{align:justify|Chân Minh's wife went on:}
{align:justify|"Since practicing, whether cycling or transplanting… whatever I do, my heart ever recites: 'Nam mô A Di Đà Phật!' Off work, homeward, walking the road I still: 'Nam mô A Di Đà Phật!' None around, I recite louder—my heart truly peaceful and blissful… home to eat, no more carrying my bowl out gossiping with neighbors—for after eating I must still recite the Buddha's name, ever feeling too little practicing time—so idle talk ended…"}
{align:justify|Many, seeing my house daily prosper, crowd asking what we did, poor turned rich?… Puzzled, I say:}
{align:justify|"'Through Buddhism'!"}
{align:justify|Chân Minh's wife tells:}
{align:justify|"Now more and more imitate our recitation—for half a year ago a villager fell witless (a ghost possessing)—all day muttering nonsense, rampaging… I led my son to see; just entering the courtyard we heard him indoors shouting. Mother and son through the door—and the patient's manner instantly changed—he grew most gentle, honest—staring fixedly at mother and son.}
{align:justify|Someone asked him:}
{align:justify|"Why no more rampaging and racket?…"}
{align:justify|He said: These two just entered, bodies wholly covered in the Bodhisattvas' blessing golden light—so I dare not rampage.}
{align:justify|Chân Minh's son said:}
{align:justify|"Then ghost, please leave here?!"}
{align:justify|The possessing being said:}
{align:justify|"Untaught by you, how dare we leave!"}
{align:justify|So Chân Minh's son told him:}
{align:justify|"Then friend, recite 'Nam mô A Di Đà Phật!'…"}
{align:justify|The being, memorizing this one line, said:}
{align:justify|"Then I may go take rebirth!"}
{align:justify|Finished, the patient dropped unconscious as if sleeping; family carried him to bed. A sleep done, next morning the patient woke—remembering nothing before—spirit normal again.}
{align:justify|The matter spread through the whole village—people telling: "Ghosts fear vegetarian Buddha-reciters"—so they flocked seeking Chân Minh's wife, crowding to learn vegetarian Buddha-recitation.}
{align:justify|Idle farming seasons, from Chân Minh's house often ring many people's sincere sutra and Shurangama Mantra recitations.}

{align:justify|"Inviting Buddha statues must be done before eleven! Else, eye-opened or not, not only no blessing—but calamity befalls!…"}
{align:justify|The result: within days my bicycle was stolen. Evenings still I fear—not knowing what calamities henceforth may strike? So I come asking you—how to escape disaster?}
{align:justify|Answer: If "carrying Buddha statues home at wrong hours draws calamity upon families" were true—who would dare enshrine Buddhas? Laypeople, never trumpet such muddled theories misleading people! Spreading deluded, witless, ignorant claims makes evil teachers! Using such false theories guiding people defies cause and effect!}
