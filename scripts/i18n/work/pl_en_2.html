HOANG TE

Great Master Hoang Te, of the Yao family, styled Dongzhou, was a native of Yuyao. As a small child he already wore his hair in tufts while dwelling at Baoji Temple in his village. At sixteen he fully shaved his head and received the novice precepts, and at the age of twenty he received the complete precepts.

The Master strictly observed the precepts and studied the Tiantai doctrinal meditation under Dharma Master Benshan Quan. Before long his study had reached a point of penetrating mastery. He regularly practiced repentance rites such as those of the Pure Land, the Lotus Sutra, and the Golden Light Sutra. One day in meditation, he saw the Venerable Siming present him with a wish-fulfilling rhinoceros-horn scepter, after which his eloquence flowed forth like a gushing spring.

Under the Yuan dynasty, in the first year of the Taiding era, he was invited to preside over Yuanjue Temple in Wanshou county. The following year, in the nearby county of Yanguan, violent storms arose and the sea dikes were about to be breached by the waves; the people of the whole region trembled in fear. The Grand Chancellor Toghon also grew anxious and had the local officials earnestly invite the Master to the sea dikes to establish a great Water and Land assembly for prayer. When he arrived, he entered the Loving-Kindness samadhi, recited the Great Compassion Mantra over sea sand, and scattered it along the embankment. Wherever his feet tread, the dikes were made firm, and the wind and waves gradually calmed until they ceased entirely. Everyone praised it as miraculous! Afterward the Master successively presided over the great monasteries of Jiqing, Xianci, and Binjiang. In old age he returned to Baoji Temple in his homeland and devoted himself to the Buddha-Recitation samadhi.

In the sixteenth year of the Zhizheng era, on the third day of the tenth month, the Master felt slightly ill. He summoned his disciples and instructed them on the meaning of the Mind-Only Pure Land. When some in the assembly did not yet understand, he raised his voice and sternly rebuked them, saying: "The shore of birth and death is most perilous and affords no place to rest in peace. Why are you still not awakened?". Having spoken, he joined his palms, recited the Buddha's name, and passed away.

DE QING

Great Master De Qing, styled Chengyin, in later life took the name Old Man Hanshan. He was of the Cai family of Jinling. His mother dreamed that Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva carried a young boy in her arms and presented him to her, and thus she conceived him. When he was born, he was wrapped in two white coverings. At nineteen he left the household life and devoted himself to Buddha-recitation. One night the Master dreamed that Amitabha appeared in the sky toward the setting sun. The Buddha's features were clear, radiant, and majestic, and from then on that vision often appeared before him. He then went to Mount Wutai, where through meditation he awakened to his original mind, and vowed to copy the Avatamsaka Sutra in his own blood, reciting the Buddha's name with every stroke of the brush. After long practice, stillness and activity became as one.

In the tenth year of the Wanli era of the Ming dynasty, the Master settled at Mount Lao. The Empress Dowager Li, hearing of his virtue and admiring him, provided funds to have a monastery built for him there, bestowing the plaque Haiyin Temple. At that time the Empress Dowager often sent eunuch officials to build and repair temples and stupas in various places. Among the powerful there were those who bore a grudge against the eunuch officials and plotted with a group of Taoist priests of the Eastern Depot to beat drums and submit accusations, claiming that he had seized Taoist cloisters and converted them into Buddhist temples. The affair implicated the Master: he was stripped of his monastic robes and exiled to Leizhou. When bidding farewell to the assembly, he remained serene and wrote a verse expressing his resolve:

The kasaya cast off, exchanged for common dress,

Wherever I may go is a place of the Way!

Though I meet conditions like burning fire,

How could a heart of ice and snow melt away?

Wherever he went, the Master still expounded the Dharma in lay dress, and vowed to propagate the Mahayana sutras, composing commentaries on works such as the Shurangama, the Lankavatara, and the Complete Enlightenment Sutra. In the forty-second year of Wanli, he received an imperial pardon restoring him to the monastic order. On his way back, passing Mount Lu, he delighted in its pure and secluded scenery and built a hut beneath Five-Nipple Peak. Following the example of Master Huiyuan, he dug a lotus pond, divided the hours of the day for Pure Land practice, and cultivated with great diligence.

At that time a Zen practitioner of Haiyang, while seeking to receive the precepts, asked about the essential meaning of the Pure Land. The Master said: "The World Honored One taught many skillful means of practice for leaving the round of birth and death and advancing toward Buddhahood. Foremost among them is the gate of reciting the Buddha's name and seeking rebirth in the Pure Land, which the Buddha taught unasked by anyone — enough to show how momentous it is! This gate embraces all three capacities and gathers in all four assemblies; it is not a provisional device set up only for those of dull capacity. The sutra says: 'To purify the Buddha-land, first purify your own mind!' Therefore Pure Land practitioners must take the purification of the mind as their foundation. To purify the mind, what is most necessary is to keep the precepts pure and undefiled. Though the precepts are many, in essence they are nothing beyond the three karmas of body, the four karmas of speech, and the three karmas of mind. Keeping these ten karmas pure and wholesome is the true cause for avoiding the three evil destinies. With a mind thus purified by keeping the precepts, give rise to compassion, renounce the filth and suffering of the Saha world, vow to be reborn in the Land of Bliss in order swiftly to attain the fruit of the Way and deliver oneself and all sentient beings — and establish Buddha-recitation as the principal practice. The manner of Buddha-recitation may follow each person's capacity and circumstances, but it must be done with a true mind and true practice to bring genuine results!".

A layman named Pure Mind came to request instruction and asked: "Why are there so many people who recite the Buddha's name without diligence and find it hard to bring it into a single unbroken mass?". The Master instructed him: "The foremost essential of practice is an earnest resolve to escape the suffering of birth, death, and rebirth. Without an earnest concern for this, how could one recite the Buddha's name diligently and bring it into a single mass? Since beginningless kalpas, sentient beings have let every thought follow deluded fancy, and the root of attachment runs deep. Even in the human realm there is little joy and much suffering, while in the round of rebirth, birth in the human and heavenly realms is as rare as earth on a fingernail, and falling into the three evil destinies is as common as earth upon the great ground! The ancients said: 'Once fallen into the three destinies for a thousand myriad kalpas, when will a human body be regained?' If in this life one does not grieve deeply over the suffering of birth, death, and rebirth, but lets every thought follow defiled emotion, and hopes with a shallow faith to recite the Buddha's name and gain liberation, it is like hoping to save a thousand cartloads of blazing fire with a single ladle of water! Practicing in this way, one may fear that once this human body is lost it will be hard to regain, and a moment's negligence will be regretted for long ages! Therefore resolve upon diligence, use Buddha-recitation to break through myriad deluded thoughts, and in every time and place keep the Buddha's name present before you. Determinedly applying such earnest and painful effort, in time it will become ripe and harmonious, and the recitation will naturally form a single mass. All this depends on one's own reflection and wholehearted exertion. If the Buddha's name is made a mere outward form, there is surely no hope of attaining rebirth and liberation!".

After some years at Mount Lu, the Master moved to Caoxi. In the tenth month of the third year of the Tianqi era, he felt slightly ill and told others: "This old monk's worldly karma is nearly exhausted!". He then bathed, burned incense, sat upright, and passed away. At that moment a dazzling light filled the sky. The Master lived seventy-eight years.

DI XIAN

Dharma Master Di Xian, whose Dharma name was Gu Xu and style Zhuosan, of the Zhou family, was a native of Huangyan in Zhejiang province. Endowed with planted wisdom and complete wholesome roots, at twenty he left the household life at Baiyun Temple in Linhai county. Two years later he received the complete precepts at Guoqing Temple on Mount Tiantai.

From then on he studied meditation in winter and the teachings in summer, practicing the Way with diligence. He drew close to eminent elders and was especially intimate with Old Man Minxi. He once attended a lecture series on the Lotus Sutra, and before hearing it through had already grasped the subtle meaning of the Three Truths and three contemplations. He was then asked to take the small seat and repeat the lecture; his exposition was profound and his reasoning penetrating, so that his fellow students were all startled and admiring. Venerable Min also sighed in praise, calling him a mighty pillar of the Dharma gate. At twenty-eight, the Master opened a Lotus Sutra assembly at Liutong Temple in Hangzhou. When expounding the passage on "opening the Buddha's knowledge and vision," he suddenly entered samadhi and sat in silence. A moment later he emerged from meditation and continued; his eloquence was unobstructed, answering objections and resolving doubts like water pouring from a vase, his speech opening and closing with a freedom none could restrain. From then on he regularly took the propagation of the Dharma and the benefit of beings as his responsibility. Fearing that much wisdom and little meditative stability would obstruct the Way, he sealed himself in retreat at the Holy Fruit Hermitage of Cixi Temple, studying the Mahayana scriptures in his spare hours.

Three years later, upon emerging from retreat, he at once accepted an invitation to lecture on the Lotus Sutra at Longhua Temple in Shanghai. When the series was finished he returned to Jinshan Monastery to practice Zen, then went to Guoqing Temple to practice contemplation. Through that period his understanding grew ever more profound and subtle, and Patriarch Shi Duanrong chose him as the forty-third generation heir of the Tiantai school. For more than twenty years afterward he responded to unceasing invitations to lecture everywhere, seldom idle, holding Dharma assemblies numbering in the thousands. Each day the Master recited the Diamond Sutra, the Complete Enlightenment Sutra, the Contemplation of Immeasurable Life Sutra, and the Vows of Samantabhadra, and recited the Buddha's name more than ten thousand times as his daily course. On the full moon and new moon he additionally recited the Brahma Net Sutra.

In a lifetime of teaching, he restored Tou Tuo Temple in Wenzhou, Wannian Temple on Mount Tiantai, Guanzong Temple in Ningbo, and Fantian Temple in Hangzhou. He also founded a Buddhist academy to train monastic talent and established pure cloisters for the assembly to practice. Guanzong Temple was the Master's permanent residence. His writings and instructions were numerous: below, they accorded with the capacities of his hearers; above, they deeply agreed with the Buddha's truth, so that monastics and laity alike received moistening benefit. His method of instruction expounded Tiantai doctrine while practicing Pure Land conduct, so that whenever he lectured on the sutras he always concluded by directing his hearers to Buddha-recitation. The Master himself truly practiced in that same way, perfect in both understanding and conduct, admired by all and honored as the restorer of the Tiantai teaching of his time. In matters of public benefit and charity he was likewise generous in lending his aid to their success.

In the spring and summer of the Xinwei year of the Republic, the Master lectured at Jade Buddha Temple in Shanghai. He also responded to the invitation of the laymen of Wuxi by expounding Master Jing'an's text on arousing the bodhi-mind.
Being advanced in age, oppressed by hot weather, and wearied by excessive Buddhist duties, the Master fell ill after the lecture series. Upon returning to his home temple, his spirits and appetite declined day by day. He therefore cut off all involvements and resolved to devote himself to Buddha-recitation, seeking rebirth. In the summer of the Renshen year, he entrusted the procedures and regulations of Guanzong Temple in full, designating his disciple Baojing to continue the work of propagation.

On the second day of the seventh month, before noon, the Master suddenly joined his palms, faced west, and sat quietly for a moment, then said: "Amitabha has come to escort me; this old man now bids you farewell!". He then asked his attendants to prepare fragrant water for his bath. After changing into fresh clean robes and donning his kasaya, he asked to be helped to the main hall to venerate the Buddha, then asked to be assisted down and to sit in lotus posture within the jeweled shrine. Next, he had the assembly recite the Buddha's name in unison. He spoke a brief parting verse:

Through Buddha-recitation,

The Pure Land stands before me.

Truly receiving its benefit,

Strive on diligently, all of you!

At one hour and three quarters past noon, he peacefully passed away with a smile amid the supporting recitation of the great assembly. The Master's countenance appeared very bright and fresh, and some hours later the crown of his head was still warm. This was the second day of the seventh month in the twenty-first year of the Republic (1932). The Master lived seventy-five years, with fifty-five monastic years. His remains were enshrined by his disciples in a stupa at Cixi, Mount Wulei.
