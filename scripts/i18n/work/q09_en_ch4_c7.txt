<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhĩ thời thiên ma, hậu đắc kì tiện, phi tinh phụ nhân, khẩu thuyết kinh pháp.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Then the heavenly demon, waiting for its convenient chance, sends its essence to possess another person, using that person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Then the heavenly demon gets its convenient chance.</strong> At that time the heavenly demon notices the cultivator's samadhi power is about to be perfected and grows jealous. Gaining a convenient chance, it sends its descendants to watch for lapses &#8211; the moment it sees an opening, <strong>sending its essence to possess another person, using the possessed person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Kì nhân nguyên bất giác tri ma trước, diệc ngôn tự đắc vô thượng niết-bàn. Lai bỉ cầu ứng thiện nam tử xứ, phu tòa thuyết pháp. Năng linh thính chúng, tạm kiến kì thân, như bách thiên tuế. Tâm sanh ái nhiễm, bất năng xả li. Thân vi nô bộc, tứ sự cúng dường, bất giác bì lao. Các các linh kì, tòa hạ nhân tâm, tri thị tiên sư, bổn thiện tri thức, biệt sanh pháp ái, niêm như giao tất, đắc vị tằng hữu.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: That person, originally unaware of demonic possession, likewise claims to have attained unsurpassed nirvana. Coming to the place of the good man seeking response, it spreads its seat and preaches the Dharma &#8211; able to make its listeners briefly see its body as a hundred thousand years old. Their minds giving rise to fond defilement, unable to leave it; their bodies serving as servants, offering the four requisites without ever tiring. Each beneath its seat is made to know in mind: this is my former teacher, my original good friend &#8211; giving rise to a special love for the Dharma, sticking like glue and lacquer, gaining what never was.</strong></p>
<p>Commentary: <strong>That person, originally unaware of being possessed, likewise claiming to have realized unsurpassed nirvana. The demon comes to the place of the one seeking mysterious response, spreading its seat, preaching the Dharma.</strong></p>
<p>The good man possessed by the demon, <strong>in a moment, making its listeners see its body as a hundred thousand years old.</strong></p>
<p>You should know that perception is momentary: when someone in the assembly sees him with the gray-haired head of an old man upon a child's body, they think him a veteran cultivator, an elder bhikshu.</p>
<p><strong>Their minds giving rise to fond defilement, unable to leave him</strong>: this impure fondness &#8211; entanglement and defilement &#8211; once arisen as love, brings turbidity; with defilement, purity is lost.</p>
<p>All who come to hear the demon lecture want to follow it day and night. Why? They are enchanted by its particular attraction. <strong>Their bodies serving as servants, offering it the four requisites without ever tiring</strong>.</p>
<p>The four offerings are: 1. food and drink, 2. clothing, 3. bedding and sitting gear, 4. medicines. They never feel bored or weary offering these things to the demon. Some wonder why they act so: a genuine cultivator, a true disciple of the Buddha, would never draw near and make offerings so. Only because the heavenly demon has a particular attractive, enchanting power making everyone like to stay near it.</p>
<p><strong>The assembly</strong> beneath the Dharma seat <strong>believes that person (the demon) is their teacher from a past life</strong>: it creates such confusion that everyone believes the possessed person is their good friend from a past life, thinking: &#8220;He was my teacher in a past life.&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>They give rise to a special love for their Dharma master, sticking to him like glue and lacquer</strong>, feeling <strong>as gaining what never was</strong> &#8211; thinking: never before was it so.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Thị nhân ngu mê, hoặc vi bồ-tát. Thân cận kì tâm phá Phật luật nghi, tiềm hành  tham dục.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: That person, deluded, mistakes it for a Bodhisattva; drawing near its mind, breaking the Buddha's moral code, secretly practicing greed and lust.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>That person, deluded</strong> to the extreme, <strong>deeming it</strong> one able to transform itself, hence surely a Buddha or a Bodhisattva. Fox-spirits and other kinds of demons and ghosts can all transform. But he cannot think through that one skillful in transformation is not necessarily a Bodhisattva or a Buddha. What good roots have you planted that you now expect to meet Buddhas and Bodhisattvas?</p>
<p><strong>Drawing near the demon's mind</strong>: the person draws near the demon-possessed one. <strong>Breaking the Buddha's moral code</strong>: this is a most important point &#8211; how do we know the person is a demon? We can state at once: it has broken the Buddha's precepts &#8211; and <strong>secretly practices lustful acts.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Khẩu trung hảo ngôn, ngã ư tiền thế, ư mỗ sanh trung, tiên độ mỗ nhân, đương thời thị ngã, thê thiếp huynh đệ,  kim lai tương độ, dữ nhữ tương tùy, quy mỗ thế giới, cúng dường mỗ Phật. Hoặc ngôn biệt hữu, đại quang minh thiên, Phật ư trung trụ,nhất thiết Như Lai, sở hưu cư địa. Bỉ vô tri giả, tín  thị hư cuống, di thất bản tâm.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Its mouth delights in saying: &#8220;In a past life, in such a life, I already saved such a person &#8211; then my wife, concubines, brothers &#8211; now come again to save one another; I shall go with you to follow one another to such a world, to make offerings to such a Buddha.&#8221; Or saying there is apart a Great Bright Light Heaven where Buddhas dwell &#8211; the resting dwelling of all Tathagatas. The unknowing believe such empty lies, losing their inherent minds.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: Whenever meeting someone, the <strong>demon delights in saying: &#8220;In a past life I already saved such a person &#8211; then my wife, or my brother &#8211; now come again to save one another</strong> &#8211;</p>
<p>Like the story of Liu Jin-tong I told before: she often told her victims they had been her children in a past life and her husbands in another past life. Now this one says:</p>
<p>&#8220;In a past life, in such a life &#8211; sometimes in the hundred-and-first or hundred-and-second life &#8211;</p>
<p>I already saved such a person &#8211; then my wife or my brother.&#8221; So it speaks. &#8220;Now I come to save one another; I shall go with you inseparably. <strong>I shall go with you to follow one another to such a world, to make offerings to such a Buddha.</strong></p>
<p>Or it says: &#8220;<strong>There is a Great Bright Light Heaven &#8211; we shall go there.</strong>&#8221; In fact the heaven it mentions is where the demon king dwells. It says: <strong>that is where the Buddhas and Tathagatas rest.</strong> That is where the Buddha of this life &#8211; all present Buddhas you believe in are false &#8211; and the Tathagatas rest within. When the Buddhas tire of teaching beings in the world, they return to the Great Bright Light Heaven to rest.&#8221;</p>
<p>When <strong>the unknowing believe such deceitful words</strong>, the result is they <strong>lose their inherent minds</strong>, their original faith.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Thử danh lệ quỷ, niên lão thành ma, não loạn thị nhân. Yểm túc tâm sanh, khứ bỉ nhân thể. Đệ tử dữ sư câu hãm vương nạn.</strong></p>
