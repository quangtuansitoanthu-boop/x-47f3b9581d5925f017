{align:justify|THE KARMIC RETRIBUTION OF SLANDER AND FABRICATION}
{align:justify|A friend of mine had always created bad speech karma, loving embroidered tales and spreading groundless rumors—all the more harmful since he worked in communications. {align:justify|I was eager to know what had moved him to reform and change so greatly.}
{align:justify|He told me frankly: six weeks earlier he had returned south for the funeral of his eldest uncle's wife, Aunt Thom, and learning her full story left him deeply shaken!}
{align:justify|Aunt Thom had two sons: the elder, brilliant and accomplished, died in a road accident months earlier while driving home. The younger, fresh out of university, over some heartbreak or other set himself on fire—a ghastly death.}
{align:justify|As for Aunt Thom herself, cancer of the throat and tongue required surgery on her neck and tongue; unable to speak, she lay tossing on her bed in agony, enduring every torment. When her illness reached its final stage, each time she opened her mouth the stench made everyone want to vomit—no one dared come near. Both sons were dead, and now the mother… At the funeral, only her husband (the eldest uncle) remained to preside—truly pitiful.}
{align:justify|Then the second uncle—the eldest's younger brother, who had left home more than a decade before—returned with his children and handed his elder brother a farewell letter (in his wife's own hand), containing two lines written in blood:}

{align:justify|your line shall be cut off without descendants, and after death you shall fall into the tongue-cutting hell"…}
{align:justify|The eldest uncle finished the letter, collapsed to his knees, burst into loud sobs, then kowtowed to his younger brother begging forgiveness; the two brothers embraced, weeping together…}

{align:justify|Long ago, the eldest uncle's wife, vying for her in-laws' favor (secretly fearing the old folks would bequeath more property to the accomplished second-uncle household), had fabricated a vicious rumor: "The second uncle's wife is carrying on with a coworker"… leading her parents-in-law to misunderstand the second uncle's wife, while husband and wife quarreled too…}
{align:justify|Wrongfully accused, the second uncle's wife burned with rage; she cut her finger, wrote the farewell letter in blood, then drank pesticide and killed herself. {align:justify|My friend's mother had at the time joined in spreading that vicious rumor, echoing and trumpeting the slander throughout the village. Seeing the retribution that crashed upon Aunt Thom's household, she was terrified; and witnessing her own sons suffering for years with kidney inflammation, tormented by illness, unable to work normally, my friend's mother was utterly shaken—she led the whole family before the second uncle's wife's memorial tablet, kowtowing like worshippers before the stars, begging forgiveness for those bygone sins…}
{align:justify|Learning all this, my friend's own mind was shaken as never before. He reflected that for years he too had created much unwholesome speech karma, often writing online articles that spread scandal, sowed discord, and harmed people… his heart filled with remorse and dread… so he resolved to come and beg forgiveness of his victims (myself among them), vowing to reform from here on"…}
{align:justify|My mother, hearing the story, warned me: "In choosing a lifelong partner, be extremely careful—for marry an unkind wife, foul-mouthed and ill-hearted, wanton in speech karma and crooked in talk, and calamity will reach your descendants"… Mother also reminded me always to remember Master Cheng Yen's teaching: "Speak like fragrant flowers, never like venomous snakes!"}
{align:justify|All who recklessly create speech karma… look upon the eldest uncle's wife and take warning. Today she stands as a horrifying example to the whole village. I recount this story hoping readers will guard their speech karma carefully and avoid such errors.}
{align:justify|PLEASE DO NOT DO DEEDS THAT ERODE VIRTUE}
{align:justify|Dear good teachers and fellow cultivators,}
{align:justify|I have not learned Buddhism long, my understanding of the teachings is not profound, and I dare not discourse at length—I only ask, from my own experience, to share a warning with everyone: a thousand times, ten thousand times over, do nothing that erodes virtue. Do not kill living beings, steal, engage in sexual misconduct…}
{align:justify|Here, I also thank the Buddhist website for giving me the chance to recount the offenses I once committed, so all may take warning. If reading this article you wish to revile, scold, or despise me, all are welcome—for that is my good fortune.}
{align:justify|I was born into an ordinary family, and from the time I could understand things I did something utterly virtueless: hogging the outhouse.}
{align:justify|I was very small then. At the time my great-grandfather (my maternal grandmother's father) lived with us.}
{align:justify|Great-grandfather was old; when he needed the toilet, arriving even a little late meant he could not hold it and would soil himself… all over his person. And if that happened, my grandparents would berate great-grandfather fiercely—very harsh to hear.}
{align:justify|Back then I understood nothing, but I loved mischief: the moment I saw great-grandfather rise to head for the outhouse, I would dash in ahead of him, slam the door shut, and though great-grandfather moaned and cried out outside, I would not open it. Only after great-grandfather had soiled his trousers would I come out. Because of me, grandmother scolded great-grandfather terribly, leaving him deeply miserable—and the chief cause was me; the trouble arose through me.}
{align:justify|Yet I kept playing this game, causing great-grandfather much suffering. Not until later, when great-grandfather lay unconscious, would he often moan in his daze, calling my name, pleading: "Open the door quickly." At the time I did not know I was acting virtuelessly.}
{align:justify|Then great-grandfather passed away; once he even appeared in my mother's dream, instructing her that I was small and understood nothing, begging her not to beat me! Though still very small, whenever Mother told me this I felt remorseful and sad. I would often pull the blanket over my head and weep bitterly. Even now, remembering it, my heart aches.}
{align:justify|Perhaps from those evil causes, those wicked childhood acts, I now receive their retribution. Though still young, I feel my plight differs little from great-grandfather's. I constantly need to urinate and cannot hold it—a great torment. Going to the hospital finds no cause; everything is normal.}
{align:justify|Before, I did not understand; only after learning Buddhism did I grasp that this is precisely the retribution for the evil karma I once created. Now I can only bear it, tasting its flavor to the full—known to me alone. So I beg you, fellow cultivators: ten thousand times over, do no evil—sooner or later retribution will ripen right upon your own person!}
{align:justify|As a child I was very naughty, fond of catching dragonflies, crickets, insects of all kinds… Cruel by nature then, I loved tearing off legs, snapping wings, tormenting them to death for fun. Our house stocked many medicines; I would mix boiling water with drugs into a potion, then use a syringe to inject it into crickets' bellies and other insects… I tormented them cruelly! Remembering now, I truly created oceans of killing karma. Sins piled high! Then I went on to wreck anthills, catch crickets… I poured water to flood their holes, then dammed them with mud to trap them… grave sins indeed.}
{align:justify|Later, growing up, our family kept dogs continuously (for each one died, so another had to follow)—whenever a dog disobeyed, I beat it savagely.}
{align:justify|Writing this now, recalling those scenes, I see myself as… NOT HUMAN! I sincerely repent my crimes! Sowing evil seeds then, I must surely reap their fruit.}
{align:justify|And so, a few years ago, I developed cracked skin: whenever the weather turned cold my hands swelled badly, nights I could not sleep, hands and feet aching as if gnawed by countless insects—a torment one could die of!}
{align:justify|Then, not knowing I was repaying karma, I ran about seeking cures of every kind—pills, injections, poultices… all useless; instead the illness grew worse.}
{align:justify|Only after learning Buddhism did I understand: all of it was karmic retribution, for long ago I had ravaged small creatures, so I had to bear such retribution. Rightly so. And thanks to learning Buddhism and bowing to the Buddhas, for nearly two years my illness has no longer been grave; my hands still crack, but greatly improved—for this I thank the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas! Now whenever illness strikes, especially karmic illness, I meet it calmly—for having created evil, I must accept its consequences.}
{align:justify|One last matter I wish to warn all about: ten thousand times over, do not commit sexual misconduct.
