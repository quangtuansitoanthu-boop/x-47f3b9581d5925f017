TAO VAN TON

Layman Tao Van Ton, Dharma name Lieu Nghia, was a native of Jiujiang county, Jiangxi. Poor, he earned his living by trade, filial by nature, delighting in almsgiving and good deeds. Orphaned young and unschooled, when his means had somewhat improved he donated to build a school and hire teachers to aid the district's children.

His mother, nee Dam, was Buddhist, once vowing to pilgrimage to Mount Putuo and Mount Jiuhua, but passing away before she could go. To fulfill his mother's wish, in the second month of the Guimao year under the Qing's Guangxu, Van Ton prepared offerings and pilgrimaged to the holy traces of Mount Jiuhua. That done, he pilgrimaged next to Mount Putuo. Landing from boat to shore, at Purple Bamboo Grove he saw his mother seated serenely within, and joyfully cried out, hastening toward her — when the figure suddenly dissolved. He stood grieving and weeping. A monk of the temple, seeing it strange, came forth to inquire. Learning the cause, the monk said: "That was Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva manifesting to comfort your filial devotion. Your act of fulfilling your mother's vow in her stead has received her attestation!". After venerating the Lac Già Hall and returning by boat, Van Ton saw upon the sea a thousand-petaled lotus arise, bearing Thousand-Armed Thousand-Eyed Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva seated in lotus posture, of wondrously majestic excellent marks. Beholding the scene, joy and sorrow intermingled, and profound faith was born.

Later, drawing near to Dharma Master An Quang, Van Ton first learned the Buddha-recitation gate. He visited Lo Son Temple in Jiujiang county and other temples, organizing seven-day retreats and promoting the Pure Land teaching. In the summer of the Guichou year he donated his house by the ferry gate, converting it into a Buddha-Recitation Grove. In the summer of the Jisi year, he further founded the Donglin Lotus Society, making that place its seat. Van Ton together with the resident monk signed a compact dedicating the premises to the ten directions' Sangha, welcoming assemblies from all quarters, himself bearing the expenses. Late that winter he further planned a pavilion-hall enshrining Holy Manjushri. When building materials — tiles, timber, stone — stood complete and the image had been invited home, he fell ill.

In the spring of the Canh Ngo year, on the nineteenth day of the second month, Avalokiteshvara's feast, Van Ton invited the lay practitioners to gather, saying: "Manjushri Bodhisattva has promised to escort me to the Land of Bliss three days hence; I invite you all to come at the appointed time for supporting recitation, to see one another off a last time!". Hearing this, the laymen marveled. Before the end, he called his eldest son Thien Chuong to heat water for his bath and change him into fresh clean robes. All done, he further charged: "Father's hour of rebirth has come and cannot be stayed. All matters of the Donglin Lotus Society and the building of Manjushri's hall you must continue and complete without neglect!". Having spoken, he bade the fellow practitioners farewell, sat upright in lotus posture, held his rosary reciting a moment, and passed away amid the assembly's chanting of the sacred name.

This was the spring equinox of the nineteenth year of the Republic (1930). He lived sixty-three years.
