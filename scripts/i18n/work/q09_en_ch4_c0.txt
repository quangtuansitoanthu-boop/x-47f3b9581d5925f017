<p style="text-align:center"><strong><u>THE THINKING SKANDHA</u></strong></p>

<p style="text-align:center"><strong>THE MENTAL STATE BEFORE ENTERING THE THINKING SKANDHA</strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: A-nan, bỉ thiện nam tử, tu tam-ma-đề, thọ ấm tận giả, tuy vị lậu tận, tâm li kì hình, như điểu xuất lung. Dĩ năng thành tựu, tùng thị phàm thân, thượng lịch bồ-tát lục thập thánh vị, đắc ý sanh thân, tùy vãng vô ngại. </strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Translation</u></strong><strong>: Ananda, that good man cultivating samadhi, with the feeling skandha exhausted, though not yet beyond outflow, his mind can leave the body as a bird escapes its cage. Having accomplished this, from this ordinary body he can pass through the Bodhisattvas' sixty holy stages, attaining the mind-made body, going and coming without obstruction.</strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Commentary</u></strong>: <strong>Ananda, that good man cultivating samadhi, with the feeling skandha exhausted</strong> &#8211; when the feeling skandha is purified. <strong>Though not yet beyond outflow, his mind can leave the body as a bird escapes its cage.</strong> Though delusion is not yet exhausted, he already has a little spiritual power: the mind can leave the body as a bird escapes its cage.</p>
<p>Before, the cultivator attaining only to the exhaustion of the feeling skandha was like one locked tight within his bodily shell &#8211; exactly like a bird locked in a cage. Now the bird can escape the cage.</p>
<p><strong>Having accomplished this, from this ordinary body he can pass through the Bodhisattvas' sixty holy stages.</strong></p>
<p>From the ordinary body having accomplished this &#8211; that is, having a little spiritual power &#8211; he can pass through the sixty holy stages of the Bodhisattvas: right in the ordinary body, the cultivator can become a Bodhisattva, realizing the sixty holy stages of the Bodhisattvas.</p>
<p><strong>Attaining the mind-made body, going and coming without obstruction</strong>.</p>
<p>The cultivator must clearly establish earnest, diligent effort in practice before gaining the spiritual power of the <em>mind-made body</em>. Attaining this body, the cultivator can go wherever he wishes.</p>
<p>What are the Bodhisattvas' sixty holy stages? They are the fifty-five stages taught in the earlier section, plus the three <em>gradual steps</em>, plus the <em>Dry Wisdom Ground</em> and <em>Wondrous Awakening</em> &#8211; sixty holy stages in all.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong>THE SCOPE OF THE THINKING SKANDHA</strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Thí như hữu nhân, thục mị nghệ ngôn. Thị nhân tuy tắc, vô biệt sở tri, kì ngôn dĩ thành, âm vận luân thứ, linh bất mị giả, hàm ngộ kì ngữ. Thử tắc danh vi, tưởng âm khu vũ.</strong></p>
<p><strong><u>Translation:</u></strong><strong> It is like a person talking in his sleep in deep slumber: though the person knows nothing else, his words are already formed &#8211; cadenced, ordered in sequence &#8211; so that those not asleep all understand his speech. This is called the region of the thinking skandha.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Like a person talking in his sleep in deep slumber</strong>: this is a comparison &#8211; the scope of the thinking skandha is like one talking deliriously in deep sleep: asleep, he begins saying whatever he wants to say. Though the person knows nothing of what he says, <strong>his words are already formed &#8211; cadenced and coherent</strong>: though the cultivator knows nothing of what he says in deep sleep, what he says is coherent, flowing in a logical sequence, clearly meaningful &#8211; yet he knows nothing of what he has said. But <strong>those not asleep all understand.</strong></p>
<p>For example, the person says: &#8220;Today I want to eat bean curd, so I went to the shop and bought ten slabs. Returning home, I steamed the cakes and ate them.&#8221;</p>
<p>He knows nothing of what he has said &#8211; but the others awake know very well: &#8220;Oh, today he ate bean curd.&#8221;</p>
<p>Why does he say such things? Because he has not forgotten what he ate &#8211; he still remembers the eating, so he mentions it even in dreams.</p>
<p><strong>This is the region of the thinking skandha</strong>.</p>
<p>It is analogous to the territory of the thinking skandha. But do not take this so simply as to say: &#8220;The thinking skandha is just talking deliriously in sleep&#8221; &#8211; quite mistaken to infer so. This is only an analogy.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong>THE ROOT OF THE THINKING SKANDHA</strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhược động niệm tận, phù tưởng tiêu trừ. Ư giác minh tâm, như khử trần cấu. Nhất luân  sanh tử, thủ vĩ viên chiếu, danh tưởng ấm tận. Thị nhân tắc năng, siêu phiền não trược. Quán kì sở do, dung thông vọng tưởng, dĩ vi kì bản.</strong></p>
<p><strong> Translation: When stirring thoughts are exhausted and floating thoughts removed, within the awakened bright mind, as if dust and defilement cleansed away &#8211; one stream of birth and death, head and tail roundly illumined &#8211; this is called the exhaustion of the thinking skandha. Such a person can then transcend the defilement of afflictions. Contemplate its cause: interfused false thought is its root.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>When stirring thoughts are exhausted</strong>, <strong>floating, coarse thoughts removed.</strong> The extremely subtle false thoughts are in the sixth consciousness; eliminating the floating, coarse false thoughts &#8211; since these subtle thoughts in the sixth consciousness have been removed, the coarse, false, floating thoughts are removed along with them.</p>
<p><strong>Within the awakened bright mind, as if dust and defilement cleansed away.</strong> This awakened bright mind is the eighth consciousness &#8211; now clearly the eighth consciousness has purified all defilement of false dust.</p>
<p><strong>One stream of birth and death, head and tail roundly illumined</strong>: there are twelve kinds of beings &#8211; from the womb-born and egg-born to the neither-thinking-nor-not-thinking &#8211; and the cultivator clearly knows the causes planted before and the results to be undergone after, of every kind of being.</p>
<p>That <strong>is called the exhaustion of the thinking skandha</strong>. When the cultivator attains the power of knowing past lives, <strong>such a person can transcend the defilement of afflictions</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Contemplate the cause of the thinking skandha</strong> &#8211; contemplating the deep cause of all the cultivator's creations. <strong>Its root is interfused false thought.</strong></p>
<p>By this point, false thoughts have become interfused with one another, each false thought becoming the source of itself.</p>

<p style="text-align:center"><strong>1 &#8211; GREED FOR SKILLFUL MEANS</strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: A-nan, bỉ thiện nam tử, thọ ấm hư diệu, bất tao tà lự, viên định phát minh, tam ma địa trung, tâm ái viên minh, nhuệ kì tinh tư, tham cầu thiện xảo.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Ananda, when the feeling skandha has become empty and wondrous, no longer subject to deviant concerns &#8211; the perfect samadhi disclosed &#8211; within samadhi the mind longs for perfect brightness, urging thought onward, greedily seeking skillful means.</strong></p>
