Here it says milk and foods made from milk should all not be used, yet in the precepts of both the Great and Small Vehicles this is not held to be entirely forbidden. This sutra passage speaks of those who keep the precepts at the purest level. They keep the no-killing precepts entirely. They use nothing connected with beings' lives. They do not wear silk because some silkworms' lives must die in the process of weaving silk. They do not use honey because honey comes from bees. But within the Vinaya itself this matter remains open. The precepts have a most flexible meaning. The precepts do not specifically forbid all these things. For you to avoid using them is to be most pure. That is most good.

Sutra text: 何以故服其身分皆為彼緣。如人食其地中百穀。足不離地必使身心。於諸眾生若身,身分。身心二塗不服不食。我說是人真解脫者。

Transliteration: Hà dĩ cố phục kỳ thân phần giai vị bỉ duyên. Như nhơn thực kỳ địa trung bá cốc, túc bất ly địa. Tất sử thân tâm, ư chư chúng sanh nhược thân, thân phần. Thân tâm nhị đồ bất phục bất thực. Ngã thuyết thị nhân chơn giải thoát giả.

Translation: Why? Using their body-parts all creates affinities with them. As when people eat the hundred grains of the earth, their feet never leave the ground. One must cause body and mind, toward beings' bodies or body-parts, in both body and mind, neither to wear nor eat them. I say such a person is truly liberated.

Commentary: Why? Using their body-parts all creates affinities with them. For instance, when you wear silk, you have a connection with the silkworms that spun the silk. If you do not wish to become such a creature, then do not associate with them. That is like when people eat the hundred grains of the earth, their feet never leave the ground. That is precisely the first humans on earth, who were originally beings descended from the Great Brahma heavens. This happened in the past when the kalpa-fire spread across the earth until it burned away every trace of humans. Then began a barren, desolate period lasting none knows how long. And then, one day some beings from the Great Brahma heavens alighted upon the world.

At this time, the world was covered with an entirely delightful special substance. They scooped up a handful and smelled it most delicious. So they ate. Once they had eaten this "earth's richness," they could no longer fly. They could no longer ride clouds and drive mist. They could no longer move. They stayed on earth and beckoned their brothers and sisters in the heavens. These people descended to earth and ate the "earth's richness" too, so they also became obstructed by earth. They could never return to the heavens; and that is why humans came to dwell on earth. Some say we descend from monkeys. But if that were true, what made us become monkeys? In fact, it all began when celestials descended to earth. As the population on earth grew, the "earth's richness" was entirely exhausted, and plant species died. So they had to eat the hundred grains (100 kinds of grain). They are:
1. Twenty different kinds of rice.
2. Twenty kinds of millet.
3. Twenty kinds of beans.
4. Twenty kinds of vegetables.
5. Twenty kinds of melons.
