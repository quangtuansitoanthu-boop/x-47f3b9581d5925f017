TITLE: 24. AS YOU SOW, SO SHALL YOU REAP
TOMTAT: Mr. Truong suffered dizziness from shaking a nest of baby mice to death. Meeting Master Miaofa, he learned the cause and effect, repented, vowed to kill no more, and healed at once.
-----
{align:center|**AS YOU SOW, SO SHALL YOU REAP**}

{align:justify|My neighbor Mr. Truong loves practicing qigong, and even uses qigong to heal others. Yet he heals no one, while he himself suffers tormenting illness. He has suffered dizziness for 3-4 years now. Medicines and treatments of every kind bring no result.}

{align:justify|He says:}

{align:justify|"My head reels as though I had just drunk two bottles of liquor!"}

{align:justify|Affinities ripening, he met Master Miaofa and begged him to wield qigong to treat his illness.}

{align:justify|The Master smiled and said:}

{align:justify|"I cannot wield qigong to treat illness — every illness has its cause. I can state clearly why you are dizzy. Whether the illness ends depends on you. This is also the truth the Buddha spoke: 'Fate is made by oneself.' Suppose through ignorance you do something wrong, bringing torment and affliction to yourself and others. If you meet a wise friend who explains and points out the cause — once you understand, recognize your error, and strive to correct it — affliction and torment shall vanish away. For the Buddha Dharma is not for superstitiously worshiping Buddhas, bodhisattvas, or some eminent monk — it is for each person to gain wisdom and remake his own destiny!"}

{align:justify|Mr. Truong thought a while, then nodded.}

{align:justify|The Master continued:}

{align:justify|"Your dizziness comes from harming a nest of baby mice 3-4 years ago. Is there such a thing?"}

{align:justify|Mr. Truong recalled and said:}

{align:justify|"Yes, there is! Master! You truly possess great powers! Three years ago, one day I entered the storeroom and found a nest of newborn mice, some 6-7 of them. A coward, I knew no way to kill them — so I put them in a cage and shook it hard with all my strength. Shaking a long while, I supposed them dead and tossed them on the rubbish heap. Oh! Can such a thing concern my dizziness?"}

{align:justify|"Indeed — that nest was not shaken to death by you, only made dizzy. As the cause, so the fruit. That is why you are dizzy."}

{align:justify|Mr. Truong muttered to himself:}

{align:justify|"You judge most truly. Since then my dizziness began. Fortunately the mice did not die — had they died, I know not how much worse my retribution would be!"}

{align:justify|"Indeed — killing shortens lifespan; else in this very life one meets sudden calamity. That animals can be slaughtered is also because in past lives they sowed killing causes, and in this life reap such fruit."}

{align:justify|As for us, slaughtering them in this life means borrowing a life-debt — cause and effect turning, vengeance begetting vengeance without end. Hence we ordinary beings transmigrate through the six realms with no day of escape. Only by comprehending the Buddha Dharma, deeply believing cause and effect, ending evil and practicing good, can we cross the sea of birth-and-death suffering. Well then — henceforth do you still wish to kill?"}

{align:justify|Seated Mr. Truong, frightened into a daze, hearing the Master ask, hastily answered:}

{align:justify|"Henceforth I dare kill no more."}

{align:justify|Finished, he joyfully announced:}

{align:justify|"Venerable Master, my head has suddenly cleared — not dizzy in the least."}

{align:justify|All around laughed.}

{align:justify|Mr. Truong asked on in wonder:}

{align:justify|"But mice ruin crops, gnaw household goods, even spread plague... if we do not kill them, and they multiply and overrun — what then?"}

{align:justify|"Take the whole — from the vast universe down to the mote of dust, including every kind of life... all have their laws of movement and their span of existence. If humans have 'birth, age, sickness, death,' things have 'forming, abiding, decaying, emptiness'... be it mountains, rivers, seas, flowers, grasses, dewdrops... Whatever 'takes form shall perish!' Yet this perishing — this 'cycle of birth and death' — has its own laws! If we scorn those laws, destroying at will by our theories and whims, we shall surely summon unimaginable evil fruit!"}

{align:justify|Suppose for immediate profit we blast forests and quarries, so forests are ruined, mountains slide, forest creatures slaughtered... all our heedless deeds shall drown the earth in water, shift the climate, raise floods... Do you not see forests felled in such number, animals hunted and slaughtered so ruthlessly that they vanish or perish utterly. Mice too have natural enemies: snakes, cats, kites, hawks, owls and the like... And those natural enemies may also have been slaughtered by us humans for meat, or flayed for clothing and ornaments. Hence the saying: 'Natural disaster is truly born of human calamity.' "}

{align:justify|The Master paused, then said:}

{align:justify|"I shall show you a way to end the plague of mice — will you try it?"}

{align:justify|All gazed fixedly at the Master; he smiled and explained:}

{align:justify|"First know that mice too are sentient creatures, born of mothers, nursed and raised. Like oxen, horses, cats, and dogs, some can understand human speech. Once you grasp the principle of not killing, each day prepare leftover food for them, set it where mice usually appear, and say softly: 'Long ago, not yet understanding the Buddha Dharma, I slaughtered a great many mice. Now I know the truth; henceforth I shall surely kill no more. I shall recite sutras for the deliverance of the mice I slew. I hope the mice will gnaw no more household goods'... Only do this with sincere heart, and you shall surely see results. One day even, the mice shall move their nest and leave."}

{align:justify|The Master's talk opened our eyes and ears anew. Afterward the method was tried one by one, with most good results. Some whose homes swarmed with ants applied the same way and called it most miraculous.}

{align:justify|You do not believe? Then try it yourself!}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story AS YOU SOW, SO SHALL YOU REAP is the plainest, easiest lesson in the law of cause and effect. Mr. Truong shook a nest of baby mice (nearly to death), so he suffered dizziness for 3-4 years. Repenting and vowing to kill no more, his illness healed on the spot. The story also offers a fine way to end plagues of mice or ants without killing: sincerely repent, recite sutras in dedication, and "negotiate" with them — setting out leftover food and stating plainly the resolve to kill no more. It is "knowing self and knowing other" with beings, expressing the Buddha Dharma's loving-kindness. The story affirms: "Killing shortens lifespan... We ordinary beings transmigrate through the six realms with no day of escape. Only by comprehending the Buddha Dharma, deeply believing cause and effect, ending evil and practicing good, can we cross the sea of birth-and-death suffering."}
