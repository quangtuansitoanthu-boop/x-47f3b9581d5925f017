{align:justify|At midnight, I received an urgent order from above:}
{align:justify|"Report to the scene and get to work at once!"}
{align:justify|It seems that outside the city there is a great bridge, and residents reported: "Someone has hanged themselves on this bridge!" When we reached the scene, we saw the suicide was a woman. From the examination, her resolve to die was clearly immense (the rope had tightened so hard her neck bones broke).}
{align:justify|Investigating her background, we learned the deceased lived nearby (but the story did not end there, for a still more dreadful sight awaited us)…}
{align:justify|Arriving at her home, we started at the sight of the naked corpse of a baby not yet one year old lying on the bed. When the police burst in, the child's father still did not know his wife had killed herself; he wept and wailed, reproaching his wife loudly to the police and telling the whole tale.}
{align:justify|As it turned out, the matter was nothing complicated: the husband had gone out drinking with friends, leaving his wife home alone with the child. After he returned, his wife wept and raged, insistently accusing him of seeing another woman outside, of carrying on with Ms. X… Hearing this, the husband flared up, stormed out of the house, and went back to his drinking friends. At home, the wife brooded with mounting fury and sent her husband an "ultimatum" running roughly: "Come home and mind the child—I cannot care for it any longer!"… But the husband, dead drunk, had no mind to spare for the messages on his phone…}
{align:justify|Not until he returned did he discover that the little boy, kicking about, had thrown off the blanket and frozen to death. (Country houses in winter have no heating and are bitterly cold).}
{align:justify|The poor husband, still unaware his wife had killed herself, kept venting his furious blame at the police, saying it was his wife's fault the boy had frozen to death… Until the police broke the terrible news—"Your wife has already hanged herself!"—at which the husband could not utter a word and collapsed in a faint.}
{align:justify|Over a petty quarrel, two lives were lost, and a family's happiness was shattered… We could only sigh. After a thorough examination, the case was confirmed as suicide, and the matter was quickly closed.}
{align:justify|But during the investigation we uncovered one more detail: old Luu (the bicycle-repair shop owner in the village) was the true cause of this tragedy. Asking around, we learned that Mr. Luu, past fifty, divorced for years, now lived alone.}
{align:justify|By nature he loved to fabricate gossip and spread tales—with no cause at all he could spin a great drama out of nothing. It was he who had whispered poison and told the wife that her husband was having an affair! What is more, he embroidered it with vivid, meticulous detail, as if it were solid truth. When we asked about the husband's affair that he had trumpeted, we learned it was pure fabrication.}
{align:justify|Many villagers complained and reported: "Mr. Luu loves to spin gossip and stir up trouble—many families, believing him, have fallen into discord, husbands and wives turning against each other, fathers and sons embittered, even coming to blows and quarrels"…}
{align:justify|Whenever Mr. Luu learned that his words had set people at one another's throats… he beamed with delight, sitting in the tavern looking utterly smug.}
{align:justify|Yet in the end he could not be prosecuted, for his conduct fit no criminal charge (though it sowed calamity)… The wife hanged herself for believing his wild tales… but he could not on that account be charged with murder! So we could only warn and caution him.}
{align:justify|To our surprise, within a month the local police informed me: Mr. Luu had met with trouble!}
{align:justify|"One night, Mr. Luu went to friends' house and got dead drunk. After being helped back to his repair shop, at midnight he crawled out looking for liquor, groped about, grabbed a jar, and in his stupor took a swig. Little did he know it was no wine at all, but a jar of chemicals—liquid used for painting metal engines, extremely low in moisture: once brushed on, within two seconds it can freeze solid whatever it touches.}
{align:justify|Mr. Luu collapsed unconscious on the spot; luckily someone nearby rushed him to the emergency room in time.}
{align:justify|Examining him, the doctors found his tongue frozen stiff as ice—swallow even a little of this chemical and you are done for! The doctors declared: from now on, not only would Mr. Luu never speak again, but even his palate could hardly be saved! Henceforth he could only be fed through a tube"…}
{align:justify|These are a few stories of retribution I wish to share with you.}
{align:justify|According to medicine, every death is caused by illness or injury. Even dying in one's bedroom or passing away in deep sleep always comes from disease or other causes.}
{align:justify|I am a Buddhist disciple, and also a medical doctor. Toward the scientific view I am always respectful, yet I must say: the vision of science is always limited! Each of us will certainly die, but the time of death and whether the manner of dying is painful are truly connected with what we do, and always bear the imprint of cause and effect!}
{align:justify|Had I not known the Buddha's teachings, conducting investigations I too would think simply and conclude: death comes from injury, illness… or execution for crimes, and so on…}
{align:justify|But over years of examining many homicide cases, after probing deeply into them, I have always discovered the truth that "good is repaid with good, evil with evil"—without the slightest error.}
{align:justify|The Buddha's teachings say "the karma of sentient beings is unfathomable"—how true! This single line can explain the questions of birth and death in our daily lives.}

{align:justify|Translator's note: We see clearly: words can save people, and words can kill people!}
{align:justify|Mr. Luu used his tongue to embroider and invent stories, taking it as amusement, never knowing he was planting seeds of great evil, bringing calamity to so many in the village. Had his tongue kept working, there would have been still more victims. And so his whole tongue, with the palate—every organ that could bring disaster upon the world—was… eradicated.}
{align:justify|Every day in the papers and on television we see reports of idle youths who, late at night, gather on overpasses to hurl stones that shatter car windows, or smash the signal lamps at railway stations… taking it as fun, never knowing these are acts that wreck their own blessings!}
{align:justify|Given hands yet using them for no one's benefit, but to sow harm—hurling stones that shatter glass can also gravely injure people!… Worse, a broken railway signal cannot signal, easily causing catastrophic traffic accidents for passersby. Thus not only do they erode their own blessings, rank, and power, but evildoers, besides being born into unhappy destinies, may draw upon themselves the retribution of handlessness.}
{align:justify|Whatever limb or organ performs deeds useful to people draws fine retribution; conversely, if it only sows danger… it will be removed. Depending on one's blessings, the evil retribution ripens sooner or later—but never fails to come.}
{align:justify|Only from ignorance, from never being taught cause and effect, from not understanding… do people commit offenses. So what is needed is to help people understand cause and effect clearly. Once this principle is clear, with no overseer needed, people will on their own do what benefits themselves and others, willingly shunning evil and doing good.}
