NHAM CUNG NHAN

Nham Cung Nhan was the wife of Mr. Chau Van Tuyen of Haiyan. From youth she believed in Buddhism, and after marrying into the Chau family fulfilled aid to husband and nurture of children. Dignified and upright by nature, she treated others with great kindness. In the winter of the Giap Thin year under the Qing's Guangxu, her husband passed away; Cung Nhan was then fifty-nine. Having witnessed many scenes of loss and change, moved by her husband's death, she reflected that life is illusory and existence impermanent, and suddenly aroused the resolve for liberating practice. After careful decision, Cung Nhan entrusted household matters to son and daughter-in-law, cast off myriad involvements, and kept lifelong vegetarian diet with Buddha-recitation. Her practice was most diligent, never daring neglect.

In the winter of the Dinh Ty year of the Republic, Cung Nhan suddenly suffered half-body paralysis, limbs numb and aching, needing support to walk. She had a room partitioned, a Buddha enshrined before and a resting place behind, dwelling there in quiet recitation like one in retreat. Her mind grew thereby more settled, her mindfulness more finely diligent.

In the summer of the Tan Dau year, her two sons Dieu Sanh and Cat Sanh heard Layman Pham Co Nong lecture on the Dharma, arousing aspiration to study Buddhism, and brought what they had learned home to explain to their mother. Her faith and vows for rebirth grew ever more earnest. In the first month of the Giap Ty year, Dieu Sanh had the chance to journey with lay practitioners to hear sutra lectures; Cung Nhan said: "Mother is old — do not travel long, my child!". On the twenty-fourth she caught typhoid fever, followed by asthma. On the twenty-eighth the illness worsened; the family invited women fellow practitioners to chant in support. Hearing the sacred-name chanting, Cung Nhan's mind grew peaceful, breathing even, and she dozed awhile. Waking, she said: "Just now I dreamed an old woman of ruddy face and white hair gave me a molded cake to eat. After eating, I felt clear and light, and asked: 'Shall I be freed from suffering?' The old woman answered: 'You shall be freed — strive in Buddha-recitation!' Hearing it, I recited more than a hundred utterances, then suddenly woke!".

After that, her typhoid, asthma, and paralysis were all wholly cured. That night Cung Nhan slept soundly. Waking at dawn, body and mind felt light and easy, illness gone, with a waft of fragrance. On the twentieth day of the second month, Cung Nhan developed melancholy congestion and breathlessness; the family again invited women to support. On the twenty-second, knowing she would not recover, she charged her family not to lament but to take turns chanting loudly in support, that she might recite along. At the third watch, Cung Nhan peacefully passed away.

At first her limbs all turned cold, only the breast remaining long warm. All kept still, chanting loudly without pause. The warmth gradually rose to mouth and eyes, at last reaching straight to the crown. Support continued till midnight of the next day before ending. At encoffining, Cung Nhan's limbs were still soft, her face fresh as in life. After burial, the family burned the Western merit tallies on which she had recorded daily recitation counts. When the fire died, upon the ashes appeared the form of a monk standing upon a lotus, outlines clear as if drawn.

A comment: Dharma Master An Quang said: "All principles of the worldly and world-transcending lie nothing beyond two words: MIND-NATURE. All phenomena of the worldly and world-transcending lie nothing beyond two words: CAUSE AND EFFECT.". Nham Cung Nhan's many illnesses were the fruit of killing karma from former lives; her dream-healing from suffering sprang from this life's practicing causes. At the end, warmth lodged in the breast — she should by rights have been reborn human to practice another life before liberation. Yet through the merit of family and fellow practitioners' turn-by-turn earnest supporting recitation, in the end she attained rebirth. This shows how essential end-of-life supporting recitation is.
