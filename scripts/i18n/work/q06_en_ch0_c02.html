One of them perfected his Way through the dharma of the Fire-Light samadhi. One attained perfect penetration through the element of wind; another through the element of space. One cultivated from his eye root and succeeded, and another cultivated from his ear root. Each of the six sense roots was taken up by one sage or another for cultivation. Each category within the eighteen realms was entered and cultivated to certification by one of them. Hearing these principles, you should apply them to yourselves.

You will ask, "Which root should I cultivate?" Do not be impatient. The very ear root that Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva used in cultivation is best for you. Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva perfected his cultivation effort through the ear root, and Ananda will follow Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva in cultivating this dharma-door. The Buddhas and Bodhisattvas of former lives have passed down to us a wonderful dharma-door; we should follow the cultivation from the ear root to attain perfect penetration. This is the easiest method.

At that time Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva rose from his seat, bowed at the Buddha's feet, and said to the Buddha:

"Guan" means to contemplate, to regard with attention.
Using the power of wisdom to regard attentively, the cultivator contemplates the objective world.

With the power of wisdom, the cultivator regards the realm that is being contemplated. The contemplative power of wisdom is inherent in Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's own nature. The objective world being contemplated is the sounds emitted by all living beings. You should contemplate the crying sounds of suffering, the jubilant sounds of happiness, the sounds of neither suffering nor joy, the sounds of good, the sounds of evil, the sounds of truth, the sounds of falseness — contemplate every kind of sound.

"Shi" is the world, in the sense of time — past, present, and future. Contemplate the past causes and effects of living beings. Contemplate the karma living beings are now creating. Contemplate the retribution living beings will undergo in the future. "Why does that person suffer so much?" You reflect, and then realize: "Oh! In a past life he was unfilial to his parents and generally unkind to people. That is why his retribution in this life is suffering."

Yin: regard every sound attentively.
"Bodhisattva" means "one who enlightens sentient beings." It also means "a sentient being with a vast heart for the Way." A Bodhisattva is also called "an enlightened sentient being," which refers to self-enlightenment. When we say a Bodhisattva is "one who enlightens sentient beings," that refers to enlightening others. Combining these two meanings, a Bodhisattva is a being who is enlightened and wishes to help all living beings become enlightened.

What a Bodhisattva does is to enlighten himself and enlighten others, to benefit himself and benefit others.

You who are studying the Buddhadharma must firmly remember the meaning of "Bodhisattva." Do not be like the person who chaired a conference on "Sangha Harmony," yet when asked what "Sangha" meant could only remain silent. That is truly inconceivable!
