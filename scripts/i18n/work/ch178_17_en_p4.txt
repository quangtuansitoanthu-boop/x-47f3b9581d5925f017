{align:justify|THE DRAGON-TIGER TAVERN}
{align:justify|Graduating high school, introduced by someone, I took work serving tables at a restaurant called the "Dragon-Tiger Tavern." The tavern stands in deep mountains thick with trees, teeming with wild beasts. The owner, Mr. Vi, counted a business king—shrewd, calculating investments—reaping much money; he hung a sign selling wild game, hawking "rustic delicacies"—diners flocked thick.}
{align:justify|The tavern kept expanding. Old Vi's shop grew fiercer, famed far and near. The house specialty, "Dragon-Tiger Duel"—great python flesh cooked with wildcat—became the tavern's namesake.}
{align:justify|So wildcats and pythons throughout neighboring lands met calamity. As Boss Vi always paid above others, many money-hungry villagers risked their lives braving peril, creeping into desolate unpeopled mountains seeking goods to supply the boss. The back courtyard daily reeked of blood, piteous wails echoing unceasing in our ears.}
{align:justify|One day old Vi bought a monstrous six-meter python, some inch-and-a-half thick, scales shimmering gold tinged silver. They say many died hunting this python; from its massive build one could guess the python king.}
{align:justify|The python lay senseless a day before waking. Old Vi neared the iron cage saying:}
{align:justify|"Ha! You look stately and majestic—so I shall not kill you yet, granting you a few more days' life."}
{align:justify|The python seemed to understand—rearing its head high, suddenly gaping wide, it spat a mouthful of reeking foul saliva into the boss's face; unprepared, he could only shriek piteously, then turn and flee. Hearing his roar we rushed over, washing his face with clean water. Python saliva corrodes and destroys strongly—rescue too late, and scars and sores surely remain.}
{align:justify|Old Vi blazed with rage, seizing a pot of boiling water to dash into the python's pen; vented, he snarled:}
{align:justify|"Were it not for my high purchase price, I would hack you to pieces for satisfaction! Humph! Now I shall make you live worse than death—half a month hence I shall butcher you, soaking you in wine to toast my fortieth birthday!"}
{align:justify|From that day the python was locked solitary in a great cage in the garden corner—no food or drink; sometimes the boss abused, tormented, and loudly cursed it—daily slaughtering its kind before it, flaying and carving animals in its sight to cow it.}
{align:justify|Seven, eight days passed—the great python never submitted; seeing the boss, eyes blazing wrathful, mouth gaping fangs bared. Nearing its cage, all felt dreadful unease.}
{align:justify|More days passed—suddenly something happened: the great caged python vanished wingless, traceless, its whereabouts… unknown. Hearing the news, the boss paled, eyes rolling back; he came to the cage staring, observing… The cage stood wholly intact, not a scratch of damage, no gaps around. He guessed: "Unless someone deliberately freed it? Else this monstrous python surely could never escape"…}
{align:justify|The boss stormed off, snarling:}
{align:justify|"WHO? WHO FREED IT, HUH? Confess sensibly and nothing happens—but let me find out and I shall flay you alive… just watch!"}
{align:justify|We all trembled with fear, swearing together we had done nothing. The boss, wretched-faced, returned to the iron cage observing on—at last discovering the great python's escape: two iron bars gapped rather wide—the python plainly escaped that way.}
{align:justify|We discussed all morning—at last admitting the fact before our eyes: this python's body stretches and shifts most elastically, supreme at slithering; so tiny a gap, yet it slipped through. For freedom, it could endure torment past imagining…}
{align:justify|The boss, having "vented his spleen"—his fury discharged—seemed calmed, saying nothing more. Past two in the morning, all sleeping soundly, terrified screams woke us. I started up, dashing to the courtyard to see.}
{align:justify|One look, and my soul fled. In bright moonlight, I saw dozens of pythons filling the courtyard, some slithering in through windows. Never had I witnessed pythons acting in concert. Usually the tavern's pythons seemed dull-witted—but now before my eyes, these great pythons looked most cunning, blazing with unquenchable wrath, their might fiercely menacing…}
{align:justify|I hurriedly shouted the alarm all around:}
{align:justify|"Wake up, wake up and run—the pythons are crawling into the house!"}
{align:justify|My terrified cries roused the whole area. In a blink, several snakes spread all around, many slithering to besiege the boss's room—sealed without a crack.}
{align:justify|In the tavern, chefs and kitchen hands, adept hands—having killed countless pythons, skilled and practiced—felt little horror; a flicker of fear passing, they quickly seized implements hunting pythons to kill. After a fierce battle, several people left the house, running to the courtyard. The pythons attacked at leisure—several charging straight for the boss's room, as if reinforcing.}
{align:justify|Plainly the pythons' attack centered on the boss's room. Windows open, several had entered within. I easily recognized the escaped python king—golden, largest of the pack, easiest to spot—and understood at once: it led and commanded this pack in revenge!}
{align:justify|Strangely, nowhere did I see the boss, nor hear clashing combat…}
{align:justify|I called loudly, warning those still standing in the courtyard:}
{align:justify|"Hurry upstairs—safer there!"}
{align:justify|As if waking, they dashed for the stairs—all running for their lives, rushing into rooms like whirlwinds. The pythons began climbing—we joined hearts and strength, holding the "high beats low" advantage—at last driving them back.}
{align:justify|As the pack withdrew from the boss's room, still the great golden king led proudly ahead—then swiftly they vanished into the dense woods behind the mountain.}
{align:justify|Certain danger had passed, we descended. I saw the two butchers Nhan Trung Vi and Ly Truong Huu had still not left their room. These two daily slaughtered snakes—countless pythons killed. Mouths agape in horror, we found both men's sinews severed, entrails torn through, blood streaming from all seven orifices—dead long since.}
{align:justify|The waitresses' room windows, shut tight, the pythons could not enter—so none were hurt—only scared "souls fled"!}
{align:justify|But where was Boss Vi? Wondering, we moved together to his door. Outside, chaos—yet no sign of him. Anxious, we pressed within—greater chaos, everywhere the pythons' trampling traces. Besides, a reeking stench lingered.}
{align:justify|Strangely, still no old Vi. "Could the pythons have kidnapped him, swallowed him whole? Dreadful"… Thinking it, my head numbed, spine chilled, gooseflesh rising…}
{align:justify|Then, as one, we all noticed a great jar in the corner—about one and a half meters high, wooden lid with handle—but now the lid closed upside down, handle inward; the jar's sides printed with snakes' clinging, wrestling traces… Surely old Vi was inside?}
{align:justify|We called softly:}
{align:justify|"Boss, the pythons have withdrawn—come out quickly!"}
{align:justify|Calling on with no stir within, foreboding, we neared, trembling lifting the lid. We saw Mr. Vi's whole body curled, face pallid, eyes bulging, tongue protruding, ten fingers clawing the lid till bleeding—a death too piteous to behold.}
{align:justify|Likely old Vi, seeing the pack enter, knew he could not rush out—so dove into the jar, pulling the lid shut. The python king, hating him to the bone, would hardly release him? Brimming with utmost fury, it joined the pack ringing the jar, attacking with full force… The snakes pressed the lid tight, letting no air seep in—old Vi suffocated within.}
{align:justify|This unheard-of horror swiftly spread—throughout the region, the villages… And locals henceforth dared kill no snakes.}

{align:justify|THE SCIENTISTS' STORY}
{align:justify|This article's author is layman Tu Quang—his whole family outstanding scientists. His parents are two scientists, both working at the Chinese Academy of Sciences. After studying Buddhism, the author admired it and took refuge in the Three Jewels. His filial piety and sincere Buddhist practice saved his mother even as one foot stepped through the ghost gate. Thus his parents turned their hearts, together stepping onto the Buddhist path. This story is entirely true and most trustworthy. Here is his self-account:}
{align:justify|In February 1989 I took refuge at Nga Long Temple in Tay An under Great Master Tri Chan. In 1991 I took a post teaching at a Beijing graduate school. More than twenty years now, I have taught and trained people, researching science while practicing—though not diligent enough, in life and work I have enjoyed profound benefits.}
{align:justify|Before, tales of boundless responses to learning Buddhism all came from fellow practitioners' telling. But later I lived through one myself. This true story arose in my own house—strange and marvelous past measure. To repay the Buddhas' and Bodhisattvas' kindness, I recount it here, hoping Buddhists may grow in right, steadfast faith.}
{align:justify|Early 2008, my mother's heart disease and high blood pressure grew grave—within half a year, four times her racing heart suddenly stopped and she fainted unconscious.}
{align:justify|Once my father went marketing—leaving my mother barely half an hour—returning to find her fallen on the ground, head gashed and bleeding… afterward though she woke, she lacked strength to crawl up. Taking Mother to great hospitals, she had no strength to walk. Examining, doctors saw her heart racing, oft palpitating, panicked—pulse blocked, beating erratically.}
{align:justify|The doctors said: operating at eighty-five endangers greatly—unless utterly failing, avoid surgery—so only drugs could soothe the illness.}
{align:justify|By 2009, my mother fell ill every day or two—nearly whole nights unsleeping. Doctors gave heart and blood-pressure drugs—all vain. So grieved I watching Mother's treatment fail. When weather shifted, her illness worsened. Even as the forecast merely announced cold reaching Tan Cuong, my mother already felt unwell. Her body failed to the utmost. Nights her heart often stopped or beat wildly—leaving her palpitating, panicked…}
{align:justify|The last Saturday of May 2009, I visited my parents at Trung Quan Village. My father said:}
{align:justify|"This summer heat, scorching so—likely your mother shall not survive."}
{align:justify|Unable to sit watching Mother await death, in the crisis I thought of the Buddha's teachings, wishing secretly to create merit for her. Then I had just memorized the "Diamond Sutra." Knowing its merit wondrous past thought, I resolved to recite the "Diamond Sutra," dedicating its merit to her, praying the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas bless Mother's turn for the better.}
{align:justify|So Sunday I began reciting—daily at five in the morning I rose to recite one "Diamond Sutra" round—recited, then dedicating two vows:}
