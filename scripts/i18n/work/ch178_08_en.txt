TITLE: 01. THE COUPLE WHO DIED TRAGICALLY
TOMTAT: In 2002, a 28-year-old man rose in the middle of the night and killed his parents, both over sixty. While the old couple slept soundly, their son tied them fast to the bed, then cut their throats, blood spattering the walls and pooling...
-----
{align:justify|In 2002, a 28-year-old man rose in the middle of the night and killed his parents, both over sixty. While the old couple slept soundly, their son tied them fast to the bed, then cut their throats, blood spattering the walls and pooling on the floor… The murderer fled that very night, and was later caught in a small neighborhood down south.}
{align:justify|The killer was married with a son. On the night of the murder, his wife—after a quarrel the day before—had angrily taken the child back to her own parents' home, thereby escaping the nightmare massacre.}
{align:justify|On the surface, this looked like a clear-cut case with utterly conclusive evidence: the procuratorate had taken confessions, and the full body of proof—physical exhibits and witnesses—was complete. It called for strict punishment and a swift verdict.}
{align:justify|At the first trial the death sentence was pronounced at once, but the defense lawyer argued that "the offender is severely mentally ill" and requested a psychiatric evaluation before further proceedings.}
{align:justify|The investigation dragged on with inconclusive results, so the case kept stalling… in the end the execution was suspended for two years. The killer's lawyer appealed again but was overruled, and the case closed at the final trial.}
{align:justify|It sounds quick in the telling, but in fact the case stretched over four years in all; I had to toil through painstaking investigations, gathering evidence seven or eight times, until the files piled up thick.}
{align:justify|The killer was the only son in the family, spoiled from childhood, with an extremely violent temper. After marrying he still lived with his parents and quarreled with them constantly. In the days before the murders, his parents and his wife had clashed; then the killer demanded money from his parents to buy a house, but they said they had no money for now and scolded him as a worthless idler who never worked and shamed them before the neighbors!… This provoked his murderous intent.}
{align:justify|But is that enough to explain the killer's motive? In this world, how many pampered children turn rebellious and clash with their families? Why was this killer so bent on murdering his own parents?}
{align:justify|In the files I found a chilling passage of testimony, in which the killer confessed frankly:}
{align:justify|"I had wanted to kill them for a long time—they're so useless! I asked them for money to buy a house and they refused—so why not just kill them! What use are they?… I planned it months ago (since they're my parents, I didn't want them to die too painfully). At first I thought of giving them… pesticide to drink, but halfway to the shop I turned back, thinking pesticide would burn their intestines. Then I planned to take them to the lake and drown them. But I never did it. In the end, after much thought, I concluded that… slitting their throats was best—not too painful, and death would come quickly"…}
{align:justify|Reading this confession chilled me to the bone. To speak of killing his parents as casually as killing… chickens or ducks! Investigating further, I was even more horrified to uncover this detail:}
{align:justify|The murdered couple ran a chicken-slaughtering stall, doing brisk business at the market. When I went to investigate at the market, the stall of the murdered chicken sellers was considered unlucky and bad for business, so everyone shunned it and no one would rent it. According to neighboring vendors, the couple used to tie up live chickens in batches, hang them on steel wires, then grip each chicken's head tight and slice its throat with a knife. Even the chickens' blood was never wasted—it fetched a good price. All their lives they grew rich from this trade. They say the throat-cutting technique was handed down from their ancestors.}
{align:justify|I remember this case so well because it was the one that first gave me faith in religion. As every analysis found its reasonable explanation, my belief in the law of cause and effect deepened, and the lesson was seared into my memory once I grasped the reason… That night I scarcely slept at all. The dead couple had spent their lives slicing chickens' throats, famed for their skillful killing… and because they cut creatures' throats that way, they drew upon themselves the calamity of having their throats cut by their own only son… Does such retribution not make us tremble with horror?}
