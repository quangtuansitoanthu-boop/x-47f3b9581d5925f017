Commentary: If there are gandharvas. "Gandharva" is Sanskrit, translated into Chinese as "fragrance-perfumed," because smelling fragrance creates their consciousness. They are the musicians of the Jade Emperor. When the Jade Emperor burns agarwood incense, they smell the fragrance and are at once drawn to it. They come to play music to delight the Jade Emperor. These gandharvas wish to transcend their kind; they no longer wish to be gandharvas. I appear before them in a gandharva's body, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.

Sutra text: 若阿修羅樂脫其倫。我於彼前現阿修羅身。而為說法令其成就。

Transliteration: Nhược a-tu-la nhạo thoát kỳ luân, ngã ư bỉ tiền hiện a-tu-la thân, nhi vị thuyết pháp linh kỳ thành tựu.

Translation: If there are asuras who delight in transcending their kind, I appear before them in an asura's body, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.

Commentary: If there are asuras who delight in transcending their kind. They wish to leave the retinue of the asura kind; I appear before them in an asura's body, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.

This section covers the beings of the eight divisions of ghosts and spirits, yet the Shurangama Sutra makes no mention of the garudas. But the Universal Door chapter of the Dharma Flower Sutra does mention that Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva also manifests as a garuda.

"Garuda" is a transliteration of Sanskrit, meaning the golden-winged great peng bird. The garuda is one of the Eight Divisions, and the fact that the sutra text does not mention this kind is probably due to an omission error in copying the sutra, or perhaps it is understood to be included in the general category of "living beings" mentioned above.

Garudas eat only dragons. This bird's wings span 330 great yojanas. A small yojana equals 40 Chinese li (one li being about one-third of a mile). A middle yojana equals 60 li. A great yojana equals 80 li. With a single flap of its wings, the golden-winged great peng drains the sea dry. Its strength is that great. When the sea water has been drained dry, the dragons are exposed. In this way, the garudas were about to exterminate the entire dragon race. So the dragons came to see the Buddha to beg for rescue. "The golden-winged great peng birds (garudas) are nearly driving our dragon retinue to extinction. What should we do?"

They looked up to the Buddha's compassion for rescue; they hoped the Buddha would stop the golden-winged great peng birds from eating the dragons of their retinue. The Buddha gave the dragons a strip of his kashaya robe to wrap upon their horns. After that, the golden-winged great peng birds dared not eat dragons. With nothing to eat, the golden-winged great peng birds also came to cry to the Buddha, begging him to rescue them. The Buddha asked, "No one eats you; why do you come seeking rescue?"

The golden-winged great peng birds answered: "True, nothing threatens us, but there is nothing to eat; we shall starve to death. You no longer permit us to eat dragons; we shall die." Then they asked the Buddha in his compassion to devise a way to rescue them.

"You have nothing to eat, is that so? Very well, the Tathagata will help you. Henceforth, whenever the Tathagata and his bhikshu disciples take their meal, food will be offered for you to eat. You must no longer eat dragons."
