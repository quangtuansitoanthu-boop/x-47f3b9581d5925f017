Transliteration: Thị cố A-nan, nhược bất đoạn dâm tu thiền định giả, như chưng sa thạch dục kỳ thành phạn. Kinh bá thiên kiếp kỳ danh nhiệt sa. Hà dĩ cố? Thử phi phạn bổn, thạch sa thành cố.

Translation: Therefore, Ananda, cultivating dhyana without cutting off lust is like cooking sand and stone hoping to make rice. After hundreds of thousands of kalpas, it is still called hot sand. Why? Because it is not the basis of rice; it is made of stone and sand.

Commentary: You see, now the Buddha gives an analogy. He tells Ananda, "You do not yet believe this, so the Tathagata will explain for you. Therefore, Ananda, cultivating dhyana without cutting off lust is like cooking sand hoping to make rice."

If one is not strict with lust yet daily cultivates dhyana, then on the one hand one cultivates, on the other one still leaks (outflows). Everything gained in cultivation effort will be lost tenfold through outflows; if one cultivates tenfold, one will lose a hundredfold through outflows. If one cannot rid oneself of lust, yet sits in meditation longing for enlightenment, aiming at a topsy-turvy joy, this is just like cooking sand hoping to make rice.

After hundreds of thousands of kalpas, it is still just hot sand. It is simply futile. Why? Because it is not the basis of rice; it is only sand and stone.

You hope for enlightenment without abandoning lust. That is just like cooking sand hoping to make rice.

There is nothing more to say here. If you are strict with lust, then though you spend all day with objects of lust, there is no problem. There will be nothing lustful — no male marks, no female marks, no human marks, no self marks, no being marks, no life-span marks. Some shameless people say, "That is my way of life." Just saying it is your way of life is not enough. There is no proof. How do you know it is your way of life? If you live that way, basically you will not know who you are. Do you suppose you have no lustful ideas?

If not, then simply not. No need to go around advertising it. Just show that you truly do not live that way. If you truly have no lust, then:
Nhãn kiến sắc, nội vô hữu
Nhĩ văn trần, tâm bất tri.
The eye sees forms, yet within nothing remains;
The ear hears sense-objects, yet the mind does not know. Then you have some provisions with you. And if you can reach the point of walking, standing, sitting, and lying down together with someone who is an object of lust with no problem at all — no sexual thought arising — that counts. One cannot say the mind still chases lust while gritting one's teeth and insisting, "I can still endure it." That is not enough. It must truly be that in the mind not a single lustful thought arises, the mind unmoving — only then is there truly not a trace of lust in one's mind. That is genuine. If from time to time you still notice what a woman eats, then you still fail as usual.

One day someone became enlightened and sought a teacher for certification.
The teacher asked: "What have you awakened to?"
The person answered: "Oh! Before I never realized it, but now I know clearly that bhikshunis are women."
With the Buddha eye, the teacher saw the person had indeed become enlightened. The teacher certified:
"You speak rightly."
You will say: "Who does not know that?"
