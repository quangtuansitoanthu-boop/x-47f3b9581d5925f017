THO TAM

Venerable Tho Tam, styled Chuyen Tay, of the Mao family, was a native of Fangcheng in eastern Zhejiang. From birth he took only plain and simple food, never using milk, meat, or fish. Grown up, he grew weary of the dusty world and resolved to leave secular life and cultivate the Way. At eighteen he entered Xiaoling Mountain Temple west of the city and, under Dharma Master Gioi Am, shaved his head and left the household life.

When he was about to receive the complete precepts, Master Gioi Am fell gravely ill. Tho Tam reflected: "The propagation of the Buddha's teaching depends on venerable elders such as my teacher. Compared with that, my own body, fragile as morning dew, is hardly worth cherishing!". So he prepared fragrant water, bathed, went before the Three Jewels, burned incense and made a vow, then returned to his room and cut open his own belly, intending to slice off a piece of his liver to mix with medicine to cure his teacher. But no sooner had he drawn the knife lightly across his belly than, seized by agonizing pain, he fell fainting to the ground. After a while he revived and crawled back to his bed; by then the eastern sky was already bright. The assembly discovered him and hastened to bind his wound with medicine and bandages. When Master Gioi Am learned of it, he summoned Tho Tam and comforted him, saying: "Your filial devotion is strong indeed, but it is by no means the proper conduct of a practitioner. Moreover, I myself know the season and conditions: since birth is fundamentally unborn, what is there that perishes? What you did sprang entirely from deluded fancy. Yet
out of pity for one sincere thought of filial devotion, I shall linger on a little while!". Sure enough, about a month after Tho Tam had received the complete precepts and returned, Master Gioi Am passed away. Tho Tam, filial by nature, grieved beyond measure and arranged the encoffining and burial with meticulous care in every detail.

Before long, he entrusted the temple's affairs to his Dharma brother Lien Duong and journeyed to study in various places. He strictly observed the precepts, practiced the ascetic disciplines, went barefoot and bareheaded, wearing but a single robe in winter and summer, and constantly devoted himself to Buddha-recitation. Seeing this, people called him the Barefoot Great Master. In the autumn of the Xinsi year of the Guangxu era, Tho Tam returned to Xiaoling Mountain Temple. At that time a long drought had parched the land, and the villages round about had all set up altars to pray for rain. The district magistrate, Magistrate Ton, was anxiously heartsick, praying day and night without response. Moved by compassion, Tho Tam went straight to see him, comforted him not to grieve excessively, and took the responsibility of praying for rain upon himself. The next day, a Renzi day, he took his bowl to a dried-up ravine stream and recovered an object shaped like a gecko. On the Yimao day, Tho Tam established an altar, recited mantras, and made obeisance through the six periods. In the Yin hour of the Bingchen day, rain fell from the sky but stopped after a few moments. Magistrate Ton sent the local gentry, led by Mr. Ly Tieu Nham, to request further prayers. The Master said: "Do not worry, gentlemen; tomorrow there will be heavy rain!". That night Tho Tam sincerely recited mantras and prayed, bowing in homage until dawn. Sure enough, at the noon hour of the Dingsi day, rain poured down as if overturned from buckets until evening, and the fields were filled brimful with water. The people of the region rejoiced. Magistrate Ton led the gentry up the mountain to give thanks. The magistrate, observing the manners of a disciple, personally wrote four large characters, "Eight Dragons Bestow Blessings," to commemorate the event. The Master remained calm and tranquil, showing nothing unusual. Magistrate Ton sighed: "Only now do I know how profound monastic virtue is — beyond conception!".

In the autumn of the Renwu year, Tho Tam closed his retreat hut, cutting off all involvements. Each day he recited the Great Compassion Mantra one hundred and eight times and the Buddha's name one hundred thousand times. In addition he recited the names of Avalokiteshvara and Mahasthamaprapta a thousand times, and bowed thirty times each to the Buddha Shakyamuni of the present and to the Three Holy Ones of the West. In his hut he kept a dog and a cat, for whom he daily administered refuge and prayers. Because of this, the cat would not catch mice and the dog would not eat filth. After three years, on the nineteenth day of the ninth month, he emerged from retreat. At the beginning of the eleventh month, Tho Tam caught a light dysentery for a few days and recovered. But from then on his body gradually weakened. The monks took turns attending him, but he would not allow it, saying: "As renunciates, each of us has his own course of practice; we must not cause one another to neglect it. If the time truly comes, I shall have someone call for you!". On the evening of the twenty-sixth, Tho Tam summoned his disciples to his bedside and instructed them: "Tonight I shall return to the West; quickly bring fragrant water!". After shaving his head, bathing, and changing his robes, he sat in lotus posture, intoned the hymn of praise to the Buddha, and bade the assembly join in. After reciting the Buddha's name for more than a hundred utterances, his voice grew gradually fainter and his head bowed slightly. Suddenly he raised his head upright and said: "I am going! Take care, all of you!". Then, loudly reciting the Buddha's name once more, he passed away. That night the dog and cat kept in the hut likewise sat upright and passed away. Everyone said they had followed the Master in rebirth.

This was in the Yiyou year of the Guangxu era.
