Translation: If there is a good man who delights in learning to leave the home-life and upholds the precepts, I appear before him in the body of a bhikshu, speak Dharma for him, and help him accomplish his wish.

Commentary: If there is a good man who delights in learning to leave the home-life and upholds the precepts. Such people study the sutras and resolve to live the homeless life. Which homes do they leave? They leave three kinds of homes:
1. They leave the worldly home.
2. They leave the home of afflictions.
3. They leave the home of the three realms.

The worldly home is one's family. The home of afflictions means dwelling in ignorance. We must escape from the home of ignorance. The three realms, also called the Triple Existence, refer to life in the Desire Realm, the Form Realm, and the Formless Realm. It is not until one leaves the home of the Triple Realm that one certifies to Arhatship.

When such people leave home, they receive and uphold the precepts. The precepts are most important. One who keeps the precepts cannot lie or exaggerate. At the minimum, keep the five precepts: no killing, no stealing, no sexual misconduct, no lying, and no intoxicants or drugs. The precepts here, of course, are not only the first ones. Such people receive and uphold the five precepts, the eight precepts, the Bodhisattva's ten major and forty-eight minor precepts, the bhikshu's 250 precepts and the bhikshuni's 348 precepts. They keep the precepts and never violate them. I appear before them in the body of a bhikshu, speak Dharma for them, and help them accomplish their wish.

A bhikshu has three meanings:
1. Mendicant;
2. Frightener of demons;
3. Destroyer of evil.

Every day bhikshus carry their bowls to beg for food. They do not cook food for themselves. Thus they are called mendicants. There are three ordination platforms when receiving the complete precepts. When ascending the platform to receive bhikshu precepts, the Karmadana master asks:
"Are you a great hero?"
The precept candidate answers: "Yes, I am a great hero."
The Karmadana asks further: "Have you brought forth the bodhi resolve?"
The candidate answers: "Yes, I have brought forth the bodhi resolve."
Frightener of demons: When a person has been transmitted the bhikshu precepts, the earth-traveling yakshas pass this news to the space-traveling yakshas, the news passes to the flying yakshas, until it reaches the demons on the heavens of the Six Desires. They announce: "A person in the world has just left home and become a bhikshu." This terrifies the Demon King, who says, "My retinue will lose one person and the Buddha's retinue gains one more."

Destroyer of evil: Which evil does a bhikshu destroy? The evil of afflictions. One never knows why people give rise to afflictions all the time. When they give rise to afflictions, they lose every talent except the ability to turn toward evil. For example, a lifelong vegetarian may want to quit, and first he decides to end his eating of pure food. He decides: "I will eat meat." Once affliction has arisen, they forget everything except finding ways to create evil karma. Or suppose someone has quit smoking for a long time. When he gives up, the first thing he does is grab a cigarette. And before long he will be back to heroin, opium, and every drug in a single breath!
