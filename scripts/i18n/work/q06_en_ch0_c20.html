Enabling living beings who merge into my body and mind to obtain fourteen kinds of fearlessness merit. I have attained fourteen fearlessness merits.

Sutra text: 一者由我不自觀音,以觀觀者, 令彼十方苦惱眾生。觀其音聲即得解脫。

Transliteration: Nhất giả do ngã bất tự quán âm, dĩ quán quán giả, linh bỉ thập phương khổ não chúng sanh, quán kỳ âm thinh tức đắc giải thoát.

Translation: First, because I do not regard sounds by myself, but regard the regarder, I enable the suffering beings of the ten directions to regard their sounds and at once attain liberation.

Commentary: This is one of the fourteen fearlessness merits. First, because I do not regard sounds by myself, but regard the regarder, I enable the suffering beings of the ten directions in the three periods to regard their sounds and at once attain liberation.

It is not that he regards only the sounds of himself alone; he regards the sounds of beings in the world. "Because my effort of returning the hearing to hear the self-nature has been perfected, I need not regard myself. Rather, I can regard all beings throughout the world and help those suffering beings attain liberation from suffering. I listen to their cries of suffering, and I rescue them to liberation."

Sutra text: 二者知見旋復,令諸眾生設入大火火不能燒。

Transliteration: Nhị giả tri kiến triền phục, linh chư chúng sanh thiết nhập đại hoả, hoả bất năng thiêu.

Translation: Second, my knowledge and views having turned back, I enable beings, though they enter a great fire, not to be burned by the fire.

Commentary: My knowledge and views having turned back — meaning Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva has the function of shining the light back into his own mind — so I can enable beings, though they enter a great fire, not to be burned by the fire. If beings constantly cultivate and uphold Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's name, though they walk into a raging fire, the fire cannot burn them.

Sutra text: 三者觀聽旋復。令諸眾生大水所漂,水不能溺。

Transliteration: Tam giả quán thính triền phục, linh chư chúng sanh đại thuỷ sở phiêu, thuỷ bất năng nịch.

Translation: Third, my regarding and hearing having turned back, I enable beings swept away by great waters not to be drowned by the waters.

Commentary: Someone thinks: "I will test whether Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva truly responds. I will sit upon a pile of firewood and set it ablaze to see whether it burns."

In that case, you will be burned at once. "Then why does the sutra say that if one enters a raging fire, the fire will not burn?" That is because of the word "if" — meaning it only happens when you have no calculation at all.

Someone else says, "Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva says that if a person falls into deep water, they will not drown, so I will jump into the sea to test whether I drown." Certainly you will drown. Only when you unexpectedly meet with suffering or peril will Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva rescue you. But if you deliberately scheme to test him, you will receive no response, because fundamentally you do not believe in Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva. If you truly believed, there would be no reason to test. The best course is not to test him.

Sutra text: 四者斷滅妄想,心無殺害。令諸眾生入諸鬼國鬼不能害。
