Transliteration: Chung bất tự ngôn ngã chơn bồ-tát, chơn A-la-hán. Tiết Phật mật nhân khinh ngôn vị học.

Translation: Never declaring of themselves, "I am truly a Bodhisattva, truly an Arhat," carelessly telling the unlearned, leaking the Buddhas' secret cause.

Commentary: They may be Bodhisattvas, Arhats, Buddhas manifesting in this world. But whether it is precisely Shakyamuni Buddha or Amitabha Buddha, Medicine Master Disaster-Relieving Longevity Buddha, or Ratnasambhava Buddha, Amoghasiddhi Buddha, or any other Buddhas, Bodhisattvas, Arhats, they never say "I truly am a Bodhisattva. Truly so; you should believe in me. I truly am a Bodhisattva." Such speech is impossible. If they proclaim themselves truly Bodhisattvas, truly Arhats — "Do you recognize who I am? I am an Arhat" — then you will know at once they are members of the demon kings' retinue. If someone praises you by saying you are a Bodhisattva, an Arhat, do not admit it even if you truly are so. You should not carelessly tell the unlearned, leaking the Buddhas' secret cause.

You should not reveal the Buddhas' secret cause. You must not lightly reveal your own origin. Then when may it be admitted? You may reveal it only when you are about to die. Do not reveal it before you depart.
When revealed, then do not remain.
While remaining, do not reveal.
The moment you reveal your whereabouts — for instance, that you are a transformation of that Bodhisattva — then you must leave this life's existence at once. So long as the words are unspoken, you may remain in the world, but once spoken, you will be in full trouble if you refuse to leave.

Sutra text: 唯除命終,陰有遺付。云何是人惑亂眾生成大妄語。

Transliteration: Duy trừ mạng chung, âm hữu di phó. Vân hà thị nhân hoặc loạn chúng sanh thành đại vọng ngữ?

Translation: Except at life's end, when there is a secret bequest. How can such people delude beings, committing great false speech?

Commentary: Except at life's end, when there is a secret bequest. How can such people delude beings? If you are a sage, then at life's end, you may tell people. But from now until then, you cannot tell anyone. By then, you may reveal it to those closest to you — perhaps one or two of your cloistered disciples. Those who do the opposite only cheat and delude beings by committing great false speech. If you have not attained the Way, yet say you have attained; if you have not certified to fruition, yet say you have certified — then you have spoken great false speech. In Qing-dynasty China, there was an eminent monk, Dharma Master Yin Guang. He was from Jiang Xi.
