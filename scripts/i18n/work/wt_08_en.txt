TITLE: 09. CHEWING BONES AND SUCKING MARROW
TOMTAT: A wealthy Taiwanese matron suffered chronic sinusitis from her habit of chewing fish bones and sucking out the marrow after cooking the best parts for her mother-in-law and children. Meeting Master Miaofa, she was taught about the offense of killing and eating innards, repented, and recovered.
-----
{align:center|**CHEWING BONES AND SUCKING MARROW**}

{align:justify|In the summer of 1996, the wife of a rich man came from Taiwan. She had suffered sinusitis for a dozen years without cure, so she crossed to the mainland and climbed Mount Wutai to honor the Master, begging him to reveal the cause of her illness.}

{align:justify|The Master asked:}

{align:justify|"When you married, your husband's family was none too well off?"}

{align:justify|"Yes indeed!" she answered.}

{align:justify|"After marriage you bore many children, the household grew ever poorer, life most straitened and hard..."}

{align:justify|"Yes indeed!"}

{align:justify|"Now and then you went to market and bought a few fish; cooked, you always offered the finest dishes to your mother-in-law. The body and tail you divided among the children. The remaining fish heads and bones you could not bear to throw away, so you put them in your mouth, crushing them and sucking the marrow. Later, though the household grew rich, the old habit was so deeply ingrained it continued. Even now, eating fish, you still love to chew the bones and swallow the marrow — is that so?"}

{align:justify|"It is, Master. It has become habit — I find fish bones delicious. But I thought what I eat is 'threefold-pure meat' (meat within the three conditions: eyes never saw it killed, ears never heard it killed, it was not killed for me to eat)? Is that not permitted to eat?" she asked, puzzled.}

{align:justify|"Teaching 'threefold-pure meat' is the Dharma as expounded for newcomers to Buddhism. For if they were told to give up meat at once, many, it is feared, could not do it. So they are first urged not to kill. Eating threefold-pure meat is only the Buddha's skillful means to guide beings — as their recitation and sutra-study diligently progress, they naturally cease to crave meat at all. Ultimately speaking, eating meat is killing! You must soon cut it off and strictly keep the precept against killing — that is the true principle," the Master explained.}

{align:justify|"Master, can eating fish bones and heads be an offense too?" she asked, still uncomprehending.}

{align:justify|"Flesh and bones are all parts of an animal's body — and we even say 'flay the skin, strip the sinews, chew the bones, swallow the marrow' to picture the mood of one blazing with hatred. Know that while we slaughter it, the animal's heart brims with terror of approaching death, and toward its slayer — and toward whoever devours it — with utter hatred. For all beings commonly cling to their bodies, cherishing them dearly, holding that flesh being eaten as their own. So even dead, the spirit wholly refuses to leave the body — whether its body is hacked, devoured alive, or already cooked by roasting, grilling, steaming, frying... Only the awakened, true practitioners, are unattached to the body; the poorly cultivated cling deeply, burn with much hatred. Thus, though dead, when the animal's spirit sees its body hacked, stir-fried, chewed, and swallowed, it still feels boundless agony and pain... So when the meat-eater flays and strips the animal, chews its bones and sucks its marrow, even slaughters it piteously — is it finished with one stroke of the blade? The torment sown in the animal grows without cease, and its resentment toward us runs most deep. For this very reason you carry illness into your body, sicknesses piling up, calamities never coming singly."}

{align:justify|Therefore a practitioner who honors the Buddha should not eat threefold-pure meat, still less the innards of animals — heart, liver, lungs, eyes, tongue, brains, marrow... Do not foolishly believe that eating an organ nourishes that organ; do not suppose such eating greatly nourishes. Little do you know: the more innards eaten, the deeper the enmity forged with those animals. In time, the innards of one's own body breed grave and dangerous illnesses.}

{align:justify|"Master, that is terrifying! Then can my sinusitis be cured?"}

{align:justify|"On condition you sincerely repent, vow to end meat-eating for good, do many good and meritorious deeds, and release many living creatures."}

{align:justify|"If each day you can recite one copy of the Kshitigarbha Sutra, dedicating it to beings throughout the dharma realm, a wholesome response will surely come. Release creatures where fish may easily live — do not release them into a fish farmer's pond to await being eaten. Release them into broad rivers, seas, lakes, and ponds, where they may truly escape with their lives. So too with birds and forest beasts — release them where they will be safe. If means allow, releasing turtles and many other creatures brings still greater merit, repaying the killing debts of former days. They all possess spirits; in this very life they may repay your kindness — such repayments we often see. Enough — do you now have faith enough to keep the precepts — never to kill and never to eat meat again?"}

{align:justify|"Yes — home again, I shall certainly do as you teach," she said with resolve.}

{align:justify|That chewing bones and swallowing marrow can summon illness is only natural. This time, hearing the Master's thorough exposition, I was most deeply impressed, and so recorded it with care — hoping Dharma friends will take note, take care, and all reap the benefit.}

{align:justify|As for the rich Taiwanese matron, afterward her nasal illness ended completely and for good; she even returned to Mount Wutai to pour out her gratitude and fervently thank the Master.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story CHEWING BONES AND SUCKING MARROW is a profound lesson in "eating animals' innards — summoning illness in the corresponding organ." The rich matron's habit of chewing fish bones and sucking marrow (though "threefold-pure meat") caused her unending sinusitis. Master Miaofa taught clearly: when an animal is slaughtered, its spirit still clings to its body, and seeing its flesh chewed and swallowed, its resentment runs deep. The more innards eaten, the deeper the enmity, the graver the illness in the corresponding organ. After repenting, vowing to end meat-eating for good, reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra, and releasing creatures, her illness healed completely. The story warns: "eat an organ, nourish that organ" is a mistaken and dangerous belief. Animals' innards are no tonic, but "debts of hatred" awaiting the day of collection. Whoever loves heart, liver, lungs, marrow, eyes... should awaken soon lest retribution fall.}
