Now listen attentively; I shall speak for you. Ananda and the assembly respectfully accepted the teaching.
When they heard the Buddha agree to speak Dharma, their joy grew greater. Perhaps the Buddha said, "Do you wish to hear this?" Everyone answered without hesitation. "We wish to hear. We wish to hear."
When deviant teachers teach their doctrines, the sole topic is lust. What they present runs counter to principle. This you should distinguish clearly. Sometimes Bodhisattvas also use kindness, loving speech, and protective minds to teach beings, because they know beings are submerged in lust. All beings have false thoughts of lust. So Bodhisattvas do not expect them to cut off greedy lust all at once. Rather they use every expedient to bring beings through and beyond lust. Then each being sets its own stopping point. This is the case with Bodhisattvas — entirely different from what deviant outsider teachers do.

Once Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva transformed into a fish-seller — a most beautiful woman going through the village with a basket of fish. No one in the village believed in the Buddha. But when the young men of the village saw this fish-selling girl, they all desired her. The village was not large, but there were at least a hundred men there, and everyone wished to marry this girl as wife. Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva, basket of fish in hand, said, "You are too many! I cannot marry a hundred people! But I have thought of a way to choose one of you as husband. I shall marry whichever of you first memorizes the Universal Door chapter of the Dharma Flower Sutra. Go home and study. I give you three days."

Three days later, some forty or fifty people had memorized this sutra. The fish-selling woman said, "Still too many. Though you have qualified, I cannot take fifty as husbands. Now go home and memorize the Vajra Sutra. I shall marry whoever first memorizes this sutra within five days." So these fifty top candidates went home and began studying the Vajra Sutra. Five days later twenty people had memorized this sutra. The fish-selling woman said, "Still too many. I cannot marry you all. I have another condition. Whoever can memorize the Dharma Flower Sutra within seven days shall deserve to be my husband."
The twenty men returned to study the Dharma Flower Sutra; seven days later one man had memorized this sutra. His name was Ma, born of a wealthy family. So a grand wedding took place and everyone in the village was invited. That night, after the feast ended, they went home — but who knew the bride suffered a heart attack and suddenly died.
And the groom had wasted all his effort. He had memorized the Universal Door chapter, the Vajra Sutra, the Dharma Flower Sutra, yet still had not married a wife. At once a funeral was held; a monk appeared among the mourners in a purple kashaya. The monk asked: "What has happened?"
"Our new daughter-in-law has suddenly died; we are holding the funeral."

The monk answered: "There is nothing in the coffin! For whom are you holding the funeral? Open the coffin lid and look!"
Indeed, the coffin was empty. They were all amazed. "Where has she gone? Chase her! Catch her back!"
