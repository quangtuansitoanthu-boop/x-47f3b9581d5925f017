<p><strong>Coming to the cultivator seeking still seclusion, spreading its seat, preaching the Dharma.</strong></p>
<p>When he (the possessed person) comes to the cultivator seeking still seclusion, it spreads its seat, preaching the Dharma. (24), (25). <strong>It makes all listeners clearly know their past-life karma.</strong></p>
<p>Everyone feels as if knowing clearly in a dream what they were, who they were, in a past life. Some say: &#8220;In a distant eon I was a watchdog.&#8221; Some say: &#8220;I was a cat.&#8221; Others say: &#8220;I was a chicken.&#8221; Still others say: &#8220;In a past life I was an ox.&#8221; No one was a person.</p>
<p><strong>Or it</strong> (the demon king) <strong>tells someone there</strong>: &#8220;<strong>Though not yet dead, you have already become an animal.</strong>&#8221; What does it mean? This: &#8220;You were a dog in a past life &#8211; now, though not yet dead, you have turned into a dog again. Do you not believe me?&#8221; <strong>Then telling someone behind to step on the <em>tail</em> of the one in front</strong>: it tells everyone: &#8220;He does not believe what I say &#8211; go behind him and step on his tail and see if he can rise.&#8221;</p>
<p>Then it stamps its foot hard on the ground, saying it is stepping on the tail of the one in front. <strong>Suddenly that person cannot rise</strong> &#8211; surely you would think he really has spiritual powers. After the demon says that person has a <em>tail</em>, then tells another to step on it, and the person cannot rise &#8211; the person has no choice but to believe it: &#8220;He says I am an animal &#8211; now I truly have a tail; if not, why, when someone steps on my tail behind, can I not rise?&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>Thereupon the whole assembly bows in admiration.</strong></p>
<p>&#8220;How miraculous!&#8221; they say. &#8220;He knew beforehand that person would turn into an animal while still alive &#8211; he must be a living Buddha or Bodhisattva.&#8221; In fact they have been fooled by the demon without knowing it &#8211; meeting a ghost or demon king yet mistaking it for a Bodhisattva: truly the upside-downness of beings.</p>
<p><strong>If anyone gives rise to a doubting thought, the demon at once knows the intention.</strong></p>
<p>If in the assembly someone doubts, thinking: &#8220;How can that be? Truly unreasonable&#8221; &#8211; the moment the doubting thought arises, the demon knows and replies: &#8220;So you do not believe me?&#8221; These people look back at it a moment and say: &#8220;Oh! He truly is a Bodhisattva &#8211; I never spoke what I thought, yet he knows it clearly, exposing all my doubts. How strange!&#8221; From then on he dares show no disbelief.</p>
<p><strong>Beyond the Buddha's moral code, it redoubles ascetic rigor.</strong></p>
<p>It says: &#8220;The Buddha's moral code is not enough for me &#8211; I shall establish a new code for you. I want you to become new Buddhas, and I shall found a new Buddhism: present-day Buddhism is too outdated, no longer fitting. Now is the age of science, the nuclear age &#8211; everything must be modernized and improved; old ways of thinking are no longer useful; that ancient Buddhism has no more application.&#8221;</p>
<p>That is how it transforms Buddhism &#8211; saying everyone can turn into venerable elders, bhikshus, or anyone they wish.</p>
<p>It <strong>slanders bhikshus</strong>.</p>
<p>It says: Bhikshus! What are bhikshus? It plays with the name, saying: &#8220;You call him a bhikshu? I call that a loach&#8221; &#8211; in Chinese, the word bhikshu and <em>ni-qiu</em> 泥 鰍 (loach) sound alike at the end.</p>
<p>It <strong>scolds its followers, exposing people's affairs.</strong></p>
<p>It reviles and scolds its followers whenever it pleases, calling them: &#8220;You are dogs, cats, pigs, rabbits&#8230;&#8221; The followers hear its scolding, accepting such abuse, thinking it a Bodhisattva: &#8220;He says I am a pig &#8211; then a pig I am; he says I am a dog &#8211; then a dog I am.&#8221; They dare not argue back. The demon king has a fierce power enchanting everyone, making everyone believe whatever it says.</p>
<p><strong>It exposes people's affairs</strong>: for instance, a man and woman doing something slightly improper &#8211; it tells the woman: &#8220;You did that thing with that man at that place.&#8221; The woman thinks: &#8220;How does he know?&#8221; Or it exposes before the public: &#8220;Here are two scoundrels &#8211; they did improper things, things inconvenient to speak of, at that place and that. Ask her about it &#8211; she will not dare deny!&#8221; It wants to bring up what they actually did, so they dare not deny. It does so to prove to everyone: it has spiritual powers, knowing even things about to happen. It exposes people's private faults, <strong>heedless of ridicule or suspicion</strong> &#8211; revealing each personal secret, fearing no contempt.</p>
<p><strong>Its mouth delights in foretelling coming fortune and calamity, and when the time arrives, not a hair off.</strong></p>
<p>It loves predicting fortune and calamity &#8211; loves saying things like: &#8220;Be careful &#8211; tomorrow is a bad day for you; someone wants to poison you &#8211; examine your food closely, or you will die of poison.&#8221;</p>
<p>It loves foretelling both good and bad beforehand. When the matter occurs, indeed not a hair off: when the event occurs, it exactly matches its prediction. That is why everyone believes it &#8211; such a demon king makes people believe more readily than a Bodhisattva.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Thử đại lực quỷ, niên lão thành ma, não loạn thị nhân. Yếm túc tâm sanh, khứ bỉ nhân thể. Đệ tử dữ sư, đa hãm vương nạn. Nhữ đương tiên giác, bất nhập luân hồi. Mê hoặc bất tri, đọa vô gián ngục.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: This is a mighty-power ghost, aged long into a demon, harassing this person. Sated, it arises and leaves that person's body. Teacher and disciple mostly fall into legal peril. You should be awakened beforehand, and not enter the turning wheel. Deluded and unknowing, one falls into the uninterrupted hell.</strong></p>
<p>Commentary: <strong>This is a <em>mighty-power ghost</em></strong>, <strong>a demon grown old</strong> &#8211; demons turning into ghosts in old age. Just as people who do not cultivate, planting virtuous causes, in old age turn into sprites &#8211; as I often tell you. The Chinese have a saying: &#8220;Old yet undying makes a villain &#8211; <em>growing old without dying is villainy.</em>&#8221;</p>
<p>When people live long and pass through many experiences, they often do bizarre things; likewise, long a demon, it turns into a ghost.</p>
<p><strong>It harasses the cultivator</strong> &#8211; jealous of the cultivator's effort, demons love destroying the cultivator's samadhi power.</p>
<p><strong>Sated, it leaves the possessed person's body. Then both teacher and disciple fall into legal peril</strong> &#8211; likewise tantamount to falling into the hells. Hence the saying:</p>
<p><em>The ignorant transmitting to the ignorant,</em><br />
<em>One transmission, both ignorant;</em><br />
<em>The master descends into hell,</em><br />
<em>The disciples crowd in after.</em></p>
