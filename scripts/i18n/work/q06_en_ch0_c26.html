Provisional translation:
Neither great nor small, neither inside nor outside.
One cultivates oneself, understands oneself, arranges for oneself.

That is, the Master cultivated by himself, understood by himself, and then buried himself. After the Master finished carving the couplet, he sat in the tomb, closed the stone door, and passed into stillness. He entered nirvana. That is an inconceivable matter. Thus when he had passed away, that was truly a great affair. His power of liberated ease was immensely great! I met the Master at Su Zhou on Ling Yen Mountain. The Master cultivated for himself and for everyone else. He troubled no one at all.

Thus I can enable dull and stupid beings — atyantikas — forever to leave delusion and darkness.

They have no wisdom and so they can see through and clearly understand nothing at all. Like a blunted knife that can cut nothing. They mistake right for wrong and wrong for right. But Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva can enable such people to leave far behind the obstructions that envelop them.

"Atyantika" is Sanskrit, meaning "extreme evil." If you suggest these people do something good, they never will. Rather than give someone a coin to do good, they clutch the coin in hand until the copper melts. If you tell them to help someone, their reaction is to consider it insane. "My money is for me to spend. Why should I give it to others?" Yet Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva can help such people escape their delusion and dark thinking. Those who will not help others are those with not a glimmer of light in their own natures; thus they are dark and stupid kinds of people.

The passage above mentioned greed and anger. This sutra passage speaks of the mind of delusion. The Dharma Flower Sutra says: If a person is full of anger, constantly reveres and recites Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's name, then delusion can be eradicated.

There was a very rich man, very greedy for money and unwilling to share with anyone. He had three sons. He named the eldest son Gold, the second son Silver. As for the third son, he feared the boy would give his money away in good works instead of hoarding it, so he named him Karmic Obstruction. When the old man was dying, he called the eldest son over and said, "I am about to go. Will you go with me?"

Gold said, "You senile old fool! How could I die with you? Ordinarily you loved me most. Now at death, why do you want to harm me?" Gold would not go with his father.

He thought, "Then I shall ask the second son." Then he called Silver over. "Your brother will not go with me. Will you? You have always been most filial."

The second son answered, "If you die, then die alone. Though I am your son, I cannot die along with you. You are most peculiar. I am still so young." Gold would not die along with his father; Silver neither. He called the third son, Karmic Obstruction. "Ordinarily you have been most disobedient, so I never loved you much. But now I am about to die, and your brothers Gold and Silver will not follow me. Think carefully whether you can follow me?" Karmic Obstruction answered, "I need not think at all. Of course I shall go. Now look at Gold and Silver, whom you loved most, yet they cannot grant your request. But I, Karmic Obstruction, shall follow you wherever you go. In life I accompany you, and in death I shall follow you too. See who is the most filial son?"

Nothing can be carried along.
Only karmic obstructions follow one.
