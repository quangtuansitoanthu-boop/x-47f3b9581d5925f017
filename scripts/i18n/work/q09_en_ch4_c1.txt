<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Ananda, when the feeling skandha has become empty and wondrous, no longer entangled in deviant concerns</strong> &#8211; not a single evil thought can stir the cultivator anymore.</p>
<p><strong>The perfect samadhi disclosed, within samadhi</strong> &#8211; <strong>the mind longs for perfect brightness, urging thought onward, greedily seeking skillful means.</strong></p>
<p>Urging means excessively refining the inward contemplation of the mind &#8211; also the state of perfect brightness. The cultivator longs for skillful means to teach beings, and the greedy mind thus arises.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhĩ thời thiên ma, hậu đắc kì tiện, phi tinh phụ nhân, khẩu thuyết kinh pháp.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Then the heavenly demon, waiting for its convenient chance, sends its essence to possess another person, using that person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Then the heavenly demon</strong> (of the Six Desire heavens) <strong>waits for its convenient chance</strong>: knowing the cultivator's samadhi power is nearly complete, it waits for a convenient opening to possess. It watches for some trait in the cultivator to open the way in: when the cultivator's samadhi power lacks firmness, the entrance appears. Once the cultivator gives rise to a false thought, samadhi power is no longer firm and the heavenly demon at once gets its chance to possess &#8211; hence &#8220;the demon gets a convenient chance.&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>Sending its essence to possess another person, using that person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma</strong>.</p>
<p>The heavenly demon orders its followers: &#8220;Go there and destroy that cultivator's samadhi power.&#8221; Before the form and feeling skandhas are exhausted, the heavenly demon possesses and harasses the cultivator himself. But after the form and feeling skandhas dissolve and the cultivator reaches the scope of the thinking skandha, the heavenly demon can no longer possess the cultivator; it must possess someone else's body, then preach the Dharma to that cultivator through the mouth of the one whose body the demon has seized. That person lectures Sutras, but the Dharma he lectures is deviant Dharma &#8211; so you must recognize that error.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Kì nhân bất giác, thị  kỳ ma trước, tự ngôn vị đắc vô thượng niết-bàn. Lai bỉ cầu xảo, thiện nam tử xứ, phu tòa thuyết pháp. Kỳ hình tư tu, hoặc tác tỷ-khưu, linh bỉ nhân kiến, hoặc vi Đế thích , hoặc vi phụ nữ, hoặc tỷ-khưu ni, hoặc tẩm ám thất, thân hữu quang minh.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: That person, unaware he is possessed by a demon, claims to have attained unsurpassed nirvana. Coming to the place of the good man seeking skillful means, it spreads its seat and preaches the Dharma. In a moment its form transforms &#8211; appearing as a bhikshu, letting that person see; or as Shakra, or as a woman, or as a bhikshuni; or lying in a dark room, its body emitting light.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: You can see how harmful it is to meet this demonic state.</p>
<p><strong>That person</strong> truly <strong>does not know he is demon-possessed</strong> &#8211; how absurd that a demon should come &#8220;help&#8221; him. <strong>Claiming to have attained unsurpassed nirvana</strong>: he proclaims he has gained the wondrous fruition of nirvana.</p>
<p>When <strong>the demon comes to the place of the good man seeking skillful means, spreading its seat, preaching the Dharma</strong> for the good man longing for skillful means to hear.</p>
<p><strong>In a moment</strong> &#8211; very quickly &#8211; it can transform, <strong>appearing as a bhikshu</strong>, <strong>letting the good man see</strong> &#8211; <strong>appearing as Shakra</strong> (King of Gods), <strong>a woman, or a bhikshuni</strong>. When the good man seeking skillful means sees such transformations, he thinks: &#8220;He must be Guanyin Bodhisattva&#8221; &#8211; never knowing it is a heavenly demon. That is why you should cling to no state you realize, whatever state it may be.</p>
<p><strong>Or lying in a dark room, its body emitting light</strong>: when one ignorant of the Buddhadharma sees this, he marvels: &#8220;Oh, his body emits light &#8211; if he is not a Buddha, he must at least be a Bodhisattva or an Arhat.&#8221; They do not recognize the person as demon-possessed, the demon king displaying spiritual powers to delude the cultivator.</p>
<p>Therefore, until the cultivator clearly and thoroughly understands the Buddhadharma truly, he should not let himself be turned by whatever state he sees. Do not be swayed by those states. How should you deal with such a state? Regard it as if nothing existed &#8211; as if never seen. If you grow excited on seeing demons, you will be turned by demonic states. How then should you act? Simply maintain the spirit of the Middle Way &#8211; neither liking nor disliking, seeing as if never seen, hearing as if never heard. You should not think: &#8220;How marvelous &#8211; I wish to meet it again.&#8221; With such a thought, you at once fall into error.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Thị nhân ngu mê, hoặc vi bồ-tát. Tín kì giáo hóa, diêu đãng kì tâm, phá Phật luật nghi, tiềm hành tham dục.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: That person, deluded, mistakes it for a Bodhisattva. Believing its teaching, his mind is swayed; breaking the Buddha's moral code, he secretly practices greed and lust.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>That person</strong> cultivating right samadhi, <strong>deluded</strong> at this point, <strong>mistakes it for a Bodhisattva</strong>: seeing one who can appear as a bhikshu, Shakra, a laywoman, or a bhikshuni, or suddenly change from one person to another, the cultivator deems this person surely a Bodhisattva. Then <strong>believing the demon's teaching, his mind is swayed, breaking the Buddha's moral code</strong>.</p>
<p>What should we observe in a cultivator? See whether he keeps the Buddha's precepts: if he does not uphold them, he is surely a demon; if he strictly keeps the precepts, he is truly a Bodhisattva. Yet some call themselves Buddhists, style themselves Elder Sangha Masters, piling on still more titles.</p>
<p>And <strong>secretly practicing greed and lust</strong>: secretly committing lustful acts, striving to hide them from people.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Khẩu trung hảo ngôn tai tường biến dị, hoặc ngôn Như Lai, mỗ xứ xuất thế, hoặc ngôn kiếp hỏa, hoặc thuyết đao binh. Khủng bố ư nhân, linh kì gia tư, vô cố hao tán.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Its mouth delights in speaking of calamities and anomalies &#8211; saying a Tathagata has appeared in some place, or speaking of eon-fires, or of wars and weapons &#8211; terrifying people, causing them to pointlessly squander their family wealth.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>That person</strong> possessed by the demon <strong>delights in speaking of calamitous anomalies</strong>, strange upheavals.</p>
<p>What does the possessed person love to speak of? They love speaking of disasters and tragic upheavals &#8211; telling of every calamity sure to occur exactly somewhere, or an auspicious omen occurring somewhere, or an unusual upheaval about to befall.</p>
<p><strong>Or saying a Tathagata has appeared in some place</strong>.</p>
<p>He says: &#8220;Do you know &#8211; that Buddha, of such a name, has already appeared in the world over there!&#8221;</p>
<p><strong>Or speaking</strong> of the tragedy of <strong>eon-fires</strong>, <strong>or speaking</strong> of <strong>wars and weapons</strong>: he says: &#8220;The three calamities &#8211; water, fire, wind &#8211; and the eight difficulties have come upon us!&#8221;</p>
