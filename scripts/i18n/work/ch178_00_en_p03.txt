{align:justify|Some even say the "Ksitigarbha Sutra" must not be recited at home—for reciting summons little ghosts… Others say only the topmost floor may enshrine Buddhas—worshipping where people walk above disrespects… (Then apartment dwellers may never worship?)—then the Shurangama Mantra must be recited at 3–5 in the morning, must be this, must be that, and so on…}
{align:justify|All are deviant theories obstructing sutra reciters, mantra holders, Buddha worshippers… Perhaps speakers mean well—but such trumpetings aid the demon king unawares.}
{align:justify|Understand: any hour may invite Buddhas home—and "eye-opening"? Mere form. Know: Buddhas need no eye-opening from us! Eye-opening means declaring: from today I begin bowing to, learning from Buddhas. Saying "un-eye-opened, no Buddha present" is utterly mistaken!}
{align:justify|Enshrining Buddhas at home helps us ever behold the Buddha's image—reminding that our hearts ever hold the Buddha's true wisdom light—so all we do must hold right understanding and right views. Most needed, best worship: oneself ending greed, anger, delusion, diligently practicing precepts, meditation, wisdom.}
{align:justify|So inviting Buddha statues home, enshrine them wherever pleases and suits you best. After arranging incense, flowers, offerings, offering incense and bowing—count it eye-opened! The heavenly-eyed shall see the statue radiate golden light. Even without heavenly eyes, hands may feel this energy (for instance, meters away gently pushing toward the statue, feeling an invisible resisting force). I only tell you: statues invited home, worshipped with reverent bowing, differ—the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas shall manifest divisions present there (nor cling to testing whether blessed energy is felt or golden light seen?)… Invited home, while bowing you may recite 3, 7, 21, 49 Great Compassion Mantras, or the Heart Sutra, or the Diamond Sutra, the Amitabha Sutra (or whatever sutras you wish)—all good (as for Ksitigarbha images, 1–3 rounds may be recited)… In short, whatever seems most solemn and reverent—enshrine so! The Buddha says "all is mind-made"—so after inviting statues home, strictly keeping the five precepts, practicing the ten goods, you become true Buddha disciples at once! Conversely, clinging to forms, visiting many temples for eye-openings, yet never minding the five precepts and ten goods—the Buddha shall never descend upon your home!}
{align:justify|"As for the lost bicycle, tell me: working once, did you take from your unit six meter-and-half long timbers and one dozen-meters long? Yes—old timbers the unit never used—so I took them home building a kitchen."}
{align:justify|"But your unit never permitted it—you took them home on your own! That is stealing! Seeds of evil sown then—now the bicycle lost—fruits ripened! Thanks to inviting and bowing to Buddhas' merit, karma-repayment, karma-dissolving begins. You still covet more unit property—hasten repentance, perhaps sins dissolve, or heavy retribution turns light… The lost bicycle and statue-inviting hours have nothing to do with one another! May all Buddha disciples never be so superstitious!"}
{align:justify|MY AUNT}
{align:justify|Thinking how rescuing animals and harming animals both bear retribution, I resolved to write this out for beginners' understanding.}
{align:justify|From my taking refuge to that time counted the second winter. Master Diệu Pháp was then staying at my house. One day my aunt called from Xi'an, telling: half a month earlier, snow-iced roads—walking to the plant, she carelessly fell breaking her right leg; friends carried her to hospital. Lying on the operating table she discovered fellow plant workers (herself included) totaled seven (all injured) hospitalized. Except one cracked hip, the rest all broke kneecaps.}
{align:justify|Medicines then were scarce; operated, doctors said her leg could hardly fully recover—future lameness. So she agonized.}
{align:justify|Three days later she left hospital home to heal. Past forty then, thinking "Lamed, truly unbearable!"… She knew nothing of my Buddhism, much less Master Diệu Pháp. Pure misery moved her to call and tell me. As we talked the master was present—so I deliberately spoke loud, repeating her plight—aiming for the master's ears. Then I told her how the master and I met. As I spoke she believed at once—begging the master's instruction. So I passed her the phone.}
{align:justify|The master asked her:}
{align:justify|"Four years ago did you rescue and bandage a white pigeon with a broken leg?"}
{align:justify|Hearing it she thrilled—I beside heard as follows:}
{align:justify|"Yes, Master! Four years ago visiting a coworker sister's house—just inside the courtyard I saw a dozen pigeons ringing a limping one—curious, I neared—seeing the pigeon leg-broken—I called my friend over—hastily lifting the bird—we observed together—understanding it shot (by slingshot)—so we lanced its leg draining pus, disinfecting… then with wine and ointment bandaged its leg—then splinted sticks as crutches for its walking. At last the bird's leg healed. Master! You are truly gifted—knowing my years-ago deed… Has it to do with my broken leg?"}
{align:justify|The master asked again:}
{align:justify|"Are you fond of roast chicken—especially chicken thighs, delighting gnawing legs and feet?"}
{align:justify|"Yes—I love them!"}
{align:justify|"Market-bought chickens, though already killed—still you yourself in the kitchen wield the knife gutting… chopping chicken legs—stewing, cooking, steaming… For this cause your thigh bone broke—evil repaid with evil. But having once healed the broken-legged pigeon, your wound shall leave no aftermath—persist crutch-practicing, gradually recovering…"}
{align:justify|My aunt joyfully asked:}
{align:justify|"Gutting and chopping… chicken legs earned my broken leg—then gutting its belly—must hospitals gut my belly hereafter?"}
{align:justify|The master smiled:}
{align:justify|"You wake swiftly—have Quả Khanh send you Buddhist books to read…"}
{align:justify|That is my aunt's true story—her leg later fully recovered.}
{align:justify|LEAVING LUSTFUL THOUGHTS}
{align:justify|A young couple came asking Master Diệu Pháp—telling how, refuge-taken Buddhists, vegetarianism easy, wine and drugs all quit—only the lust precept, attempts failed again and again.}
{align:justify|The layman told how, lust arising, he often silently recited Nam mô Quan Thế Âm Bồ tát! But lustful thoughts still defeated him—so he suffered greatly, begging the master's guidance.}
{align:justify|The master instructed:}
{align:justify|"Your diligent practice deserves praise. Lotuses are holy—for from mud unstained. Shakyamuni Buddha left beautiful wife and beloved children, leaving home to practice—modeling the straight practicing road for us! Home-dwellers able to end lust equal monastics—exemplary world-transcending practitioners…"}
{align:justify|Reciting Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin) Bodhisattva's sacred name can help halt lustful minds. But do not wait for lust to rise before begging the Bodhisattva's rescue—ordinarily ever recite 'Nam mô Quan Thế Âm Bồ tát!'—only so may lust be left!}
{align:justify|Most vital: first understand principles clearly—grasping the teachings, lust-precept keeping naturally succeeds. The more practiced, the more the truths dawn—faith firmer and steadier.}
{align:justify|I urge you both to study and memorize the 'Four Clear Teachings on Purity' in the Shurangama Sutra—then free moments silently recite, or softly read. Morning and evening strolls, cycling, bus-riding, outings—whatever… use all existing time to practice… Also memorize the Great Compassion Mantra, the Shurangama Mantra. Daily recite the 'Four Clear Teachings on Purity' at least one round—more, better. Above all before sleep recite one round. Beginners may take this as liturgy (home-dwellers may take the 'Four Clear Teachings on Purity' as morning-evening liturgy). Reciting aloud, not only self benefits—but surrounding beings (mortal eyes unseen) kneel listening—all benefit—so naturally boundless merit accrues.}
{align:justify|Diligently practicing so, within three months lustful thoughts in body and mind shall surely end—and ending nature ends too. Not only self ending lust—but even lustful thoughts in mind no more. Then you shall live most free and easy—no words can fully tell—this holy state only lust-enders fully feel, with wondrous peace within!}
{align:justify|Once lustful thoughts in body and mind all ended clean—even unmemorized, the 'Fourfold Pure Clear Instructions' may lifelong be practiced (for the Buddha's words are carved in the heart).}
{align:justify|Then discover the saying 'Nothing on earth more blissful than lust' is false! For lust's thrills last an instant—no true bliss at all—for continuing only wearies more—essence spent—then no bliss but misery… besides lust blazing breeding many ills—spousal conflicts… leading to divorce—even causing death, short life!}
{align:justify|Home-dwellers need proper restraint—but practitioners must end lust! For: 'Lust unended, dust unexited.'}
{align:justify|Some, lust ended a while, chance nocturnal emissions, wet dreams appear—normal phenomena—ignore them—they naturally cease.}
{align:justify|Once lust ended, not only body hale—but essence gathered, preserved—able to form relics firm beyond compare. The longer lust ended, at cremation the more relics remain. Relics are holy objects with Buddhas and Bodhisattvas—worldly sincere worship gains great benefit!}
{align:justify|So practitioners skillfully ending lust, killing, stealing, lying… shall surely attain the Bodhisattvas' supreme awareness.}
{align:justify|WHY DOES THE INFANT CRY}
{align:justify|One early spring, Tiet Nhan (a neighboring family) suddenly telephoned me for aid—telling how, New-Year visiting his boss's house, he met husband and wife fretting helpless over their newborn (barely a month old) screaming unceasing two days now. Hospitals finding no cause for the crying, their hearts burned like fire.}
{align:justify|Seeing this, Tiet Nhan hastily called me for rescue (I too clearly heard the infant wailing fiercely)—then passed the phone to the master.}
{align:justify|The master told Tiet Nhan to hand the phone to the baby's parents, asking:}
{align:justify|"Does your household love seafood (shrimp, crabs, fish…)?"}
{align:justify|They answered:}
{align:justify|"Yes. Taiwanese commonly eat these—we too!"}
{align:justify|The master said:}
{align:justify|"If now you both resolve to eat seafood no more, and recite the Buddha's name delivering the creatures you ate—your child shall instantly stop crying!"}
{align:justify|Not understanding, they challenged:}
{align:justify|"The baby's crying—what has it to do with our eating shrimp, crabs, fish…?"}
{align:justify|The master explained:}
{align:justify|"Taiwanese Buddhism flourishes—yet you two know nothing of Buddhism?"}
{align:justify|They answered:}
{align:justify|"We are outsiders—following Taiwan's Lao religion—never forbidding fresh live seafood…"}
{align:justify|The master said:}
{align:justify|"The consciousnesses of the aquatic creatures you ate hold grudges, lurking awaiting revenge. But your blessings unexhausted, young yang thriving—so these creditors turned harassing the little one. They manifest every ugly shape to frighten the child—do you see both his little arms waving ceaselessly midair? Resisting the strangely-shaped spirit-demons harassing… (frightening him crying unceasing). Creditors thus make you fret and worry—mind and strength withering—soon achieving vengeful aims!"}
{align:justify|Know: infants under three normally still hold flesh-eye powers (seeing realms human eyes cannot)—past three, worldly cravings growing gradually cloud all—so they see normally like adults. To stop creditors harassing and frightening the baby, right now husband and wife must at once vow to eat seafood no more! Declare so—then for the creatures you ate recite the sacred name 'Nam mô A Di Đà Phật' delivering them—done, the child shall stop crying at once. I know you disbelieve—but will you not test and see?"}
{align:justify|The other side hesitated a while—helpless, they vowed: "From now on we eat seafood no more!"…}
{align:justify|Then the master taught them reciting the sacred name 'Nam mô A Di Đà Phật!' They repeated but a few lines—and the baby's wails softened—then gradually ceased entirely.}
