Commentary: These Bodhisattvas and Arhats use their transformation bodies to become shramanas — those who have left home, whether having received the complete precepts or the novice precepts. Or they transform into white-robed lay followers. Lay followers do not live the family-abandoning life, and in India they are called "the white-robed." They protect the Triple Jewel. This is because monastics

Do not plow, yet still must eat;
Do not weave, yet still must wear.
So there must be at-home lay followers to make offerings.

Or some Bodhisattvas transform into kings among humans, or ministers. Or they transform into boys and girls. Thus even as wantons, widows. They may even transform into cheats, thieves, butchers, and peddlers. They may even transform into thieves, butchers, or dealers in such things as drugs. The Bodhisattvas and Arhats join them in the same deeds.

Why do they transform into such kinds of people? Because they wish to transform such people. To do that, they must use the Four All-Embracing Dharmas.
1. Giving.
2. Loving speech.
3. Beneficial conduct.
4. Same deeds.

First they embrace beings through giving. There are three kinds of giving:
1. Giving of wealth.
2. Giving of Dharma.
3. Giving of fearlessness.

If one has money, give money. If one knows the teaching, speak Dharma for them. If someone is afraid and confused, comfort and help them, thereby making them fearless — that is giving fearlessness. But to practice these methods, one must not crave or hope for return. You should not think, "Ah! Now I give in this way; in the future I shall gain many good things." Do and forget. Let go. That is called "the three wheels empty." The three aspects are: the giver, the gift given, and the receiver. You should practice giving in the spirit that it is what one should do, rather than accumulating merits. Giving Dharma is likewise. When you speak Dharma for listeners, you should not think, "My Dharma-speaking merit is immense; you should make offerings to me." Giving fearlessness is likewise. When giving, you should not calculate how much benefit you gain from it. Nor should you intend to give when you see benefit for yourself, while refusing to give when you see no benefit.

Second is embracing them through loving speech. For instance, the Buddha says to Ananda, "Excellent! Excellent!" And in that way, Bodhisattvas praise beings, saying, "You truly are a good boy. You are truly clever. You have most good roots."
Third, they embrace beings through beneficial conduct, meaning doing everything to help others.
Fourth, they embrace beings through same deeds. Meaning whatever they do, the Bodhisattvas do with them. Sometimes a Bodhisattva wishes to rescue a prostitute whose good roots have ripened, as Matanga's daughter mentioned in this sutra is an example.
