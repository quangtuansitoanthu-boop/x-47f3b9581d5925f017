Translation: World Honored One, I recall that in the past, countless kalpas ago as numerous as the sands of the Ganges, a Buddha appeared in the world named Avalokiteshvara. I brought forth the bodhi resolve in the presence of that Buddha. That Buddha taught me to enter samadhi through hearing, contemplating, and cultivating.

Commentary: Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva said to Shakyamuni Buddha: World Honored One, I recall that in the past, countless kalpas ago as numerous as the sands of the Ganges — reaching back a very long time, a great many kalpas too numerous to count, like the sands of the Ganges River — a Buddha appeared in the world named Avalokiteshvara. This is the Avalokiteshvara of the past. That is the Tathagata Avalokiteshvara, who also cultivated perfect penetration through the ear root. I brought forth the bodhi resolve in the presence of that Buddha. I brought forth the resolve to cultivate and certify to the Way of liberation. That Buddha taught me to enter right concentration through hearing, contemplating, and cultivating. The past Buddha Avalokiteshvara taught Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva the process of hearing, contemplating, and cultivating — that is, to enter samadhi through the wisdom of hearing, the wisdom of contemplating, and the wisdom of cultivating. "Contemplating" here does not mean using the discursive thought of the sixth consciousness. Rather, it means using the effort of sitting in meditation in cultivation.

Sutra text: 初於聞中,入流亡所。所入既寂,動靜二相,了然不生。如是漸增,聞所聞盡。盡聞不住,覺所覺空。空覺極圓,空所空滅。生滅既滅,寂滅現前。

Transliteration: Sơ ư văn trung, nhập lưu vong sở. Sở nhập ký tịch, động tĩnh nhị tướng liễu nhiên bất sanh. Như thị tiệm tăng, văn sở văn tận. Tận văn bất trụ, giác sở giác không. Không giác cực viên, không sở không diệt. Sanh diệt ký diệt, tịch diệt hiện tiền.

Translation: At first, dwelling in hearing, I entered the flow of perfect penetration and the state of what-is-heard ceased to be. The entering having become still, the two characteristics of movement and stillness clearly did not arise. Thus progressing step by step, hearing and what-is-heard both came to an end. Not dwelling in the ending of hearing, awareness and what-is-awakened-to became empty. With the emptiness of awareness perfected to the utmost, the emptiness and what-is-emptied both vanished. With arising and ceasing already quenched, still extinction manifested.

Commentary: At first, dwelling in hearing, I entered the flow of perfect penetration and the state of what-is-heard ceased to be.

With the wisdom of hearing, the cultivator hears what is within, not what is without. Not following after sense-objects of sound means not running after them. Earlier, the sutra text already spoke of not chasing after the six sense roots and not being directed by them. This is called:

Returning the hearing to hear the self-nature — turning the faculty of hearing inward to recognize one's own nature.
Turning the hearing inward means not listening to outer sounds, but instead turning back, directing inward, to hear one's own nature. That is,
gathering it into one's own body and mind.
"Within hearing." Pay special attention to these two words: it is not within the physical eye, not within ear-consciousness, not within mind-consciousness..., but precisely within the hearing-nature that neither ariss nor ceases — which is precisely the Tathagatagarbha nature.

Turning the light around to shine within — revolving the light back to illuminate the inside.
That means not seeking outside.

This passage of sutra text says that Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva "entered the flow," which means he turned his hearing inward to hear his own nature.

Entering the Dharma-nature flow of the sages — entering the flow of the Dharma-nature of the sagely ones.
