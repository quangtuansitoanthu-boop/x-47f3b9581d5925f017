TITLE: 03. THE UNFILIAL YOUTH
TOMTAT: The story of a wayward Taiwanese youth — disobedient, unfilial, a gangster who beat his parents and caused their deaths, then fell gravely ill with no cure. Meeting Master Miaofa at Mount Wutai, he was sternly rebuked, repented, took refuge, and recovered.
-----
{align:center|**THE UNFILIAL YOUTH**}

{align:justify|One day in 1995, in the guesthouse of a Mount Wutai monastery, some twenty people waited to consult Master Miaofa. They came from north and south, taking turns with their questions. And that master, replete with wisdom — no matter how weighty the questions his visitors raised, he resolved them all with perfect ease. Everything was like "treating an illness with instant effect, shaking a man awake" — all who heard felt as though drinking sweet dew, brimming with the joy of the Dharma.}

{align:justify|"Venerable Master! I have come from Taiwan to the mainland to have my illness examined. Three years ago I fell gravely ill; then my whole body broke out in pain, tormenting me until I could neither eat nor sleep, my limbs weak and strengthless — suffering beyond measure. The great hospitals of Taiwan examined me but found no illness; they only said I suffered from 'autonomic nervous disorder'... I have had injections, acupuncture, and every medicine, all to no avail. And taking so much medicine brought side effects besides. My body grows weaker by the day. This time I came to Beijing for examination, and they diagnosed exactly as in Taiwan. No one can find the cause of my illness. I am utterly disheartened. Before returning to Taiwan, I wished to climb Mount Wutai to honor the Bodhisattva; just now an elderly gentleman on the same bus kept praising you without cease — if you could heal my illness, no matter the cost, I would pay it all..."}

{align:justify|The speaker was past thirty, thin and gaunt; though his features were regular, his face was most gloomy. From him emanated a dark, sinister aura, in stark contrast to the virtuous assembly seated there.}

{align:justify|The Master's voice was not loud, but stern and cold. He said:}

{align:justify|"You are no disciple of mine; no need to call me Master! Nor am I a doctor — I examine no illnesses, much less dare take your money. Go back and find some other wise physician!"}

{align:justify|With that, the monk ordered him out, to the astonishment of all seated there. Never before had he treated anyone so. The Taiwanese (I have forgotten his name) felt humiliated; his sallow face suddenly flushed red.}

{align:justify|He said angrily:}

{align:justify|"Does not Buddhism preach compassion above all? Why can you examine others but refuse to examine me? And order me out besides — when I have never offended you!"}

{align:justify|The Master's voice was cutting and most severe:}

{align:justify|"You who dared strike and curse the very parents who bore and raised you — what offense against me would you fear?"}

{align:justify|These words deflated him at once like a punctured ball. He froze, eyes wide with shock, his face turning from flushed red to deathly pale, unable to utter a word. The guesthouse fell silent; every eye turned upon him.}

{align:justify|More than a minute passed before he humbly said:}

{align:justify|"How... do you know of my affairs? No one here knows me at all..."}

{align:justify|"I need no informer, for across your chest are written four great characters: 'WAYWARD AND UNFILIAL'!"}

{align:justify|Hearing this he started in terror and bent to look at his own chest; every eye in the room fastened on his chest too, searching and scrutinizing — but nothing unusual was to be seen.}

{align:justify|At this, he abruptly rose, stepped forward several paces, fell prostrate at the Master's feet, knocking his head until it bled, trembling as he begged the Master to save him.}

{align:justify|He told how since childhood he had been a delinquent youth. Stealing, fighting, cursing all under heaven... he had done it all. He disobeyed his parents' teaching, was expelled from school, drifted everywhere, and joined a triad gang. He collected protection money, deceived young women, robbed and plundered... there was no evil he did not do. When his father angrily beat him, he struck back so that his father fell sprawling; he shoved his mother down too and declared that if anyone tried to discipline him, he would set fire and burn the whole house down. His aged father, furious beyond bearing, fell sick and lay bedridden; his mother nursed him daily. Fearing besides that he would cause calamity outside with his evils, his parents soon followed one another to the grave...}

{align:justify|Kneeling before Master Miaofa, he confessed his past cruelties; when his account reached his parents' deaths, he could not hold back his sobs and burst into loud weeping. And so a "boulder" — hard, stubborn, difficult to tame — was softened by the Master's majestic virtue into lying humbly prostrate...}

{align:justify|My heart sighed deeply: it seems that good or bad, all people appear before the Master's eyes as though with writing upon their bodies — he sees through them all!}

{align:justify|"Very well, rise!" the Master said gently. "If you call me Master, you must obey my teaching. Can you do it?"}

{align:justify|He joyfully repeated:}

{align:justify|"Yes! Yes! I can. I will certainly mend my faults and repent, and be a good man from this day on."}

{align:justify|"Very well, I accept you as my disciple."}

{align:justify|He blissfully knocked his head again and again. The people in the room also rose in a stir, palms reverently joined. Today they had witnessed a moving scene, and their joyful praise knew no end.}

{align:justify|The Master told a young monk:}

{align:justify|"Take him to the main hall, teach him how to bow in repentance — let him perform a thousand prostrations in the great hall, and return only when his atonement is done."}

{align:justify|An hour and a half later, when the Taiwanese returned to pay homage to the Master, he looked clear in spirit and bright in vigor — lively and fresh, utterly different from before. He told us he had vowed before the Buddha to take refuge in Buddhism from that day on and mend his ways toward the good. And after making his vow, he felt his whole body light and at ease, as though his illness had healed.}

{align:justify|All that happened before our eyes drew unceasing praise from everyone present.}

{align:justify|The Buddha said: "All beings possess the Buddha nature; all can become Buddhas. Only because of deluded thoughts and attachments can they not realize it." The Buddha also said: "The sea of suffering is boundless; turn back and the shore is there. Lay down the butcher's knife and at once become a Buddha"... Before everyone's eyes, the delinquent youth of former days who had committed the unfilial, wayward offense — condemned by heaven and earth, bearing his punishment, suffering present retribution and unending illness — fortunately met an illumined master to convert him, and learned repentance and awakening, washing his heart and changing his face. This is truly the experience and proof of what the sutras say: "The Buddha is the true great king of physicians; the Buddha Dharma can cure all forty-eight thousand illnesses"...}

{align:justify|Once more I witnessed the boundless majesty of the Buddha Dharma. "The Buddha Dharma is in the world, never apart from worldly awakening" — we need only live each day "without the slightest carelessness," and our daily life will always accord with the Dharma, and we shall surely sweep away the misunderstandings that slander "Buddhism as superstition."}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story THE UNFILIAL YOUTH is forceful proof that "wayward unfilial conduct" is the cause sown, and lingering illness and suffering its fruit. The Taiwanese youth once beat his parents, threatened to burn the house, and caused both parents to die in grief — the gravest unfilial offense. When he met Master Miaofa, the Master needed no informer to "see" the four characters upon his chest. This reminds us that karma is never lost, and before illumined masters the human heart lies like an open book. Fortunately, when he sincerely repented, knocked his head until it bled, and performed a thousand prostrations, his karmic retribution was transformed and his illness dissolved. The story affirms: "The sea of suffering is boundless; turn back and the shore is there." However heavy one's sins, a single sincere thought of repentance, refuge in the Three Jewels, and true practice can turn evil to good, and lead one out of a living hell into the light.}
