NGO DOAN THANG

Layman Ngo Doan Thang, styled Thuong Dao, was a native of She county, Huizhou. In youth he often traveled trading about Suzhou and Hangzhou. While touring Tiger Hill temple and mountain, a monk chanced to meet him, studied his face, and said: "You too have wholesome roots, but alas, at twenty-nine you will meet drowning — what is to be done?". Frightened, Doan Thang asked for a means of rescue. The monk pondered a moment, then taught him: "From now on, refrain from killing and release living beings. Each day sincerely recite the Great Compassion Mantra and the Buddha's name, and you may yet escape!". Doan Thang believed and practiced, and often urged this upon others.

At twenty-nine, the layman took ship from Hangzhou homeward, with sixteen fellow travelers, seventeen in all. After sailing some dozens of miles, the tide suddenly came roaring, great waves rising in peril. Doan Thang recalled the monk's words and hastily joined his palms in Buddha-recitation. In a moment the boat capsized, and he and all the passengers were swept away by the waves. Amid dazed confusion, he heard a voice: "Ngo Doan Thang, for urging others to recite the Buddha's name, shall escape this calamity!". Hastily opening his eyes and looking about, he found himself lying on the shore, fishermen rousing him. Checking, hat, shoes, and baggage had all been swept away — only the eighteen-bead rosary he daily recited remained gripped in his hand. The other sixteen all sank without trace. From then on he believed all the more in the inconceivable merit of Buddha-recitation! Not returning home, he stayed and opened a physiognomy shop, using the connection to teach cause and effect and urge practice and recitation.

After the calamity, Doan Thang practiced with redoubled diligence. He branded his arm with incense, forming the four characters "Seeking Birth in the West" to declare his vow. In Hangzhou, following Patriarch Lianchi's legacy, many were devoted to Buddha-recitation, but the true practitioners were the elderly — no youths among them. Each month they gathered at a temple for recitation, calling it the Society of Old Fellows, not daring to assemble in great numbers for fear of suspicion as a heterodox political plot. Doan Thang declared boldly to all: "Buddha-recitation knows no distinction of male or female, old or young, noble or base. Buddhism is the true teaching, favored and recognized by the court — why fear?". At that time Dien Kiem Dien, a powerful official inspecting the salt tax in Hangzhou, was an old friend of the layman. Seizing the opportunity, he opened a subscription, first approaching Mr. Dien, who contributed two hundred taels of gold to lead the list. With the funds raised, the layman established an endowment whose light interest supported a recitation society at Baocheng Temple on Zi Yang Mountain, along with charitable works. On each assembly day he expounded the Buddha's teaching to the multitude, speaking of cause and effect, urging the unawakened to resolve and the resolved to practice diligently. He opened further branches at Xianlin Temple within the city and elsewhere for exhortation. Believers in recitation grew daily into the thousands. At each gathering all were solemn and reverent, never lax, noisy, or disorderly. The sixteen-foot Three Holy Ones in the Western Hall of Xianlin Temple were also erected by the society. One morning during assembled recitation, the assembly saw from the nose of the Mahasthamaprapta image a shaft of white light issue forth, circling the hall for over an hour before dissolving.

From then on, the manner of Buddha-recitation flourished in Hangzhou. Surrounding regions felt its transforming influence; old and young, men and women, rosary in hand and Buddha on lips, became commonplace, no longer strange. Doan Thang's name was known to every woman and child. At each assembly, local groups took turns inviting him to lecture. — West Lake's Lingfeng Temple, originally the site of Zen Master Phuc Ho, had long lain in ruins. In the first year of Daoguang, Doan Thang vowed its restoration, completing the work after several years. The layman passed away at sixty-six. At the end he kept right mindfulness clearly, saying: "I see countless bodhisattvas walking in meditation before me!". Then, seated upright, he quietly passed away. This was the first day of the fifth month in the ninth year of Daoguang.

A comment: Lo Khon recited to seek rebirth and was also freed from present sickness. Doan Thang recited only hoping to escape calamity, yet also received blessed signs at the end. Who then says: "The merit of Buddha-recitation benefits only after death"? See in these examples: both strove in true practice with unshaken faith. People today offer trifling effort yet crave excessive benefit, and seeing no effect declare the Buddha's teaching ineffective and abandon practice — is that not lamentable!
