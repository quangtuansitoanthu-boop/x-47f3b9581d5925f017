He had "entered the flow and forgotten the abode" — meaning that all defiling objects, the six sense-objects perceived by the six sense roots, had been entirely cut off.

This means he had entered the illuminating flow of his own nature, no longer turning outward to seek and pursue; the mind, luminous, constantly dwelt within. To be outside the flow is to remain bound to external conditions. If one pursues external sense-objects of sound, one is entangled in the turning wheel of birth and death. When all outer sounds are forgotten, there is no longer the characteristic of movement — that is, the first knot, that of agitated sense-objects, has been untied. Hence it is called "entering the flow and forgetting the abode." "Forgetting" means liberation; "abode" means sense-objects of sound.

The abode entered into also became still, and the two characteristics of movement and stillness clearly did not arise.

The source of the six sense roots and the six sense-objects all ceased. This is most difficult. Here Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva had entered the flow of his own nature. When the utmost stillness was reached, the characteristics of movement and stillness both vanished. Since the realm of stillness belongs to the region of the form skandha, one must not dwell in the realm of stillness. Only when neither movement nor stillness arises has one reached what is called the stillness of what is entered. At that point, the second knot — that of stillness — has been untied. Sense-objects of sound are entirely stilled, and the two characteristics of movement and stillness clearly no longer arise. That is, the form skandha has been destroyed.

Thus progressing step by step, hearing and what-is-heard both came to an end.

The bonds tied at the sense roots have been untied; cultivation advances gradually, samadhi power is strengthened, so the hearing root accords with what is heard and both are entirely ended — there is no more experiencer and experienced. That is, the third knot — that of the sense roots — has been untied, and at the same time the first five consciousnesses of the feeling skandha have been destroyed.

Not dwelling in the ending of hearing, awareness and what-is-awakened-to became empty.

Hearing and what-is-heard have both been exhausted, yet perception remains, self-image remains, so one must continue advancing in cultivation until reaching "one should produce the mind without dwelling anywhere," arriving at knowing yet not knowing, awakening yet not awakening, until both awareness and what-is-awakened-to are empty — only then is the fourth knot, that of awareness, untied. Only when the awakener and what-is-awakened-to are both gone is it called being entirely free of attachment — that is, the sixth consciousness, the thinking skandha, is destroyed.

With the emptiness of awareness perfected to the utmost, the emptiness and what-is-emptied both vanished.

The awakener and what-is-awakened-to have both become empty; one must continue to advance, investigating what this emptiness relies upon to exist, until the emptiness-nature of awareness is ultimately perfected, and then the emptier and the emptied are both entirely eliminated. The mind capable of creating is eliminated, and the realm that this mind rendered empty is also eliminated, to the point where even emptiness no longer remains. For as long as emptiness remains, one is still attached to the nature of emptiness. And now, for Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva, even the nature of emptiness no longer remains — that is, the fifth knot, that of the emptiness of awareness, has been untied, and at the same time the seventh consciousness, the formations skandha, is destroyed.
