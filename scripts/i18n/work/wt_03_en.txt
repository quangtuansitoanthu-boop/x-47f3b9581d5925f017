TITLE: 04. A RUDE AWAKENING
TOMTAT: The story of Truong Nghia, 24, struck with ankylosing spondylitis from eating too many frogs, fish, and eels. Meeting Master Miaofa, he was shown the karmic cause, became vegetarian, recited the Buddha's name in repentance, and recovered.
-----
{align:center|**A RUDE AWAKENING**}

{align:justify|My name is Truong Nghia and I live in Tianjin; this year I am 24. At 19 I fell gravely ill: at first my legs went numb and walking grew difficult; that night the illness struck hard — my hip bones and joints ached unbearably, my legs could not move, and the lightest touch sent pain shooting through my whole convulsing body.}

{align:justify|After examining me the doctor said:}

{align:justify|"This illness is extremely rare and cannot be diagnosed; we can only tentatively call it 'ankylosing spondylitis.' It cannot be treated — you can only go home and rest."}

{align:justify|Back home, I lay in bed all day; at night the pain grew fiercer still, and there was no way to sleep. By day my parents ran everywhere seeking physicians; by night they watched me groaning in pain, their hearts cut as with knives, and they too could not sleep. Within days my parents' bodies were haggard and sallow; they had aged greatly.}

{align:justify|My mother had a colleague, Mr. Duong, who in his youth often studied Buddhism and deeply understood its principles. Mr. Duong had taken refuge under Master Miaofa of Mount Wutai and diligently cultivated discipline, concentration, and wisdom, propagating the Dharma to save beings. With my illness beyond all cure, we heard Mr. Duong say that Master Miaofa understands cause and effect profoundly — whoever suffers an incurable illness and seeks him is freed from the long torment of sickness. All recover without treatment, as though dead and come back to life.}

{align:justify|Hearing this, my parents grew all the more anxious and hurried to find Master Miaofa and try.}

{align:justify|When they had told him my symptoms, the Master asked:}

{align:justify|"Is your son very fond of eating frog meat?"}

{align:justify|My mother answered:}

{align:justify|"Yes! He loves frogs stir-fried with chili, two or three times a week."}

{align:justify|The Master continued:}

{align:justify|"Are his legs now shaped like the character for 'bow'?"}

{align:justify|As he spoke he sketched the shape with his hand.}

{align:justify|"Yes, his legs look exactly like that," my mother answered.}

{align:justify|The Master asked further:}

{align:justify|"Does that shape not look like a frog?"}

{align:justify|Seeing my parents bewildered, he declared:}

{align:justify|"His illness comes from eating too many frogs — that is what summoned it. This is what Buddhism calls 'karmic retribution.'"}

{align:justify|He then explained the principles of the Dharma, speaking clearly of karmic retribution with true and proven examples. Moreover, the Master personally came to our home to see me. First he urged me to face my illness with resolve; then he said that if I did exactly as he taught, I would surely be cured. His teaching on cause and effect, explaining transmigration through the six realms, moved me deeply — I felt like a man walking in pitch darkness suddenly shown a lamp. I felt that only he could save me.}

{align:justify|As the Master taught the Dharma he prepared to massage my legs with his hands. Seeing this, my mother hastily stopped him:}

{align:justify|"Please do not! The slightest touch makes him convulse in pain, trembling all over."}

{align:justify|The Master smiled and asked me:}

{align:justify|"Then shall I massage you or not?"}

{align:justify|Afraid of pain, I dreaded anyone touching me — but my faith in the Master ran so deep that I agreed. Strangely, when the Master's two hands touched my legs, I felt not the slightest pain, only warmth, relaxation, and great ease. A moment before, my parents and I had been full of dread; now we were at peace, and I was moved to streaming tears. If my legs could recover their health, it would be the happiness and fortune of a lifetime. My heart overflowed with faith and reverence for the Master. Once more he had kindled in me the optimism to live joyfully. He also charged me that from then on I must eat no more frogs, must no longer kill living creatures for food, and if possible should become vegetarian to help my body swiftly recover.}

{align:justify|He explained: "However mountainous the offense, repentance dissolves it." If I sincerely confessed and repented the killing karma I had created, and each day, for all the lives I had harmed, devoutly recited the Buddha's name praying for their deliverance, my legs would gradually recover.}

{align:justify|I obeyed him and began a vegetarian diet, reciting the Buddha's name. My parents recited too in support, praying for my swift recovery. And my illness gradually turned for the better.}

{align:justify|But when friends visited and saw how wasted my body was, they all urged me to eat fish and meat for nourishment. My parents too grew anxious and told me to wait until I was fully well before being vegetarian. My faith was not firm; they persuaded me, and I went back to fish and meat. Only later, reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra, did I learn that I belonged to those who "no sooner arouse a wholesome resolve than in a flash it withers away."}

{align:justify|The consequence was that after I returned to meat, my legs began to ache violently again. No injections or medicines of any kind had any effect. My parents then rushed to find Master Miaofa, but Mr. Duong said the Master had gone to a southern monastery to teach the Dharma.}

{align:justify|After many twists and hardships, we finally reached him.}

{align:justify|Over the telephone the Master asked:}

{align:justify|"Have you been eating fish and eels again?"}

{align:justify|Burning with anxiety, my mother broke in:}

{align:justify|"Yes — fish and eels can enrich his blood and strengthen his body..."}

{align:justify|The Master sighed:}

{align:justify|"Alas! Why do you not remember my instructions? Eating fish and meat does the body no good at all — it only harms, deepens the offense, and makes the illness worse! Truly, old karma not yet erased, and fresh karma heaped on! Last time I showed you how to nurse him back; had you persevered in vegetarianism, Buddha-recitation, sincere repentance, and vowing to end evil, the illness would have turned for the better — this bad turn could never have happened."}

{align:justify|Hearing this, the whole family was remorseful. We expressed our repentance and earnestly promised to obey the Master this time. Then together we begged him to teach us how to be saved. The Master told me to recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra and the Great Compassion Mantra, with true sincerity of repentance — only so would the karmic hindrances gradually dissolve. He required me to recite diligently every day, dedicating the merit of the mantras to beings throughout the dharma realm — only then would the effect be good.}

{align:justify|From that day I truly began to learn and practice the Buddha Dharma. Each day I earnestly recited the Kshitigarbha Sutra and the Great Compassion Mantra. Many characters I did not know, so I bought a recorder to recite along; I persevered, never daring idleness. Before long I could recite the Great Compassion Mantra by heart, and finish the Kshitigarbha Sutra within two hours. I believe it was through the blessing of the Buddhas and bodhisattvas that the effect came so swiftly. My parents too, through my sincere study and practice, received immeasurable comfort.}

{align:justify|Day after day I concentrated on reciting sutras, mantras, and the Buddha's name, devoted to practice. If others urged me to eat meat, I was unmoved. And so the illness turned ever better. Later I went to the hospital for re-examination. The doctors diagnosed necrosis of the femur. I also took Eastern medicine; mine was a rare and complex intractable illness. But my miracle gradually appeared: I could walk without pain. I thought: "This is the realm turning with the mind."}

{align:justify|Since it is karmic retribution, there is no need for fear, grief, or tears — only to face it calmly, believe deeply in the Buddha Dharma, and practice with all one's heart, and the evil scene will surely turn into a good one. Through this experience, I hope all who read my story will take it as a mirror and stay vigilant. I beg you: kill no more, lest you follow in my tracks. For had I not personally endured this bone-deep painful experience, I too could hardly have believed in retributive karma, in the offense of killing! Truly, karmic retribution is utterly clear — like a shadow following its form, without the slightest error.}

{align:justify|Therefore I beg you all to awaken soon, to understand clearly that killing surely summons evil retribution, and to end meat-eating for good. Look at my story: only after suffering evil retribution did I learn to awaken — and thereby grow in wisdom and in fortune. I always remember: "Evil has its evil reward; good has its good reward."}

{align:justify|Whatever we do — every gesture, every arising thought — is followed by its fruit. One who has cultivated and realized sees the cause at a glance; if not, how could Master Miaofa have perceived so clearly that the root of my illness lay in eating frogs, fish, and eels? I beg you to reflect carefully.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|Truong Nghia's story A RUDE AWAKENING is a bitter lesson in "eating beings, suffering it in the body." His fondness for frogs, fish, and eels directly summoned ankylosing spondylitis, his legs curling like a frog's. Persevering in vegetarianism and reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra and the Great Compassion Mantra dedicated to the beings he had harmed, his illness turned for the better. But when his resolve withered and he returned to meat, the illness relapsed at once. This proves karmic retribution comes swiftly and plainly, like shadow following form. The story also stresses the importance of "firm faith" — never letting others shake it — and the merit of repenting killing karma by reciting sutras in dedication. It is a wake-up call to all who still take killing lightly: "Every mouthful eaten is a mouthful of debt. For health, end killing for good."}
