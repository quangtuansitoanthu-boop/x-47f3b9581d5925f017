Though the Buddha has many secret dharma-doors, you still have not rid yourself of desire-outflows. "Desire" is truly most hard to subdue. The moment you have lust, you have outflows. The gravest lust, which everyone finds hardest to cut off, is lust for fame and beauty. If you can rid yourself of this, you are extraordinary. When you study the Buddhadharma, you must rid yourself of lust before you can eliminate all outflows.

The fourth fruition of Arhatship is without outflows. The first fruition of Arhatship is the stage of seeing the Way — the result of cutting off the eighty-eight portions of view-delusions. The second fruition of Arhatship is called the stage of cultivating the Way. The fourth fruition of Arhatship is called the stage of certifying to the Way. At the first, second, and third fruitions of Arhatship, one is not yet called certified to the outflow-free fruition. Only at the fourth fruition is the outflow-free fruition certified. Attaining the outflow-free fruition is most important. Why has Ananda not reached this fruition? Because Ananda has not rid himself of lust. Ananda still delights in certain things. Ananda is not yet pure; hence the Dharma Prince Manjushri says to Ananda, "If you do not cultivate and extinguish the greedy mind for fame and beauty, you will not accord with the Buddhadharma. If you devote yourself solely to erudition, you will fall into error. That is why you met with calamity with Matanga's daughter. If you renounce lust and outflows, you will no longer meet such calamities."

When we see a clever person like Ananda still making this mistake, we should note that not only have we not been strict with lust, we even intend to pursue it. We have never thought of ridding ourselves of it. Do you see that as another error? By now, everyone should turn the light back to shine within. Do we still have lust? Have we rid ourselves of it? If not, there is nothing to discuss. If you wish to rid yourselves of it, then quickly begin cultivating the effort of returning the hearing to hear the self-nature. Study Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva's dharma-door of the ear-root's perfect penetration.

Sutra text: 將聞持佛佛 　何不自聞聞。

Transliteration: Tương văn trì phật phật Hà bất tự văn văn?

Translation: Taking hearing to uphold the Buddhas' Dharma — Why not hear your own hearing?

Commentary: "Taking hearing to uphold the Buddhas' Dharma. You have wasted effort studying the Buddhadharma," Manjushri says to Ananda. "With that effort, why not hear your own hearing? Why do you not turn the hearing back to recognize your own hearing-nature? Why do you not develop this cultivation effort? Why do you not turn back to look into yourself?"

Sutra text: 聞非自然生 　因聲有名字 旋聞與聲脫 能脫欲誰名。

Transliteration: Văn phi tự nhiên sanh Nhơn thinh hữu danh tự Triền văn dữ thinh thóat Năng thóat dục thuỳ danh.

Translation: Hearing does not arise of itself; Because of sound it has a name. Turn the hearing back, apart from sound; What name for that which is apart?
