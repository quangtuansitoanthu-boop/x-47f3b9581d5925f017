{align:justify|III. BUDDHA-RECITATION IS WONDERFUL}
{align:justify|My path of practice is peculiar indeed. Pressed hard by the ghost, I had to stay alert and practice at all times. To quickly be rid of the she-ghost (to send her far away), I had to exert my utmost.}
{align:justify|New to practice, first I recited sutras and mantras: late nights the Shurangama Mantra, mornings the Amitabha Sutra, at nine the "Ksitigarbha Sutra," afternoons the "Diamond Sutra" with three Great Compassion Mantras, evenings yet another "Ksitigarbha Sutra."}
{align:justify|Sutra recitation took long and wearied me. An atheist by birth, haunted by a ghost into a devout believer (forced to practice hard). Keeping such recitation for nearly a year, one morning as I reached the dedication verse, the ghost covered my sutra and harassed me, leaving me sad (tinged with despair too, seeing the ghost fear no sutra or mantra)…}
{align:justify|(In truth it was not that sutras and mantras lack power, but that newly practicing, my mind still brimmed with scattered greed, anger, and delusion, so no swift result could come.) But then I lost faith and sought a new way.}
{align:justify|My husband and I rented a house on a desolate hill (a dozen graves around) and spent six straight months there practicing "seated contemplation."}
{align:justify|First contemplating, we contemplated the Sutra of Immeasurable Life. At sunrise we sat contemplating; at dusk we sat two hours watching the sky darken. Free daylight hours we recited the Buddha's name. Months of contemplation yielded nothing—and worse, we went astray! For closing my eyes showed only blackness: our practice simply did not reach. Worse still, my temper flared fiercer and fiercer, and I saw ghosts ever more clearly. We endured about half a year, then gave up, packed up, "lowered the flag," and returned.}
{align:justify|Sutra and mantra recitation, seated meditation, contemplation… nearly two years seemed hopeless, so I tried turning to single-minded recitation of Amitabha Buddha's sacred name—and suddenly discovered: daily, I need only rise at midnight and recite the Buddha's name with utterly sincere heart, and the ghost absolutely dares not near the bed—vanishing without a trace. Sometimes in summer, around eight in the evening I would stroll, walking and reciting, perfectly peaceful. Meeting ghosts, I would see them withdraw to both roadsides, kneeling with palms joined in utmost reverence. (Truly, the Buddha's name manifesting before one makes ghosts retreat in reverence.) In truth, the earlier hard "grinding" practice laid the foundation, training our minds to grow familiar with the Buddha's teachings, more easily gathered in stillness.}
{align:justify|In March 2010, I met and heard Dharma Master Tịnh Không expound "Master Shan Dao's Pure Land Thought" and understood the sacred name's wonder ever better—realizing my former fruitless methods simply did not fit.}
{align:justify|For our capacities are base and mean, our bad habits many. Trusting deeply in Amitabha Buddha's compassionate vow-power to gather us in, thinking quietly "if I strive in single-minded recitation I shall surely be reborn in the Pure Land"—from utter misery thirsting for liberation in this very life—we cultivated bravely, reciting the Buddha's name with earnest resolve, single-mindedly relying on His shelter. So we bent our hearts to His name at every moment: walking, standing, sitting, lying… however long, reciting single-mindedly without pause, striving to form assured karma.}
{align:justify|Through long Buddha-recitation, great change appeared (clearest in my husband). Long dwelling with a ghost, his spirit was dimmed, his face darkened. Once he learned to gather his mind in practice, persisting in single-minded recitation, his nights' sleep grew peaceful, his body's condition began returning to normal, his face no longer ashen but bright and clear.}
{align:justify|In early practice, reciting the Buddha's name made him drowsy—but now he can recite diligently all day; though the ghost never wishes to release him for Pure Land rebirth, she can no longer approach to harass, nor hinder his recitation in any way. She stopped seeking me to trouble, and I found the knots of attachment in my own heart fading too—willingly letting go of every worry and clinging, entrusting all to the Buddha's arrangement. My mind at peace, I became happy!}
{align:justify|Coming to Buddhism, I moved from a mixed-practice mindset toward single-minded Buddha-recitation, forming assured karma. Through clearly understanding and deeply trusting that wholehearted single-minded recitation ensures rebirth, I settled my mind securely in the recitation—and went on to introduce this Dharma gate to others.}
{align:justify|Also through single-minded recitation, the Buddha's power blessed me, opening my mind to see clearly the thousand-year cause and effect of three lifetimes, remembering all three of our former lives; I have even beheld Amitabha Buddha's holy countenance and gazed upon the West's holy realm—all bestowed by the sacred power of the Buddha's name.}
{align:justify|I share my story with all. May all who read it benefit.}
{align:justify|Thừa Ngọc – April 11, 2011}
{align:justify|Translator's note: Perhaps from strong emotion the writer repeats, narrating confusedly… I have done my best to arrange it for readable order. Where passages remain hard to follow, I beg readers' pardon—my ability is limited. If readers gain benefit, that is the translator's great reward.}
{align:justify|THE MIDNIGHT CALL}
{align:justify|I am twenty-eight this year. After years of tender love, on May 28, 2011, my boyfriend and I walked hand in hand into the wedding hall to build a marriage. Those days, I was immersed in brimming happiness. One day late in August, I visited my mother.}
{align:justify|By chance I heard Mother say: someone or other, every night at exactly 10:05, calls my name from downstairs! Hearing it frightened me—it seemed too strange, so I kept pressing Mother:}
{align:justify|"Did you mishear, or suffer some auditory hallucination?…"…}
{align:justify|Mother insisted:}
{align:justify|"Absolutely no mishearing. At first Mother thought this area has many named Oanh and assumed people were calling by coincidence, so I paid no mind. But gradually I had to notice, for it kept repeating—three months now by count."}
{align:justify|Now I recall it, admiring Mother's courage: all that time, every evening before ten she would climb to the rooftop terrace, gaze down, scanning all around hoping to catch the prankster?… She waited long and saw nothing—but the moment she came inside, that woman's voice would ring out at exactly 10:05, calling my name.}
{align:justify|Helpless, Mother had to tell me. Then, worried and afraid… I too assumed some cunning ruffians were at work—for under heaven how could so queer a thing occur?}
{align:justify|Full of anxious questions, fearing trouble might befall Mother, wanting to verify truth from falsehood… I decided to sleep at Mother's that night.}
{align:justify|I stared at the clock on the wall, its tick-tock ticking steadily, my heart uneasy and roused to the utmost. To ease the dread, Mother and I chatted while we waited…}
{align:justify|At exactly 10:05 my ears clearly heard a woman's gentle voice calling my name; gooseflesh rose all over me, sweat pouring down. Then, unplanned, Mother and I blurted as one:}
{align:justify|"Did you hear it?"}
{align:justify|Indeed! At last I had to believe the strange thing was real! It turns out in this world there is such a thing as "Ghosts calling at the door!"…}
{align:justify|Puzzled past understanding, my mind terrified, panicked to the extreme! Yet that calling voice felt somehow familiar, intimate…}
{align:justify|That night I lost all sleep—more precisely, "dared not sleep," from fear… All night I sat on the bed, daring not to doze, frightened to the limit. Truly, I had never heard anyone tell of such a thing. Panic besieged me until my mind went limp. Past midnight I dozed a little and dreamed of a thin woman with short hair, hugging a great pile of money to her breast; as I moved closer, she shot me a hateful glare… Thus I endured till dawn. I was truly frightened, not understanding what all this could be.}
{align:justify|At dawn I sought masters to take a look. They could not see the cause, only saying "It seems this one has come demanding your life"… and warning me not to let Mother go out at night, lest danger strike with no safety assured. Besides, the master told me to use five kinds of grain against the ghost. I did exactly as told, but to no effect—the calling continued.}
{align:justify|Mid-Autumn Festival approached; the master said if by Mid-Autumn the name-calling ceased, thereafter it would call no more. But the strange voice kept calling my name every night without pause—what could it be?}
{align:justify|In a blink it was the ninth of the eighth lunar month, only days to Mid-Autumn. My gut burned like fire, fearing trouble for Mother. Father died early; only Mother and I keep each other company, leaning on one another to live. I hold Mother dear as my own life—without her, how could I face the remaining years?}
{align:justify|That morning I kept calling around for a master skilled at readings; I desperately wanted the ghost to leave—she seemed to bear me immense hatred. Zealous friends pointed me to various masters' addresses. Then I was bewildered, often asking myself: "What wrong could I have done—if indeed I have…" what should I do?…}
{align:justify|Amid the turmoil, I suddenly remembered: years before I had visited a gifted lady reader. So I decided to go to her house. It was long ago, so I remembered little of her. After contacting her by phone, she asked me to come after eleven in the morning. So past eleven, I arrived as agreed.}
{align:justify|Arriving, I learned she follows Buddhism. Her house was airy and clean, enshrining a statue that looked familiar, though I could not tell which deity it was. After I told my story, the lady (let me call her Lan) recounted a thousand-year-old tale of vows, resolving my doubt.}
{align:justify|My first question then was:}
{align:justify|"Is my mother in any danger?" (For I feared for Mother most.) Lan answered:}
{align:justify|"Nothing at all!"}
{align:justify|Only then was I relieved, breathing easily. Then Lan settled her mind a while and told my former lives' tale. Briefly summarized:}
{align:justify|"In the first life, under the Zhou dynasty, we were husband and wife—the one (who calls your name nightly now) was then male, a prime minister. I was female, his concubine. But I killed my husband, forging the tangled grievous affinity of the first life.}
{align:justify|In the second life, under the Tang dynasty, the other was master, I servant. Again, listening to villains' instigation, I killed my master.}
{align:justify|Down to this present life, she is a ghost, come demanding my life." Lan also wrote me a poem, whose last two lines bear pondering, like: "Meeting Bodhi guarding us in peace, helping me dissolve this enmity"…}
{align:justify|Lan asked me:}
{align:justify|"Do you wish to dissolve it?"}
{align:justify|I nodded hastily. Obeying her, my mind raised no stray thoughts, thinking but one thing: I would surely carry out every request well. Then she had me kneel before the Bodhisattva's statue; I could not hold back tears, not knowing why I so wanted to weep. She set me three days: each day I must recite Amitabha Buddha three thousand times and chant two repentance sutras; she also gave me a copy of the "Universal Gate Chapter of Avalokiteshvara (Guanyin) Bodhisattva" and the "Diamond Sutra," and furthermore chanted mantras for me—though I could not understand, yet reciting Amitabha Buddha's name my tears streamed down, sobbing…}
{align:justify|Afterward, Lan said:}
{align:justify|"Rest easy! Tonight she will come no more…"}
{align:justify|My heart then half-believed, half-doubted; sincere though I was, meeting such a thing the first time, my heart brimmed with questioning doubt…}
{align:justify|Leaving Lan's house, it was already late afternoon; friends and I ate while discussing the matter. A companion who came along declared:}
{align:justify|"Who knows if it works—but I don't believe it anyway!"}
{align:justify|Just as she spoke, the poem's line surfaced in my mind: "Meeting Bodhi guarding us in peace… helping me dissolve this enmity"…}
{align:justify|I explained to my friend, but her face stayed full of doubt…}
{align:justify|Whatever my friend's criticism and scorn, I held my ground: "Promised is promised—I must do it!"}
{align:justify|After supper I went to Mother's, telling her everything. Mother too brimmed with doubt, finding it beyond imagining—living more than half a life, she said, this was her first encounter with so strange a thing. No one believed—even I still doubted—yet I had to act. For Mother, I must act!}
{align:justify|Bidding Mother farewell, I returned to my own warm nest; my husband worried for me but utterly disbelieved such queer affairs. So he said little about it.}
{align:justify|That evening, alone at home, I reread Lan's poem, recalling the ancient former lives recorded there, unable to stop tears flooding my face.}
{align:justify|Boundlessly sad, I thought: "How could I commit so grave a sin? A human body is so hard won, so precious… yet I heartlessly killed them twice—how could my hand be so cruel?"…}
{align:justify|Then I wept again, wondering silently why she had not awakened me sooner. Feeling utter misery, I opened the repentance sutra and chanted, tears streaming.}
{align:justify|Then I stayed three days at Mother's. On the road my mouth never ceased reciting the Buddha's name! At Mother's I chanted repentance sutras. Though my mind tensed to the utmost, sitting on the bed I recited the Buddha's name unceasingly, not knowing why my heart so wanted to weep. Glancing at the clock, I saw past 10:30. Today, truly, the voice calling my name came no more. Past midnight I dozed off, dreaming the girl held my hand in bright joy, her gaze seeming to say: "Very well done!"… I passed a peaceful night.}
{align:justify|Waking in the morning, my mind settled for now, though still a little worried. By evening, seeing Mother bustling about, my heart felt panicked and sad; wanting only quiet, I asked Mother to stop working and recite the Buddha's name with me. Then I noticed: wind blew in gusts, very cold. So cold I broke out in gooseflesh, sweating all over. I asked:}
{align:justify|"Did you open the window, Mother?"}
{align:justify|She answered:}
{align:justify|"No!"}
{align:justify|My hands froze; I felt yin energy flooding in to cover all—she had come beside me. It seemed she did not trust me. I told her to rest easy, solemnly promising I would recite the Buddha's name well, praying for her escape from misery's sea—begging her to trust me.}
{align:justify|Reciting the Buddha's name, I wept, reciting on until my mind grew peaceful and glad, able to smile. Then my hands gradually warmed again.}
{align:justify|On the third morning (the final day), I resolved to strive in recitation and repentance: mornings I chanted ten rounds, afternoons two, evenings two; throughout these three days I stayed in touch with Lan. When I visited Lan, she gave me layman Quả Khanh's "Karmic Retribution in Present Life." Reading it, I found so many excellent stories of cause and effect… I thought "I should recite the Ksitigarbha Sutra, dedicating it to those I once killed and harmed"… So that evening I chanted five rounds.}
{align:justify|That night I slept deeply. One thing I forgot to tell: on the second afternoon, something strange occurred. Then I craved to hear Amitabha-recitation music, so I searched online… Sitting quietly before the computer listening, tears covered my face, feeling myself a sinner… So I knelt, expressing remorse and repentance—only then noticing: the computer screen trembled violently while I wept on, and suddenly a scene appeared: I was carrying two kinds of fruit—bananas and grapes—to Lan's house. I did not understand it, so I paid little mind…}
{align:justify|The matter was at last resolved; on the third day, sure enough, the ghost's calling came no more. Brimming with gratitude, on the fourteenth lunar day I bought grapes and bananas for Lan (exactly the scene I had seen on the computer). Lan told me to bow before the Buddha's statue; bowing to Him, my heart swelled with emotion, moved to tears—a very strange feeling.}
{align:justify|On the afternoon of the fourteenth, my husband and I visited Mother; he bought donkey meat, which I normally love—I can eat seven or eight slices… But that day, at the fourth slice I could eat no more. Then my stomach suddenly ached, and I vomited violently.}
{align:justify|Then I thought: "Oh no! Could I have done something wrong again?"}
{align:justify|The next day, Mid-Autumn Festival (the full moon of the eighth month), I dreamed a terrifying nightmare: in the dream, a vast abyss yawned bottomlessly out of sight, echoing with the ghost's scolding voice:}
{align:justify|"Living as you do, when shall I ever escape misery? Prepare for your death!…"}
{align:justify|Startled bolt upright, dawn not yet fully come. Glancing at my husband sleeping soundly beside me, I gently composed myself, silently reciting: "Amitabha Buddha! Grant me the answer—just now the dream terrified me; what have I done wrong?"…}
{align:justify|Reciting a while, I dozed off; in dream a line of verse appeared—waking, I hurriedly wrote it down but could not recall it all, remembering clearly only four words: "GO VEGETARIAN, RECITE THE BUDDHA'S NAME!"}
{align:justify|Morning came, Mid-Autumn day; I told Mother everything and declared that from here on I would be vegetarian. The family all wondered why I refused meat; Mother, ever loving and caring toward me, guessed I must be dieting…}
{align:justify|Watching everyone eat meat and drink wine, I craved none of it. Thus I have eaten vegetarian ever since, never craving meat.}
{align:justify|The household gradually calmed; Mother's spirits improved too. Once very brave, now climbing to the rooftop terrace she feels a little fear.}
{align:justify|At work, in free moments she too recites the Buddha's name! She says her spirits are much better now. My husband, though unbelieving, does not oppose my vegetarianism and recitation—he needs only my peace and safety.}
{align:justify|Since meeting the Buddha's teachings, more than a month has passed; at first I practiced diligently because creditors demanded their debts—recently, hearing the teachings often, my heart stirs and tears flow unchecked.}
{align:justify|I am young, early successful, holding fame and fortune in hand… After living through this, I understand people's ties to one another, and see how precious life and deep affection are. Most important, I have truly awakened to and felt the Buddha's teachings' immense, inconceivable power—this moves me to the core—}
{align:justify|I deeply believe in karmic retribution; what happened has educated me, taught me life's meaning, and shown me to "tolerate others' faults, be strict with myself, live filially toward parents—in all deeds benefit self and others."}
{align:justify|In learning Buddhism I keep correcting, adjusting each small fault of mine. Naturally this reform demands sustained, persevering practice over long years—for whatever you do, the Buddha must always dwell in your heart.}
{align:justify|Thu Oanh}
{align:justify|Translator's note: In the Ksitigarbha Sutra, chapter six, it is recorded:}
{align:justify|"The Buddha taught: O Universal Vastness! If in future ages sentient beings dream of ghosts, spirits, and dreadful shapes or other apparitions, grieving, weeping, gloomy, sighing, and so on… these are all fathers, mothers, siblings, spouses, and kin of one life, ten lives, a hundred lives, a thousand lives past, now fallen into evil destinies, unable to escape, with no blessed power anywhere to look to for rescue—so they inform those of flesh-and-blood ties or past-life connections, hoping to find a way to save them from the evil paths. Therefore you should use spiritual power to make those (who meet such strange scenes) before the images of Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, wholeheartedly recite this sutra, or invite others to read it three or seven rounds complete. Thus, though their kin dwell in evil destinies, hearing the recitation of those rounds complete, they shall be liberated, and appear in dreams no more."}
{align:justify|At first I doubted this "night-calling" phenomenon, and wondering, I could not finish translating the story—when suddenly I heard that a friend's relative died in a sudden accident, leaving life very young and an infant child behind. After her burial, every night the whole family heard her voice calling her child's name piteously…}
{align:justify|My friend is a monastic—naturally no lie in the account. So, confident and reassured, I strove to finish translating this story to share with readers.}
{align:justify|Below is the self-account of a psychic. Master Hsuan Hua often said: "One penetration, all penetration! Attain the path and psychic powers follow." The "psychic" here refers not at all to the weak-nerved haunted by yin attachments, nor the weak-willed who constantly see ghosts (while ordinary folk of strong yang never see them). The psychic meant here is one of special attainment, ever using his ability to guide the world toward the path of true self-reform.}
{align:justify|A few lines introducing Lien, the king of prophets:}
{align:justify|He is Taiwanese, famed as a great fortune-teller, able to contact the invisible realm we cannot see through. The one Lien Sinh communes with is named the "God of Fortune"; in the course of his work, the deeper he understood cause and effect, the more he revered Buddhism—taking refuge, vowing to strictly keep the five precepts, lifelong vegetarianism, dedicating his special ability to helping the world turn back toward goodness.}
{align:justify|Here is his fine essay sharing the law of cause and effect:}
{align:justify|THE GOD OF FORTUNE}
{align:justify|Friends, never lament your bad luck! For in truth, at the start of this human life, our blessings and destiny may have been enormous—meaning you could have attained and enjoyed superb, optimal fortune… But through ignorance of cause and effect we have unwittingly wrecked our blessings (through unvirtuous thoughts, words, and deeds).}
{align:justify|Our own unwholesome thoughts, words, and deeds have summoned and shaped our own stumbling, thwarted fate. Do not hastily call this reasoning superstition! Life truly holds a destiny (though we make that destiny ourselves, and it then "follows" us, binding us). But "destiny can still turn, according to the good or evil karma we create!"}
{align:justify|All whose careers succeed, who attain great wealth and rank, believe this principle. Living without wisdom, one easily sinks into hardship and poverty. Hearing me say this, please do not rush to scorn it as "superstition"—take the trouble to think and ponder to its source, or test it yourself and see (then conclude).}
{align:justify|I tell you: the senses' perceptions are untrustworthy—we are so often fooled by them! Consider: what percent of all existing light can human eyes see? What share of all existing sound can our ears hear? What percent of the world's smells can the nose smell? What percent of all flavors can the tongue taste? We can feel things—but what percent of all beings?}
{align:justify|Just because our senses cannot reach or fathom something (cannot see, hear, taste, or touch it)… shall we flatly declare it does not exist?…}
