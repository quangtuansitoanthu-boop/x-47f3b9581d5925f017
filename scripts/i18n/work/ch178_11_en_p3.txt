{align:justify|THE LOVE OF THE MANDARIN DUCKS}
{align:justify|I am an atheist, following no religion at all. But seven years ago a strange thing happened that changed my whole life.}
{align:justify|I had been married just six months. Late that night I woke to use the toilet, my head still groggy with sleep, when suddenly a white blur swept past me—so I quickly opened my eyes… and saw clearly a female ghost in white, her face smeared with blood, filthy…}
{align:justify|Even now I don't know how I found the courage to swing my hand… and slap the ghost hard across the face, striking her until she vanished!}
{align:justify|After that night, though I never saw the ghost again, a sense of ill luck grew ever clearer.}
{align:justify|First my husband fell ill and had to see the doctor for days on end. Then my turn came: at midnight my belly suddenly ached violently, and only hospital treatment cured it.}
{align:justify|So we both plainly felt we had been "hit by evil." To dispel it, we went everywhere seeking masters (to perform exorcisms). But nothing worked; the calamities never eased, raging on as before.}
{align:justify|We were utterly miserable then, with no solution to be found.}
{align:justify|By chance I met a friend (a Buddhist disciple) who urged me to learn Buddhism—to use the Buddha's teachings to solve this thorny problem!}
{align:justify|Gravely ill and desperate for a good doctor, with no other way (we just had to rush to embrace the Buddha's feet)—and so we came to Buddhism…}
{align:justify|After learning Buddhism, we began vegetarianism, releasing captive animals, and reciting the "Ksitigarbha Sutra"—after several dozen copies, one night I dreamed the female ghost appeared, weeping as she told me her life story.}
{align:justify|It turned out that centuries ago she had been my husband's wife; in that life she hanged herself and fell into the ghost realm! She had searched for her husband for hundreds of years, until in this life she finally found him here.}
{align:justify|Learning the truth, I felt sympathy, yet my heart burned with resentment. For now I understood why my husband suffered frequent nocturnal emissions… It was this she-ghost (every night she crept in to sleep with my husband)!}
{align:justify|I refused to yield: knowing the ghost could come (only at midnight) to bewitch my husband, I stood guard—near that hour I would strike the wooden bell and recite the Buddha's name loudly. From chronic worry, and rising every midnight to recite the Buddha's name "on guard" for my husband, my health steadily failed from severe lack of sleep—yet I stubbornly held out, "fighting" thus for nearly two years. At last I reaped the result: heart disease—I collapsed! (Not frightened senseless by the ghost, but fainting from utter exhaustion)…}
{align:justify|This could not go on—I could bear it no longer; tormented in body and mind, my heart disease lingered, and peaceful life seemed impossible. So in early spring, my husband and I decided that this April we would together enter a temple and leave home, ending this bizarre war of grievances between humans and ghost… But before we could act, the she-ghost struck first: this time she entered my husband outright, savagely bent on killing me.}
{align:justify|Truthfully, years of grueling battle with her had left me exhausted and heartbroken… so at death's door, I too felt like giving up. I thought: "Let me take her blade and end all debts—only wishing to be reborn in the Pure Land at once, shaking off every sorrow"…}
{align:justify|Just as I closed my eyes awaiting… the she-ghost's killing stroke through my husband's hands, suddenly came a "Clang!"… the ghost had flung the knife to the ground, grumbling: "Kill you and I fall into hell besides! What a wretched bother!"}
{align:justify|Now it was my turn to rage—I seized my husband's body (with the ghost soul still lodged inside) and beat it black and blue!… Only then did I learn what it means: "to beg life and be refused, to beg death and be refused!"}
{align:justify|My heart and mind tormented beyond bearing, I had but one recourse: lodging my husband in a nearby temple. After that, every night the she-ghost came seeking me to harass, wailing noisily in my ears, lamenting: the temple has Dharma protectors guarding it—I cannot get in at all… The ghost's racket left me no sleep at all; at last I had to plead, begging the temple master in his compassion to hold a deliverance rite for the ghost.}
{align:justify|As this temple practices Buddha-recitation single-mindedly, the rite aimed to deliver the ghost to the Pure Land. When the Buddha-recitation assembly was held, its effect was triumph beyond compare! For under the Buddha's empowering might, I beheld the former lives of our trio, seeing plainly all that happened in past lives, untangling the knot for all three (two humans, one ghost)—the thousand-year wheel of rebirth at last unveiled:}
{align:justify|The pair of mandarin ducks}
{align:justify|Countless ages ago, a beautiful pair of mandarin ducks dwelled by a still, secluded lake. A little boy often came to play and discovered them; he would bring them food over a long time. One day at the lake he found the drake standing alone and sorrowful; the duck was nowhere to be seen? Searching everywhere, he spotted a vicious hawk in the sky carrying the duck away in its talons.}
{align:justify|Helpless, the boy brought food to comfort the drake. But the drake only drooped, refusing to stir. The next day the boy visited again; still it fasted, refusing everything. Days later the drake died. Grieving, the boy buried it, and never came to the lake to play again.}
{align:justify|The mandarin ducks take rebirth}
{align:justify|Time flew; centuries slipped by. The mandarin ducks circling through rebirth at last met again, both born into the human realm: the drake as a man, the duck as a woman, wedded as a devoted couple knit in deep affection.}
{align:justify|One day the husband was away, and his wife quarreled so fiercely with her mother-in-law that in a moment of despair the young bride hanged herself. Dead, she fell into an evil destiny, bearing a ghost's body; but unable to let go of her love for her husband, the ghost single-mindedly sought her lord through boundless misery…}
{align:justify|Through round after round of the six realms' rebirth… down to the present life, centuries later!}
{align:justify|The ghost's husband in this life was again born a man, while I was that little boy of long ago who once fed the mandarin ducks… (in this life born female); our affinities ripened, and I wed the man (the drake of ages past) as husband and wife, while the harassing ghost is the later incarnation of the duck. For years our three tangled in combat—only now is the connected truth laid bare… It turns out to be a "tragicomedy" woven of converging affinities.}
{align:justify|Under the Buddha's profound, inconceivable, wondrous power, I was humbled into absolute faith; from then on we cultivated diligently and tasted the sweet fruit of practice. Summing up seven years of practice, I draw a few fine lessons, briefly noted here to share with all:}
{align:justify|I. VEGETARIANISM BRINGS MANY BENEFITS:}
{align:justify|Four years a lifelong vegetarian, I have found vegetarianism has these merits:}
{align:justify|– Grave illness heals easily: that year my husband's white blood cells fell until he had no immunity left; thanks to lifelong vegetarianism combined with treatment, little money spent and the illness cured. Now his white cells are back to normal, and the doctor exclaims: "A miracle!"}
{align:justify|– Blessings increase: since going vegetarian I have done no work at all, living only on rental income from leased rooms—yet money remains to save, life leisurely… For vegetarians seldom fall ill, and even catching something, a little medicine cures it. Vegetarian food is very cheap; daily, my husband and I cannot eat more than ten coins' worth.}
{align:justify|– Karmic obstructions dissolve: vegetarians forge no enmity with beings, so karma melts faster than for meat-eaters, and practice meets fewer obstacles.}
{align:justify|– Rejuvenation: I am thirty-eight this year, yet thanks to vegetarianism I look ten years younger than my age. (Young ladies who love youth and beauty—go vegetarian!)}
{align:justify|II. ADVERSE AFFINITIES ARE BLESSINGS TOO}
{align:justify|At first I always thought meeting that ghost was utter bad luck, utter misery! Sickness meant no work, no earnings. But through it I savored the suffering of "parting from what we love" and understood the wheel of rebirth and the law of cause and effect. Because the ghost harassed us unceasingly, our life grew extremely bitter; she pressed and drove us until two atheists like us had to step onto the path of learning Buddhism. What is more, with the ghost appearing daily, I had to recite the Buddha's name nearly ten hours a day, at least fifty thousand invocations daily—I persisted thus for three years. Thanks to it, the great gate of the Pure Land world has opened wide before my eyes (such an ending is truly a gift!)… No words of mine could ever tell it fully… So when adverse affinities appear, they are good chances for practice (like one chased by a fierce tiger—naturally running for dear life, cultivating as if life depended on it)…}
