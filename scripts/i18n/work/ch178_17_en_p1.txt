{align:justify|Should readers wonder: Mrs. Truong created offenses and bore suffering—that is settled—but why did her kin also invite shared calamity? If you have read the retribution books, you will understand "collective karma drawing together"! The corrupt Tao Wu of old did not create evil alone—he had his whole faction. Superiors' and subordinates' ties can become kinship in later lives. So by the workings of cause and effect: "With hatred, they come seeking revenge; with debts, they must be repaid." Thus her whole clan had to taste Dou E's revenge. I sought out and translated the extra story "Thai Minh Thong" from another book, placing it here to help readers see the principle of karmic retribution more clearly—understanding that one person may be reborn back and forth within a lineage, bearing retribution in succession…}
{align:justify|Finishing this story, I was most astonished: Professor Truong in this life is a virtuous woman, yet in a former life a wicked corrupt official? (Comparing past and present, they seem two different people—utterly opposite.) But it is easily understood: after judging Dou E's case, merely witnessing blood flying to the white banner, snow falling in June, then three years' drought… added to the people's laments, blame, and curses… sufficed to drench him in sweating fear, giving rise to a fiercely repentant, practicing mind… And precisely through that remorseful, earnest reforming heart, Tao Wu passed from a wicked criminal of a past life into a virtuous person of this life—every trace of evil faults erased, so that examining this life, not a shred of past wicked habit remains—only the victim's hatred follows lifetime after lifetime seeking revenge.}
{align:justify|As for Dou E, she is a special girl: usually daughters-in-law clash with mothers-in-law, but Dou E differed—she treated her mother-in-law most well; unable to bear seeing her beaten, she accepted sacrifice, confessed, and bore a wrongful death sentence. One may say hers was a virtuous person—so every vow she uttered came true.}
{align:justify|I recall reading a Western story (though I know not whether it is true) of a death-row inmate wrongly sentenced, who pointed at the courtroom authorities, swearing his grave would never grow grass—to prove his innocence.}
{align:justify|Afterward in the cemetery, all other graves greened with grass—only the convict's grave never grew grass at all, unsettling the powerful figures; they had grass brought and planted upon it, but the grass died—the wrongfully executed man's grave forever grassless… (it seems the curses of the virtuous wrongfully sentenced are always fulfilled)…}
{align:justify|Usually in common folk status, where possessions are modest, people have less chance to err—compared with holding high office and rank. I recall Princess Thang Hoa in "Karmic Retribution in Present Life" vols. 1–2: as a princess she seized commoners' husbands and killed without blinking—cross her and she killed; arrogant, overbearing, powerful, cruel… But reborn a commoner, she is most gentle—daring not kill even a crab, even earnestly reciting the Buddha's name praying her husband would not kill it… (the same person—past cruel and wicked, present gentle and good)… All such changes generally spring from remorseful repentance, a fierce vow to end evil and practice good… Perhaps thereby, reborn, those wicked ones erased all base habits, carrying only good ways and wholesome conduct… as provisions into the next life… a joy for them. But not all know how to wake and reform so strongly…}
{align:justify|How true the Buddha's words: creating deluded blessings (creating without wisdom) is third-life karma! For blessings created in abundance, if enjoyed in heaven till exhausted, one shall fall. If enjoyed on earth in fortune, one is born powerful, rich… and precisely through that wealth, power, beauty… people gain more chances to sin! So the Buddha ever reminds us to practice the true teachings, so lifetime after lifetime wisdom lights our road, saving us from falling.}
{align:justify|CONFESSIONS OF A WOMAN ENGINEER}
{align:justify|I live in Bao Thanh County, Heilongjiang Province—a woman with an incurable cancer.}
{align:justify|At thirty-seven, I developed end-stage gallbladder cancer. My gallbladder has since been removed, but removing it at the terminal stage helped little, unlike catching it early. For at the final stage the cancer cells had spread throughout my body, easily flaring everywhere. After the gallbladder's removal, my uterus grew three more tumors, then my left ovary one, and within half a year my right ovary another. Next my throat grew a tumor—all who hear me speak notice my hoarse voice.}
{align:justify|My health utterly failed; I could undergo no major surgeries—so uterus, ovaries, and the rest could not be operated on… Only the throat tumor, plainly visible with a mirror down an open mouth, could be cut. My voice remains hoarse. Then my stomach grew a tumor too, my rectum diseased as well—people call it hemorrhoids, but wrongly: that spot too grew cancer.}
{align:justify|My torments were not yet done—the disease spread to my lungs, most viciously: the upper half of my left lung held five tumors. Sickness racked and tormented me… till I fainted clean away.}
{align:justify|I often coughed blood, blood thick with clotted impurities, coughing up mouthful after mouthful.}
{align:justify|All my illnesses came from the evil karma of killing. I sold fishing gear for catching seafood and supplied all kinds of aquatic creatures for people's tables.}
{align:justify|I worked from 1981; though my registration was in Bao Thanh County, my trade spread everywhere, from countryside to city…}
{align:justify|The cancers tormenting my whole body all came from killing karma (only after learning Buddhism did I understand).}
{align:justify|Before knowing the path, I had two abortions—great evil karma. When illness felled me, I collapsed onto bed, lying gasping… I tasted misery to the full: above, racking coughs; below, urine spurting to soak my trousers—so I wore pads, for each cough leaked urine at once—besides other diseases racking and tormenting me, misery past telling.}
{align:justify|Then the doctors declared I had one month to live.}
{align:justify|My hair turned wholly white from drugs and chemotherapy. Then chemo could continue no more—I was too weak to bear it. For chemo kills cancer cells by half, healthy cells by half. And in my condition, further chemicals were impossible. Too weak to walk or stand, a few sentences left me dizzy, near fainting. I could neither speak nor muster any strength.}
{align:justify|Then my child told me Master The Hue was holding a Buddha-recitation retreat, rumored powerfully blessed… so I wished to try attending… But my husband, mother-in-law, and birth mother all forbade it. Above all my birth mother, who dotes on me, firmly declared she would rather I lie home doctored till death than walk so superstitious a road!… She said if I recklessly went, in such failing health I might… lay my bones upon the train before arriving!}
{align:justify|Then my son pleaded hard, telling my mother:}
{align:justify|"Grandma, stop barring Mother—you love your daughter, and I love my mother too?! If Mother must lie home awaiting death… better grant her wish, let her come to the Buddha's teachings and test it once?…" }
{align:justify|Then the doctors told my whole family:}
{align:justify|"Her life has one month left—let her eat whatever she likes…"}
{align:justify|Mother barred my going, wanting in this final time to pour out all her love for her daughter… When Mother asked to wash my feet, I truly could not bear it—but to please her I let her have her way… (not wanting her haunted after my death)—so I stretched my feet out for her washing… As Mother washed my feet, my tears streamed…}
{align:justify|When Mother agreed to let me go, my son (then a graduate student at a research institute) asked leave from school to escort me to the retreat.}
{align:justify|The retreat was held at Bao Lam Zen Monastery in Cam Chau (Liaoning); then I could not walk steadily—my son and a woman friend from Heilongjiang half-carried me there.}
{align:justify|At the retreat, daily I must bow to the Buddha a thousand times, practicing wholly Buddha-recitation, Buddha-bowing… And miracles appeared.}
{align:justify|At the retreat the master taught me: "Reciting the Buddha's name, think of nothing—mind un scattered—only mindfully recite the Buddha's name"…}
{align:justify|Then the master taught me to bow; I received the Eight Precepts, practicing with the assembly. Then within three to five days, joyfully I coughed no more. Seeing my cough cease, the master set me bowing—a thousand bows daily—yet in my condition I could hardly stand, let alone bow? I thought "Why does the master still make me bow?"… so I wept, for unable to bow, I burst into tears…}
{align:justify|But the master never softened—he firmly forced me to bow! Unable or not, bow I must! Meaning bow I could or not, bow I must!}
{align:justify|A week of bowing, and the master began circling the practice hall swiftly—I must "run" after him. With lung disease, my lungs choked with impurities, unventilated. Breathless, voiceless—yet I must "run," easily suffocated dead… I dared not run, but the master encouraged me; I tried, stepping swiftly—oh, gradually I felt ease—so confidently I strode after the master; up and down, my heart glad.}
{align:justify|Then the retreat's second week: climb the mountain. The master guided me. Seeing him climb, I dared not follow, fearing death. For I knew my strength could hardly bear it—but the master came, took my arm, and said:}
{align:justify|"Trieu Le Binh, strive up the mountain!"}
{align:justify|I thought "Obey the master"—whatever he teaches, I follow—so I strained up. The mountain high, slopes steep, all path incline… I crawled step by step; reaching the summit, tears of joy covered my face. Standing atop, I thought: "Truly beyond imagining—never dreaming I could climb and stand upon the summit"…}
{align:justify|Gazing at the boundless blue sky, green trees, I was infinitely moved—for the master's retreat had saved and granted me a second life…}
{align:justify|Afterward I attended retreat after retreat the master held, down to today. Then, two parts of my hair had whitened, one part ashen gray. But single-mindedly I followed the master in retreat. Now when fellow practitioners meet me again, they exclaim:}
{align:justify|"Oh! Le Binh's hair is black again—wonderful!"}
{align:justify|They stand before me admiring, praising on and on. I am truly glad.}
{align:justify|Before, at home each meal I must eat porridge. So coming to the retreat I worried—here all eat rice. I thought "my stomach so gravely ill—how eat rice?"… But after attending, now my habits differ nothing from others'—I eat easily, and my face glows rosy, truly!}
{align:justify|Now, circumambulating with all, reciting the Buddha's name, I long to recite loudly. Before, newly attending, I recited breathlessly, voiceless—now I can voice it, loudly…}
{align:justify|As for other ills—uterine tumors, ovarian, and the rest… now I never seek exams or checks—I know myself they have settled. Why?}
{align:justify|Past thirty I developed cancer; menstruation ceased three years… Before, doctors warned me: "Should bleeding recur, count yourself done for!" I asked:}
{align:justify|"Why?"}
{align:justify|He answered:}
{align:justify|"Your uterus is full of tumors, crowded and crushing—rupture means hemorrhage, then death! Both ovaries tumorous besides—most grave…"}
{align:justify|After attending the master's retreats, then further ones, suddenly in this time my belly ached fiercely, violently…}
{align:justify|The retreat done, the master returned to Taiwan. I returned home to Bao Thanh County, thinking: "If the master holds another retreat, I shall attend"…}
{align:justify|Home again, suddenly my belly burned extremely hot. As if electric currents roasted and spread heat through my lower belly, with some pain. I wondered: "Why so strange?" Thinking my plight might match the doctors' warning—"life… done for, great hemorrhage likely"… I rushed to the toilet—suddenly below a loud "splash," something gushing from my belly… then ease and comfort… Rising, I looked into the pan: all red, with a round tumor the size of a small bowl, flat-surfaced, ringed like a six-petaled flower.}
{align:justify|Afterward, not knowing whether well or ill, I went for ultrasound: "Whatever has happened?"…}
{align:justify|Examined, the doctor said:}
{align:justify|"Your illness is healed—nothing wrong."}
{align:justify|Since then, I recovered like a normal person. Only lung disease remains—but I firmly seek no exams or cures, thinking: "My hair is black again; though not wholly healed, my strength is excellent—healthy as this suffices."}
{align:justify|Now I can work and live normally like everyone.}
{align:justify|Through my fierce illness, father-in-law, mother-in-law, birth mother, youngest uncle and aunt, younger brother and sister… every relative witnessing my passage from gasping sickness to hale health—now able to do anything—all exclaim: "The Buddha's teachings are truly wondrous, inconceivable!"}
{align:justify|Nor merely praise—all together now vegetarianism and Buddha-recitation. My father-in-law, eighty-one this year—before 1949 an old revolutionary, meritorious, medaled—believed only in Chairman Mao, nothing else. But now all alike: turned to vegetarianism and Buddha-recitation, cultivating vigorously.}
{align:justify|Second Uncle (my husband's brother) once owned Bao Thanh's famed eatery. A grand renowned restaurant, known to all. Daily killing vast quantities—someone always booking at least ten tables—so the shop must kill every fresh seafood: fish, shrimp, crabs, sea cucumbers… meaning countless killings beyond count… and great profits.}
{align:justify|But through my violent illness, Second Uncle and wife firmly quit the trade. They moved to Thong Hoa Street, laboring for others, utterly abandoning killing.}
{align:justify|Witnessing my plight, not only my family turned to vegetarianism and Buddha-recitation—friends throughout Bao Thanh County all deeply believe in Buddhism.}
{align:justify|True practice is such. I am especially grateful to the master; since attending his retreats, I have gained immense benefit. Not only great bodily gain, but my mind ascended higher. I learned many fine things from my kind teacher. His retreats not only saved my life, but helped me follow his lofty virtue (I speak truly—for I am no good person!). At first, hearing people say:}
{align:justify|"Old Master The Hue has come from Taiwan to the Mainland to hold retreats"…}
{align:justify|Then, unbelieving, ignorant of Buddhism, I was most arrogant. Knowing nothing of him, still I slandered and gossiped… With atheist prejudice I measured and imagined ill, freely criticizing and sneering… I jeered:}
{align:justify|"This monk is bad in every way… nothing good about him!…"}
{align:justify|Writing these lines now, I repent deeply, longing to cry aloud: "Master! Your disciple sincerely repents to you!"…}
{align:justify|Not till gravely ill at death's door did I wish to attend a retreat—having heard its blessings strong and efficacious! (I went as a gamble).}
{align:justify|My evil deeds—killing, abortion, slander, backbiting him…—naturally the master knew all, knew full well, yet ever tended with kindness a great evil, great killer, great slanderer… like me! Wherever going, whatever eating, he saved portions for me, explaining: Trieu Le Binh is sick and weak—bring her some…}
{align:justify|At the retreat he saved my life teaching stern yet all-embracing… and an arrogant atheist like me was moved and humbled by his supreme virtue… The kinder the master, the more favored, the more remorseful and ashamed I felt, the more admiring and grateful…}
{align:justify|Since then, sincerely attending his retreats continuously for nearly three years. The master has held more than fifty retreats; throughout, he was good not to me alone but to all… So I deeply revere him. Healed, still I wish to attend his retreats—to learn his virtue.}
{align:justify|At Mount Wutai, I met a girl of sixteen from Tay An, afflicted so that eating meant vomiting. Once most beautiful, lovely, refined… Since illness, her weight ballooned—from forty-five kilos to eighty-five—puffy and bloated. She sought masters everywhere; an orphan without parents, most pitiable.}
{align:justify|The master allowed her to follow him. Then I thought: "Master, why do this—this child bears a grave illness; keeping her beside you, should she not heal, your good name suffers!" Then, caring for the master, I thought so—but the master cared nothing for this.}
{align:justify|He told me: Le Binh! The child is pitiable! Treat her as your own, tend and care for her!}
{align:justify|The child single-mindedly followed the master's retreats all the way to Mount Wutai. Afterward she followed no more—her illness gone. She had to go home to work.}
{align:justify|All along following the master, I learned many precious lessons from his virtue—lofty qualities never once witnessed before. So healed, still I attend the master's retreats, longing to learn from him. In my heart he is a supremely virtuous high monk, a most great spiritual teacher in this world.}
{align:justify|He won my admiration, moving me to tears. The more retreats with the master, the more his virtue shone—for in this mundane life I never met anyone of such style; meeting a true exemplary cultivator like him, I learned what the Buddha Jewel, Dharma Jewel, and Sangha Jewel are…}
{align:justify|My father was a postgraduate lecturer, my mother a university professor. Not only did I fully absorb my parents' learning, I graduated with high degrees—so all I met were highest intellectuals. My youngest son studies at the Wuhan Agricultural and Forestry University's research institute… One might say all my life I mixed only with high intellectual circles—those I esteemed. For they say: "Of all trades the lowest, only learning ranks highest." So the ancients declared. So in my heart I ever aspired to this creed, watching the "highest learned circles" to see whether they truly bore the fine graceful character the ancient sages ever praised? But frankly, after meeting the master, I found: the master towers luminous, far surpassing the highest intellectuals!}
{align:justify|Sometimes, circumambulating the Buddha, I silently confessed:}
{align:justify|– O World-Honored One! Now I understand and thoroughly feel the line: "Thập phương thế giới ngã tận kiến, nhất thiết vô hữu như Phật giả!" (Surveying all worlds of the ten directions, I see none as wondrous as the Buddha!). The Buddhas are truly compassionate and most great!}
{align:justify|Trieu Le Binh}
