{align:justify|WHERE SUICIDES GO}
{align:justify|I am a lifelong-vegetarian layperson keeping the Buddha's five precepts. I deeply admire Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva's vow: "Until the hells are empty, I vow not to become a Buddha!" In the "Ksitigarbha Sutra" the first chapter tells how the Saintly Maiden, reciting the Buddha's name day and night, saw herself descend into hell, learning where her mother was reborn…}
{align:justify|I love and find true comfort reading this passage:}
{align:justify|"If one can, for twenty-one days, single-mindedly gaze in worship at Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva's image and recite His name ten thousand times complete, the Bodhisattva shall manifest His boundless body, telling that person which realm their kin were born into. Or in dreams, the Bodhisattva manifests great spiritual power, personally leading that person to worlds to meet their kin."}
{align:justify|Whenever papers and television report suicides thick as flies, I grieve boundlessly. So I thirst to know: the dead by suicide—where do they go?}
{align:justify|In the Ksitigarbha Sutra, chapter twelve, it is recorded:}
{align:justify|"Those who hear Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva's name, behold His image, and sincerely take refuge—able daily to recite the Bodhisattva's name a thousand times, for a thousand days running—their good men and women's prayers shall swiftly succeed, forever unhindered."}
{align:justify|So I practiced abundantly—more than the sutra teaches: long-term recitation, reforming each thought, earnestly bowing to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva over a long time—wishing but one thing: "Grant me wisdom opened, to see and thoroughly understand where suicides go?"}
{align:justify|At last the wish came true. In dreams, I truly visited the realm where suicides dwell, understanding all questions. (The sutras speak of six realms: gods, humans, asuras, hells, hungry ghosts, animals. So describing the Ghost realm, I hope you are not startled.) A few directly see, but most do not. (Yet the near-death, their energies failing, minds weak, easily see those of the yin realm.)}
{align:justify|Now I invite Dharma friends to view and share this precious experience with me:}
{align:justify|"The yin realm's energy is most cold—so contacting or visiting it needs strong meditative power, great vows, a clear spirit. The Ghost realm too stretches boundless, divided into many zones. Never think suicide ends misery—it opens the curtain upon sky-high torment. Whether for love, for debts, for whatever reasons… you would leap from buildings, drown in rivers, drink poison, slit veins, cut throats, hang… thoughts of suicide… in every way… but once suicide, after death the torments received grow more dreadful still. Suicides dead, their conscious spirits are at once imprisoned, sunk in endless misery.}
{align:justify|Know that suicide bears three grave offenses:}
{align:justify|Unfilial to both parents.}
{align:justify|Destroying one's own form, betraying the rare blessing of human birth, not cherishing life.}
{align:justify|Foolish and witless.}
{align:justify|Our bodies come from our parents; living, never repaying them with utmost filial piety, never working to benefit life and people—yet foolishly destroying, killing one's own body. This is a most grave offense. Know: a human body is hard to gain, the Buddha's teachings hard to hear. This life granted human form—why despise and destroy it? For abandoning the human body, regaining one shall be most hard. Suicide is utter folly.}
{align:justify|Suppose debts drive you to suicide—understand: this life's debts unpaid, hereafter you repay still more—now shouldering besides suicide's great offense—misery heaped on misery! Know: suicides dead suffer piteous karmic retribution. Suicides after death shall endlessly replay the suicidal act.}
{align:justify|Suppose Mr. A leaps to suicide at two in the afternoon—dead, his conscious spirit is imprisoned suffering, lingering about the suicide's ground. Daily, exactly at the suicide's hour, he returns to that very place, that building… performing suicide once. Every day performing so, performing the self-destroying act until his lifespan ends. Suppose his fated age was seventy, but at forty he suicided—then dead, daily he must replay this suicide scene—meaning he must… leap from the building for thirty years, till his worldly lifespan exhausts.}
{align:justify|Moreover daily, that suicidal moment's excruciating thoughts, added to bodily pain, replay all together—exactly the same. Meaning daily he must re-enact and re-experience the suicidal tragedy once—outside which hours he is locked in the "Suicide Offenders' Zone" enduring punishment. This "suicide dead" dwelling is extraordinarily gloomy, most damp and foul. Here every heart brims with resentment—moans, weeping, laments unceasing… Here are no houses or beds, no television, feasts, no food… nothing at all! Only torment and weeping.}
{align:justify|I approached a cell holding suicides packed full, resentment soaring sky-high, most oppressive—every face most pitiful. I neared a suicide ghost, seeing his whole body soaked, eyes sunken deep. I greeted:}
{align:justify|"Are you well?"}
{align:justify|He tilted his head eyeing me, asking:}
{align:justify|"Who are you?"}
{align:justify|"I am an ordinary person, vowing to visit the yin realm to learn how suicides fare after death?"}
{align:justify|He said:}
{align:justify|"Why ask—one look tells you my utter misery! I know not how to voice my present heart—only saying I am… MISERABLE! SO MISERABLE!… and that is all!"}
{align:justify|"Why is your whole body soaked?"}
{align:justify|"I drowned myself in a river…"}
{align:justify|"But why did you suicide?"}
{align:justify|"I will not tell—ask no more!"}
{align:justify|"Very well! Farewell!"}
{align:justify|I walked elsewhere. Before me a woman—I asked:}
{align:justify|"Why are you in this place?"}
{align:justify|She answered:}
{align:justify|"Can you help me find a small child? I beg you."}
{align:justify|I asked:}
{align:justify|"Your child?"}
{align:justify|"Yes. I took my daughter suiciding with me—but now I know not where she is? For after suiciding I was at once escorted here, while my child vanished without trace. I suffer greatly…"}
{align:justify|"Your daughter now dwells in another zone, awaiting rebirth. For you willfully ended the young child's life wholly without her consent—so your offense is most grave. Since your child was small, innocent, knowing nothing, hers counts not as suicide—so she was taken elsewhere."}
{align:justify|"Truly? None told me this—I beg you, lead me to meet her, will you?"}
{align:justify|I cannot lead you there—for this is the yin realm, with the yin realm's own laws. You must bear suicide's retribution here—that is the law!}
{align:justify|She burst into bitter weeping…}
{align:justify|I walked on a stretch, crossing a mountain, reaching another place. Here another block confining other suicide dead. I met a man with a long deep gash across his belly, blood streaming… I asked:}
{align:justify|"Sir, what happened to your belly?"}
{align:justify|He answered:}
{align:justify|"This wound is from my own knife slicing my belly—for my wife cheated, following another man… so I hate 'her'!"}
{align:justify|"Even so, you should not have killed yourself!"}
{align:justify|"First I killed her, then suicided! Dead, I am locked here. Daily I must return home replaying the suicide scene. I ache and suffer past telling…"}
{align:justify|I sighed:}
{align:justify|"Ah! For love again! Sir, suicide is a most grave offense—can you recite the Buddha's name?"}
{align:justify|Then I taught him to recite: "Nam mô Địa Tạng Vương Bồ tát!" adding:}
{align:justify|"You must recite—it shall lighten karmic retribution! Moreover, repent—for killing was utterly wrong; having killed and suicided besides, your offenses grow heavier! Hear my urging—strive to recite the Buddha's name!"}
{align:justify|He only nodded, saying nothing.}
{align:justify|Before my eyes appeared ghost after ghost, hearts full of hateful resentment, most dreadful—some drenched head to toe in blood flowing unceasing. Witnessing these scenes with my own eyes, I was utterly terrified.}
{align:justify|Worldly people mostly think suicide ends all torment—never knowing "suicide opens the curtain upon torment past imagining." Utter folly! No wonder places of suicide often tell of "hauntings" though none are seen.}
{align:justify|For if a house or room… once held a suicide, the suicide ghost must daily return performing the suicidal act—so if that house or room knew no "ghostly disturbance," that would be strange! So we often hear "suicide spots often echo weeping… though none are seen." It is the suicide dead returning to perform suicide, sunk in the self-destroying moment's grieving thoughts…}
{align:justify|For my part, though by Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva's vow-power I witnessed the yin realm—frankly, were I to lodge in a suicide's house or room, surely I would suffer deeply. Why? For daily my eyes would plainly see ghosts returning to replay suicide, plainly seeing how piteously they died. How then find peace and ease? The teachings hold most fine, wondrous deliverance sutras. But you cannot rely on another's deliverance while refusing to practice. If your heart brims still with evil, venomous thoughts… none can save you. For scenes accord with minds—good minds draw good scenes! Deliverance helps you give rise to goodness, repent and correct faults. Perfect yourself while living, relying on none—that is best.}
{align:justify|In human life, who lives without cares, without pain? Those guarding body, speech, mind wholesome keep hearts ever glad. Misery comes… from evil seeds we once sowed. Repaying evil fruits, settling old debts—who feels light and easy? Ten thousand times I beg you: never raise suicidal thoughts. Understand: suicide solves nothing—dead, torment grows worse! If earthly life seems misery, know that suicides dead are locked in the suicide-dead zone. Fools think death ends all—wipes cares, ends every sorrow… wholly unaware that after suicide, torment but begins descending—ten thousand times fiercer than living pain. And why not pity the kin who shall grieve so at your foolish act? Drive yourself not onto the dead-end road.}
{align:justify|Suicides dead, having borne suicide's retribution complete, must proceed to greater and lesser hells enduring further torment, as the Ksitigarbha Sutra describes… till karma exhausts they may escape and be reborn—but where, depends still on their created karma.}
{align:justify|Buddhist Thieu Hoa}
{align:justify|Translator's note: I weighed and pondered long—at last deciding to translate this piece, hoping to wake those bent on suicide.}
{align:justify|In life, however utter the misery, however hopeless, never wish for suicide. For present pain unbearable means post-death pain fiercer still! Remember: suicide does not end pain—it opens the curtain upon endless torment… For in this life all bear pain, not you alone—some bear pains still more towering, yet never flee but face them, striding bravely through to grow and strengthen… (for the abyss is the ladder of heroes). Though our bodies die, minds still work… Killing oneself too bears killing's sin: violating the ten evils, barring rebirth. The body dead, feelings of cold, heat, hunger, thirst, resentment… still cling, tormenting consciousness unceasing. Minds full of evil thoughts must fall, sinking… Minds full of loving kindness shall rise high. As when you give, release captive animals, or help someone—night brings deep sleep, hearts floating glad… that is the wholesome mind's realm. So robbers and murderers, doers of utter evil, surely never sleep peacefully, ever meeting nightmares, though law has not yet punished…}
{align:justify|When suicidal wishes arise, let the thought rise no further (nurse not vain grief—it will goad you to folly)—strive steering the mind to wholesome brightness. Truly no dead end or cul-de-sac exists—only your mind imagines so. "Sweet seeds sown reap sweet fruits." So the escape lies in your own mind—not in others or outward scenes. Open your own way out, drown not in deadlock. Joy and misery self-made—seeking joy, end evil, do good—ever guarding body, speech, mind wholesome: the good your mind holds, the peace you shall enjoy—here no debate argues it; to know true from false, only practice, experience, and draw your own answer.}
