TITLE: 05. THE GOLDEN-FEATHERED ROOSTER
TOMTAT: Mr. Chuong lay dying of late-stage lung cancer from killing a great many chickens — above all a majestic golden-feathered rooster. His wife repented and recited the Kshitigarbha Sutra to deliver the chickens, and he made a miraculous recovery.
-----
{align:center|**THE GOLDEN-FEATHERED ROOSTER**}

{align:justify|There was a farmer named Chuong suffering from lung cancer so advanced that two great hospitals had both given up and told his family to prepare his funeral.}

{align:justify|A relative of his knew that I often studied Buddhist principles, and brought Mr. Chuong's wife to my home to beg our help. Most fortunately Master Miaofa was present there, and they urgently implored the Master in his compassion to save them.}

{align:justify|Master Miaofa said:}

{align:justify|"The patient's killing karma is far too heavy — above all he has killed a great many chickens. Is that so?"}

{align:justify|Mrs. Chuong answered:}

{align:justify|"Yes! My husband loves fried, stewed, roasted dishes... Whenever the village has a wedding, a celebration, or a funeral, they always ask him to be head cook. And he scorns others' chicken-killing skill, so he always seizes the knife himself. Each feast he kills dozens of chickens, their red blood dyeing the whole ditch crimson."}

{align:justify|The Master asked again:}

{align:justify|"Did you secretly kill someone's big rooster? Its belly feathers all golden, its body pinkish-brown, its tail deep leaf-green, its whole body glistening — when it raised its head, from head to foot more than half a meter — it looked most heroic and majestic..."}

{align:justify|Hearing the Master describe it, Mrs. Chuong was thunderstruck, her face draining of color; she collapsed with a thud. Knocking her head again and again, she stammered:}

{align:justify|"Heavens, Bodhisattva!... We never knew... killing chickens... was an offense?"}

{align:justify|That year of famine, every day the house went hungry! That day a neighbor's big rooster flew into our yard. Truly we did secretly kill and eat it — who knew the Bodhisattva's eyes see through everything. This is what they call 'evil has its evil reward.' Never again will we dare casually take what belongs to others. And in that famine year we also stole state grain to eat, stole crops besides... Now I know our guilt. It was all my husband's recklessness — please take years from my life instead! I would die in his place... Huhu huhu!"}

{align:justify|The wife was utterly shaken in spirit, weeping as she poured out her tale in one breath, her mouth never ceasing its repentance — moving all who heard. Who would have thought this woman possessed such keen spiritual perception: a few words and she understood all. Her speech was honest and plain, and the couple's devotion truly precious.}

{align:justify|I hastily helped her up and told her to listen to the Master's teaching.}

{align:justify|The Master too was deeply moved. His voice trembling slightly, he said:}

{align:justify|"Your weeping confession just now proves you are most sincere and repentant. When you return home, remember to explain to your husband so he knows his fault and repents urgently. From now on neither of you may ever kill again. Go to a pagoda and request a copy of the Kshitigarbha Sutra — because your husband killed so many chickens, he must recite it 49 times to dedicate to them. And remember: your husband must diligently recite the Kshitigarbha Sutra — he bears besides the guilt of stealing and killing that great rooster. It was a king among chickens!"}

{align:justify|And the Master asked further:}

{align:justify|"Was it after killing that 'heroic' rooster that your husband fell ill with headache?"}

{align:justify|Mrs. Chuong thought back and affirmed:}

{align:justify|"Yes, yes it was! Truly, at that time he developed a headache lasting two whole days — a throbbing ache, unable to eat a thing!"}

{align:justify|The Master said:}

{align:justify|"After it was killed, the chicken constantly haunted him for revenge, standing upon your husband's head, sometimes pecking his brain with its beak. How could he not suffer? Enshrine a memorial tablet for that chicken in a pagoda; both of you must recite 7 copies of the Kshitigarbha Sutra for it and invite monks to help deliver it. It may be reborn in the heavens as a phoenix!"}

{align:justify|Mrs. Chuong said:}

{align:justify|"We barely know characters; if we cannot recite, what then?"}

{align:justify|"Reciting sutras is to save your husband's life! For you too it brings immense benefit. If you do not know characters you may consult a dictionary, or ask one who does; if you truly cannot recite, you may invite monks to recite — but that is not as good as reciting yourselves, for the merit is all of your own making. Take absolute care: during the deliverance period, cut off all meat foods — meat, onions, scallions, garlic, liquor, tobacco... All must be wholly ended."}

{align:justify|For if you eat foul-smelling foods and then recite sutras, no devas, humans, ghosts, or spirits will come to hear them, and beings will gain no benefit. Nor is it reverent toward the Buddha Dharma. How then could there be merit? If the slaughtered animals cannot be delivered, your husband's illness will be most hard to heal. If both of you can strictly observe the discipline, the Buddhas and bodhisattvas will surely bless you both."}

{align:justify|The Master urged her most earnestly. Mrs. Chuong asked again:}

{align:justify|"My husband now lies critically ill — he may die at any moment. Will reciting sutras be in time?"}

{align:justify|The Master answered:}

{align:justify|"Had your husband not slaughtered countless chickens, his lifespan would not yet be exhausted. Killing shortens life! But because your confession and repentance just now came from the bottom of your heart, your husband's illness now has a chance to turn. Repent with all sincerity and help your husband dissolve his karmic hindrances. If your husband himself sincerely repents, devoutly recites sutras and the Buddha's name, danger will surely turn to safety. Seize the time that remains!"}

{align:justify|Mrs. Chuong hurried to the hospital to find her husband already able to sit up — sitting on his bed. Asking, she learned he had just coughed up more than two and a half bowls of purulent phlegm into the spittoon. His breathing had cleared. It was the first time since his grave illness that he could expel phlegm. Mrs. Chuong rejoiced exceedingly and took the occasion to tell her husband all the Master had taught. Hearing it, he felt both glad and afraid.}

{align:justify|The next day both decided to leave the hospital and go home. They told the hospital:}

{align:justify|"We are past all cure in any case."}

{align:justify|Afterward the couple did exactly as the Master had taught. Before long, that Mr. Chuong — "dying of lung cancer" — stepped out of death's door before the astonished eyes of the whole village. Nor is that all: he could even ride a bicycle — a topic of inquiry and gossip for the entire village.}

{align:justify|Then a great rain poured down and leaked through his roof. He climbed up to mend the leak, leaving the villagers marveling and sighing without end.}

{align:justify|COMMENTARY:}

{align:justify|The story THE GOLDEN-FEATHERED ROOSTER plainly shows that "killing — above all killing many chickens — summons lung cancer." Mr. Chuong not only slaughtered chickens in droves but secretly killed a majestic golden-feathered rooster (a king among chickens), so retribution came swiftly and heavily. His wife, with a sincere repentant heart, knocked her head and pleaded, confessing on her husband's behalf and vowing to recite sutras for deliverance. The result was Mr. Chuong's miraculous recovery — riding a bicycle, mending his roof. The story stresses: killing karma afflicts not only oneself but one's family; and the merit of repentance and reciting the Kshitigarbha Sutra dedicated to slaughtered beings can turn danger into safety. It is a warning to all who cook, butcher, or love chicken meat: "Every slaughtered chicken bears a grudge — sooner or later the debt must be repaid."}
