<p><strong>Translation: This is called a pestilence-ghost, aged into a demon, harassing the cultivator. Sated, no longer possessing that person. Both teacher and disciple fall into legal peril.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>This is a kind of <em>pestilence-ghost</em></strong>, after more than three to five thousand years, <strong>aged into a demon</strong>. It <strong>harasses</strong>, <em>torments</em> <strong>the cultivator</strong>, striving to destroy the cultivator's samadhi power. But after a time, <strong>when it</strong> has played all its cheating tricks and the games hold nothing new &#8211; <strong>sated, no longer possessing that person.</strong> <strong>Then both teacher and disciple fall into legal peril.</strong></p>
<p>Once the heavenly demon leaves that person's body, it no longer has its alluring demonic power, so people begin to doubt it &#8211; at last it is sued and jailed.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhữ đương tiên giác, bất nhập luân hồi. Mê hoặc bất tri, đọa vô gián ngục.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: You should be awakened beforehand, and not enter the turning wheel. Deluded and unknowing, one falls into the uninterrupted hell.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>You should know these matters beforehand</strong> &#8211; early awaken to this principle &#8211; <strong>lest you enter</strong> the <strong>turning wheel</strong>; do not fall into the demon's tempting net, do not enter the demon king's turning wheel of birth and death. <strong>Deluded, unknowing</strong> this principle, one is sure to <strong>fall into the uninterrupted hell</strong>.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong><em>6 &#8211; GREED FOR STILLNESS</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Hựu thiện nam tử, thọ ấm  hư diệu, bất tao tà lự, viên định phát minh. Tam-ma-địa  trung, tâm ái thâm nhập, khắc kỉ tân cần, nhạo xứ âm tịch, tham cầu tĩnh mật.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Again that good man &#8211; the feeling skandha empty and wondrous, untroubled by deviant concerns, the perfect samadhi disclosed. Within samadhi the mind fond of deep entering, restraining the self in diligence, delighting in dwelling in gloom and quiet, greedily seeking still seclusion.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Again that good man &#8211; the feeling skandha empty and wondrous, untroubled by deviant concerns,</strong></p>
<p>Again, in the empty, penetrating, wondrous state after the feeling skandha has dissolved, the cultivator cultivating right samadhi no longer has deviant concerns &#8211; <strong>the perfect samadhi disclosed,</strong> dwelling in the bright, perfect samadhi. <strong>Within samadhi the mind</strong> gives rise to longing, fond of <strong>deeply entering diligent, austere effort, delighting in dwelling in gloom and quiet</strong>.</p>
<p>The cultivator restrains himself very tightly, putting all strength into effort &#8211; fond of dwelling where yin energy abounds with little sunlight, fond of unfrequented places like caves, deep mountains, or remote valleys, choosing a place to live in seclusion. Because he <strong>overly craves stillness</strong>, the demon king comes.</p>
<p>In cultivation effort, one should not crave excessively: crave neither good things nor bad &#8211; <em>the ordinary mind is the Way</em>. Mainly keep the mind ordinary, giving rise to not a single thought. Whatever you greedily seek is wrong.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Nhĩ thời thiên ma, hậu đắc kì tiện. Phi tinh phụ nhân, khẩu thuyết kinh pháp. </strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Then the heavenly demon gets its chance, sending its essence to possess another person, using that person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: This state is like the previous case. <strong>Then the heavenly demon gets its chance</strong> &#8211; when the convenient moment comes. At once <strong>sending its essence to possess another</strong> person, <strong>using that person's mouth to expound sutras and Dharma</strong>: the demon sends its disciple to possess someone else, then makes that person go to where the craving cultivator is, to expound sutras and Dharma.</p>
<p><strong>Sutra text: Kì nhân bổn bất giác tri ma trước, diệc ngôn tự đắc vô thượng niết-bàn. Lai bỉ cầu âm thiện nam tử xứ, phu tòa thuyết pháp. Linh kì thính nhân, các tri bản nghiệp. Hoặc ư kì xứ, ngữ nhất nhân ngôn. Nhữ kim vị tử, dĩ tác súc sanh. Sắc sử nhất  nhân, ư hậu đạp vĩ, đốn linh kì nhân, khởi bất năng đắc. Ư thị nhất chúng, khuynh tâm khâm phục. Hữu nhân khởi tâm, dĩ tri kì triệu. Phật luật nghi ngoại, trùng gia tinh khổ. Phỉ báng tỷ-khưu, mạ lị đồ chúng, kiết lộ nhân sự, bất tị cơ hiềm. Khẩu trung hảo ngôn, vị nhiên họa phúc, cập chí kì thời, hào bị vô thất.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: That person, originally unaware of demonic possession, likewise claims to have attained unsurpassed nirvana. Coming to the place of the good man seeking seclusion, it spreads its seat and preaches the Dharma &#8211; making its listeners each know their past karma. Or in that place, telling one person: &#8220;Though not yet dead, you have already become an animal;&#8221; ordering a person behind to step on his tail, at once making that person unable to rise. Thereupon the whole assembly bows in admiration. If anyone gives rise to a thought, it already knows the omen. Beyond the Buddha's moral code, it redoubles ascetic rigor &#8211; slandering bhikshus, scolding its followers, exposing people's affairs, heedless of ridicule and suspicion. Its mouth delights in foretelling coming fortune and calamity, and when the time arrives, not a hair off.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>That person, originally unaware of being demon-possessed.</strong> He proclaims as the demon king says &#8211; <strong>having attained unsurpassed nirvana.</strong></p>
<p>What evidence judges someone a demon king? In that demons almost invariably love praising themselves.</p>
<p>It says: &#8220;Do you know? I have just realized nirvana&#8221; &#8211; or it may say: &#8220;I have just realized the first Arhat fruition&#8221; &#8211; or: &#8220;I have attained the fourth Arhat fruition&#8221; &#8211; or &#8220;I have now become a Bodhisattva &#8211; do you recognize it?&#8221;</p>
<p>The moment someone proclaims so, you know at once he is a demon, no need to ask. The Buddha never says:</p>
<p>&#8220;I am a Buddha &#8211; quickly come bow to me. If you do not bow to me, you have missed a great chance, for I am a Buddha &#8211; how stupid not to bow to me.&#8221;</p>
<p>Whoever proclaims himself a Buddha, a Bodhisattva, an Arhat is a demon and nothing else &#8211; you can know at once, no need to ask. Those are demons' words. A Buddha never claims to be a Buddha; a Bodhisattva manifesting in the world never styles himself a Bodhisattva. Even when someone honors them as Bodhisattvas, they do not acknowledge it.</p>
<p>Even if the person is a Buddha and someone says: &#8220;I know you are a Buddha,&#8221; they do not acknowledge it either. One truly awakened never admits to others his awakening. If someone says &#8220;I am awakened,&#8221; pay him no mind &#8211; he is not worth comparing even to an animal. Do not listen to such boasting self-praise.</p>
<p>Hearing sutra lectures equips you with a demon-revealing mirror: once demons, ghosts, and goblins show their forms, you know them at once. But if you do not clearly understand the Buddhadharma, you will not know what they say and will at once be fooled by them.</p>
