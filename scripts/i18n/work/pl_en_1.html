Foreword

This volume has been selected and translated by the author from the original Chinese Buddhist work, the Records of Pure Land Saints. That collection was compiled under the Qing dynasty, in the reign of Qianlong, by the layman Peng Tesheng together with his nephew Xisu, who gathered accounts of people who attained rebirth in the Pure Land through Buddha-recitation. Toward the end of the Daoguang reign, the layman Hu Ding of Lian'gui collected further accounts of rebirth and continued the compilation. Then, in the era of the Republic of China, Dharma Master Desen gathered additional verified accounts of Pure Land practice and supplemented the work. Altogether, from first to last, there are nearly a thousand stories.

In truth, those who have practiced the Pure Land teaching and attained rebirth are far more numerous than that. The old records concerning the Pure Land were partly lost, the chronicles were incomplete, and in former days communication was inconvenient,
so accounts of rebirth through Buddha-recitation were never widely circulated nor fully recorded. Consider the scenes when Master Shandao was at Chang'an and Master Shaokang was at Xinding, when the sound of Buddha-recitation filled every street: one may be sure that those reborn in the Pure Land numbered more than tens of millions. Still, the accounts collected here may serve well enough as models for Pure Land practitioners of later generations.

Some years ago, the author had recorded several dozen accounts of rebirth through Buddha-recitation in Vietnam, but they were regrettably lost during the upheavals of war. Hence I have had to present these Chinese accounts of rebirth as examples to encourage Pure Land practitioners. In this volume I have selected and translated only the most remarkable accounts from the original work, fearing that too many would weary the reader. Earlier, Venerable Van Duc (Thich Tri Tinh) translated about one third of the collection under the title The Road to the Land of Bliss, which many readers cherished. Several fellow practitioners asked me to continue that work and to translate accounts of lay practitioners of recent times, so that people of our own day might follow in their footsteps. For that reason, this volume gives special attention to stories from the recent Republican era.

The words and deeds of the saints and sages who practiced the Pure Land teaching in former times are like music and bright mirrors that encourage and illuminate fellow practitioners. An ancient author once wrote a Pure Land book entitled Sounds of the Lotus Hour: the lotus hour refers to the lotus clock of the White Lotus Society of Master Huiyuan, and the sounds are the pure melodies that urge all beings to recite the Buddha's name. Following that intention, I have entitled this volume MELODIES OF THE PURE LOTUS.

Every school, besides its doctrine, has its record of lived experience and practice. Practice both confirms doctrine and stands as a bright mirror that reminds and encourages practitioners. This volume is a historical testimony confirming that the Land of Bliss truly exists and that the merit of Buddha-recitation brings benefit both in this present life and at the hour of death. Since the Land of Bliss truly exists, it follows that the heavens, the hells, the three realms and six destinies, the law of cause and effect in matters of wrongdoing and blessing, and the realms of saints, spirits, and ghosts are likewise all real and not illusory. Reflecting deeply and broadly upon this point, one will surely be able to cut off evil, turn away from wrongdoing, and incline toward the wholesome path.

"Leaning on my desk, I gaze upon the ancients;

Catching my shadow in the mirror, I ponder the affairs of life."

I hope that readers of this volume, awakening to the truth that this life is suffering and impermanence, will soon resolve to seek liberation, cultivate wholesome blessings, keep a vegetarian diet, and recite the Buddha's name. Beginning with self-cultivation and then urging relatives and all others to practice, even though this world has not yet become the Land of Bliss, still within each region and locality there will be much blessing, benefit, and peace.

Moreover, the details throughout this volume may guide the reader to profound and subtle meanings, like chewing sugarcane from the tip down to the root: one will gradually enter ever deeper stages of realization.

LIEN DU
