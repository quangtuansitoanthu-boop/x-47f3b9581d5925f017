TRAN TANH LUONG

Tran Tanh Luong, styled Tich Chau, was a native of Wuwei county, Anhui, an upright merchant. In middle age he had but one son named Thien Tho, naturally bright, who alas died young at fourteen. Reflecting that he had ever kept a kindly heart and done meritorious deeds, yet was left heirless, Tanh Luong wholly disbelieved cause-and-effect retribution. His second wife, Lady Ho, gentle and deeply Buddhist, knew her husband's fixation beyond argument, and only quietly devoted herself to practice, reciting Holy Avalokiteshvara's name to pray for a child. Before long she conceived, but near delivery fell gravely ill. For twenty-nine days she could neither eat, drink, speak, nor turn over; her body burned like fire, wasted like dry kindling. Famed physicians Chinese and Western alike were helpless, with no hope of saving her.

One night Lady Ho dreamed an old woman holding a long-stemmed lotus, brushing her from head to foot, saying: "Through past karma you suffer this grave illness, yet fortunately your sincere practice avails. Now I sweep away your karmic hindrances and grant you a fine son!". She felt body and mind cool, woke instartled joy — and the illness seemed to vanish nowhere; she rose and walked as usual. Next morning she bore a heavy healthy son of handsome features. This marvel startled Tanh Luong! But questioning his wife and hearing every detail, he newly believed the truth of three-lives cause and effect, no empty falsehood. Deep within, he thanked the vast compassion of Buddhas and bodhisattvas. From then on husband and wife together kept lifelong vegetarian diet and Buddha-recitation, resolving upon practice: aiding the poor, sick, and afflicted, building temples, feeding monks — every good deed pursued with full strength. Knowing Lady Ho's healing and childbirth came through Avalokiteshvara's rescue, Tanh Luong often pilgrimaged to Mount Putuo to venerate the Great Being. Both took refuge with Dharma Master An Quang: Tran Tanh Luong's Dharma name was Lieu Vien, his wife Lady Ho's Lieu Thuong. About the first years of the Republic, Putuo's temples lacked provisions, and the whole region was requisitioned for German residents. Tanh Luong hastened among official friends to intervene, and the danger passed. He further donated to restore the Many-Treasures Stupa and other works rebuilding the mountain, repaying the Three Jewels' kindness.

In the ninth year of the Republic, husband, wife, and five children together received the bodhisattva precepts at Phap Nguyen Temple in Beijing. Three years later, Lieu Thuong suddenly fell ill. On the twenty-eighth day of the second month, while reciting, she suddenly saw two youths bearing banners inscribed: "Western Escort.". Knowing rebirth neared, she had her husband invite four monks to aid in repentance and recitation. By the twenty-eighth of the next month, the illness healed. But early in the leap month, Lieu Thuong again felt unwell, knew her appointed time approached, and single-mindedly recited, praying for swift rebirth. On the fifth, Tanh Luong invited monks to the house, and with the household took turns in supporting recitation, the Buddha's voice day and night unceasing. Lieu Thuong silently recited along. On the morning of the sixth she had the family heat water for her bath and change of robes. Then, striving before the Buddha altar to offer incense and worship, she had her bed turned for lying on her right side facing west. Lying settled, she devoted herself to recitation until the Hai hour, when suddenly she sat up, joined her palms, and said: "The Three Holy Ones of the West have come!". She added: "Three thousand recitations still lacking — recite them full and I shall go!". Then monastics and laity, more than thirty, loudly chanted the sacred name. Lieu Thuong chanted loudly along awhile, her face brightening into a radiant smile as she passed away. Strange fragrance drifted through the house. At noon next day, at encoffining, her crown was still warm, limbs soft, fragrance lingering.

When Thien Tho, the first son, died young, longing for an heir Tanh Luong had taken a concubine, Miss Sa, only seventeen. After some years in the house, through guidance she too aroused true faith, taking refuge with Dharma Master An Quang under the Dharma name Lieu Tu. From then on Miss Sa firmly kept the five precepts, lifelong vegetarian diet, and Buddha-recitation. Later Lieu Tu suffered lingering illness unhealed, lying abed two whole years. Yet her faith and vows held firm, never parting from recitation. On the full moon of the second month in the nineteenth year of the Republic, the World Honored One's Nirvana feast, the whole family invited monks to assemble at home for the Great Compassion Repentance. Just at noon as the rite ended, the daughter, bearing a cup of water, said she saw it ripple forth light. Tanh Luong beside her hastened to hold the cup. She then said she saw his hand holding a lotus — fearing his hour of rebirth had come. Hearing this, Tanh Luong went out to invite the monks into the room for supporting recitation. Lieu Tu suddenly knelt upon the bed, bowing: "Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva from the Land of Bliss emits light illumining my whole body.". Tanh Luong urged her to cast off all and seek only the Great Being's escort. Lieu Tu obeyed, joining her palms in vow: "In illness I have suffered much; I pray all people soon gain liberation, parted from sickness and suffering. May the Bodhisattva pity and escort me to the Land of Bliss!". Past noon by two hours, she said: "The Bodhisattva has come! I beg the monks to withdraw, that I may wash and change.". But just changing robes, she said: "Amitabha has come from afar, extending his hand emitting escorting light, with Weituo and the vajra guardians attending — quickly invite the monks in for supporting recitation!". Then, without time to don her robe, Lieu Tu sat in lotus posture, palms joined, repeatedly chanting Amitabha Buddha, saying: "I am going!". Then, closing her eyes, she quietly attained rebirth, hands still joined at her breast. All in the house smelled boundless rare fragrance. Till noon next day her crown stayed warm, her countenance fresher and brighter than in life.

Beholding with his own eyes wife and concubine both gaining blessed signs and verified rebirth through recitation, Tanh Luong's faith grew ever firmer. Afterward he adorned his front hall solemn as a temple, hanging the plaque Recitation Lay Society Grove, often gathering fellow practitioners for mutual practice and exhortation. In the winter of the Tanmui year the layman fell lingeringly ill, inviting monks of Thien Phuoc Temple to lodge at the Grove, daily voicing the sacred name in his room, that hearing the Buddha's name ever might perfect right mindfulness. On the eleventh day of the third month in the Nhâm Thân year (1932), at high noon, Tanh Luong said: "I behold Buddhas and bodhisattvas of majestic excellent marks, and lotuses most large and fair!". Monks and family, knowing his western hour had come, gathered in the room chanting loudly. The layman too sat telling his beads, softly chanting along, and past noon by two hours quietly attained rebirth.

That evening Layman Tu Huyet Nhu, Tanh Luong's close friend and the Grove's warden, hearing the notice hastened to invite the fellowship to condole. When the party arrived at eight at night, Tanh Luong still sat upright in lotus posture, rosary in hand, face fresh as in life. Testing, they found his crown still warm, limbs soft and supple.
