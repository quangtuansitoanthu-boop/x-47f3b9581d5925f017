<p>If awakened thus, no longer deluded, that tense state vanishes of itself. <strong>But if you think you have realized sagehood</strong> &#8211; if you think: &#8220;Oh! Now I have the mind of <em>same-substance great compassion</em> &#8211; my cultivation effort has truly succeeded&#8221; &#8211; then <strong>a demon of compassion enters the very heart</strong>: meeting anyone at all, you burst into tears for no reason. This demon <strong>on seeing people pities them, weeping without restraint</strong>, weeping without knowing why it is so sorrowful.</p>
<p>When I was in Manchuria, I knew a woman named Liu Jin-tong who met with such a state: whenever meeting anyone, she would burst into tears and say: &#8220;In a past life you were my child. Now the child has returned &#8211; found its mother.&#8221; With tears she thoroughly perplexed people, making them believe they had truly met their mother. In fact that person had met a demon. (8)</p>
<p><strong>Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall</strong>: if you obey the demon kings, you will fall into the hells.</p>


<p style="text-align:center"><strong> 2 &#8211; EXCESSIVE ZEAL</strong></p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: A-nan, hựu bỉ định trung, chư thiện nam tử, kiến sắc ấm tiêu, thọ ấm  minh bạch, thắng tướng hiện tiền, cảm kích quá phần. Hốt ư kì trung, sanh vô hạn dũng, kỳ tâm mãnh lợi, chí tề chư Phật. Vị tam tăng kì, nhất niệm năng việt.</em></strong><em> </em></p>
<p><strong>Translation: Further, Ananda, when good men in this samadhi see the form skandha dissolve and the feeling skandha clearly revealed, a surpassing appearance manifests and they are stirred to excess. Suddenly within it arises boundless valor: the mind keen and sharp, aspiring to equal the Buddhas, claiming to transcend three asamkhyeya eons in a single thought.</strong></p>
<p><strong>Commentary</strong>: <strong>Further, Ananda, when good men</strong> &#8211; including all good people cultivating the Buddhadharma &#8211; <strong>in this samadhi-state, see the form skandha dissolved and the feeling skandha clearly revealed</strong>: among the five aggregates, clearly knowing the form skandha has dissolved and fully, brightly knowing the feeling skandha.</p>
<p>Then <strong>a surpassing appearance manifests</strong> &#8211; a special, rare vision appears in the mind.</p>
<p>The cultivator feels very buoyant and at ease. Yet excess is as bad as deficiency: there is little difference between going too far and falling short of the goal &#8211; neither accords with the <em>Middle Way</em>. As when traveling: overshooting your destination is the same as not arriving. So in this samadhi-state one is stirred to excess, then <strong>suddenly gives rise to a valorous resolve, keen and sharp, aspiring to equal the Buddhas</strong> &#8211;</p>
<p>valorously vigorous as never before, deeming oneself equal to the Buddhas, saying the Buddha and oneself are the same &#8211; claiming to transcend the three asamkhyeya eons in a single thought.</p>
<p>Claiming to transcend the first, second, and third asamkhyeya eons in a single thought &#8211; hence declaring oneself a Buddha. Not only calling oneself a Buddha, but calling everyone Buddhas. Such a person has fallen into wrong knowledge and wrong views.</p>
<p>In fact, everyone has a Buddha in mind, but cultivation effort is needed to recognize the Buddha-nature. Even with determined cultivation, one cannot become a Buddha in a single thought &#8211; a very long time is required. Though that time may be shortened through understanding and practicing the right Dharma, becoming a Buddha in a single thought is still hardly possible. One who cultivates without a good spiritual friend's guidance, however bitterly he practices, will only grow wrong views throughout his cultivation: seeing that after such long cultivation it amounts to becoming a Buddha anyway, he decides he is a Buddha &#8211; elevating himself to equality with the Buddhas. This situation occurs as the feeling skandha dissolves: he says he is equal to the Buddhas. Indeed, with that single mistaken thought he joins Mara's retinue.</p>
<p><strong><em>Sutra text: Thử danh công dụng, lăng suất quá việt. Ngộ tắc vô cữu, phi vi thánh chứng. Giác liễu bất mê, cửu tự tiêu yết. Nhược tác thánh giải, tắc hữu cuồng ma, nhập kì tâm phủ, kiến nhân tắc khoa, ngã mạn vô tỉ. Kỳ tâm nãi chí, thượng bất kiến Phật, hạ bất kiến nhân. Thất ư chánh thọ, đương tòng luân trụy.</em></strong></p>
<p><strong>Translation: This is called over-exceeding vigor in effort. Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization. If awakened and undeluded, in time it vanishes of itself. But if interpreted as sagehood, a demon of madness enters the very heart, and on meeting people one boasts of oneself, incomparably arrogant &#8211; the mind to the point of above seeing no Buddhas, below seeing no people. Losing right samadhi, one is sure to fall.</strong></p>
<p>Commentary: Now you are hearing the <em>Shurangama Sutra</em> lectured: we have reached the very important chapter on the fifty demonic phenomena arising from the five skandhas. If cultivators do not understand these <em>Fifty Skandha-Demon Phenomena</em>, they will easily go astray in their effort. If you recognize these skandha-demon phenomena, you will not lose self-mastery and recklessly boast, pretending to be someone important. So I urge you to encourage your relatives and friends to come hear this section on the <em>Fifty Skandha-Demon Phenomena</em> lectured &#8211; it will help you know each kind of state in cultivation.</p>
<p><strong>This is called over-exceeding vigor in effort.</strong> This state occurs as a result of the cultivator's effort in practice: the consequence of excessive effort transforming the inner self-nature makes him so valorous. There is nothing wrong in being valorous and vigorous if you use it to advance your cultivation of the right Dharma. But you should not proudly say: &#8220;Oh! I am a Buddha!&#8221; Are you a Buddha? The Buddha taught the Tripitaka and the twelve divisions of sutras &#8211; how many divisions, how many pitakas have you spoken? You do not even understand the sutra pitaka &#8211; what talk of your being a Buddha? Is that not absurd? The Buddha spoke the entire sutra pitaka, while you have never spoken a single sutra or pitaka &#8211; how dare you proclaim you have become a Buddha? Even if you could master every principle and clearly lecture the Tripitaka and twelve divisions, at most you could call yourself a disciple of the Buddha, not a Buddha. But this person feels everyone in the world reveres and treasures the Buddha, so he wishes to become like the Buddha himself, so that after death people will make offerings. In fact after death he will be buried deep in the earth, turned to dust &#8211; nothing remaining with him at all.</p>
<p><strong>Recognized, it is without fault &#8211; not sage realization.</strong></p>
<p>It is perfectly fine to have such valorous vigor, so long as you understand: it is only a state, and does not mean you have realized the sage fruition. That state is not sage realization. <strong>If awakened thus and maintaining clarity, in time that state vanishes of itself.</strong></p>
